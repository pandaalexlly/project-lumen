# Project Lumen - Critical Mystery Review

**Desk review only; no unbriefed human session occurred.** This critiques the current [cognitive framework](PROJECT_LUMEN_COGNITIVE_MYSTERY_FRAMEWORK.md), [spatial rules](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md), [knowledge progression](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_PROGRESSION.md), and [ending structure](PROJECT_LUMEN_ENDING_STRUCTURE.md). The three voices below are analytical perspectives, not three actual testers. No new law, room, or solution is proposed here.

**Finding: revise the evidence and decision contracts before detailed room design.** The central insight is promising, but logical consistency alone does not establish that a blind player will infer it. In particular, the documents sometimes treat an experiment that *could* distinguish theories as though players will notice the need for it, be able to perform it, and understand its result. TASK-038B technically validates the smaller assembly, not human comprehension of even that prerequisite.

## Strongest parts

1. **The opening remains true.** The new interpretation enlarges the object's boundary rather than declaring the Cube observation lesson false. That preserves player trust and gives later discoveries a causal vocabulary.
2. **Fixed versus travelling structure is testable.** Keeping hub, sockets, contacts, and exterior ground fixed gives a way to distinguish a room exchange from global building motion or a map edit. Persistent repair and door state can distinguish *which* room moved from *where* it was seen.
3. **The anti-elevator criterion is concrete.** Empty movement, non-Cube observation, candidate exclusion, and persistent architectural members predict outcomes an ordinary passenger lift does not.
4. **Knowledge is not a stored flag.** Both routes remain physically accessible when lawful. An early correct guess is accepted, and records corroborate rather than activate the world.
5. **The proposed camera rule is constrained.** Treating a live lens as another direct observer, while stored footage remains evidence, is cleaner than inventing observation by memories, monitors, light, or narrative attention.

These are design strengths, not evidence that players will notice them or that a room-scale exchange is feasible.

## Reviewer 1: first-time blind player

**Likely experience.** “The Cube makes things move” is plausible around the connected apparatus. Repeated rooms also plausibly look like cost-saving architecture, a security system, or navigation error in an abandoned facility. I may not independently think to look for one repair across two sockets, especially if a change happens only when I am not watching. If I happen to redirect a camera and a wall changes later, I may credit the control as a remote door switch. The documents' diagnostic contrasts are convincing *once described*; the present design has not shown why I would set them up myself.

**Potential confusion rather than intrigue:**

- I need a memorable fixed reference and a distinct room identity in normal sight, not only in a designer's after-the-fact comparison. If both are subtle, I cannot tell whether I moved, the room moved, or I misremembered a symmetric corridor.
- A powered camera can prevent a transition invisibly. Unless I have already seen a simpler camera-only contrast, repeated failures will look like timer randomness or a broken puzzle. The live-feed-versus-recording distinction is coherent but not an intuitive first guess.
- A full room includes floor and walls. If I stand in it, almost every ordinary glance should observe it. An independent fixed shielding surface may solve the physics while feeling like an artificial “face this wall to travel” instruction. Its existence, width, and apparent facility purpose need proof before this becomes a player task.
- If I escape through C, I may feel finished. A later label of “bad ending” would be unfair unless the unresolved architectural question was tangible before I left. Conversely, if Q and M's doorway clearly show the final alignment early, I may infer the answer before the intended middle investigations. Early inference is not a failure, but it changes the promised pacing.

**Blind-player test:** after a first empty exchange, can I identify two *different* rooms and one *unchanged* fixed feature without being asked a leading question? If not, the revelation is being supplied by the document, not the world.

## Reviewer 2: puzzle designer

The proposed laws produce useful counterfactuals, but several are not yet reliable play-space affordances:

| Assumption | Design risk | Proof required before room layout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two occupied room sockets can exchange only when both complete shells are unobserved. | One visible wall, fixed camera, or inaccessible viewpoint may hold the pair indefinitely. “Both independently rearmed” can become an invisible extra condition. | An observer/state table for **both** composite states, every accessible fixed vantage, source controls, and recovery; no hidden wait caused by one module's unrearmed state. |
| A camera is a legible observer rather than a lock. | A player can toggle it while inadvertently watching the room; no movement follows, apparently disproving the camera theory. | A repeatable camera-only A/B contrast with player gaze and Beam held constant, plus a safe route for removing the player's own view. |
| Candidate observation transfers to rooms. | With only two fully occupied P/Q sockets, seeing a candidate surface may also mean seeing a current shell. The distinction can disappear in practice. | A geometrically credible case where a prospective member is visible through an aperture while both current shells are hidden, or remove candidate exclusion from the room-level learning claim. |
| Door and equipment state follow room identity. | A shared control network or paired prefabs can still explain synchronized settings. A door that starts open may never be manipulated or compared. | One identity-changing comparison in which unique structural damage, an ordinary changed condition, and unchanged fixed contacts agree. |
| Room-floor passenger inclusion extends TASK-038B. | The larger observation envelope makes comfortable self-concealment and safe mapped arrival far harder than the 4 x 4 m deck case. | A paper sightline/support proof for normal stances and looks at both sockets before treating room travel as a future puzzle. |

The reciprocal swap is not merely “the same system at a larger scale.” It is an atomic two-object transition with a joint candidate and independent rearm history; that new causal complexity could be the true stumbling block. The review does not recommend another mechanic. It asks that this extension be justified as essential to the mystery and made externally predictable. A two-socket binary exchange also weakens the teaching value of destination exclusion: without a third alternative, it often acts only as another unexplained veto.

## Reviewer 3: mystery structure editor

**The reveal may be a change of scale, not of worldview.** “Rooms are big quantum objects moving inside a fixed facility” is close to the player's earlier “objects move inside a stable facility.” The documents call the facility's *topology* quantum, but the emotional claim is stronger: “I misunderstood reality.” The story must demonstrate that a supposedly fixed spatial relationship—what a doorway connects to, which architecture defines a route—was a contingent property of *room identity*, not simply show a larger transport platform. This distinction must be clear before drafting individual mysteries.

**Duplication and exchange are different truths.** Shared construction plus later individual damage can prove two persistent room identities and their exchange. It does not, by itself, prove that an accident *created* a duplicate instead of staff building two similar modules. The spatial rules rightly call duplication's origin a historical starting condition. Decide whether that historical claim is indispensable to the final revelation or optional human context; otherwise the true ending may demand an inference only a document can certify while promising a non-lore-first mystery.

**The current ending tests a route more than a mind.** The true path can be reached by accidentally redirecting a camera, waiting, and walking through an open door. That must remain lawful without a knowledge flag, but it means the route cannot literally “require understanding.” The design can make the route *most naturally predicted* by understanding; only a pre-action prediction from an unbriefed player can show that happened. The bad path offers genuine survival, while the true path currently offers another exit and a better view of two sockets. The personal or historical consequence that makes the latter emotionally preferable is not yet established. This is not a request for a new reward system; it is a question about what the existing evidence and final perspective mean to the player.

**The middle may be too diagnostic or too vague.** If a unique repair, persistent open door, both sockets, fixed marks, and a camera-control contrast appear together, an attentive player could reach the full theory almost at once. If split too far apart, those same facts may never be connected. Non-linear discovery increases this tension: the design needs redundant ways to recall and compare evidence without arranging it as four lessons or withholding an otherwise lawful conclusion.

## Knowledge-point audit

“Encounterable” is weaker than “likely to be discovered”; “useful later” is weaker than “required by the finale.” This table separates them.

| Knowledge | Emerges from observation? | Future use / mental revision | Current weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current sight holds an object | Yes, in Awakening if the player performs a repeated look-away comparison. | Baseline prediction for every larger object. | Its spontaneous discovery has not been established by blind testing. |
| Observed candidate is excluded | Potentially, through the existing assembly's empty receiver comparisons. | Supports state selection and explains missing destinations. | In a two-occupied-socket swap it may only veto, not help choose; the finale might not use the player's distinction. |
| Beam is an artificial observer | Yes, with a real reach/no-reach contrast. | Prepares reasoning about other observer sources. | A player may read it as light/power, especially if the contrast changes several visible effects at once. |
| Fully supported passenger can travel | Technically present on the smaller assembly. | Enables the C route and changes player from experimenter to possible member. | The true empty-exchange route does not need it; room-scale transfer may become a distracting, harder echo of the smaller discovery. |
| Live camera is an observer | Only if a camera-only contrast is naturally available. | Explains a held pair and permits prediction of release. | Currently concentrated near the proposed ending, so it may feel like a final lock rather than a transferable law. |
| Internal state follows object identity | Yes, if a player can alter or reliably recognize a condition across placements. | Makes a door/wall discrepancy an identity question. | A pre-opened door and passive instrument may be insufficient to defeat “networked rooms” without an actual controlled comparison. |
| Room-scale exchange changes connectivity | Only after the player can compare both room identities against fixed references. | Supplies the intended reinterpretation of earlier routes. | Its decisive evidence may occur only *after* the player has solved the camera/release situation; explanation can arrive as a post-hoc reward. |

Before claiming a complete knowledge progression, assign each point at least one **observable consequence**, one **later prediction that uses it**, and one **reasonable wrong model it overturns**. Do not add a new challenge merely to fill a row. Where a point does not matter to the true route, call it a valuable optional insight rather than pretending the ending requires every lesson.

## Minimum acceptance test for the spatial revelation

Without explanatory text, a player should be able to gather all four facts, in any order, and use them to make one prospective prediction:

1. **Two identities:** M and N are simultaneously real and distinguishable by persistent, not just decorative, features.
2. **Fixed frame:** P/Q contacts and the hub/exterior reference stay in place as the rooms change.
3. **Whole-shell movement:** wall, doorway, ordinary door condition, and mounted equipment follow one identity in an *empty* exchange; the other room occupies the vacated socket.
4. **Causal observer:** watching a participating wall or a live camera sightline holds/excludes the correct configuration under a controlled comparison.

Then, **before** a final exchange, ask what should be at P and Q afterward and which fixed route will become possible. If players can only narrate the truth after seeing the ending, it is hindsight rather than an earned deduction. If they can explain the event as a lift, remote switch, synchronized duplicate rooms, or whole-building rotation with equal predictive success, the evidence chain is not yet discriminating enough.

## Required revisions before detailed room design

1. **State the exact intended revelation in player terms.** Is the essential correction “a wall belongs to a movable room,” “two distinct rooms exchange,” or “an accident duplicated one room”? The documents currently lean on all three, but only the first two are physically provable by the proposed comparisons. Mark the historical origin's role explicitly.
2. **Prove the P/Q observation and recovery envelope on paper.** Include both occupied states, camera/Beam/player views, candidate-only sight, individual rearm, fixed access, and a genuinely broad place from which an included player can avoid seeing the room shell. A beautiful room layout cannot repair a hidden impossibility here.
3. **Specify the *player-visible* camera-only comparison.** Identify what the player can hold constant, what they can actually change, and what result would refute “camera is just a door switch” without UI explanation. Do not design a final camera control until this contrast works.
4. **Define an evidence cadence rather than a room order.** Identify the earliest believable Cube-as-controller reading, the first troubling architectural discrepancy, the first independently trackable room identity, and the first controlled empty exchange. Test both early-guess and missed-clue paths. Avoid relying on a single precise repair being noticed.
5. **Clarify the two endings' differential payoff.** The C route must feel complete enough to be fair; the P route must reveal something more than a shortcut. Describe the physical comparison and unresolved human question each ending leaves, without adding flags, rewards, or a final speech.
6. **Audit the seven knowledge points against future predictions.** In particular, decide whether supported room travel and room-level candidate exclusion are essential, optional, or misleading in the ending model. Do not claim that a principle “changes future puzzles” solely because it can be demonstrated once.
7. **Validate the existing cognitive baseline.** TASK-038B's technical GO did not show that blind players infer collar membership, candidate exclusion, or supported travel. Record that uncertainty before building a higher-level reveal on it.

These are design and validation requirements, not authorization to implement new systems or redesign the current world.

## Questions to answer before implementation

- What exact observable event first makes “Cube controls machinery” a worse prediction than “the architectural member is part of the object”?
- What evidence distinguishes accident-created duplication from two purpose-built near-identical rooms, and must the player know the historical answer to understand the finale?
- How can the player see both room identities and fixed socket references often enough to compare them, without placing the entire explanation in one obvious panorama?
- In the two-occupied-socket exchange, when can a candidate member be seen while neither current shell is seen? If that comparison cannot be staged, what claim about room-level candidate observation should be withdrawn?
- What happens after only one room is reobserved? Can a player predict that wait state from the world rather than from designer notes?
- Can a standing person on a room floor genuinely conceal every participating wall/floor surface over ordinary movements, and understand why the fixed shielding is independent?
- What does a powered camera physically show the player about its aim and reach? How can a failed attempt be attributed to the camera rather than player gaze or Beam state?
- What earlier anomaly is reinterpreted *before* the true-route door aligns? What is the player's prospective prediction, not just their retrospective explanation?
- Why would a player who has survived via C care enough to investigate P/Q, and what truth does the P route physically confirm that C cannot?
- Which claims have passed unbriefed testing, and which are still only internally consistent design hypotheses?

**Review boundary:** no new mechanics, no altered ending design, no scene plan, and no human-readability success claim. The next decision should be about the unresolved evidence and spatial proofs, not implementation.
