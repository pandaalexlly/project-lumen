## 2. One apparatus, several relationships

### 2.1 Minimum physical vocabulary

Use one distinctive, rigid bearer integrating the later Cube, its cradle and a collar:

- **Cube mount:** visibly part of the bearer, not a loose sample resting on a pressure plate.
- **Cradle:** a broad load-bearing service deck. Its edge exposes the bearer rather than hiding it beneath an indistinguishable room floor.
- **Collar:** initially resembles the metal surround of an ordinary service opening. On inspection, its lower structural rib continues into the cradle and Cube mount.
- **Travelling signature:** an asymmetric gusset and a repaired fracture crossing the collar-to-bearer joint. Their shape, alignment and relation to the Cube remain unchanged in every placement. Do not duplicate this signature on other equipment.
- **Fixed reference:** an independently supported pier with a recognizable broken edge, beside a fixed aperture framing an outside structural landmark. These retain their relationship to the building and to one another.
- **Docking separation:** an inspectable clearance gap between bearer and receiving structure; the receiving feet/contact surfaces are visibly distinct from continuous structural joins.
- **Fixed shielding:** the already proposed independent service hood/wall. Its attachment to the building is traceable separately from the bearer. It supplies generous real occlusion, not darkness or a special viewing target.

The fracture is identity evidence, not a luminous clue. Its repair has a structural purpose; the docking wear has a contact cause. Readability comes from silhouette, relief, scale and accessible viewpoints, not membership colors, symbols or explanatory labels.

No operating cable needs to cross the moving boundary to establish this evidence. Do not add a cable-cutting task. Existing Beam equipment remains visibly outside the bearer; its coverage, not a fictitious power tether, explains its quantum effect.

### 2.2 Relationships across the existing placements

| Placement | Physical relationship | Initial interpretation | Later reinterpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: internal inspection | Collar fits an internal service opening; cradle meets a fixed maintenance approach | Ordinary doorway surround beside a specimen machine | An entire bounded structure is occupying this opening; the wall and approach merely receive it |
| B: internal return | The same rigid collar/cradle fits the second internal receiving relationship | Another machine station or a duplicate frame | The distinctive structure left A; its unchanged geometry now meets different fixed surroundings |
| C: exterior transfer | The same bearer fits the already proposed outside receiver and safe landing | Inaccessible service hardware with an apparently missing connection | The missing connection is a possible placement of the familiar structure, not a door awaiting a key |

These are different relationships to one fixed facility, not three newly designed rooms. No door opens into a secretly remapped corridor. A and B retain fixed access to inspection positions and source controls when the apparatus is absent. C's landing and outside route are ordinary fixed geometry.

Before-and-after comparisons must preserve recognizable surroundings. Do not arrange identical elevator lobbies or surround the player with a cab that hides every reference. Conversely, the fixed release cover must conceal every participating body/payload surface while release occurs. Actual authored lighting must separately avoid conspicuous watched shadow discontinuities; indirect evidence does not hold the state. The player inspects references before and after, not by watching the participating geometry transform.

## 4. Minimum physical evidence chain

Six evidence packages cover the six required claims. They reuse the same few features; they are not six clue objects to collect.

| ID / claim | Immediate evidence and curiosity | Comparison or experiment | What it establishes; limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E1 — A bounded moving structure exists | A doorway-like collar has an underside rib continuing toward the Cube. A visible seam alone could describe ordinary machinery, so E1 is not yet a quantum-membership proof |
| E2 — A non-Cube member belongs to it | The collar joint is continuous with the same bearer; the gusset/repair gives it a memorable identity | With the Cube hidden, hold by direct sight of the collar; conceal that too and compare. | E3 — Fixed surroundings remain fixed | The independent pier and aperture frame an outside landmark; receiving wear belongs to the fixed floor | Compare those relationships from fixed ground before/after and on return, while the travelling signature changes placement | Identifies the stable reference structure. | E4 — Another configuration exists | A second receiver has geometrically plausible contact surfaces and a different relation to the fixed facility | Find the same repaired joint and rigid Cube/cradle/collar arrangement there; observe the actual resting assembly and permit a later return | Demonstrates repeatable placement rather than a once-only disappearance. Wear predicts a possibility; an actual placement confirms it |
| E5 — A supported player can travel | Broad structural deck, ordinary human use wear, visible return access and an inward-facing joint worth inspecting invite boarding | Deliberately compare independent-floor and fully supported releases; compare aboard-and-watching with aboard-and-concealed | Establishes supported inclusion without cancelling observation. | E6 — The exterior is this structure's possible placement | From existing fixed survey access, exterior contacts match the bearer footprint and asymmetric clearance; beyond is safe ordinary ground | An empty C placement presents the same travelling signature in that receiving relationship; reobserve and recall it, then predict a supported journey | Connects identity to an outside route before final commitment. A matching generic platform, drawing or surprise vista is insufficient |

The minimum is not complete until E2's shared-observation comparison and the destination-exclusion comparison in section 5 are possible. Six attractive environmental clues without those experiments would still support “a Cube-powered lift.”

### Evidence strength and redundancy

- **Boundary:** continuous structural rib and docking separation are distinct cues; actual absence against fixed receiving geometry corroborates both.
- **Identity:** the asymmetric shape and repaired joint preserve an easily recognized relationship. Material resemblance alone is not an independent source.
- **Possible placement:** contact geometry suggests fit; repeatable actual occupation confirms it. Historical wear corroborates former use but does not certify a quantum destination.
- **Passenger:** structural support and human-scale use make the hypothesis reasonable; a reversible personal comparison tests it. Optional carried equipment is not part of the minimum.
- **Exterior escape:** observable C occupation corroborates its physical fit; a fixed onward landing establishes that arrival offers a real exit.

Do not rely on finding one tiny scratch. The paired shape/joint signature must be readable from ordinary inspection positions, including the exterior survey view. If it requires a zoom device or a text identification, revise scale or sightline; do not add a mechanic.

All evidence persists. No scratch appears only after a clue is read, and no dock is created after an internal trial. Missing the first relocation cannot remove the opportunity to discover the rule.

## 5. Causal experiments: what makes the evidence trustworthy

These are authoring obligations, not instructions placed in the game. They constrain the later spatial proof; no coordinates, scene geometry or test fixture are created here.

### 5.1 Separate observation from power, proximity and scripting

Provide a fixed-ground comparison position where:

1. The Cube is hidden by genuinely fixed structure while a substantial integrated collar surface is visible.
2. Turning within a comfortable range conceals the entire assembly's body/payload geometry, including directly visible edges and silhouettes. Under S2, cast shadows do not supply observation; check their presentation separately.
3. At least one complete alternative stays hidden, safe and eligible across both views.
4. Current inspection Beams are off or genuinely not reaching the assembly, and their settings remain unchanged between the compared views.
5. Seeing the collar is a real observation that rearms release; the hidden trial is not accidentally testing an already spent opportunity.

The player need not know this checklist. The surroundings must let their simple experiment mean what it appears to mean. A collar-only hold followed by a release with otherwise equivalent conditions is stronger than waiting at a motionless machine.

Watching a new member provides a transfer test: the player predicts that it also holds the whole. Watching the independent pier provides the contrasting case. This is the point where “the Cube operates architecture” ceases to be the most useful explanation.

A finite experiment cannot logically disprove an imagined invisible controller designed to imitate every observation. The goal is a compact, reusable explanation supported by visible boundaries and successful new predictions.

### 5.2 Separate present holding from excluding an empty placement

Retain the existing proposed sources: inspection Beam I can reach A or C through real sightlines; Beam R covers B. Do not add a selector or another station.

Use the same apparatus for an optional reversible comparison:

- With a real current observer on A, changing R does not release A.
- With current A released and C otherwise eligible, R covering prospective B prevents B; C remains available.
- With R removed, B becomes possible again if safe and hidden. This does **not** guarantee the next random result is B.
- If B and C are both observed while A has no current observer, no alternative is available; the assembly stays. Removing one actual exclusion makes that alternative available.
- A Beam reaching the prospective collar is enough to exclude the complete placement, even if the prospective Cube is hidden.

Repeated random outcomes alone are weak evidence. Prefer the inspectable all-alternatives-blocked versus one-alternative-open comparison when a player seeks certainty. The emitter field must actually reach the candidate envelope; a decorative pool of light or an untraceable ray is not evidence.

Player sight must not unintentionally cover the candidate being tested. Departing geometry must not be treated as permanent candidate cover. These are explicit unresolved spatial/query checks inherited from TASK-032, not matters solved by attractive dressing.

### 5.3 Give passenger curiosity a credible experiment

The inward face of the familiar collar joint can be inspected from the broad structural cradle. This supplies a reason to step onto it other than obeying a boarding instruction. Keep an accessible independent-floor view too; stepping aboard is a choice, not a compulsory trigger.

The player can see that an internal receiving placement has safe fixed return access. The same bearing surfaces and generous fixed cover used in the final journey are available for a reversible personal experiment. No newly introduced hood or special ride mode appears at the ending.

A supported player can inspect a member and hold the assembly; turning toward independent shielding removes their own observation only when all participating geometry is concealed. The hood itself must remain outside the assembly. At arrival the retained pose must still conceal transported body/payload surfaces; afterwards, ordinary looking reveals the unchanged local joint relation against different fixed surroundings. Cast-shadow continuity is an actual-light presentation check, not a hidden release or arrival veto.

A first accidental ride can provoke the hypothesis. Confirmation is a deliberate prediction and repeat, not merely arrival. Do not force an internal first ride, deny a lawful early C arrival, or reset the sources to manufacture a lesson.

First evidence must not depend on straddling the docking gap, standing on a strapped crate, touching a side panel or finding one invisible foot position. Direct-support, partial-support safety and concealed-arrival questions remain open contract risks. Broad clear geometry must make those edge cases irrelevant to the intended discovery, not invent exceptions to them.

### 5.4 Repeatability and recall

Every actual resting placement must be reobservable; every internal control approach remains on fixed geometry. A failed experiment is recoverable through the same rules, not a reset console added for this task.

| Situation | Existing-contract recovery requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Assembly at B | Reach controls by fixed access; observe/rearm actual B with R, exclude C with I, then remove R and player sight while A remains hidden and safe |
| Assembly at C, player inside | Observe/rearm actual C with I through the real survey aperture, keep B excluded by R, then remove I and player sight while A remains hidden and safe |
| Assembly at A | Direct sight or I holds it for inspection and boarding; actual observation permits a later release |
| Player has arrived at C | Safe fixed dismount and onward ground remain available; no instant look-back challenge or stay-until-exit flag is required |

These are inherited logical requirements, not verified ray diagrams. An unavailable cradle must never be needed to reach its own recall control. If actual geometry cannot satisfy the table, this evidence proposal needs revision before implementation.

## 6. Facility relationship and nonlinear access

### What looked ordinary

The collar reads as a service doorway surround because it fits an ordinary opening; the cradle reads as its maintenance threshold. The receiving wall, independent approach and fixed hood genuinely are building structure. The first impression is incomplete, not a dishonest mesh pretending to be welded into a wall.

### What is reinterpreted

The continuous collar/cradle bearer belongs to the Cube's object. The wall opening is a receiver. An apparently pointless outside contact surface is another receiving relationship. An instrument aimed at a vacancy is maintaining an exclusion, not monitoring broken equipment.

The fixed facility was designed to work with these possible placements. That is a meaningful architectural reinterpretation without making the entire facility quantum.

### How the player verifies the distinction

Trace the bearer and its isolation boundary; compare its travelling signature with stationary receiving wear; hold through the collar; release while looking only at independent architecture; inspect the old opening after the move. Both construction and behavior support the boundary.

Never infer membership from common paint, nearby equipment or any contact. A flexible service line, floor joint or matching fabrication style may connect functions without joining a quantum object.

### More than one path to the inference

- **Architecture-first:** notice the suspicious collar joint, inspect the vacant opening after a change, compare identity, then test whether observing the collar holds it. Investigate the already visible outside receiving relationship.
- **Observation-first:** the hidden Cube still will not move; discover the visible collar is holding it, follow its bearer, then recognize the same arrangement at another placement.
- **Exterior-first:** notice inaccessible receiving hardware from survey access, later recognize its fit to the bearer, then confirm C empty before considering a personal journey.

These are available interpretation routes, not promised traversable routes in a built scene. The later spatial plan must preserve their access without requiring an assembly that is absent. No four-wing attendance check is justified.

Existing future research-area purposes can corroborate the same chain without becoming one-rule-per-wing: Containment exposes joints and support; Power provides the distinction between ordinary services and observation; Signal exposes actual fields aimed at vacancies; Records can corroborate identity/history after direct investigation. No area owns an indispensable text explanation, and this document adds no rooms or wing content list.

## 7. Anti-teleport substitution test

A deck carrying a person is inherently transport. Styling it as abandoned research equipment does not solve the problem. Test each proposed *revelation*, not whether an ordinary elevator could share a prop.

| Revelation element in a weak design | Could a normal elevator replace it? | Required revision embodied in this proposal | What the replacement would fail to explain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| “A bounded platform moves.” | Yes; a cab has a boundary | Initially misclassify the collar as fixed architecture, then discover its continuous bearer and shared observation through a non-Cube surface | Why merely watching the apparent doorway holds the Cube/cradle as well, with controls unchanged |
| “Several parts move together.” | Yes; welded cab parts do | Combine the rigid travelling signature with collar-only and new-member observation predictions | Why the relevant boundary is the complete observed object, not just mechanically attached cargo on a Cube-triggered machine |
| “The building stays while a vehicle moves.” | Yes; ordinary lift surroundings stay fixed | Keep the independent reference visible during before/after investigation and show that viewing it, unlike the adjoining collar, permits release | Why two adjacent architectural surfaces have different causal roles under the same gaze, even without calling transport |
| “The same object reaches another station.” | Yes; matching landings are normal | Make configurations different fits within fixed architecture, with a prospective non-Cube member's observation excluding the whole fit | Why watching an empty structural volume removes that possibility instead of selecting a floor |
| “I can ride the structure.” | Yes; boarding alone is not the revelation | Contrast supported-but-watching, empty-and-hidden and supported-and-hidden cases; retain the rider's observer role and require independently fixed cover | Why boarding is neither activation nor immunity to sight, and why identifying which cover belongs to the object matters |
| “A vehicle takes me outside.” | Yes; an exterior stop supplies transport, not reinterpretation | Let the player recognize C's physical fit and identity before riding, then reason that selective observation leaves that configuration available | Why escape follows from the boundary and the remaining possible placement rather than an exit button or floor destination |
| “Old wear shows people travelled.” | Yes; transport history is generic | Demote wear to corroboration and a reason to experiment; do not use it as the reveal | Wear alone explains none of the causal distinctions above and is not counted as proof |

A simple lift could reproduce the *outcome* of travelling outside. It cannot replace the accepted explanation and solution without importing the observation/boundary constraints that define this mystery. Unusual lateral movement, an absent shaft or a dramatic vista alone would merely turn the lift into a teleport lift; none is credited as the deciding evidence.

Failure criterion: a player can explain every useful action as “call the machine, stand in it, select outside” without accounting for observed members, independent cover or excluded placements. If that remains the strongest explanation, revise evidence and spatial relationships rather than add controls or laws.

Success criterion for later evaluation: before travelling, the player can predict what stays with them, what stays with the facility, why watching only the collar prevents departure, and why observing an empty unwanted placement helps. Calling the apparatus an elevator does not invalidate those predictions.

## 8. Final escape knowledge model

### Must know for an informed, deliberate escape

| Causal understanding | Evidence basis | Practical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This Cube, bearer, cradle and collar are one observed object | E1, E2, E4 | Concealing only the Cube is insufficient; the apparent doorway travels too |
| The receiving building, cover and onward landing are independent | E1, E3, E6 | Looking toward fixed cover can remove sight without leaving structural support; dismount has ordinary safe ground |
| Player sight and all reaching enabled Beams still count | Collar-only experiment and boarded hold | Boarding does not cancel any observer; current observation can hold the structure during preparation |
| Observation of an empty complete placement excludes it | Section 5.2 | Preserve the internal alternative's exclusion, rather than turn every source off |
| Full direct support includes the player, not merely touching or standing nearby | E5 | Stand on the broad structural deck; release empty if remaining on independent floor |
| C is a real placement of this same structure and provides a fixed outside route | E6 | Outside is a reasoned destination, not faith in a hidden ending trigger |
| Actual source state matters; all current observation must cease | Direct experiments with unchanged controls and inspectable field coverage | Remove any current inspection and own sight while retaining the desired candidate exclusion |

For the proposed normal case: A is current; R observes the complete relevant B envelope; C is hidden, safe and unoccupied; I no longer observes A; the player is fully supported and no current member/payload geometry is in view. Under those conditions C is the sole alternative. The ordinary release carries the whole structure and passenger there. Indirect evidence alone cannot hold, exclude or rearm it under S2.

That is a sufficiency statement, not a new state-selection rule or a required checklist of gestures. If R is off, B may remain possible; if I still sees A, it stays; if the player stays on independent floor, it may go empty. No clue read repairs these conditions.

The player need not know timing numbers. Waiting is an ordinary response; broad cover, preserved observation while boarding and safe dismount prevent timing choreography. Recall and actual reobservation enable further experiments when needed, not a ritual demanded on every successful run.

### May misunderstand

- The words “quantum,” “coherence,” “configuration,” “entanglement” or “elevator.”
- The apparatus's exact historical purpose, who used it, and why its observers remained active.
- Whether its builders discovered or engineered the phenomenon.
- Why the possible placements exist at a deeper physical level.
- Nonessential service functions and the identities of former personnel.

A player may retain uncertainty about history and still have a correct predictive model. Do not demand a lore answer to validate escape.

### Must not need

- Collected records, room attendance, discovery flags, a journal state or a completed dialogue.
- An ordered tour, a compulsory failed ride or a prescribed number of relocations.
- A hidden destination switch, inventory item, password, exact instructions or final-only activation.
- Precise camera angles, pixel hunting, numerical release timing or knowledge of probe sampling.
- Darkness as blindness, a moving hood treated as fixed cover, or an internal-observer exemption.
- Engine terminology, collision tags or knowledge of an unexplained prospective-camera veto.
- The original Awakening Cube being secretly part of the later apparatus.
- Seeing the actual transition or accepting an explanatory cutscene.

These are requirements for understanding the planned solution, **not knowledge conditions enforced by the game**. A legal accidental exterior arrival is valid success. Completion does not prove the intended revelation occurred.
