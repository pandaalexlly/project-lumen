# Project Lumen — Four-Area Puzzle Architecture V2

**Status: area-level design, not room construction or a human-readability pass.** This revises the puzzle *roles* in [Four-Area Core Puzzles](PROJECT_LUMEN_FOUR_AREA_CORE_PUZZLES.md) and [Area Mystery Layout](PROJECT_LUMEN_AREA_MYSTERY_LAYOUT.md) against [Knowledge Architecture V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md), the [Complete Player Journey](PROJECT_LUMEN_COMPLETE_PLAYER_JOURNEY.md), and the [Core Mystery Cognitive Integration Plan](PROJECT_LUMEN_CORE_MYSTERY_COGNITIVE_INTEGRATION.md). The isolated M/N, attached-state, and camera prototypes establish technical contrasts, **not** unbriefed comprehension or a connected final-facility layout. No scene, mechanic, new moving root, or detailed room is authorized here.

## One mystery, four kinds of work

Awakening remains the completed, truthful prologue. The player has encountered Cube relocation and learned that player sight and an actually reaching Beam constrain it (K1/K3). Their useful incomplete theory is: *unusual objects move inside a normal facility*. The four research areas do not each dispense a rule. Their different jobs give the player four independent ways to ask **what was observed, which material object was present, what stayed fixed, and what connection existed at the time**. A clue can be seen before it is understood, and no area records a solved state.

The fixed Central Atrium is the common measuring frame, not a fifth lesson or a level selector. Its plant, vestibule, conduits, and P receiver are dependable references on every visit. The proposed bounded shells M and N can exchange fixed P/Q positions while retaining their distinct repair histories; the Atrium and whole facility do **not** transform. A second fixed reference at Q must be reachable from Signal-side/perimeter circulation. The A/B/C coherent assembly and its independent receivers remain a separate bounded system. Exterior C is genuine ground and an honest escape path; proposed exterior spur X is fixed ground that an existing M doorway could meet at P. The geometry and player readability of the full P/Q/X arrangement are still gates.

| Area as a workplace | Question it keeps alive | Primary physical contribution | Other areas needed to check the first answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Records** — survey and repair history | “Did an address or a material room acquire this history?” | Fixed survey datum plus two distinct, traceable structural histories. | Atrium P, Signal Q, and Power's attached/fixed comparison. |
| **Power** — supply and observation services | “Did power move it, or did sight and connection determine what could happen?” | Beam reach versus power, fixed contacts versus carried condition, a genuinely vacant candidate where available. | Awakening's K3, Containment's member/receiver, and later P contact. |
| **Containment** — handling an apparent specimen | “Where does the object end, and what may it carry?” | Collar-only hold, travelling repair/deck versus fixed A/B/C beds, supported travel to real C. | Power's source/connection evidence, Signal's possible unseen observer, and Atrium's room boundary. |
| **Signal** — surveillance and exterior survey | “Who is still looking, and what occupies the other fixed approach?” | Same-powered live lens sightline contrast on a known member; independent Q/exterior reference. | Awakening's observer rule, Containment's known member, Records' shell histories, Atrium P. |

This is a **web of comparisons**. Records can be visited before a player believes rooms move; Signal can offer a Q view before its camera is understood; Containment's exterior can be found before the player names an assembly. The most economical test route in the cognitive-integration plan is not a mandatory facility route. K-numbers below are designer vocabulary, never player-facing objectives.

## Records — a history that an address cannot own

1. **Initial belief.** Similar construction and contradictory surveys suggest clerical error, renovation, or repeated standardized rooms. A player arriving directly from Awakening still has every reason to regard walls as fixed. A returning player may suspect larger moving objects but not know whether a particular scar is attached to a shell or fixed to P.
2. **Existing knowledge used.** K1 makes repeat visits meaningful; K6's distinction between an object's current attached condition and a fixed socket helps, if already discovered. Neither is a file-reading prerequisite. Records must be intelligible enough to raise a question for someone who has not visited Power or Containment.
3. **New understanding gained.** The durable shift is from “the room *at this address* was repaired twice” to “two coexisting structures have different repair histories, and an address can meet either.” This contributes to K6/K7 but cannot prove exchange from paperwork alone. Dates and motives remain historical questions; present material identity can be tested physically.
4. **Main physical experiment.** Inspect a fixed survey support and the load-bearing repair it frames. Walk the ordinary fixed P↔Q comparison route, find M's integrated seam/opening and N's different wound/solid face **at the same time**, and note which receiver scar each is beside. After a lawful empty reciprocal exchange, revisit the survey support and both sockets; predict which complete structural package will appear at each. A mounted ordinary condition, where present, follows its shell rather than the record's address. No document read triggers or certifies the change.
5. **Misinterpretation path and fairness.** An inaccurate survey or renovation remains plausible after one discrepancy. It fails only when both incompatible histories coexist, trade fixed contexts, and remain attached through a repeatable comparison while the survey instrument does not move. The player must be able to inspect both shells before an exchange without needing a named M/N diagram. A single spectacular changed frontage is insufficient.
6. **Later reinterpretation.** Power's contact evidence distinguishes the fixed socket from a carried state; Containment's repaired collar makes a larger-than-Cube boundary thinkable; Signal's Q view independently checks the other shell. On a revisit, a staff note can be honest about what occupied P when written yet wrong as a timeless description of “the P room.” Records contributes human stakes and provenance, never an answer archive or a required page count.

Records' visible materials belong at used survey benches and fixed sighting instruments. An optional tucked-away repair comparison may deepen the human story but must not be the only way to recognize M/N. The actual scars, door/solid face, fixed datum, and reciprocal before/after view carry the deduction. An early player who forms the right hypothesis may test it immediately.

## Power — the source, the carried setting, and the fixed contact

1. **Initial belief.** Half-operational machinery invites “restore power and the transport works.” A lit socket or Beam control can look like a destination selector; a dark output can look like the travelling object's condition was lost. These are reasonable interpretations of real facility equipment.
2. **Existing knowledge used.** Awakening has **already** established K1/K3. Power does not teach “Beam equals vision” for the first time. It asks the player to apply real Beam reach to a larger member or a truly vacant candidate, and to distinguish that observation effect from electrical supply. K6 may be inferred here before or after Containment. K2 may be strengthened only by an isolated empty-candidate view at A/B/C, never by a binary P/Q occupation that cannot separate current hold from destination exclusion.
3. **New understanding gained.** “Powered” and “observing” are not synonyms. A Beam that reaches a participating surface can constrain a current state; a live fixed contact can provide output only when a compatible object occupies it. The object keeps its *present* attached setting through movement, while fixed conduits and contact scars remain. This is K3 applied, K2 where geometrically demonstrable, and K6 as an ownership inference—not a new generator command.
4. **Main physical experiment.** Trace one fixed conduit to its receiver contact and inspect a recognizable repaired travelling member with an ordinary attached condition. Compare a Beam that actually reaches that member with the same powered source missing it, holding other observers and candidate eligibility constant. Let the member lawfully relocate while empty; compare the unchanged old contact to the member's still-attached condition at its new bed. Change that ordinary condition and predict its value and A's live output if the member later returns. A B-OFF → A-OFF result rejects snapshot recall. If a candidate-only sightline really exists, observe only the vacant candidate with current member hidden, then remove that view and compare lawful outcomes without claiming to have *selected* a destination.
5. **Misinterpretation path and fairness.** “The generator called it” survives a mere switch toggle; it fails a reach/no-reach contrast with power unchanged. “A saved the old ON state” survives A→B→A; it fails only after the attached condition is changed at B and remains changed at A. A lamp going dark at B must be traceable to an absent fixed supply, not presented as an unexplained reset. Visible leads, scars, and travel of the attached side do more work than a maintenance card naming the rule.
6. **Later reinterpretation.** The P contact becomes a fixed reference when M/N move. A stubborn room may be watched rather than underpowered; Signal's camera is another actual sightline, not a security key. M's existing doorway meeting X is an external placement relationship, not a breaker that opens a wall. Power records may accurately describe outages while being incomplete explanations of a quantum hold.

Power remains a service route with plausible cooling, damage, and working equipment; its action is a *comparison*, not “restore three substations.” The isolated state-persistence fixture technically validates the attached/fixed contrast, but no unbriefed player has yet shown they notice it or transfer it to room architecture.

## Containment — the apparent machine has a bounded history

1. **Initial belief.** The familiar Cube appears to sit inside an ordinary cradle, collar, and deck. Repeated A/B/C beds look like stations of a transport machine. A person-sized deck naturally suggests that stepping aboard activates a lift.
2. **Existing knowledge used.** K1/K3 from Awakening suggest a watched/unwatched test. K2 helps only where an empty bed can be viewed without seeing the current assembly. K5 is useful if a camera still sees a member, but must not be a hidden, unexplained veto. K6's repair/condition continuity can be noticed here before Power gives its fixed-supply contrast.
3. **New understanding gained.** The question changes from “what does the Cube operate?” to “which surfaces belong to this one bounded object?” A non-Cube collar member can hold the whole assembly; its repair and supported deck travel while independent beds stay. A wholly supported rider who ceases all relevant observation can accompany a lawful move (K4), but remains an observer if looking at a member. Boarding is not a command. The true surprise is **object boundary and relationship**, not transportation.
4. **Main physical experiment.** From fixed inspection access, conceal the Cube while leaving only a collar member visible; compare the hold to a broad fully concealed release with Beam/camera and candidate lines accounted for. Identify the same asymmetric repair at a new receiver and the unchanged bed/hood left behind. Where the geometry allows, watch only a vacant candidate to test K2 separately. From safe positions, compare full deck support while a member remains visible against full support and full concealment; use fixed seams and exterior landmarks to verify passenger inclusion at C. Do not require a hazardous partial-support stunt.
5. **Misinterpretation path and fairness.** “The Cube powers a lift” initially explains repeated beds and a deck. It cannot explain a collar-only visual hold, the same integrated repair travelling with the deck, a fixed bed left empty, and boarding that does nothing while a member is seen. A player must be able to inspect the collar without inadvertently seeing the Cube; full concealment must be comfortable, not an exact camera-angle trick. A no-move attempt may have a real observer or candidate veto and needs a diagnosable retry path, not a scripted success on boarding.
6. **Later reinterpretation.** Containment makes it credible that an apparently architectural wall or floor at P might belong to M/N. Power explains what remained at the receiving bed; Records shows why a room's repair history might be mistaken for an address history; Signal explains a stubborn assembly state through another live observer. C is genuine exterior ground and can give a fixed perspective on Q/X. Ordinary survival via C remains valid even if the player never solves the room exchange.

Handling sheets and repair accounts belong near actual work. They can show what staff thought they were touching, not tell the player how to ride. The existing A/B/C technical work does not prove a first-time player will infer collar membership, support, or K2; these remain separate human/spatial gates.

## Signal — the unseen witness and the second address

1. **Initial belief.** CCTV documents accidents or controls security doors; its footage might “keep watching.” The outer survey view may look like an unrelated duplicate room or a teleport destination. A player can reach Signal before knowing K6/K7 and should find ordinary monitoring work, not an exit-control booth.
2. **Existing knowledge used.** K1/K3 make a camera-as-observer hypothesis fair. A known moving member from Awakening/Containment or an equivalent already understood bounded object is needed for a clean sightline test; K6 helps track its identity but is not a logical camera prerequisite. Records and the Atrium provide independent M/N and P references for reading Q. K5 must be evidenced *before* a consequential Q camera hold is made the only obstacle.
3. **New understanding gained.** A **live powered lens with actual direct view** is another observer (K5); camera power, console interaction, and stored footage are not selectors or old-state memories. Separately, Q's fixed contact and exterior buttress provide an independent address against which the occupant's repair/door or wound/solid face can be checked. Signal contributes two linked but not co-located answers: *why a known object stayed* and *which room Q currently meets*.
4. **Main physical experiment.** On a known member, keep the same camera powered and the member's condition, player view, Beam reach, candidate B, and rearm state controlled. Compare clear lens-to-member sight with a physical obstruction or off-member aim. With the lens blocked but the player still watching, there is no move; after all actual views are absent, a legal move becomes possible. Repeat and verify the same object/condition survived. Then inspect Q's fixed buttress/contact and current shell from ordinary survey/perimeter access; compare both shells and P/Q after an **empty** reciprocal exchange, using fixed ground rather than a monitor image as the decisive reference.
5. **Misinterpretation path and fairness.** The camera can first be dismissed as security equipment. A powered lens holding a known object contradicts the player's “nothing watches it” account, but a single failed move is weak evidence. If moving a cover precedes relocation, “the cover is a switch” remains plausible until player-sight hold and same-powered optical-path comparisons are predicted and repeated. A recorded image must not silently count as a live source. Q cannot be an invisible CCTV veto: its lens target, power, occlusion, and any observed candidate must be inspectable, and the player must have a fixed comparison and recovery route in either M/N occupation.
6. **Later reinterpretation.** An earlier live lens can explain a stubborn Containment or room state without changing the quantum law. Q's stable outer contact disproves whole-building movement when M/N identities trade P/Q. Signal's survey materials and ordinary footage can corroborate *when* staff saw one shell, not teach the movement rule. A proposed Q lens may constrain a later exchange only if players first predict the effect of its actual sightline; turning it away merely removes an observer, never selects M@P.

The isolated camera greybox passes a powered-clear versus physically blocked technical test with player-view control; human discovery and the “cover is not a key” interpretation remain **UNVALIDATED**. The fixture contains no Beam and does not by itself validate a three-source or Q-wall transfer.

## Distributed information and the hidden, fair true-route question

No wing owns the answer. Common paperwork sits where that job used it; misleading notes are honest local diagnoses; contradiction material is paired with physical features a player can inspect; later historical/core accounts deepen human meaning. A document's *meaning* may be hidden until a revisit, but the facts needed for a lawful route cannot be locked behind a rare page, mandatory reading, or a prior ending. Optional material may describe how staff were misled by an address, why a lens was installed, or who repaired a collar; it may not name the quantum law or activate a socket.

| Essential route evidence | Available from normal investigation | Why it does not give away the whole answer alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **M has an existing usable doorway; N has a solid face.** | Records' fixed survey view and ordinary Atrium/Signal approaches expose integrated repairs and both shells. | A first view can still suggest renovation or two conventional rooms; it does not show reciprocal movement. |
| **P/Q are fixed addresses; M/N are persistent occupants.** | Atrium plant/P scar, Signal-side Q buttress/contact, Power-side fixed supply, and repeatable empty exchange. | Each single clue is compatible with ordinary maintenance; the two-sided before/after prediction supplies proof. |
| **A live observer can hold a participating shell.** | Awakening's sight/Beam rule and Signal's controlled camera test on a known member; any later P/Q lens must have a real inspectable line. | It explains a hold, not which of two legal occupations is desired. No camera console is a route key. |
| **A fixed exterior spur X exists at P, separate from C.** | Ordinary exterior/perimeter views, including C if reached, plus fixed survey and P-side architectural seams. The essential relation cannot exist only on a hidden map. | Without room identity, X can look like inaccessible service infrastructure; without M@P, N's solid face appears to end the approach. |
| **M's current condition and P placement together can connect to X.** | Track M's attached opening/condition across occupations, inspect P/X independently, then make a prospective M@P prediction. | The connection is a spatial consequence, not a solved-flag reveal or new final device. |

The proposed true route is therefore: if a lawful **empty** exchange yields `M@P, N@Q` and M's existing doorway is usable, its opening meets the fixed X spur. The player may infer and take it on a first visit. No document count, four-wing completion, camera-off flag, Bad Ending replay, or named knowledge state is required. Camera and Beam views matter only if they actually reach a current member or relevant candidate. The exterior C/ordinary evacuation route is genuine survival, not a fake exit. The proposed down-only evacuation lift beyond C reaches **distinct fixed lower landings** with progressively fewer built connections; it does not switch a quantum state or transform the facility. Optional lower fragments can recast Records' addresses, Power's contacts, Containment's repair, and Signal's lens history, but cannot be route credentials or the only explanation. K8's proposed original/duplicated/core history can recontextualize the human story but still lacks a nontextual cross-site proof; it must **not** be a hidden eligibility condition or be treated as established by M/N alone.

## Exploration and access without a checklist

- Awakening restoration leads to the fixed Atrium. The four work approaches are legible by use, damage, sound, and visible infrastructure, not by mechanic-revealing titles or quest markers. Fixed service/perimeter links should allow revisits and more than one way to compare P/Q where geometry requires it; those links are still spatial obligations, not declared built routes.
- Records, Power, Containment, and Signal offer evidence in **multiple orders**. Architecture-first players may see competing shell histories before learning why a wall can move. Observation-first players may suspect a camera after Beam experience. Exterior-first players may reach C and notice Q/X from outside. Each can return to reinterpret an earlier clue; none is refused for lacking a read or completion flag.
- The first Atrium visit establishes fixed memory, not an exposition panorama of both shells, both contacts, lens ray, and X. Later proof uses several ordinary viewpoints and repeated visits. Do not hide an essential object until a knowledge stage, or make one document the only bridge between viewpoints.
- Access friction must have an ordinary spatial or safety cause: a route is physically distant, damaged, currently meets N's solid face, or depends on lawful supported travel. “Complete Power to unlock Signal” and similar sequencing are forbidden. Every legal M/N resting state needs fixed-ground access, observation-preserving comparison, a separate comfortable blind release, rearm, and recovery.

## Acceptance gates before detailed area rooms

1. **Spatial closure:** paper-prove both P/Q occupations; independent fixed views of both shells; camera/Beam/player current and candidate sightlines; a broad blind release distinct from the comparison path; safe empty exchange; M doorway/X clearance; and recovery from either occupation. The isolated M/N fixture does not prove connected-world geometry.
2. **Causal contrasts:** K2 requires a real empty-candidate trial at A/B/C; K4 requires safe support and full-concealment evidence; K5 requires a same-powered-lens on/blocked contrast on a *known* member before any essential Q hold; K6 requires a changed attached condition returning without snapshot reset. Existing technical prototypes support K5/K6/K7 components, not all final-area relationships.
3. **Unbriefed cognition:** test the four-phase [integration plan](PROJECT_LUMEN_CORE_MYSTERY_COGNITIVE_INTEGRATION.md) and then test order variation. Players must predict A/OFF after B/OFF, a camera-only hold versus blocked lens with player-view control, both M/N identities after exchange, and the fixed M@P/X relationship *before* seeing outcomes. If they solve by reading one file or toggling a cover at random, the mystery is not validated.
4. **Historical claim discipline:** do not call K8 or three-site duplication proven by two exchanging room shells. Before K8 becomes a required revelation, define continuous fixed cross-site references and an object-specific comparison the player can perform. If that proof fails, keep site history as optional interpretation rather than gate the true route.
5. **Presentation restraint:** visible purpose, wear, sightlines, and repeatable consequences must bear the causal load. Documents may ask questions and carry human history, never supply the only operative rule. No tutorial labels, glowing object boundaries, area-clear banners, forced wing order, or hidden knowledge variables.

**Decision:** this is the proposed final *puzzle-role architecture*, not approval to build final rooms. The technical prototypes make its central contrasts feasible; the connected P/Q/X spatial proof, K2/K4 presentation, four-phase cognitive transfer, and true-route readability remain unvalidated. If a gate fails, revise the smallest evidence or geometry link instead of adding a mechanic or explanatory lock.
