# Project Lumen - Cognitive Mystery Framework

**Status: design proposal only.** Nothing here is implemented or established by human playtesting. This proposal deliberately reopens decisions in [the earlier core framework](CORE_MYSTERY_AND_KNOWLEDGE_FRAMEWORK.md) and [assembly law contract](COHERENT_ASSEMBLY_LAW_CONTRACT.md): those documents rejected literal whole-facility motion and deferred live cameras and simultaneous assemblies. TASK-038B proves only one bounded assembly at A/B/C. The new brief asks for a broader facility-scale mystery; it does **not** retroactively make cameras or exchanging rooms working systems. Keep the implemented opening and assembly rules intact until the new laws pass separate design, feasibility, and player-readability gates.

## The intended correction

The player's first theory should be useful, not false: observation constrains a Cube moving within a mostly stable research facility. The deeper error is assigning the boundary of the anomaly too narrowly. Some seemingly ordinary rooms are bounded architectural objects with possible placements. Two look-alike room structures are distinct, persistent objects that can exchange sockets. The facility's *usable spatial configuration* is therefore quantum even though its hub, circulation spine, and exterior reference ground are fixed. This is not a claim that the entire map teleports, that the Awakening experiment was staged, or that a wall can become a door without physical structure changing.

The human question remains concrete: **why do reliable accounts of this place disagree, and why does an apparently complete building have routes that seem to end in the wrong places?** The first answer is damaged machinery and escaped specimens. The later answer is that investigators trusted coordinates and doorways as identifiers when they should have tracked the identities of the structures occupying them. The final thought should be approximately: *I learned the rule correctly on the Cube, but I kept applying it to the wrong-sized object.*

This is a controlled misunderstanding, not a withheld definition. Every major correction needs a repeatable, nontextual experiment. A player who guesses the full truth early may use it early. The game does not inspect their knowledge or require a route-completion flag.

## Mental-model trajectory

| Period | Reasonable player belief | Evidence that makes it reasonable | Observation that should revise it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Awakening | “That specimen relocates when I cannot see it.” | The Cube's before/after placements; direct sight and Beam hold it. | None: this remains true. Later content broadens the category of specimen. |
| Connected worksite | “The Cube powers or controls a transfer machine.” | A Cube sits amid a bearer, deck, collar, power instruments, and receiving beds. | With the Cube hidden and power unchanged, seeing only a participating collar holds the whole assembly; empty receiving positions can be excluded. |
| First repeated architecture | “A machine moved contents between two otherwise normal rooms,” or “these two rooms are duplicates.” | Similar shells, contradictory door/equipment locations, old surveillance hardware. | A unique post-incident repair, an opened door, and attached equipment retain their mutual arrangement while the *entire bounded shell* changes fixed socket. A fixed pipe and floor mark remain at the old socket. |
| Surveillance discrepancy | “The room sometimes refuses to move; perhaps it is scripted or broken.” | The player and Beam appear to be out of view; a camera looks like mundane dressing. | A powered, unobstructed fixed camera predicts holds and exclusions; changing its real sightline changes the outcome while other conditions stay constant. Its recorded image is not the observer. |
| Spatial correction | “The room teleports me,” or “the whole building shifted.” | Entering from one fixed corridor and emerging beside another is a dramatic experience. | Empty exchanges occur; the hub's unique fixed references never move; two room identities trade sockets, carrying walls, door condition and mounted equipment. The player can later travel by full support without exempting their own sight. |
| Final synthesis | “An exit must be unlocked or discovered behind a secret wall.” | An interior approach terminates at a solid wall; an exterior spur seems disconnected. | A particular room's already-open doorway fits that spur when the *room* occupies the interior socket. The previously seen wall belongs to the other room, not a locked facade. |

The order is a set of plausible revisions, not six mandatory acts. Architecture-first players may notice duplicate damage before camera causality; camera-first players may understand an artificial observer before identifying the room boundary. The physical world must support either path.

### Earlier experiences that must change meaning

| Observed event | First defensible reading | Later physical reinterpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I entered from the left but returned beside a right-hand corridor.” | A lift, shortcut, or confusion in a repetitive building. | The supported player and the room's complete shell changed socket while the left/right fixed approaches did not. The two fixed approaches can be compared afterward. |
| “That door became a wall.” | A security shutter closed or the game replaced a room. | The door remains on module M in its prior hinge state; module N's intact wall now occupies the same fixed opening. Both identities are inspectable elsewhere. |
| “The machine vanished, but its connection remains.” | A piece of equipment teleported out of a fixed room. | Mounted equipment, its enclosing wall and floor departed together; the socket's independent pipe end and contact scar remained. |
| “The same room has a different arrangement.” | A scripted reset, reconstruction, or unreliable memory. | M and N shared original construction but have different later repairs and retained door/equipment states. They exchanged placements rather than one room changing its contents. |

Each reinterpretation needs a before/after view from fixed ground. A written account can prompt the comparison, but if the player cannot verify the third column physically, the account is lore rather than mystery evidence.

## Misconceptions to cultivate, and ones to eliminate

- **Useful:** specimen teleportation, Cube-as-controller, ordinary security-camera surveillance, machinery failure, duplicated construction, and an elevator interpretation. Each predicts some early facts and can be separated by one later comparison.
- **Dangerous:** “the game changes rooms behind my back without a stable law,” “the opening taught a fake rule,” “all lights or photographs count as observers,” or “the story requires finding a secret document.” These indicate missing physical evidence or inconsistent presentation, not a need for explanatory UI.
- **Especially dangerous:** a spectacular player ride with no earlier empty-room exchange. That proves transportation, not mistaken architectural identity. The player must have had a chance to identify a travelling wall, an independent fixed reference, and two distinct room histories *before* a personal ride becomes the whole reveal.

## Evidence grammar and placement

Every important inference should have three different supports rather than three copies of a log:

1. **Immediate physical question:** a continuous structural seam, a duplicate pre-incident fracture, an independent fixed contact, a doorway that does not fit the socket behind it, or a camera with a physically legible sightline.
2. **Controlled contrast:** compare the same socket before and after an unobserved interval, or compare both sockets from fixed circulation. One changing variable must make a prediction (which surface was seen, which camera could see, which room's repaired joint moved).
3. **Durable corroboration:** later revisit from another route, inspect an unpowered camera's history, or read an optional record that dates the incident without naming the rule. A record may explain *why staff cared*, never the only way to learn *what changes*.

Evidence must survive non-linear arrival. A player may first see the exterior socket, the camera, or a duplicate shell. Each encounter should pose a question that later evidence answers. Do not force a “correct” first reading through voice-over, map icons, colored quantum paint, required scans, or a room-per-rule sequence.

The facility should preserve a recognizable fixed hub and service spine. Their unchanging scratches, cables, windows, and routes are measurement references, not arbitrary safe zones. Paired room modules should have both shared pre-incident construction features and individually acquired damage. Shared features support a duplication history; later unique wear lets a player track **which** room moved. A single feature cannot do both jobs reliably.

## Structural inspiration, not borrowed story

The useful lesson from *Ever17* and *Remember11* is to control the relationship between the player, viewpoint, and available evidence so that an earlier scene remains true but acquires another meaning. Their creators discuss aligning player/protagonist knowledge, maintaining mysteries across possible orders, and the readability risk of a complicated structure. Project Lumen should apply those *design constraints*, not copy their characters, timeline, perspective device, or explanations. The player here tests a spatial theory through first-person movement and repeatable world behavior. Sources: [Infinity Plus creator interview](https://adayem.wordpress.com/2018/04/27/infinity-plus-premium-interview/) and [Remember11 visual fan book creator interviews](https://adayem.wordpress.com/2019/01/15/visual-fan-book-interviews/) (fan translations of primary interviews).

## Decision tests before layout or implementation

Ask unbriefed participants what moved, what remained fixed, and what they predict will happen if they watch only a wall, watch an empty socket, or redirect a camera. Record their *predictions before results*; mere completion cannot certify understanding. Remove optional documents and test again. If players need a narrator to identify the two room objects, this architecture fails. If they can explain the story only as “I used a quantum elevator,” the paired-shell evidence is insufficient. If they claim Awakening was fake, restate the physical link between Cube and room-scale assembly in the world rather than adding a retcon speech. Human comprehension and emotional payoff remain **UNVALIDATED**.
