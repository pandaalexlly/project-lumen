# Project Lumen — Four-Area Knowledge Design

**Status: design only; no new access gates or gameplay implied.** This maps the [knowledge progression](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_PROGRESSION.md), [evidence contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md), [player cognition route](PROJECT_LUMEN_PLAYER_COGNITION_ROUTE.md), [facility layout V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_FACILITY_LAYOUT_V2.md), and [spatial identity contract](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md) onto one connected research workplace. The existing F5 world has Awakening, a fixed atrium, four connected approaches, and one A/B/C assembly. Live camera observation, M/N room exchange, P/Q sockets, and ending routes remain proposals requiring spatial and human validation. Names below are author-facing workplace functions, not mechanic-revealing signs.

## A progression of beliefs, not a walking order

| Mental stage | Player's useful model | Evidence distributed across the facility | Question that revises it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Quantum objects appear abnormal** | “The Cube is strange; the research building is ordinary.” | Awakening's Cube and physical restoration; the atrium's stable plant and return route. | Does the change follow a timer or what can be seen? |
| **2. Observation becomes a fundamental rule** | “Seeing a quantum object constrains it; sight can also exclude an empty placement.” | Awakening comparisons, A/B/C receiver views, Power/Containment Beam reach, fixed survey positions. | Must an observer be a person, and what counts as the object? |
| **3. Observer–object relationships matter** | “A Beam, and perhaps a camera, can hold a larger bounded structure; identity is not its current coordinate.” | The A/B/C collar and fixed receiver contrast; Signal camera-only comparison; Power contacts; Records-side structural views. | Is a familiar wall a member, or only a backdrop? |
| **4. Architectural space has a quantum configuration** | “Two persistent rooms can exchange fixed sockets, changing what a corridor connects to.” | Atrium P scar, Q survey, M/N's separate damage histories, an empty reciprocal exchange and unchanged fixed circulation. | Which room must occupy P for its existing doorway to meet the fixed exterior spur? |
| **5. Camera and history reframe the exit** | “The earlier stubborn state had a live observer; staff may also have confused room identity with location.” | A repeatable lens-on/member versus lens-off/member contrast; optional Records evidence of the incident; atrium/exterior alignment visible before crossing. | Can I predict a lawful M@P/N@Q configuration, rather than find a switch that opens a wall? |

This is an inference dependency, not a sequence enforced by doors. K1–K3 are small-scale foundations, K4 makes the existing C exterior trip possible, K5 is a proposed observer extension, and K6–K7 concern identity and facility topology. K2's candidate-only test may belong at A/B/C if the two occupied P/Q sockets cannot isolate it. K4 and room-floor passenger travel are **not** prerequisites for proving M/N's empty exchange. A perceptive player may infer K7 early. The physics must not wait for the player to visit four wings or read a file.

## Access is physical; comprehension is not a lock

The player begins inside Awakening. Its existing restoration and lifted bulkhead provide the first ordinary walking route into the atrium. **After that, the four current greybox approaches are connected and traversable** through the fixed workplace circulation. This design does not reseal them or invent four “knowledge doors.” What a player cannot access at the start is the *facility beyond the opening chamber*, not a selected wing that the game has chosen to teach later.

| Place | Why it is not available immediately from spawn | What is reachable once the atrium opens | What prior understanding helps, without being checked by the game |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Awakening** | It is the spawn space; the outside bulkhead is initially physically closed. | Cube, observation equipment, restoration handle, and later a return route. | None. Experimentation can establish K1 before or after local restoration. |
| **Central atrium** | The Awakening bulkhead separates it from the isolated chamber until existing restoration. | Plant loop, fixed return, all four approaches, and in the future a P-facing comparison position. | K1 helps the player later read P as more than damaged architecture; no knowledge is required to walk in. |
| **Records** | It is across the atrium's west approach, not attached to the spawn chamber. | A fixed work route and physical survey/comparison views; optional historical material. | K6 makes repeated shell features meaningful, but the player may notice a mismatch before knowing why. Reading is not an access requirement. |
| **Power and Cooling** | It lies beyond the east approach and ordinary plant circulation. | Fixed conduits, Beam/service context, A/B/C-related receiving infrastructure, and later P/Q contact comparisons. | K1 makes a source-reach test meaningful; K3 and K6 make the fixed/travelling distinction predictive. Neither enables the passage. |
| **Containment Works** | It lies beyond a rear work approach; the existing A/B/C workface is reached by the connected apron. | Close inspection of one bounded assembly, its fixed landing, support, and safe return. | K1–K3 help explain its behavior, but a player can arrive here first and form the wrong “Cube powers a machine” hypothesis. Reaching exterior C is a separate lawful supported-travel problem, not a wing gate. |
| **Signal Array** | It lies beyond the other rear approach and fixed survey/service route. | Survey views, ordinary instrumentation, and—if later proven—inspectable live-camera sightlines. | K3 suggests a camera could be an observer; an early visitor may first read it as security hardware. The proposed camera rule does not unlock entry. |

Future **P/Q proof views** must be reachable from fixed interior circulation in either M/N occupation; close exterior inspection of Q may additionally be reached from the existing C yard. The **true-route crossing** depends on M's existing doorway physically aligning with the fixed exterior spur at P, not on visiting Records, powering four stations, or acquiring a permission flag. Because the present world ends at implemented content boundaries, this table describes design access, not a claim that proposed cameras or room shells already exist.

## Awakening — the trustworthy small case

1. **Belief before entering:** The player has no quantum model. The chamber reads as a damaged, abandoned experimental workplace.
2. **New phenomenon:** A distinctive Cube can be found in another plausible placement after an unobserved interval; direct sight and the existing artificial source can constrain it. Restoration physically changes local services and opens the atrium.
3. **Initial misunderstanding:** The Cube is on a timer, reacts to a nearby machine, or moved because the service handle was used.
4. **Experiment the player can perform:** Keep equipment unchanged; compare watching the Cube, losing direct sight, and revisiting its possible positions. A Beam-reach contrast can later distinguish actual observation from electrical power alone.
5. **Knowledge gained:** K1, with possible early K2/K3: observation is a causal constraint on a bounded object and its candidate positions. The facility beyond appears stable.
6. **Future reinterpretation:** When a repaired collar or room wall later holds a larger structure, the player need not discard Awakening. They revise the *boundary of the object* they are observing. Returning to the small Cube remains a control case, not a tutorial replay.

Awakening's only mandatory physical exit step is the already established restoration interaction. Its exact Cube discovery order is not a knowledge flag and the chamber does not tell the player a rule in UI.

## Records — a coordinate is not a room's name

1. **Belief before entering:** A player may think the facility's written surveys are outdated, or that matching rooms were built to a standard plan. An architecture-first visitor can arrive before understanding the A/B/C assembly.
2. **New phenomenon:** From a fixed workplace view, an apparently ordinary structural seam and repair can be compared with another near-matching shell. Physical survey tools, maintenance traces, and inconsistent recorded locations suggest people once tracked *where* a room was instead of *which* room they saw.
3. **Initial misunderstanding:** Staff renovated a room, copied equipment, or filed conflicting plans. A document may seem to contain the solution if staged too prominently.
4. **Experiment the player can perform:** Without reading, identify a shell-attached repair or damage feature from fixed ground, follow the short atrium/work-route comparison to P and Q, and revisit after a lawful exchange. Compare that feature with fixed contact scars. Optional dated material can then corroborate history, not decide which shell is present.
5. **Knowledge gained:** A first part of K6/K7: M and N can have separate physical histories even where their construction looks alike. A coordinate and a persistent structure are different things. This does **not** yet prove an accident created duplicates.
6. **Future reinterpretation:** An earlier “same room, different equipment” sighting becomes “one fixed place met another room.” Later, Records can humanize why people misreported evacuations, while the true route remains discoverable without opening a file.

Records contributes observation viewpoints and memory anchors, not a research-log key. Its west route stays part of ordinary circulation; no archive page authorizes P/Q movement.

## Power and Cooling — connection is not membership

1. **Belief before entering:** The Cube or Beam seems to power a transfer machine. Fixed conduits are easily mistaken for evidence that the entire apparent frame is bolted to the facility.
2. **New phenomenon:** Independent pipe supports and socket contacts stay put while a repaired member or mounted setting appears with the same bounded structure at another placement. A Beam can visibly reach a member or miss it while ordinary power remains available.
3. **Initial misunderstanding:** The generator sends a room to a selected destination, or a network synchronizes door and instrument settings at two fixed rooms.
4. **Experiment the player can perform:** Compare the same contact scar before and after an empty A/B/C move or later M/N exchange. With player view controlled, change only a real Beam line's reach to a known member or empty candidate; watch whether the object is held or a candidate excluded. Later compare a room-attached ordinary state against a fixed contact that did not inherit it.
5. **Knowledge gained:** K3 plus K6: source *reach*, not an abstract power switch, matters; internal state travels with an object, while live output may depend on a fixed connection. The supply route does not define room identity.
6. **Future reinterpretation:** A dormant lamp at P need not mean N “lost its quantum state”; it may simply meet another contact. M's existing door alignment at P is a changed relationship, not a generator unlocking a wall.

Power's equipment has a believable service function. It should not become a required generator-repair quest or a selector that commands a quantum destination.

## Containment Works — the specimen is not the whole object

1. **Belief before entering:** The player knows or suspects a Cube moves. The collar, deck, and receiving hardware look like fixed machinery for handling it.
2. **New phenomenon:** The existing A/B/C Cube–bearer–deck–collar assembly presents a non-Cube structural member, independent receiving beds, and a support surface large enough to carry a qualified passenger. Different fixed viewpoints reveal different parts of one continuous boundary.
3. **Initial misunderstanding:** The Cube operates an elevator; a Beam “calls” it; merely stepping aboard triggers travel; everything near the cradle is part of one moving object.
4. **Experiment the player can perform:** From fixed access, watch only the collar while the Cube is concealed; compare that hold with full concealment. At B or C, compare the same structural repair with the vacant A contact. Observe an empty candidate without seeing the current object where geometry permits. If curious, compare boarding while still looking at a member with full support and genuine concealment, preserving ordinary safety.
5. **Knowledge gained:** K2, K4, and an early K6 can be inferred in overlapping ways: observed candidates can be excluded; a supported person can accompany the bounded object without gaining an exemption from their own sight; the collar belongs to the same identity as the Cube while the receiver does not. These are possible discoveries, not three required trials.
6. **Future reinterpretation:** A later wall/floor seam at P may likewise mark a larger object. The exterior C yard becomes a real but incomplete vantage on the facility: it offers Q comparison and an honest escape, not a failure ending for players who have not investigated M/N.

The ordinary fixed Containment cradle prop stays ordinary. A/B/C remains **one** implemented assembly; M/N is a separate proposed room pair. Neither the exterior C trip nor a room-scale passenger ride is necessary to prove an empty M/N exchange.

## Signal Array — the observer nobody initially counts

1. **Belief before entering:** A camera is security equipment, a monitor stores history, and a powered lens may seem irrelevant once no person watches the object.
2. **New phenomenon:** A visibly live lens has a real path to a known quantum member, while another aimed or occluded state does not. The fixed survey route also relates the outer Q socket to exterior ground and the atrium's stable frame.
3. **Initial misunderstanding:** A camera console is a remote lock or the footage itself keeps an object stable. A stubborn state may be blamed on random timing.
4. **Experiment the player can perform:** Hold player and Beam sightlines constant; compare the **same powered camera** aimed at a known participating member with it aimed at fixed scenery or physically occluded. Reobserve/rearm and repeat. The quantum response must follow the lens's direct view, not a change in general power. From the survey route, compare Q's fixed contact with whichever room shell occupies it.
5. **Knowledge gained:** K5 extends K3's observer family without inventing a camera-specific power. Q's survey view also supplies one half of the K7 fixed-frame/room-identity comparison; stored footage is only historical evidence.
6. **Future reinterpretation:** An earlier “stuck” assembly or room may have been continuously watched by an unnoticed *source*, not disobeying the known rule. At the final P/Q relation, redirecting or occluding a real lens is a release of observation, not an “open exit” command.

The camera's causal role must be discoverable **before** it is essential to a final hold. The current greybox does not implement live-camera observation, so this comparison is a design gate, not a claim of present functionality.

## Central atrium — the fixed place that changes meaning

1. **Belief before entering:** After Awakening, the atrium appears to be a stable coordination hall with plant machinery, four work routes, and an apparently dead service frontage at P.
2. **New phenomenon:** On repeated visits, the same atrium plant, vestibule, broken tray, and P contact scar can meet a *different identifiable room shell*. A separate fixed route toward Q lets the player check the other shell and the exterior relationship.
3. **Initial misunderstanding:** The player took the wrong corridor; equipment was moved; a shutter or door changed; a large machine rearranged an ordinary room; eventually, perhaps the whole building moved.
4. **Experiment the player can perform:** Track the atrium-to-P route and P scar, compare M and N at both fixed sockets, then use an observation-preserving comparison path versus a genuinely blind fixed return. After both modules are rearmed and all sources/candidates are clear, an empty reciprocal exchange can be inspected from fixed ground. Holding one participating wall or a live lens should prevent a repeat. The comparison path and release path still require geometric proof.
5. **Knowledge gained:** K6/K7: the atrium is the stable reference, but its **connectivity** can differ because M or N occupies P. M's repaired seam and door do not become N's solid face; each carries its own history to another socket.
6. **Future reinterpretation:** The first “wrong wall” was not bad memory or an opened secret door. If M occupies P, its existing doorway can align with a previously seen fixed exterior spur. The player should be able to predict that relationship before crossing, while the ordinary C/perimeter escape remains lawful.

The atrium is the **main spatial evidence instrument**, not merely the menu for four zones. It must offer a stable memory anchor, a P comparison, a route to Q's view, and a way to leave both proposed shells unobserved. It must *not* reveal two identity marks, both sockets, the camera sightline, and the exterior spur in a single obvious panorama. Repeated visits change interpretation because physical occupancy changes lawfully—not because the game spawns new hint props after a knowledge milestone.

## Cross-area circulation and evidence overlap

```text
Awakening -- existing restoration --> fixed atrium loop
                                     |-- Records -- fixed comparison view --|
                                     |-- Power ----- service contacts -------|-- shared work apron / A-B workface
                                     |-- Containment -- bounded assembly ----|
                                     `-- Signal ---- survey / camera views -- Q view
                                      \-- P approach (fixed) -- M or N -- conditional exterior spur
```

This is a relationship map, not a coordinate drawing or new corridor authorization. The existing connected work apron and fixed returns allow a player to encounter clues in multiple orders. The proposed P/Q comparison and blind return must fit the current fixed facility without making a travelling room required floor for ordinary circulation. An exterior C placement does not create a normal interior S-to-C walking edge.

| Evidence question | One place it can arise | Another place that can test or reinterpret it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does vision hold a current quantum object? | Awakening Cube | Containment collar, then an apparent room wall at P/Q |
| Is an empty placement excluded rather than selected? | A/B/C fixed receiver view | Beam reach from Power/Signal; P/Q only if candidate-only geometry is genuinely proven |
| Where does the travelling boundary end? | Containment bearer/deck seam | Power's unchanged contact; Records' apparent structural seam |
| Is a room identity the same as its coordinate? | Records' differing repairs | Atrium P scar and Signal Q view after empty exchange |
| Did a hidden observer hold the state? | Containment or atrium “stubborn” object | Signal's camera-only contrast, then return to the earlier site |
| Why does this doorway connect differently? | Atrium P frontage | Fixed exterior spur seen from Signal/C yard and M's unchanged doorway |

No one row needs a special completion state. A Records-first visitor may suspect two room identities without knowing why they move; a Signal-first visitor may suspect the camera before noticing either shell; an exterior-first visitor may see the spur before considering P. Each can return through fixed circulation and test the same law.

## What Stage 5 may and may not reveal

The proposed true-route inference combines three things: **M's persistent doorway**, the **fixed spur's location**, and a **live camera sightline** that can lawfully hold the pair. The player must be able to encounter all three as physical facts before attempting the route. Optional Records evidence can explain why staff accounts disagree or suggest a duplication incident; it cannot be the only proof of M/N exchange and cannot unlock the camera or doorway. The final crossing may supply the strongest visual confirmation of the facility's mistaken boundary, but it must not introduce a new rule.

Stage 5 is therefore a *synthesis*, not “finish Signal, then read Records, then use exit.” The C route remains genuine survival; M@P alignment is lawful whenever observation and safety permit it, even if the player reaches it early or by luck. Human testing must distinguish a predicted deduction from a player who happened to toggle a camera and walk through a door.

## Validation questions before detailed room design

- Can an unbriefed player describe a plausible wrong model in each area without being given mechanic names or a wing order?
- Can a player approach any wing first after restoration and still find a useful question, a fixed return, and at least one other place to test it?
- Can they distinguish **access** from **understanding**—especially exterior C travel and the proposed P/M doorway—without interpreting every fixed seal as a quest lock?
- Can they recognize M and N from ordinary viewpoints, compare P and Q without an unintended exchange, and deliberately create a full unseen interval without exact camera movement?
- Does the camera-only test isolate lens sight from power, player gaze, and Beam state? Does it explain an earlier anomaly rather than behave as a final switch?
- Before the proposed true-route crossing, can the player predict which room will occupy P, which will be at Q, and what atrium/exterior references will remain fixed?

These are open gates. The current connected assembly has technical validation, but spontaneous K1–K7 comprehension, live-camera semantics, and atomic M/N exchange have **not** been established by unbriefed players or implementation. This document maps a testable cognitive world; it does not declare the final facility ready to build.
