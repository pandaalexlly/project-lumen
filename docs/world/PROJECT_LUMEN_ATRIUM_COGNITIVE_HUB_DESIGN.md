# Project Lumen — Atrium as Cognitive Hub

**Status: design contract, not final geometry or human validation.** This document uses the [Complete Player Journey](PROJECT_LUMEN_COMPLETE_PLAYER_JOURNEY.md), [Four-Area Puzzle Architecture V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_FOUR_AREA_PUZZLE_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md), [Spatial Topology Design](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_TOPOLOGY_DESIGN.md), and [Core Mystery Cognitive Integration](PROJECT_LUMEN_CORE_MYSTERY_COGNITIVE_INTEGRATION.md). It defines what the Atrium must allow players to **compare and infer**, not its dimensions, floor plan, art pass, scripts, or final sightlines. The isolated M/N, persistence, and camera prototypes are technically checked; their combined player readability and the connected P/Q/X arrangement remain **UNVALIDATED**.

## Cognitive purpose: a place that keeps the same question open

The Atrium is the first large shared workplace after the completed Awakening Chamber. Its primary function is not to present four wing entrances. It is a **stable measuring frame** that the player crosses for ordinary reasons and gradually uses to distinguish a material room from the address where they met it. A first visit should support the sensible thought, “the building is stable; strange objects are inside it.” A later visit should make that thought testable rather than simply wrong.

The Atrium must preserve two truths simultaneously:

- **H0 and its fixed infrastructure remain where they are.** The plant, circulation, P receiver, and service datum are not one transforming quantum root. Repeated visits can be compared because this frame is trustworthy.
- **An apparent part of its architecture may be a bounded occupant.** M and N are two materially distinct room shells that can exchange fixed P/Q sockets while keeping their own structural histories and ordinary attached conditions. A changed P frontage need not mean H0 changed. The player must infer this from paired identity evidence and unchanged ground, not be told it by a label.

The Atrium's emotional turn is therefore not “my hub rearranged.” It is **“I trusted an address as if it named one enduring room.”** The opening Cube experiment remains true at its small scale; later evidence expands the player's estimate of an object's boundary, not the rule itself.

## Initial player interpretation

Leaving Awakening after its existing restoration, the player finds a coordination hall with a practical central plant, partial service power, signs of abandonment, and several work approaches with distinct purposes. They can choose where to investigate without a room title advertising a mechanic. P's apparent service face plausibly looks damaged, closed, or repaired. A slight discontinuity may invite a second glance, but it should not announce a “quantum wall.” The Atrium reads first as an inhabited facility's ordinary circulation and maintenance center.

The expected early model is not foolish: the Cube and Beam were unusual laboratory equipment, while the larger building seemed to contain them. If a player later sees a changed frontage once, renovation, a shutter, bad memory, or a different approach are all fair temporary explanations. The Atrium must give them reasons to **return and check**, not force an immediate revelation. An unusually attentive player may infer the shell boundary early and must be free to test it.

## Fixed references: what a repeat visit can trust

These are *evidentiary roles*, not final placements or a geometry drawing. Each reference needs a plausible work function and material continuity; a painted A/B marker, glowing seam, or map label is not a substitute.

| Reference that stays fixed | Ordinary facility purpose | Comparison it enables | Boundary it must not blur |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H0 plant and worn vestibule from Awakening** | Central utilities and a heavily used arrival threshold. | The player can retrace a familiar route to the *same fixed hall* after visiting any wing. | The plant cannot move with M/N or be visually reset to imply a new H0 copy. |
| **Continuous service conduit/survey datum** | Staff trace supply and measurements across work approaches. | Independent fixed ground can be followed toward P, Q survey access, exterior C, and eventually lower shaft references if the proposed topology is proved. | Its visibility does not automatically create a walkable shortcut or prove the three-site history by itself. |
| **P receiver edge, contact scar, and local wear** | A serviced interface at the Atrium face. | The *address* remains recognizable while M's repair/door or N's wound/solid face occupies it. A live output can be related to fixed supply without being mistaken for identity. | P's scar must not be part of either moving shell. A lamp alone cannot identify which shell is present. |
| **Q outer buttress/contact seen from fixed Signal-side access** | Independent survey and service interface. | Both persistent shells can be checked against two stable addresses before and after an **empty reciprocal** exchange. | Q is not a remote “destination button,” another whole building, or a candidate-only K2 proof in a fully occupied binary pair. |
| **Fixed exterior spur X in a separate prospective view** | An existing service route outside the P-side structure. | The player can ask whether M's already-existing doorway would meet it at P. | X may be visible without being directly walkable from C; separation must later be spatially fair, not an invisible barrier. |

M's integrated repair and existing doorway, N's distinct wound and solid face, and any ordinary attached condition are **not fixed Atrium references**. They belong to their shells and must be recognizable in either socket. Two separate shell histories and two fixed contexts are essential: a single changed wall plus a stationary plant cannot by itself disprove a door animation or one-room teleport. A repeated central architectural style on lower levels is also not material identity; H0 has its own wear and position in a continuous fixed world.

| **After Records evidence** | A survey at one address and a present structural repair may not describe the same material shell. | “A log was mistaken or the room was renovated.” | Seek both M and N in the same occupation and compare their integrated marks with P and Q. Records-first, Containment-first, Signal-first, and exterior-first discoveries remain legitimate. The Atrium does not wait for a wing-cleared variable. A player may overlook early clues, leave, and return with a changed hypothesis; the same fixed marks and shell histories should still support the experiment. The actual frequency and salience of these revisits require unbriefed playtesting.

## Knowledge interactions at the Atrium

- **Observation (K1/K3):** Awakening supplies the rule and Beam equivalence. At H0, watching an identified participating room wall should hold the M/N pair; a genuinely unobserved interval can permit an *empty* exchange once actual current/candidate views, rearm, and safety conditions allow it. The Atrium is an application across scale, not a second Beam lesson. It must allow a comfortable observation-preserving comparison route and a **different** broad blind-release route; exact sightlines are future spatial proof obligations.
- **Attached state versus fixed connection (K6):** M keeps its repair, doorway, and present ordinary condition wherever it is. P keeps its contact and adjacent fixed route. Their relationship can change when M changes socket. The existing isolated state-persistence fixture proves this technically for a small module, but the present M/N fixture's doorway is not a player-changeable condition; room-scale cognitive transfer remains a human question. An old socket does not restore an earlier snapshot.
- **Camera (K5):** A live lens matters only if its actual powered optical path reaches a participating shell or eligible candidate. The player should first have a fair same-powered-lens contrast on a **known** member, as Signal and the isolated camera fixture propose. A later Q hold may be considered only if lens orientation, obstruction, and target can be inspected from fixed access. Turning it away removes one witness, never selects M@P or opens X. No invisible surveillance veto is acceptable.
- **Spatial identity (K7):** P and Q are places; M and N are persistent shells. Both must be inspectable in one occupation, and both must be checked again after a reciprocal change while H0 and outer references stay. This is stronger than noticing a new door at P. A player's prediction of both occupants and the fixed scar is the meaningful evidence of understanding.

K2 needs an isolated **empty-candidate** view, which a binary occupied P/Q pair cannot honestly supply. K4's supported trip to C can offer an exterior perspective but is not a prerequisite for the first empty room exchange. K8's original/duplicated/core history is not proved by H0's changed frontage or by lower-plan resemblance; it needs a separate continuous fixed-reference and object-specific comparison. None is a stored access requirement.

## Ending preparation without an ending switch

**Bad Ending interpretation.** The lawful A/B/C passenger trip can reach real exterior C, and an ordinary down-only evacuation lift may carry the player through L1–L4 to genuine refuge. Those are *different fixed landings* on a continuous shaft, with four/three/one/zero built lateral research approaches—not versions of H0 and not a facility-wide quantum transition. Their repeated institutional central grammar can make the player question why a familiar plan is an unreliable identity cue. The Atrium provides the counterexample they can recall: H0's plant and P scar remained, yet a materially distinct room could occupy its frontage. Optional lower fragments may connect earlier work histories; descent and reading are not prerequisites to infer M/N or reach X. A player who only evacuates has survived, not failed a secret knowledge check.

**True Ending deduction.** From normal, independently reachable viewpoints, the player should be able to see or infer (1) M's existing usable doorway and integrated identity; (2) N's solid alternative; (3) fixed P/Q addresses; and (4) a fixed X spur near P that N's face does not meet. The proposed initial `N@P, M@Q` occupation makes P appear sealed, but the player can predict that a lawful empty exchange yielding `M@P, N@Q` would align M's carried doorway with X. Any real camera/Beam/player view must be accounted for; its removal permits, not commands, the other occupation. The player checks both identities from fixed ground and crosses an ordinary connection. No document, four-area completion, Bad Ending replay, camera-off flag, or named “understanding” variable creates the passage. An early correct deduction remains actionable.

The Atrium's payoff is a revised *relationship*, not a newly spawned exit: the player once had the relevant object and route in view but misidentified what was fixed. This remains a proposed spatial and emotional outcome, not a proven one.

## Fairness and anti-confusion audit

| Risk | Required evidence or restraint | What cannot be used as a repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Atrium is merely a four-way menu.** | Its plant-to-P comparison, independent Q reference, and changing ordinary connection must matter on repeated passages even when no wing is “complete.” | Area-clear banners, keycard routing, four switches. |
| **One changed frontage reads as a door animation or memory mistake.** | Two simultaneously distinguishable shells, integrated multi-distance identity cues, fixed P/Q references, and a repeatable two-sided before/after comparison. | A caption saying “the rooms exchanged” or a single dramatic cut. |
| **A quick look-away changes P before the player has any baseline.** | Natural first-view opportunity to notice stable H0 and inspect both shells from fixed ground; repeated safe comparison if they miss it. Early change may be lawful and must not be secretly blocked by an “inspected both” flag. | Hidden staged reveal, mandatory inspection marker. |
| **The entire building seems to move.** | Plant, vestibule, conduit, receiver scars, fixed Q reference, and exterior ground remain materially continuous across exchange. | Whole-facility morphing, silent map remap, lore-only correction. |
| **Camera becomes an exit key.** | A previously testable live lens-to-known-member contrast; at Q, physically readable target and same-power/blocked comparison. Player sight can still hold when the lens is blocked. | “Disable CCTV to unlock P,” unexplained all-seeing camera, recording as observer. |
| **Relocation feels random or irrecoverable.** | Stable observer/candidate rules, independent rearm, fixed access to inspect both legal occupations, and recovery from either state. A no-move interval should have a diagnosable cause. | Scripted exchange on entry, exact-angle hiding, invisible reset. |
| **X is a hidden or obvious exit marker.** | It has a plausible service purpose and an inspectable relation to P before final use; the M doorway is visible elsewhere. It may be noticed early without announcing the answer. | Glowing objective route, invisible barrier from C, late-spawned spur. |
| **Lower landings appear to be H0 transforming.** | Continuous shaft depth, distinct integrated repairs, and built approach-count differences; H0 keeps its unique upper references. | A supernatural loop or walls that visibly disappear during descent. |

No essential deduction may depend on a document read. Routine notes should be near their real work: an address-based dispatch sheet at H0, repair histories in Records, fixed-contact service records in Power, handling traces in Containment, lens maintenance in Signal. They supply human motives and plausible wrong explanations; material scars, sightlines, and repeatable consequences supply the causal proof. The first visit need not show both complete shell histories, both sockets, lens ray, and X in one panorama. **Distributed evidence is not hidden evidence:** each necessary comparison must remain reachable without prior knowledge and its individual clue must be legible from an ordinary viewpoint.

## Design gates before final geometry

1. **Paper spatial closure:** for both `N@P, M@Q` and `M@P, N@Q`, prove fixed P↔Q comparison access, independent exterior/X views, a separate broad blind release, every player/Beam/camera current and candidate sightline, safe empty exchange, M doorway/X clearance, rearm, and recovery. This document assigns evidentiary duties; it does **not** choose coordinates or declare those duties geometrically solved.
2. **Cognitive transfer:** unbriefed players must distinguish attached repair/condition from P's fixed contact, predict both M/N identities after exchange, and diagnose a visible camera hold using the earlier known-member contrast. The [integration plan](PROJECT_LUMEN_CORE_MYSTERY_COGNITIVE_INTEGRATION.md) is a protocol, not a passed study.
3. **Journey and ending interpretation:** test whether return visits naturally create a question about P; whether lower landings read as distinct fixed places; and whether an unbriefed player can predict the M@P/X connection before crossing it. Correct retrospective language or accidental arrival is not enough. If players need a document or an area-completion prompt, revise the smallest failed evidence link.

**Current decision:** the Atrium's cognitive role is specified, but its final geometry, connected-world sightlines, human discovery, Bad Ending reinterpretation, and true-route deduction remain **UNVALIDATED**. Do not convert the hub into a tutorial or add mechanics to compensate before these tests.
