# Project Lumen — Knowledge Architecture V2

**Status: design revision only; no gameplay or scenes authorized.** V2 supersedes the *allocation and numbering* of K1–K7 in [Knowledge Progression](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_PROGRESSION.md), [Evidence Contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md), and [Four-Area Core Puzzles](PROJECT_LUMEN_FOUR_AREA_CORE_PUZZLES.md). It does not silently change the implemented Awakening Chamber or the proposed [spatial quantum law](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md). Awakening is a completed prologue that already establishes Cube relocation, observation's effect, and Beam/vision equivalence. Power **applies** that understanding to larger systems; it does not introduce the Beam rule again. Live cameras, M/N room exchange, and the three-site mystery remain proposals requiring technical and unbriefed-player validation.

## The player is reconstructing one world, not completing eight lessons

The player's early, correct but incomplete theory is: **quantum objects move inside an otherwise stable facility**. Later evidence changes the assumed boundary of “object,” the meaning of a place name, and finally the relationship among three facility sites. The player may encounter evidence in several orders. K1–K8 below are author-facing causal claims, not gates, upgrades, UI entries, or required wing completions.

The eight-point revision makes **Quantum State Persistence** distinct. Position alone is not the full state of a bounded quantum object. Its material identity, attached internal configuration, and connections among its own members persist across a lawful relocation. An ordinary activated condition on attached equipment—an opened mounted door, set instrument, latched service component, or stored charge—remains with that object unless an ordinary physical cause changes it. A connection to *fixed* facility infrastructure is different: it is an external relationship that can disconnect and form a new compatible connection when the object occupies another socket. The object's capacity and attached side of the connection persist; fixed wiring, corridor, supply, and contact scars do **not** travel. This is physical continuity, never a hidden “puzzle solved” flag.

K6 is not a new “remember the puzzle solution” mechanic. The existing spatial proposal already says an object's mounted door/equipment state travels and live fixed-socket output may change. V2 elevates that fact from implementation detail to a **major discovery with a controlled comparison and later predictive use**. If a proposed “activated condition” cannot be tied to an attached physical component, it does not belong in K6. If a fixed corridor seems to move merely because a door is open, the model is wrong: the room's internal condition persisted while its external alignment changed.

K8 is presently a **knowledge hypothesis**, not a proven extra relocation law. The current two-room M/N proof establishes bounded architectural movement and changing topology; it does not prove that an entire original or duplicated building teleports as one root, nor that an accident generated a second full facility. A literal whole-facility transition would require an explicit revision of the law and its technical/readability gates. The three-site fiction may be larger than the currently proven mechanics, but must not be presented to the player as a fact that only a document can certify.

## Three sites, three honest perspectives

“Site” is a player-meaningful place with its own human use and evidence, not a menu destination or an automatic level swap. This is the proposed information structure; exact geography and access to the deeper core remain open.

| Site | What its people could truthfully know | Dominant physical evidence | Natural but incomplete reading | Contribution to the deeper truth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Original facility** | Staff measured fixed plant, serviced Beams, and recorded rooms by familiar addresses. Their early observations were accurate at the time. | Awakening, fixed atrium and socket contacts, A/B/C worksite, independent conduits, ordinary repair history. | “Quantum specimens and transfer machines are inside a stable building.” | Establishes trustworthy observation law and fixed reference ground. Its address-based documents become misleading only when a room identity changes socket. |
| **Duplicated quantum facility** | People worked in a materially distinct, near-matching architectural branch; their observations of its own repairs and activated equipment were also real. They may have used the same room names for different shells. | M/N structural identities, different later damage, attached-state persistence, reciprocal P/Q occupation, contradictory connections to the original site's fixed approaches. | “The original site was copied, or I have entered a variant of the same room.” | Forces the player to distinguish *two persistent structures* from one room changing décor. The word “duplicated” describes their historical relationship, not runtime cloning. |
| **Deeper hidden/core facility** | Researchers closest to the incident may have recognized that observation, bounded object identity, and persistent conditions affected the site's routes; their conclusions may still be incomplete or self-serving. | Fixed foundational references, old experimental infrastructure, consequences of prior activation and connection histories, optional corroborating accounts. | “Here is the final answer room.” | Reframes the human cause and cost of the misunderstanding. It should test or corroborate a prediction the player could already make physically, not dispense the first usable solution. |

The current fixed atrium and M/N pair remain the minimum spatial proof. The “duplicated quantum facility” may contain more architecture in the eventual world, but V2 does **not** authorize another moving root, simultaneous whole-site swap, or unseen scene remap. A future site layout must say exactly which surfaces are members, which infrastructure is fixed, and how access remains recoverable. Likewise, the hidden/core site is a proposed perspective, not a new locked wing or a promise of an implemented passage.

## Information is encountered where people used it

No site owns all of the story. A document's *provenance* matters: maintenance staff, containment researchers, signal operators, and core investigators described the same event from different fixed positions and at different times. Their accounts can conflict without any being an artificial lie. Physical evidence carries the causal rule; documents add motives, dates, and human consequences. A player should be able to ignore most text and still reason about K1–K7 and the lawful route.

| Document type | Distributed locations and examples | Why a player naturally finds it | Hidden knowledge required to **find** it? | Proper evidentiary role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Common** | Awakening work orders; atrium shift notes; Power maintenance cards beside fixed contacts; Containment handling records; Signal camera service sheets; everyday notices at both original and duplicated workstations. | They sit where the relevant task happened: a damaged instrument, abandoned desk, receiver, or service cabinet already attracts inspection. | **No.** Ordinary exploration suffices; a player may not yet understand their significance. | Establish human routine, terminology, and the belief that the building was stable. Never define a quantum rule outright. |
| **Misleading but honest** | Original-site address-based room inventory; a Power note attributing a stalled transfer to “low supply”; a Signal operator's assumption that stored footage was still monitoring; a duplicated-site worker's report that “the same room” had been repaired twice. | Each appears next to the real equipment or location that prompted the author's conclusion. They are useful local hypotheses, not planted false answers. | **No.** The point is to encounter them *before* the correcting contrast is known. | Give the player reasonable wrong models that later physical tests overturn. Their statements should not contradict what the author actually witnessed. |
| **Contradiction** | Different physical repair surveys near Records **and** Containment; fixed-contact diagrams in Power versus attached-machine condition at the duplicated site; Signal lens records versus a repeatable live-camera hold; two accounts of P/Q occupancy from separate site viewpoints. | A visible scar, dead contact, persistent open door, or stubborn object gives a reason to check the nearby comparison. One clue can point to another without a quest marker. | **Not to find the first example.** K6/K7 helps *recognize* the contradiction; some counterpart evidence may require reaching the other site by ordinary lawful exploration. | Prompt a specific player experiment. No pair of documents alone proves room exchange or historical duplication without fixed-ground before/after evidence. |
| **True-ending / core context** | Partial structural overlays near an exterior or atrium service approach; more complete incident testimony and design rationale at the deeper/core site; matching human traces in the duplicated site. Do **not** put the entire explanation in one terminal. | The player already sees a route or relationship that does not fit the current map. Following that physical question leads to the service context; later core documents reward investigation of *why* it existed. | **Some later examples may require K6/K7** to infer which persistent doorway or connection reaches a core approach, and **K5** to account for a visible camera hold if one exists there. No document supplies that access condition; the exact core geometry remains undecided. | Corroborate origin, responsibility, and emotional meaning. They may refine the player's historical account, but no read flag, page count, or exact quotation activates the true route. |

The types describe **how a document functions in the player's reasoning**, not collectible rarity tiers. A common work order can become a contradiction after a room exchange. A once-misleading note can become poignant when the player understands why its author was mistaken. The same physical artifact may be read differently on a revisit; the game need not mark that change. If the player reaches a site early, its paper is already there and remains truthful in context.

Documents mounted inside a moving room may travel only if their physical holder is authored as part of that bounded module; loose physics clutter is not silently carried under the proposed safety law. Fixed service boards and socket-side archives remain fixed. This distinction can itself support K6, but should not become a document-sorting puzzle.

| Area | Physical question | Common or misleading perspective | Cross-site contradiction and later use |

| **Central Atrium** | What stayed at P when its apparent wall changed? | Operations notes use P as a room name because that was practical for staff. | Same plant/contact with different M/N occupant; the hub becomes a measuring instrument and route predictor. |
| **Records** | Why do surveys of one address disagree? | Dates and room names are reliable about staff intent but weak about physical identity. | Repair evidence must be checked against live shells and Power/Signal references; Records does not monopolize the answer. |
| **Power and Cooling** | Which parts of a working system are attached, and which are fixed supply? | Maintenance sheets can blame supply for a hold, while the prologue's Beam law already tells the player to inspect sightlines. | K6 persistence: a latched attached condition/setting follows a member; external live output changes with socket connection. | **Containment Works** | What is the boundary of the Cube/bearer/deck/collar object, and can it carry a person? | Handling notes assume “specimen” versus “machine,” an initially plausible split. | Empty movement and supported travel challenge that split; an attached setting or repair persists across A/B/C. |
| **Signal Array** | What source still observes, and what does Q actually contain? | Security reports treat lenses as cameras for evidence, not active observers. | K5 live aim/occlusion contrast; Q's fixed survey view tests M/N identity against the atrium. |

At the **duplicated site**, similar ordinary work materials carry a different repair and activation history. At the **core site**, source material can explain why these contradictory accounts accumulated. Neither site is “the lore wing.” Information density should follow plausible work and damage, with quiet exploration between anomalies; no room-per-K rule or mandatory read order is specified.

## Three non-linear reconstruction paths

- **Object-first:** Awakening K1/K3 → Containment A/B/C boundary and passenger evidence → Power distinguishes attached state from fixed contact (K6) → atrium/duplicated-site room histories (K7) → Signal camera and core history revise earlier holds (K5/K8).
- **Architecture-first:** A P frontage discrepancy or Records survey contrast → compare Q and fixed contacts → use Awakening's observer rule to test a wall → discover persistent door/equipment state (K6) and reciprocal room identity (K7) → Signal supplies the missing camera cause. No prerequisite document tells the player to do this.
- **Exterior-first:** Lawful A/B/C ride to C, or an early Signal survey of Q → see another room identity and a fixed spur → return to the atrium to test what P meets → later core/duplicated-site materials explain why staff maps conflict. Leaving by the ordinary exterior route remains genuine survival.

These are examples, not paths checked by the game. A player who predicts M@P early is allowed to act on it. A player who reads nothing can still prove present-day room exchange physically. K8's historical site relationship may require more human evidence, but the *route* may not depend on finding a true-ending document.

## Fairness and revision gates

1. **Awakening boundary:** Preserve its completed Cube, observation, Beam, Plate, restoration, and first mental model. Remove any future plan that sends players to Power to “learn Beam equals vision.” Power must present a new *application* or it adds no knowledge value.
2. **Persistence proof:** Define one ordinary attached condition that can be changed or clearly verified before relocation and seen afterward on the **same identifiable object**. Show that a fixed socket did not inherit it and that changing live supply is not a reset. If this cannot be demonstrated, K6 is a slogan rather than a discovery.
3. **Site ontology:** Before drawing the duplicated or core facility, specify fixed references, bounded moving members, and how these sites are physically related. The three-site plan must not silently turn M/N's room exchange into whole-facility teleportation. Decide whether K8 can be proven nontextually or is explicitly historical corroboration.
4. **Information audit:** For every major historical claim, identify at least two *independent* perspectives from different jobs/sites and one physical consequence the player can compare. For every essential mechanical claim, require a player-visible experiment; no document may be its sole source.
5. **Access audit:** Common and first contradiction materials must be found by ordinary curiosity. If deeper/core material requires knowledge to reach, the route's premises must be available outside it. Do not require document counts, map pins, keycards, hidden read flags, or an information-room visit.
6. **Human test:** In unbriefed sessions, ask what the player expected before testing a Beam, camera, attached condition, or P/Q exchange; ask which site account changed meaning afterward. If they finish by reading a single “answer” file, toggling a camera at random, or treating the duplicated site as a loading trick, revise evidence placement before implementation.

The current spatial-duplication verdict remains **REVISE BEFORE DETAILED ROOM DESIGN**. This document is a revised knowledge and information architecture, not validation that K5–K8, three-site geography, or true-ending comprehension are ready to build.
