# Project Lumen — Spatial Duplication Mystery Validation

**Verdict: REVISE BEFORE DETAILED ROOM DESIGN.** The proposed mystery has a logically discriminating evidence chain, but its decisive P/Q sightlines, repeatable camera-only contrast, recovery, and unbriefed comprehension have not been demonstrated. This is a design audit, not a human playtest or a technical feasibility pass. No gameplay, scene, script, or new rule is proposed here.

This audit tests the [spatial rules](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md) against the [evidence contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md), [cognition route](PROJECT_LUMEN_PLAYER_COGNITION_ROUTE.md), [facility layout V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_FACILITY_LAYOUT_V2.md), and [critical review](PROJECT_LUMEN_MYSTERY_REVIEW.md). Awakening and the single A/B/C assembly are existing technical demonstrations. Live cameras, the two-room M/N exchange at fixed P/Q sockets, and the ending relationship remain design proposals. Human discoverability is **UNVALIDATED**.

## The exact truth to prove

The physical revelation is: **two materially present, near-matching room modules M and N retain separate identities and can exchange their placements at fixed sockets P and Q; as a result, a familiar fixed approach can meet a different room and connect to different fixed space.** The Operations Atrium, socket beds, survey spine, and exterior ground remain fixed. This is a spatial relationship changing, not a random room makeover, a player teleport, or the whole facility moving.

“The facility contains duplicated spatial states” should mean that its *usable configuration* has two lawful M/N occupations. Present-day observation can prove the two room identities and their exchange. It cannot, by itself, prove that a past accident **created** the near-duplicate rooms rather than staff building two similar ones. That historical claim may be corroborated by records and shared pre-incident construction evidence, but must not be a prerequisite for the spatial inference or true route. Neither a cloned entire building nor a room spawning on demand is part of the current law.

## Minimal player-visible evidence chain

These are logical steps, not mandatory areas or scripted beats. An architecture-first player can encounter step 3 before step 1; the world must still permit the later comparisons. Each step needs a prospective prediction, not just an explanation supplied after success.

| Step | What the player observes | Likely incorrect explanation | Why that explanation eventually fails | Necessary new conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. A reliable small rule** | Awakening's Cube stays while directly watched and can be elsewhere after an unobserved interval. At the existing A/B/C worksite, Beam reach and visible empty candidates can constrain the same kind of movement. | “The specimen teleports on a timer; the Beam or machine chooses its destination.” | Repeated gaze/no-gaze and source-reach contrasts, with other conditions stable, predict holds and exclusions better than a timer or selector. | Observation constrains quantum *objects*. This does not yet implicate the building. |
| **2. The object boundary can be misread** | A collar or other non-Cube structural member keeps the known A/B/C assembly stable even when the Cube is hidden; its distinctive repair travels with bearer/deck while fixed contacts remain. | “The Cube controls a fixed transfer machine,” or “boarding calls an elevator.” | Seeing only the non-Cube member holds the whole object; an **empty** move carries its repaired structure but leaves receiver beds and service scars behind. Passenger travel is not required for this contrast. | A participating object can include what first looked like architectural equipment. Follow identity and support, not a Cube-shaped icon. |
| **3. Two room histories, not one changed room** | From fixed access, the player can inspect both near-matching shells: M has one later repair and ordinary door/equipment condition; N has different later damage and an intact face. Both exist at the same time, one at P and one at Q. P/Q contacts and atrium/exterior landmarks are separately recognizable. | “These are two ordinary rooms built alike,” or “I forgot which corridor I entered.” | The two shells retain their *different* scars and internal conditions through a later change; those features do not stay with the corridor. A short repeatable fixed-ground route checks both identities without trusting memory of wallpaper alone. | M and N are distinct persistent structures; “the room at P” is not a sufficient identity. This step alone does not prove either is quantum. |
| **4. The shells, not their contents, exchange** | After both complete shells are genuinely unobserved, the same fixed P approach meets M where N stood, while the Q comparison now meets N where M stood. M's repaired wall, doorway, and attached ordinary state remain together; fixed P/Q contact marks, atrium plant, and exterior ground have not moved. The change can be inspected again after a lawful return. | “Someone moved equipment,” “the door opened remotely,” “one room teleported,” or “the whole building shifted.” | Moving loose items cannot relocate a repaired wall/floor boundary. A remote door cannot make N appear at Q as M arrives at P. One-room transport cannot explain **reciprocal** occupation by two tracked identities. Global motion predicts shifted fixed references, which do not occur. | A bounded inhabited space can be the quantum object; M and N exchange fixed spatial placements. The approach's connectivity depends on room identity. |
| **5. Observation predicts the exchange** | When player/Beam views are controlled, a live camera's direct view of a participating shell holds the pair; the same powered lens aimed at fixed scenery or physically occluded does not. Looking at a structural member also holds it. After actual reobservation/rearm of both rooms, the contrast can be repeated. | “The camera console is an exit switch,” or “the room swap is a one-off script.” | The result follows the **sightline**, not merely power or console use; the same observer law already held the Cube and assembly. Stored footage does not hold anything. A second prediction of which identities will occupy P/Q can be checked before using either route. | Cameras are observers under the existing rule, and the spatial exchange is causal and repeatable rather than a hidden scene transition. |

Steps 3–5 are the smallest proof of the spatial mystery. Steps 1–2 make the inference *plausible to seek* rather than a rule introduced at room scale. K2 (candidate exclusion) is useful at the A/B/C worksite but should **not** be claimed as separately demonstrated by a two-occupied-socket swap unless a genuine candidate-only sightline is proven. K4 (supported passenger travel) gives the C route and confidence about moving objects, but is **not** required to prove the empty M/N exchange. An unbriefed player may understand steps in another order; no knowledge flag should enforce this table.

## Room identity: what would actually prove two rooms?

A single before/after view of a changed P interior is ambiguous. The minimum identity comparison needs **two simultaneous occupants**, **different persistent post-incident details**, and **fixed socket features independent of either occupant**. Shared construction helps the player first mistake M and N for one familiar room; differing wear lets them later correct that mistake. A shared older flaw may suggest a common origin, but cannot carry the present-day identity proof by itself.

| Thing being tracked | Memorable evidence in ordinary traversal | Prediction after a lawful exchange |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **M — room identity** | A repair integrated into a structural seam, associated with M's ordinary door and attached equipment state. | The repair, doorway, and state stay together at the other socket. |
| **N — other room identity** | A different physical wound on the shell and a solid face where M has its doorway. | N's wound and face occupy M's former socket; they do not become M's repair or door. |
| **P — fixed interior socket** | A distinctive contact/pipe scar tied by sightline and walking route to the atrium plant. | The same P contact and atrium relationship remain beneath whichever shell arrives. |
| **Q — fixed outer socket** | A different independent contact and exterior buttress/survey relationship. | The same Q contact and exterior relationship remain, now meeting the other shell. |

These are **roles**, not instructions to add colored symbols or four oversized clues. Each feature must be visible at a normal player viewpoint and be attributable to either shell or fixed frame. If M and N are visually indistinguishable except for a log entry, players cannot prove two room identities nonverbally. If the repair is only a loose prop, “staff moved it” remains viable. If the rooms are visually too distinct from the outset, the initial same-room mistake becomes implausible. The exact wear geometry and visibility need testing, not assertion.

## Spatial exchange: the discriminating comparison

The strongest experiment is an **empty reciprocal exchange observed only in its before/after results from fixed ground**. Before release, identify M@Q and N@P (or the reverse), note the same P/Q contacts, and leave both full shells unobserved. Afterward, revisit P and Q through fixed circulation. The critical result is *paired conservation*: M is at one socket, N at the other, and no one has seen one vanish into a temporary empty bay. Internal hinge/equipment state travels with its shell. Fixed plant, pipe scars, and exterior ground keep their relationships.

The player must not watch the rooms morph under direct sight; that would violate the proposed observation law. Nor can a camera cut show the “answer” instead of player-verifiable geometry. The comparison must be reproducible from both legal states after real reobservation of **both** modules. If only one room is rearmed, the pair may wait; this must not present as an inexplicable random delay. A final room ride may corroborate the rule, but a passenger-only demonstration would still look like an elevator.

An ordinary lift explains transportation; it does not predict a non-Cube wall holding the pair, two different shells exchanging occupied sockets, an ordinary door condition travelling with one shell, and a fixed exterior spur becoming connected after an *empty* exchange. If any of these are unobservable, the “quantum elevator” interpretation may remain just as good as the intended model.

## Why the atrium is the stable reference

The central atrium supplies more than navigation. The player first learns its vestibule, circulation plant, broken service tray, and returns to four working approaches as stable geometry. P's threshold/contact should be read against those references on each visit. Another fixed route—through a work or survey approach—must allow comparison of Q without treating a ride as the only way to see it. The player can then ask whether the *same fixed approach* meets the same room before and after an unobserved interval.

For the whole-building-motion explanation to fail, the atrium landmark must be recognizable **in both composite states**, and its relationship to P/Q must remain inspectable. For the lost-in-identical-corridors explanation to fail, the player needs a short retraceable path from the plant to P and a separate legible route to Q's view. For the experiment to work, the atrium cannot provide one all-seeing perch that simultaneously exposes both current shells and prevents their release. Partial views, ordinary architectural occlusion, and a nearby fixed return route are necessary; a tiny camera angle or “darkness makes it unobserved” exception is not.

## Player memory: recognition before explanation

The player is unlikely to remember a serial number, identical wall panels, or a precise floor plan. They are likelier to remember a **functional landmark** (atrium plant), a **unique physical history** (a repaired seam or later damage), and a **surprising relationship** (that repair now meets a different fixed contact; a doorway now faces a known spur). Each memory anchor should appear during ordinary use of a place, not only at a dedicated inspection peephole.

Use different kinds of detail for different cognitive jobs: shared construction supports “these might be copies,” asymmetric wear tracks **which** copy, an ordinary altered state rebuts synchronized settings, and fixed wear tracks **where** it is. The player need not memorize all four labels. A successful blind player might say “the repaired one is here now, the dented one is over there, and this pipe did not move.” That is stronger evidence than correctly repeating “M@P.” Revisited views should retain framing and scale so this comparison is fair, but should not flash or highlight the marks after a state change.

## Fairness without notes, guides, or brute force

- **Discoverable cause:** The lens, its orientation, and occlusion path are physically inspectable. “Hidden observer” means the *role* of an ordinary-looking camera is initially misunderstood, not that an invisible sensor vetoes progress. Before the final hold, a camera-only contrast on a known quantum member must be possible with player and Beam views controlled; a console power toggle coinciding with a door change is not proof.
- **Controlled alternatives:** The player can compare actual-member sight, empty candidate sight where geometry allows, and no sight without several unrelated systems changing at once. A no-move result alone proves little: it may be current observation, candidate observation, unsafe occupancy, or unrearmed state.
- **Repeatable and recoverable:** Either P/Q occupation retains fixed access to identity views, observer-source service, and rearm. A wrong hypothesis can be tested again; no required evidence is consumed by an early escape via C or a premature exchange.
- **No answer gating:** Optional records may date the incident but neither name a required rule nor unlock a state. M's door/fixed-spur relation is prospectively inferable. A lucky player can reach the route early; the game does not pretend to detect understanding.
- **No random search requirement:** A reasonable clue should motivate the next comparison—repair versus contact, live lens versus blocked lens, two rooms versus one—without cycling every switch, staring at every wall, or consulting external instructions. If the solution can only be found by exhaustive toggling, the evidence chain has failed even if the physics are consistent.

Fairness cannot be certified from this document alone. It requires a paper sightline/recovery proof, then unbriefed sessions in which players generate their own predictions. The existing A/B/C technical validation does not establish that people already infer its collar membership or passenger rule.

## Revelation moments, premature answers, and failure readings

**Strongest possible moments:**

1. Return from an ordinary atrium loop: P's pipe scar and plant are exactly where remembered, but M's distinctive repaired seam and open doorway now occupy the frontage where N's solid face stood. From the Q comparison, N's different damage appears at the other unchanged socket. The player can explain an earlier “door became a wall” sighting without dialogue.
2. A previously stubborn state moves only after the powered camera's real view is blocked or aimed away, with player/Beam observation otherwise unchanged. The player recognizes that an earlier failure was not random and predicts a repeat.
3. From the fixed exterior spur, the player looks back through M's unchanged door toward the atrium and can still identify N at Q. This confirms a predicted connection; it must not be the first time either room identity is visible.

**Clues that could reveal the answer too early:** a first-visit panorama containing both room scars, both sockets, the camera ray, and the exterior spur; a large quantum-styled seam/color code on every moving wall; a record headed “two rooms exchange”; an exact duplicate of a unique repair on both shells; or a visible room swap despite direct observation. Do not respond by hiding lawful behavior behind a stage gate. Distribute ordinary viewpoints so an early guess remains possible but the full proof requires comparison.

**Likely misunderstandings and what would cause them:**

| Player reading | Design condition that permits it | Evidence needed to correct it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The game swapped a room asset when I left.” | P and Q cannot both be compared, or identities are only cosmetic. | Two simultaneous distinguishable shells, fixed contacts, and repeatable reciprocal results. |
| “I got lost in repeated corridors.” | No remembered atrium reference or short fixed return. | Same plant/P scar before and after, plus an independently located Q view. |
| “The Cube powers a room elevator.” | Only passenger rides are shown; walls are never held or tracked. | Non-Cube structural hold, empty paired exchange, unchanged receiving infrastructure. |
| “Turning off CCTV opens a door.” | Lens power and door state change together; no known-member camera test. | Powered aimed-on versus powered aimed-off/occluded contrast with other observers unchanged. |
| “The entire building teleported.” | Atrium and exterior references cannot be checked together. | Stable hub route, P/Q contacts, and exterior ground before/after the same exchange. |
| “Awakening lied; quantum rules change later.” | Room move violates direct observation or passenger vision is silently exempt. | Preserve the same current/candidate sight law and show which larger surface belonged to the object. |
| “Nothing happened, so the rule is random.” | An unseen camera, observed candidate, only one rearmed room, or unsafe geometry quietly blocks the pair. | A legible source/state contrast and recoverable reobservation path; do not turn a hidden veto into fake chance. |

## Decision gate

**Current determination: not ready for detailed P/Q room design or implementation.** The proposed chain is causally coherent on paper, and the atrium can plausibly supply the stable reference. That is weaker than proving a player can notice, test, and predict the truth. The next validation must establish, without changing the rule:

1. A two-state P/Q spatial proof: whole-shell boundaries, both candidate poses, ordinary door clearances, every accessible player/Beam/camera current and candidate sightline, both rooms' independent rearm, safe fixed circulation, and recovery from either occupation. Do not promise room-level candidate-only K2 evidence unless the geometry actually isolates it.
2. A repeatable **camera-only** comparison with a known quantum member, visibly live aim/occlusion, and unchanged player/Beam views. The result must separate observer causality from a remote power/door switch.
3. A player-visible identity comparison in which M and N are both recognizable from fixed access before and after an empty exchange; shell-attached ordinary state follows one while P/Q contacts remain. Test it without explanatory records.
4. Unbriefed prediction tests across at least architecture-first, observation-first, and exterior-first approaches. Before revealing a result, ask what will remain at P/Q and why; after it, ask what earlier atrium discrepancy now means. Record whether players infer two persistent rooms and a fixed reference, or only describe a lift, random teleport, or scene trick.
5. An ending comprehension check: before the proposed true-route crossing, can players predict why M's existing doorway would align with the fixed spur, while the C route remains a genuinely sufficient escape? No mandatory “bad ending first,” checklist, or solution speech may stand in for that inference.

If these tests fail, revise evidence placement, vantage, and memory anchors before drafting rooms. If they pass, the mystery can advance to detailed spatial design; the historical origin of the near-duplicate rooms can remain an optional narrative uncertainty. This verdict does **not** authorize gameplay implementation.
