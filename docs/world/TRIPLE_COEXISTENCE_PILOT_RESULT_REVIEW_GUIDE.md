# Triple Coexistence — Logistics Pilot Result Review Guide

**Status:** staff-facing review guide for the two unscored logistics pilots. No pilot results are recorded here. Human comprehension remains **UNVALIDATED**.

Use the [current cognitive blind run sheet](../../tests/manual/TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_COGNITIVE_BLIND_PLAYTEST.md) to conduct sessions and the [pilot checklist](../../tests/manual/TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_COGNITIVE_PILOT_CHECKLIST.md) to prepare them. The [mental-model scoring revision](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_MENTAL_MODEL_SCORING_REVISION.md) defines the evidence that a later scored session would need for complete-facility understanding. The older proof-only protocol is not the operative script. This guide reviews whether the procedure can collect that evidence fairly; it does **not** decide whether the puzzle succeeds.

## 1. Classify the result before proposing a fix

| Category | Meaning | Evidence to record | Permitted conclusion from a pilot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Procedure success** | The assigned A- or B-first route, neutral moderator script, prior visits, T1 baseline, T2 return, first-view T3 account, and T4 diagnostic are separable and recoverable. | Build/settings ID; clean reset; visit and first-view timestamps; recording and sketches; exact moderator interventions; assigned and actual route; atrium-cue and complete-stack viewing opportunities. | This *procedure* can measure a future participant's changing spatial model. It says nothing about whether the pilot participant understood it. |
| **Procedure issue** | Test setup, wording, timing, route assignment, assistance, recording, or exposure makes an otherwise possible deduction unmeasurable or biased. | Exact prompt/help and time; missed or unusable record; route mismatch; inconsistent primer/settings; early reveal; actual first-view duration; what measure is affected. | Repair or standardize the procedure, then repeat the affected pilot. Do not infer a puzzle defect from contaminated data. |
| **Prototype issue discovered during testing** | A reproducible scene, navigation, sightline, occlusion, or handoff behavior prevents the planned exposure even under the correct procedure. | Participant video plus operator-only reproduction on the same build; camera pose/path; whether both A and B are affected; comparison with the technical validation assumptions. | Pause scored recruitment and investigate the isolated prototype under a separately scoped decision. A review guide does not authorize geometry or gameplay changes. |
| **Possible human comprehension issue** | A player received a clean, sufficiently inspectable proof, yet their recorded map and explanation do not connect prior visits to three fixed, complete QER–Atrium–Awakening units—or they inferred the answer before the proof. | T1/T2/T3 sketches, verbatim account, first-view footage, cue and complete-stack opportunity, actual attention, prompts, and competing explanations. Apply Levels 0–3 only as a diagnostic description. | A **hypothesis** for scored blind testing, not a pilot pass/fail judgment on the puzzle. One or two pilots cannot establish a comprehension rate or justify a design change. |

A case can carry more than one tag, but order the causal account. For example, a player who never found the viewpoint has no usable evidence about comprehension at that viewpoint. A clean view followed by a stable one-changing-place explanation is a legitimate *possible* comprehension signal, not automatically a geometry defect. Do not relabel a procedure failure as a cognitive failure because the player's final words were incorrect.

## 2. Diagnose the failure mode

First reconstruct the timeline from screen/audio capture, sketches, and intervention log: what was visible, what the player did or said **before** each prompt, what the moderator supplied, and what became visible afterward. Separate cue availability from attention; separate the run sheet's three-atrium-cue exposure from a fair opportunity to inspect all three upper/central/lower stack relationships. Candidate A may require looking up/down or moving, not one still frame. When evidence is absent, record **not assessable**, not failure.

| Case | Competing causes | Evidence needed before any design recommendation | Immediate pilot disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Player cannot find A or B observation viewpoint** | Assigned ramp/ground instruction ambiguous; route choice given too late; participant did not seek the path; route geometry or sightline is obstructed. | Quote the route instruction and time; compare assigned versus actual first approach; replay footage of fork and path; operator-only retrace on the frozen build. Did the player reach the correct route but lack a view, or never find the route? Was the eight-minute no-view limit reached? | If wording/timing is the obstacle, standardize and repeat the pilot. If the route is physically inaccessible or consistently hides the proof despite correct procedure, log a prototype issue and pause scored testing. No-view is not a comprehension failure. |
| **B. Player misunderstands controls** | Controls response absent/inconsistent; mouse capture or display setup failed; ordinary unfamiliarity or comfort issue. | Capture the exact request and response, input behavior, cursor state, build/settings, and whether the same problem occurs on an operator run. Check whether help supplied spatial information. | Fix setup or deliver only the allowed verbatim controls answer and repeat if the baseline/view was lost. Do not redesign evidence because of an input barrier. |
| **C. Moderator accidentally hints** | Script wording, improvisation, pointing, camera movement, praise, or visible staff material supplies a place count, cue, or explanation. | Intervention log and audio/video with exact words/gesture and time; identify which T0–T3 statements occurred before versus after the hint. | Preserve the raw case, mark post-hint interpretation moderator-influenced, repair training/script handling, and repeat the affected pilot. A correct answer after the hint is not independent deduction. |
| **D. Player reaches the proof but cannot explain it** | Insufficient exposure; missed identity cues; upper/lower stack relationship not inspectable; too much cognitive load; the physical contradiction fails despite adequate evidence. | T1/T2 account; first adequate frame and duration; actual A/B view; opportunity and apparent inspection for atrium identities **and** all three complete stacks; T3 sketch and exact explanation before crossover; surviving alternative theory. Compare against another clean case and technical sightlines. | If exposure or records are incomplete, classify procedure/non-assessable and repeat. If clean and inspectable, retain as a possible comprehension issue for scored testing. Investigate prototype only if a physical evidence gap can be demonstrated, not merely from one wrong answer. |
| **E. Player understands multiplicity before the final view** | Genuine early inference; early triple sightline leak; prior spoiler; moderator cue; T1 sketch question creating an account; handoff reading as travel between fixed places. | Earliest quoted statement or sketch, time relative to each visit, return and proof; visible frames before the fork; spoiler screen; exact prompts; whether the player maps complete stacks or only guesses a count. | If leaked/primed, repair procedure and repeat. If genuinely early, record a G1/lead-in hypothesis; the later view cannot be credited with reversal. Do not weaken clues or change geometry on this pilot alone. |

The “ordinary multi-wing building” answer is not diagnosed by vocabulary. Inspect the player's map: three remembered atria alone can reach at most Level 2; a supported assignment of each atrium to its own upper and lower structure may reach Level 3 even if the player calls them wings. A confident “three facilities” with no such mapping is not Level 3. A coherent wrong single-facility theory is not Level 0. These distinctions help judge whether the **record** is rich enough; the pilots remain unscored for puzzle success.

## 3. Decision gate after both pilots

Run two separate unbriefed, **unscored** logistics pilots, one assigned A-first and one B-first, on the same frozen setup. Do not pool them with the later scored sample. Review each recording independently before comparing the two. A second reader should try to reconstruct T0 → T1 → T2 → T3 from raw footage and sketches without relying on moderator memory. Preserve disagreement as evidence of a measurement problem.

- **Proceed to real blind sessions** only if *both* pilots have clean H0 → H1 → H2 → H0 order; a pre-proof T1 model and any T2 correction are identifiable; no answer-bearing prompt or early proof leak; recording, sketches, timestamps, and interventions are usable; assigned and actual first view and exposure can be distinguished; T3 is locked before T4/crossover; and the record can distinguish atrium-cue opportunity from complete-stack opportunity. Lock one primer, route wording, confidence-prompt policy, build/settings, and the Level 0–3/V rubric before scored recruitment. Proceeding certifies **measurement readiness only**.
- **Adjust the test procedure** for a reproducible ambiguity in moderator wording, route assignment, timing, input help, setup, recording, or scoring capture. Write the exact change and the measure it protects; do not change a prompt to steer players toward the answer. Then **repeat the affected A- or B-first pilot** with a new unbriefed participant. If the change alters shared primer, timing, or scoring comparability, repeat both.
- **Repeat a logistics pilot without a design change** when recording fails, a participant is spoiled, an accidental hint occurs, the wrong build/route is used, or a one-off technical interruption destroys the timeline. Keep the failed record as a deviation, not an invisible deletion.
- **Investigate a prototype issue before scored recruitment** when correct procedure still cannot deliver a walkable, measurable, nonleaking first view or a fair complete-stack inspection, especially if operator reproduction confirms it. Document the exact physical failure and request a separate prototype decision; this guide authorizes no geometry edit.
- **Carry a comprehension hypothesis into scored testing** when a clean pilot participant has an inspectable view but retains a rival model, reaches only Level 1/2, or infers the truth early. That response alone neither blocks a procedurally sound study nor validates or invalidates the puzzle. If the procedure cannot distinguish Level 2 from Level 3 in either pilot, repair measurement before proceeding.

## 4. Guardrails against overinterpretation

1. **Do not change geometry from one response.** First rule out exposure, navigation, prompting, input, recording, and alternative interpretations; then seek a repeated clean pattern or a reproducible physical fault. A pilot is not a design-effect sample.
2. **Do not treat navigation as reasoning.** Failure to reach or inspect the view is an exposure outcome. Record where access failed and whether the cause was procedure or prototype.
3. **Do not count words as models.** “Three facilities” may be a count/guess; “three wings” may express the correct physical map. Use what the player connects, predicts, and cites, including QER-like upper, atrium, and Awakening-like lower relations.
4. **Do not backdate insight.** T2 deduction, second-view T4 understanding, and post-hint answers cannot become T3 first-view evidence. Distinguish spontaneous from neutral-question/sketch-elicited conclusions.
5. **Do not infer attention from a frame.** A visible cue is an opportunity. Record gaze/action/speech separately; if complete-stack inspection was not fairly available, Level 3 is not assessable.
6. **Do not convert pilot observations into a success rate.** Report both cases and their limitations. Technical validation says the experiment can run; human comprehension validation requires later clean, unbriefed, scored sessions.

It must not state that players understand the intended one-changing-facility → three-fixed-facilities reversal merely because the prototype runs or a pilot participant used the right words.
