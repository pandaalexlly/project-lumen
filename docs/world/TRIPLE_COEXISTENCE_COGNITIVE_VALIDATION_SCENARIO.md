# Triple Coexistence — Minimal Cognitive Validation Scenario

**Design only; not implemented. Human comprehension is UNVALIDATED.** This is a lead-in for the existing [triple-coexistence greybox](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_GREYBOX_VALIDATION.md) and [blind protocol](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_BLIND_PLAYTEST_PROTOCOL.md). Its question is not whether someone can count three structures. It is whether an unbriefed player first treats successive visits as changes to one place, then uses the simultaneous view to identify those *previously visited* places as three fixed, coexisting facility units.

## Boundary: what the test may and may not assert

Follow the [canonical spatial rulebook](PROJECT_LUMEN_CANONICAL_SPATIAL_RULEBOOK.md): H0, H1, and H2 and their QER–atrium–Awakening stacks remain fixed throughout. Only the two inner quantum rooms can exchange; this test needs **no exchange**. The player cannot walk directly from an atrium into a quantum room or through an exposed H0–H1–H2 corridor. QER-to-quantum-room transfer and room-to-atrium exit are the broad access pattern, but destination mapping, door faces, and one-way details are unresolved. The lead-in's deterministic handoffs and one-way exits are explicitly **test scaffolding**, not a proposed canonical transfer or door law. Each short receiver is a separate, visually similar *stand-in for passage through one of the four allowed quantum rooms*; it is not a fifth or shared room, and its mocked route does not assign a canonical destination to any QER.

All three stacks should inhabit the same running greybox from the first frame. A handoff moves the *player* only after sightlines are fully occluded; it never swaps atrium geometry, resets damage, or loads a different version of the world. The last return deposits the player inside H0, from which they walk continuously to existing viewpoint A or B. Do not insert a cut, title, explanatory prompt, or scene load at the proof moment. If the minimal route cannot hide the stacks before that moment without a visible changing gate, treat that as an unresolved staging problem, not a license to call the test clean.

## 1. Minimum playable sequence

The minimum is **three distinct first visits plus a short H0 return**. Because both existing proof viewpoints are reached from H0, a third visit ending in H2 cannot flow directly into the present proof without either that return or a noncanonical cross-facility walkway. The return is setup, not a fourth lesson. Record any realization it causes *before* the triple view separately.

| Beat | Player experience and available evidence | Reasonable provisional reading | Required transition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Visit 1: H0** | Start within H0's atrium. Freely inspect its recess, side ribs, reachable upper QER approach, and paired narrow seam repairs. The lower Awakening tier need not be entered; the outside three-stack sightline is not available from this interior route. | “This is the atrium; its repair pattern is part of this place.” There is no reason yet to postulate duplicates. | Player follows a legible internal path to H0's QER. An occluded, test-only QER handoff leads into a neutral quantum-room receiver shell, then a one-way exit to H1. |
| **Visit 2: H1** | The atrium has the **same proportions and vertical grammar** but now shows a diagonal splice repair. Let the player inspect the difference and a reachable QER approach; do not spotlight either. No line of sight connects it to H0. | “The same atrium has altered or I reached another condition of it.” The interrupted QER/room passage and known quantum effects make this plausible; the distinct repair also permits a more perceptive multiple-place hypothesis. | Repeat the same kind of occluded QER → receiver → atrium passage, this time to H2. No voice, label, or animation states that an atrium moved. |
| **Visit 3: H2** | Again preserve the atrium/QER/Awakening layout, but expose the offset capped-post repair. The other two atria remain out of sight. Let the player compare in memory or backtrack within the local space. | “This place has another persistent condition” can still explain sequential differences. Three separate sites is also a legitimate early inference; do not suppress it. | Ask for the **pre-proof sketch here**, before the return. Then use the same occluded transfer grammar to reach H0 through a receiver shell. |
| **Return and proof** | H0's paired seams are unchanged. From its interior, the player walks to either existing A or B viewpoint and can see H0/H1/H2 simultaneously, separated in depth, with each remembered repair attached to its own complete vertical stack. | A return to an earlier “state” may remain plausible until the simultaneous view. Any correct inference made on return but before the view is logged as early, not credited to A or B. | No moderator explanation. Lock the first-view response before allowing the other viewpoint. |

The identical traversal grammar is a **fair source of ambiguity**, not a theatrical trick: same-sized atria and interrupted interstitial travel make “one changing facility” economical, while the durable differences are available to contradict it later. Do not force a participant to say that theory. If the lead-in mostly produces “three places” or “I have no idea,” it has failed to establish the intended starting model, regardless of the final image.

### Pre-reveal sightline condition

Use fixed, opaque **interior** baffles/turns and the QER receiver shells to prevent co-visibility during the three visits. They must stay physically unchanged throughout the run; they must not cover the external repair silhouettes, QER/Awakening stack relationship, or parallax at A/B. A final H0 return may use a different one-way receiver exit on the observation side of the same fixed partition. Do not bridge the three atria, hide a facility by despawning it, force the camera, or close an obviously artificial door that opens only because the test has reached its final beat. Pilot the entire route on video; any participant who discovers the three-stack vista early remains in recruited/staging diagnostics as a **lead-in leakage** case, but not in the clean first-view revision denominator.

## 2. Persistent identity evidence

Reuse the three signatures already in `triple_coexistence_probe.gd`; do not add colored tokens, labels, glowing markers, or lore. Each atrium's primary signature must be visible during that facility's visit and in the final exterior view. Its repetition on the QER and Awakening frontages, even if the lower tier was not entered earlier, is a *second, relational* confirmation that each vertical unit has its own material history, not three arbitrary decals. Confirm the prior-view cues in the wrapper before recruiting players; the current proof scene alone does not establish their interior legibility.

| Fixed unit | Identity available on first visit | Likely first reading | Later reinterpretation in the triple view |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H0** | Two narrow parallel seam repairs on the atrium approach, at a consistent position against its ribs/recess. Their upper/lower echoes can be seen at the final view. | Repair state of the first atrium; later reappearance may suggest a state returning. | The paired seams are still on the foreground H0 stack while the other remembered repairs are present elsewhere **at the same time**. |
| **H1** | One slanted splice on the same atrium grammar; its upper/lower echoes can be seen at the final view. | A changed/repaired version of the place, perhaps caused by the QER passage. | The diagonal splice identifies the *second visited* atrium at a separate, fixed position; its upper and lower continuations belong to it. |
| **H2** | An offset post with a cap against the same shared proportions; its upper/lower echoes can be seen at the final view. | A further local condition, not automatically a third site. | The capped-post silhouette identifies the *third visited* atrium, distinct from both paired seams and diagonal splice while all three remain visible. |

Do not rely on cue color alone or on a feature so subtle that it can only be recognized from a developer's camera. Conversely, do not make the differences so monumental that the player is told “three facilities” by the first two visits. The pilot question is whether a player can remember each repair as a *place-specific history* without being told what it means.

## 3. What the final view must change

The strongest spontaneous account is: “I took the changing atrium to be one place; the paired seams, diagonal splice, and capped post are the places I visited; those three whole QER–atrium–Awakening stacks are now present together. My passage changed **where I was**, not which atrium existed.” The player need not use these words or know the transfer mechanism.

The simultaneous separated stacks rule out a **single current atrium toggling between H0/H1/H2**; remembered attached repairs and fixed vertical relationships connect the current view to earlier visits, rather than to three newly seen facades. Lateral movement and parallax help rule out a flat display. The proof does **not** by itself mathematically disprove arbitrary offscreen creation, time travel, or every possible hidden transport; those are unfalsifiable additions unless the player supplies positive evidence. Ask which account best fits the observed continuity, not for metaphysical certainty. Do not claim that ordinary adjacency alone proves the intended earlier journeys: the player must map *their own* visits onto the three stacks.

## 4. Smallest Godot implementation to test later

**This task creates no scene or script.** A subsequent, separately authorized implementation should remain an isolated test:

- **Scenes:** one temporary cognitive-test wrapper using the existing `scenes/tests/triple_coexistence_probe.tscn` geometry, player, three repair identities, and A/B viewpoints. The three units remain resident. Add only identical, plain internal stairs/ramps to each QER approach, receiver shells, interior sightline baffles, short one-way local exits, and the H0 return approach needed for the four beats. Prefer one continuously loaded world over three swapped copies.
- **Test logic:** one small local sequencer for deterministic, fully occluded player handoffs H0 → H1 → H2 → H0; spawn/position and collision safety checks; optional timestamps for each arrival and first triple view. The apparent QER movement is a **surrogate**. Do not infer production destination selection, room-door orientation, or a general teleport rule from it. If an ordinary existing interaction is needed to leave a receiver, reuse it unchanged; the receiver's already-open exit stands in for completed room work rather than inventing a puzzle.
- **Remain fake:** exact QER eligibility and destination law, quantum-room interior puzzles, source-specific door faces, and the reason for the route. Keep these off the interpretation being tested. Record whether any surrogate handoff itself causes “scene load/teleport” theories.
- **Do not implement:** final facility, exposition, documents, UI explanation, new global observation rules, Left/Right exchange, passenger carriage, Beam/camera puzzles, production doors, or arbitrary atrium-to-atrium corridors. Do not alter Awakening, existing systems, or the technically validated proof geometry merely to make the lead-in work.

The wrapper's feasibility is **not yet technically established**. It requires every earlier arrival to show its repair from inside **without** showing the other stacks, and both final A/B views to retain the exterior relationships. The original greybox's sightline and traversal passes do not validate a wrapper with baffles, QER access, and receiver exits. Before human recruitment, separately verify PlayerController traversal for every beat and both final approaches, player-camera cue visibility, collision and arrival safety, and absence of an early triple vista from *all reachable pre-proof positions*. A hidden scene change at the proof boundary, or a visible bridge between units, invalidates the intended causal test.

## 5. Blind test and decision record

Use the existing [blind protocol](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_BLIND_PLAYTEST_PROTOCOL.md) for eligibility, A/B balancing, first-view locking, and full/partial/failure codes. The more neutral before/after sketch wording below supersedes its example sketch questions for this combined sequence. Add these lead-in measurements; no participant-facing H labels or designer hypothesis are supplied.

1. **Before play:** for the controlled A/B comparison, recruit unspoiled players with the **same experiential** observation/room-persistence prerequisite, not a verbal explanation. If experience varies, analyze those sessions separately rather than treating a small-sample covariate as a cure for the confound. Give controls and “explore as you normally would,” not a mystery goal. Record any prior spoiler or assumed world model.
2. **During visits:** record repair attention, time in each atrium, whether the player compares the spaces, any volunteered “same place,” “different places,” or uncertainty, and any premature view or transfer artifact. Do not ask “is this one facility?” and do not correct an early three-facility inference.
3. **At H2 before return:** first record any theory volunteered during play. Then offer blank paper: “Could you sketch your route so far and talk me through it?” Follow with “What makes you think so?” and “What would you expect if you returned?” Code the **participant's own** baseline as one changing place / multiple coexisting places / uncertain / other, without showing these options. Keep volunteered versus sketch-elicited theories distinct; the sketch itself can provoke insight. Photograph it. Report the baseline distribution for *all* eligible participants; the targeted reversal denominator is only those who independently held the one-changing-place model. A small no-sketch observational pilot may estimate prompt reactivity, but cannot prove a silent participant's baseline belief.
4. **On H0 return and at first A/B view:** record any pre-view revision separately. Randomize the first assigned viewpoint using the same neutral route instruction, log actual first adequate view, allow the protocol's unassisted window, then ask its neutral follow-up only if needed. Before crossover, offer new blank paper: “Please sketch what you think you have seen. Talk me through it.” Follow with “What led you to draw it this way?” Do not imply that their view *must* have changed, ask for a count, or suggest copies.
5. **Code blind to condition if possible:** a full *revision* requires the participant to connect all three prior visit identities to three simultaneously present, fixed physical stacks, cite both an identity detail and co-presence/depth/continuity evidence, and abandon their own earlier single-changing-place account **before explanation**. Merely pointing at three buildings, repeating the moderator, or adopting the right noun without a causal account is partial or failure. Early-correct participants can demonstrate a stable correct model, but not the targeted reversal. Keep spontaneous and neutrally elicited responses separate.

Run a small balanced exploratory round (the existing protocol suggests 6–8 eligible first-view sessions per A/B condition), preserving raw counts and quotations. Do not declare a population pass from a small sample. Record **two independent gates**: (a) did the lead-in naturally generate the intended mistaken model often enough to test revision, and (b) among those who held it, did the first view repeatedly produce evidence-backed correction? If gate (a) fails, fix the lead-in before judging the proof. If gate (b) fails despite strong baseline formation and adequate views, revise evidence/viewpoint presentation before asserting the mystery works. The current result for both gates is **NOT TESTED**.

## 6. Failure diagnosis

| Observed response | What it may mean | Discriminating check and next decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **“Three facilities” before the proof** | Early correct inference, spoiler, too-distinct repairs, or the route leaked an exterior sightline. Not an invalid participant merely because they were right. | Compare their pre-proof sketch, explanation, video sightlines, and transfer experience. If repeated unspoiled players infer three sites from legitimate clues, the intended *wrong-to-right* arc is not reliably induced; do not manufacture a false belief. If the view leaked, keep the session in staging diagnostics but exclude it from the clean first-view denominator; repair staging and rerun with new participants. |
| **Counts three spaces but cannot connect them to earlier visits** | Visual numerosity works but identity memory/recognition fails. | Review whether each cue was in the player's camera during its visit, whether they attended to it, and whether interior/exterior views match. Do not call this proof of reinterpretation. Improve cue continuity or prior-inspection opportunity only, then retest. |
| **Keeps “one facility in three quantum states”** | Coexistence, physical separation, or the old model's contradiction is not legible; a cut/load may also have taught scene replacement. | Before score lock, use only “What do you make of what you have seen?” Review spontaneous movement/parallax and handoff artifacts afterward. If they never saw all three clearly, it is an exposure problem; if they did, the evidence chain remains insufficient. An obvious player jump or load that independently produces the correct answer contaminates the lead-in, not a valid proof-view success. |
| **“Just a large building with three wings”** | Could be a harmless name for three coexisting fixed units, or failure to reidentify prior visits and the QER/atrium/Awakening stack boundaries. | Judge their sketch and prediction, not “wing.” Can they place *each earlier visit* in a different persistent stack and reject the one-changing-atrium theory? If yes, count the correct model while noting scale terminology; if no, diagnose a spatial-boundary gap. |
