# Core Mystery and World Knowledge Framework

TASK-031 — design proposal, not an implementation specification or approval to build.

This document recommends a long-term mystery and the smallest set of additional laws worth validating. Awakening, the Operations Atrium, all prototype rooms, and all implemented mechanics remain as they are. Proposed phenomena below do not yet exist in the game.

## The ultimate mystery

The player should finally understand:

> The facility was built as a quantum transport instrument. The Cube is its simplest visible component. Some of the building's structures share a state, and a person supported by one of those structures can travel with it. Observation was keeping the apparatus safe, but also keeping its occupants in a configuration with no way out.

The initial question is personal and concrete: **Where did the people go, and why does this place appear to have no usable exit?**

The early explanation is reasonable: an unstable specimen caused an accident; the facility contained it; restoring the facility should release the player. The revised explanation is that researchers incorporated the phenomenon into transport infrastructure. During evacuation, an emergency observation network held that infrastructure in its internal configuration. Some people departed through a configuration the player has not yet understood; the remaining equipment was left maintaining containment.

This is not a reveal that observation was fake, that the Cube never moved, or that the player was dreaming. The early experiments remain true. What changes is the assumed boundary of the quantum object and the player's relationship to it.

The emotional turn is from repairing a prison to recognizing a stranded means of travel. Restoration gives useful access and readable instruments, but restoring every observer is not equivalent to restoring freedom. The player must understand which observations to preserve and which to remove.

The final action should confirm this understanding physically: familiar structural features occupy a previously impossible relationship to the outside, and the player crosses an ordinary threshold. A terminal, voice-over, or explanatory ending screen must not supply the missing inference.

### Proposed incident history

1. Researchers first isolated relocatable samples.
2. They learned to construct small coherent assemblies and mapped their repeatable configurations.
3. Transfer cradles made it possible to move supported equipment and personnel.
4. A safety incident prompted broad artificial observation, fixing several assemblies in their internal service configurations.
5. Evacuation records describe apparently incompatible routes because people departed through different configurations of the same infrastructure.
6. The present facility has a mixture of failed services and still-functioning observation equipment.

The incident's precise cause and the player's identity remain narrative decisions. Neither should add another law or be necessary to solve the escape. Do not invent a mastermind, simulation, time loop, or secret player power to make the ending work.

## What the player can trust

Existing behavior is the foundation:

- Direct player observation holds the current quantum object in place.
- An enabled, unobstructed Stabilization Beam is an artificial observer.
- Observed destination envelopes are unavailable.
- Observation of the current object and observation of an empty candidate position have different consequences: hold the present state versus exclude a possible next state.
- Release has a cancellable interval. Ordinary lights, darkness, recordings, and the player's intention are not additional observers.
- The player cannot block a Beam with their body.
- Inferring a state from a gauge, a diagram or memory is not direct observation. Only actual sight of the relevant envelope or an enabled artificial source reaching it supplies the implemented observation constraint.
- Existing objects move at most once per uninterrupted unobserved period. Reobserving them permits another later release.

Current tuning is not hidden lore. The shared 0.05-second release lead-in, 0.2-second hidden grace, probe sampling, and optional immediate-return exclusion should not become codes the player must infer.

One particularly important constraint: **turning off the lights is not currently equivalent to removing observation**. The design must use real occlusion, field direction, and looking away. A pitch-black screen cannot secretly count as blindness while the current geometric observation system still sees the object.

## Candidate laws and recommendation

| Candidate | Mystery value | Main danger | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supported-player entanglement | Changes the player from an external experimenter into a possible participant | Unexplained contact rules or an ordinary teleport disguised by effects | Validate a narrow, physically readable support rule |
| Coherent facility assemblies | Explains contradictory architecture and expands the meaning of “object” | Arbitrary map swaps, unseen locks, combinatorial complexity | Make this the central new law; keep bounded assemblies and a stable reference structure |
| Residual states | Lets the player investigate history and distinguish competing explanations | Photographs that unexpectedly observe, time travel, or hidden sequence puzzles | Use persistent physical evidence; defer a separate quantum-memory mechanic |
| Literal whole-facility superposition | Very large final recontextualization | Every visible wall locks everything; internal-observer exceptions break trust | Reject for the first complete game unless a simpler model demonstrably fails |
| Relative spatial invariants | Lets a player recognize the same assembly across configurations | Abstract matching puzzles or another vocabulary burden | Treat as evidence of coherence, not another mechanic |

### A. Supported-player entanglement

Working rule:

> A person fully supported by a quantum assembly can be included in its transition, provided the assembly is unobserved and the whole resulting configuration, including space for the passenger, is safe.

This should be discovered late relative to looking away and destination exclusion, but it is a property of the world from the start. No research flag, upgrade, journal entry, or visit order enables it.

“Physically connected” needs a precise boundary. Recommend **standing with full support on the assembly**, not touching any quantum surface:

- A rigidly integrated cradle belongs to its quantum assembly.
- A player standing on that cradle is a temporary passenger.
- Brushing a side, standing nearby, looking at it, or touching it through unrelated floor geometry does not attach the player.
- Ordinary contact does not recursively entangle the floor, facility, and planet.
- Leaving the support before a transition cancels passenger inclusion.
- A blocked passenger destination prevents that transition; the assembly must not depart and leave a supposed rider behind or place them inside geometry.
- The player retains their relative position and facing on the support. No scripted camera turn or teleport button is required.

This is a proposed additional rule, not behavior already supported by QuantumRelocator. Its physical boundary is a major design-validation risk.

The rider remains an observer. Standing on the cradle and watching its Cube or any changing structural member must still hold it in place. Entanglement does not grant an exception to vision. A player can release it by looking toward fixed, nonparticipating architecture while every participating member/payload surface is occluded or outside view. Under [TASK-034.3's direct-geometry contract](OBSERVATION_EVIDENCE_CONTRACT_REVIEW.md), a direct silhouette counts, but a cast shadow on independent architecture is indirect evidence, not an observer. Boarding does not cancel an external Beam either.

**How it changes exploration:** a previously unreachable position becomes somewhere the player can inhabit; an occluding service structure becomes a useful place from which to stop observing; a safe random relocation can become a deliberate journey by excluding alternatives. Early experiments can involve unattended equipment secured to a cradle, followed later by a safe personal experiment.

**Why discover it later:** the player first needs a reliable external model. The unsettling extension is “I can be inside the experiment,” not “there is now a teleport ability.” Foreshadow the possibility through equipment designed to support weight, matched docking scars, and records of people emerging where a walking route did not exist.

**How it differs from simple teleportation:** destination choice remains constrained by present observation; supporting and occluding the assembly matters; external observers can prevent travel; the visible infrastructure participates in the same transition; there is no destination menu, address, activation button, or privileged transport mode. If replacing it with an ordinary lift preserves both the solution and the revelation, this candidate has failed its purpose.

### B. Facility quantum state

Working rule:

> Some objects and architectural members form one coherent assembly. Each allowed configuration is a complete, correlated arrangement of those members. Observing any member holds the assembly's current configuration; observing any member's prospective envelope excludes that complete candidate configuration.

The building does not respond to a Cube by receiving a conventional switch signal. The Cube and associated structure are members of the same state. Evidence must distinguish this from a Cube acting as a key on a pressure plate.

Recommend a network of small coherent assemblies within a stable building, rather than one global state variable for the entire map:

- The hub's main floor, perimeter, major landmarks, and return routes remain a reliable reference.
- Selected transfer structures, collars, or service partitions can participate in assemblies.
- Begin with one assembly and two configurations on paper. The escape may require three configurations.
- Two assemblies never acquire unexplained long-range coupling merely because both are quantum.
- Give participating components recurring construction evidence: continuous chassis, distinctive fracture relationships, matching fabrication seams. Avoid colored destination pads or magic link lines.
- A changed route results from the placement of real architecture. Never silently remap an ordinary door to a different room.
- The state determines geometry and ordinary consequences such as a conduit physically meeting a socket. Avoid arbitrary “Cube at C means remote door D opens” logic.

A global facility interpretation is still achieved: the research site contains distributed pieces of the apparatus, and its working connectivity depends on their states. It does not require every wall to move at once.

**Critical observation contract:** a visible participating doorframe must hold the same assembly as its visible Cube. Inhabiting an assembly does not exempt its surfaces from observation. Only static architecture outside the assembly is safe to look at during release. No partly observed room changes around the player.

**Critical transition contract:** validate the complete candidate arrangement together, including collision, player clearance, and observer paths after departing members are removed. A visible member must never be allowed to change because only a remote Cube was tested. Do not fake a correlated assembly by letting several ordinary relocators roll independent destinations.

These contracts are new design work. Reusing current observation queries might be appropriate later, but the existing relocator does not already implement them.

**Literal whole-facility alternative:** allowing the entire building to relocate while an occupant sees its interior would require a new distinction between internal and external observation. That might support another game, but here it undermines “player vision counts” and makes the opening lesson incomplete in a misleading way. Do not adopt that exception to rescue a spectacular finale.

### C. Quantum memory and residual states

Three distinct possibilities should not be conflated:

1. **Ordinary persistent traces:** wear, condensation, impact marks, mechanically recorded pressure, unplugged equipment, and construction joints show that another configuration existed. These survive because the surfaces carrying them did not participate in the transition.
2. **Assembly-carried history:** a distinctive scratch or damaged fitting travels with the assembly, proving identity across locations. It records no new behavior.
3. **Active quantum memory:** the system prefers, reenacts, or responds to a sequence of former states. This adds another mechanical rule and a hidden dependency on history.

Recommend the first two. Defer the third.

A stationary docking recorder could retain a physical mark from contact with a cradle; the mark is evidence that the cradle arrived, not an observer maintaining its presence. A pressure gauge's current response can be a normal consequence of aligned plumbing. Neither requires the world to remember an invisible puzzle sequence.

Static photographs or drawings may be historical evidence, but they do not stabilize anything. Do not introduce the idea that looking at an old image counts as current observation. Avoid live camera feeds until their semantics have been separately justified; dead inspection cameras remain scenery.

Residual evidence has no mandatory countdown and does not disappear after the player overlooks it. At least one durable corroboration exists for every essential inference. Historical traces must be attributable to particular physical causes rather than arbitrary glowing ghosts.

### D. Other mechanisms considered

| Possibility | Use | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occlusion geometry | Let a player reason about how to stop observing while remaining in a useful place | Use existing sight and Beam rules |
| Spatial invariants | Match a unique fracture or spacing across changed positions | Use as evidence of assembly identity |
| Instrument consequences | A connected pipe or loaded cradle changes a passive mechanical instrument | Use sparingly as physical feedback |
| Observer networks | Several existing Beams impose simultaneous constraints | Use only where individual sources and their reach are inspectable |
| Reflections or live image observation | Observe around corners or through devices | Defer; expands the observation contract substantially |
| Sound as an observer | Broader perception-based puzzles | Reject for this direction; sound may provide feedback only |
| Attention, belief, or remembered images as observation | Make the mind part of the mystery | Reject; cannot be consistently read from ordinary player behavior |
| Rewinding earlier configurations | Investigate alternate history | Reject; adds time mechanics and sequence management |
| Arbitrary linked switches, collected research tokens | Enforce area order | Reject; substitutes access bookkeeping for understanding |

The recommendation is two related extensions—coherent assemblies and supported passengers—with residual evidence supporting both. Do not greenlight every candidate as an additional system.

## Knowledge progression through revised hypotheses

The following is an inference map, not a room order. Discoveries may overlap, happen early, or require revisiting evidence. Knowledge is never checked by the game.

| Phenomenon encountered | Plausible initial hypothesis | Evidence that challenges it | Better model and new possibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cube changes position while the player explores | A timer or proximity trigger moves it | Staying nearby while looking away differs from walking away while watching | Observation matters; deliberate experiments become possible |
| An apparently unobserved object remains still | This object is broken or different | Its behavior changes when an existing Beam's actual coverage changes | Observation has more than one source |
| An empty position never receives the object | The object dislikes that location or follows a fixed cycle | Excluding one position changes the available outcomes without attracting anything | Watching can remove possibilities, not merely freeze a present object |
| Two structures repeatedly change together | A sensor on the Cube operates a door | Observing either changing member holds both, even without a power connection | The assumed object boundary is too small |
| Plans disagree with today's route | One plan is wrong or construction changed | The same unique fitting and joint pattern recur in both configurations | Several accounts describe real states of one facility |
| A secured load appears with its cradle elsewhere | Only laboratory objects can travel | Human-scale supports, evacuation evidence and a safe personal trial agree | Physical support can make the player a passenger |
| Restoring services prevents a useful transition | More power should always improve the facility | Inspection fields preserve an internal configuration while other services work normally | Operation and observation are different; selectively maintaining constraints matters |
| No ordinary exit route joins the occupied interior | The missing exit must be built, unlocked or found elsewhere | A documented exterior configuration shares the same transfer structure | The apparatus already connects outside; the player must occupy the right configuration |

The last inference combines earlier knowledge. It must not arrive as a newly announced rule at the final door.

Two plausible routes through this understanding:

- **Historical route:** investigate contradictory Records plans, inspect Containment's matching hardware, discover an observer in Signal, then use Power's physical service relationships to distinguish “powered” from “observed.”
- **Experimental route:** investigate Power's inconsistent equipment positions, use Containment to test linked members, discover Signal's exclusion effect, then reinterpret Records' evacuation traces.

An informed player may skip evidence and use the rule early. An uninformed player may enter the same spaces but fail to predict outcomes. Neither a four-wing completion requirement nor a research checklist should exist.

## Four research areas

Each area needs a practical former purpose, a question visible from ordinary exploration, and evidence that becomes more useful after another discovery. The following are future content briefs, not authorization to expand the current hub.

### Records wing: incompatible accounts

**Former purpose:** preserve facility drawings, survey results, incident records and staff movement.

**Curiosity:** how can two plausible plans disagree about a major connection while sharing the same distinctive damage?

**Evidence:** overlapping survey diagrams, physical rubbing or photographs of matching fittings, an evacuation route that reaches a landing absent from today's layout, and ordinary personal belongings left along that route. Human artifacts establish departure rather than merely decorating the room.

**Partial understanding:** the discrepancies may describe real configurations, but the records do not explain how to reproduce them.

**Cross-area revision:** the fittings can be matched in Containment; a Signal observation record explains when the connection stopped changing. Two stationary docking traces suggest other arrivals even if the player never reads a technical note.

**Behavior showing understanding:** the player returns to compare a specific feature or predicts that a different configuration will reconnect the route. Reading every document is not required.

### Power and cooling: restoration has consequences

**Former purpose:** supply circulation, transfer equipment and inspection services.

**Curiosity:** why does restoring one service make machinery less flexible while another restoration simply makes it easier to inspect?

**Evidence:** physically traceable supply lines, a manifold whose connections differ across states, a loaded mechanical gauge, a disconnected inspection branch, and an existing Beam that can be inspected from both sides of an occluder.

**Partial understanding:** energy and observation are separate relationships. A powered light is not automatically an observer; an enabled Beam is.

**Cross-area revision:** a coupled conduit helps explain the Records drawings. A Beam-controlled candidate here foreshadows destination exclusion without turning the area into a required switch exercise.

**Behavior showing understanding:** the player predicts which source must remain active and which must cease observing, rather than turning everything off or restoring everything indiscriminately.

### Containment works: the boundary of the object

**Former purpose:** inspect and move unstable assemblies while protecting workers.

**Curiosity:** why are a supposedly isolated Cube, a transfer frame and a load-bearing cradle built as one structure?

**Evidence:** repeated structural seams, a fixture whose distinctive damage moves with it, secured equipment occupying the same relative position after transition, and ordinary docking access allowing safe support-based experiments.

**Partial understanding:** some things travel as an assembly. Its boundaries are physical and consistent; touching unrelated equipment is not enough.

**Cross-area revision:** Signal demonstrates that observing the frame alone can pin its remote member. Records supplies corroboration that passengers, not only instruments, used the cradles.

**Behavior showing understanding:** the player tests looking at different members, then intentionally boards and removes observation while remaining supported.

The first personal transition must have a safe return and a visible, inspectable result. Do not make the finale the first place the player can discover transport works on people.

### Signal wing: what the facility is watching

**Former purpose:** coordinate inspection and status across transfer infrastructure.

**Curiosity:** why are some inspection fields directed at empty places?

**Evidence:** existing Beam emitters, traceable sight paths through fixed apertures, selected vacant envelopes under observation, dead non-observing instruments, and historical observation schedules that can be compared with evacuation evidence.

**Partial understanding:** absence can be maintained intentionally. A structure may remain unavailable because its prospective position is observed, not because it lacks power.

**Cross-area revision:** this explains a missing Records connection, a seemingly immobile Containment assembly and why a restored Power branch can preserve confinement.

**Behavior showing understanding:** the player explains why “switch all Beams off” permits several outcomes, then selectively excludes an unwanted one.

### Evidence redundancy

| Essential inference | Direct experiment | Independent corroboration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation matters | Awakening's unchanged Cube behavior | Existing world objects in another functional setting |
| Beam observes and excludes | Inspectable Signal coverage | Power equipment behavior under the same geometry |
| Structures share a state | Observe either member in Containment | Records identities and Power's changing physical connections |
| Supported passengers can travel | Safe cradle trial | Secured-load behavior and evacuation traces |
| An exterior configuration exists | Survey view of real exterior docking evidence | Records plan matched to the transfer hardware |
| Selective observation enables escape | Reversible candidate-selection experiment | Reinterpretation of the emergency observation arrangement |

Essential knowledge must have a reproducible source and corroboration. Lore can deepen the mystery, but should not be the only source of a control instruction.

## Proposed final escape: the transfer assembly

### What exists before the player solves it

One coherent assembly has three authored configurations. Its members are a Cube, a supported transfer cradle, and a structural collar. The collar's relationship to fixed openings changes alongside the cradle; it is not a remote door driven by Cube position.

| Configuration | Physical relationship | Player meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — internal inspection | Cradle accessible from the facility, collar aligned to an internal service opening | Present starting point |
| B — internal return | The same assembly occupies a second internal landing | Safe alternative and a chance to learn from an incomplete model |
| C — exterior transfer | Cradle and collar align with an existing exterior receiving landing | The escape route already exists |

These letters appear only in design documentation.

The fixed building includes a survey position from which the exterior receiving hardware can be identified, an accessible Beam supplying current-state inspection, and a separate stationary Beam capable of covering B's candidate envelope. The inspection emitter can also be aimed through a real aperture toward C for internal recovery. Controls directly operate those instruments. Their functions and geometry can be understood before boarding; there is no master “escape” switch. Fixed internal walkways connect both internal landings to these controls regardless of assembly state.

An opaque fixed service hood lets a supported player look at static architecture while every changing member and its observation envelope is out of view. The hood belongs to the fixed structure, not the assembly. Darkness is unnecessary. It must not be an interactable blindfold or a last-minute exception to vision.

Each receiving berth has equivalent fixed overhead shielding, so the passenger can retain the same relative pose without immediately seeing participating geometry change on arrival. Validate current and arrival body/payload views, including peripheral surfaces. Separately review actual authored lighting for conspicuous shadow discontinuities; cast shadows are not an arrival veto. The controls and boarding approach preserve a continuous view of A until the player intentionally turns away; execution must not require racing the release interval.

The same safe boarding and occlusion arrangement must have been available during the earlier personal experiment. The finale adds synthesis, not an unannounced execution technique.

### Reasoning and action

1. Match the exterior docking evidence to the assembly and infer C as a real configuration.
2. Keep B's empty candidate envelope under Beam observation.
3. Remove the inspection Beam from the current assembly without removing B's exclusion.
4. Walk onto the supported cradle while observing it; seeing any of its changing members still holds A.
5. Look into fixed occluding architecture, leaving the entire assembly unobserved while staying supported.
6. After the ordinary release interval, C is the only safe alternative. The assembly and passenger transition together.
7. Look back at the arrived assembly to hold it, then step onto the fixed exterior landing and walk out.

After arrival, seeing the outside is a consequence and confirmation, not a required timing window.

### Why the solution is sufficient

At release, A is the current state and is not a relocation candidate. B is excluded by an unchanged existing Beam. C is unobserved, structurally safe, and has space for the passenger. Exactly one alternative remains. No favored random roll, clue flag, chapter completion, or hidden move count is needed.

This future assembly must permit return to any otherwise legal configuration. The current singleton Cube's optional immediate-return exclusion is not a universal law and must not silently invalidate C. This is a future assembly-policy decision to validate, not a change to the present Cube.

Every proposed essential inference contributes:

- Current observation explains why A remains while inspected.
- Artificial observation explains both the inspection lock and B's exclusion.
- Empty-destination observation makes the final result deliberate.
- Coherence explains why both the cradle and physical exit alignment will change.
- Supported transport explains why the player can accompany the change.
- Residual evidence establishes that C exists and is the same apparatus, not a hypothetical leap into unknown space.

“I already had everything I needed” means the player has always had the necessary body, perception, interaction controls and accessible apparatus. It does not mean every current TASK-030 object secretly implements this solution today.

### Failure, recovery and counterexamples

| Attempt | Expected consequence | Recovery or design obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remain watching a changing member | No transition | Turn toward real occlusion; no false activation feedback |
| Remove player observation but leave current inspection Beam | No transition | Inspect the source; boarding does not bypass it |
| Remove all Beam constraints | Either B or C may be selected | B is safe and returnable; reaching C by chance is a valid success |
| Release without boarding | Assembly moves without the player | Fixed observation positions and controls allow recalling an internal configuration |
| Try to shield a Beam with the body | Beam remains effective | Preserve current player-transparent Beam behavior |
| Observe C from the survey position | C is unavailable while observed | Leave that sightline before boarding |
| Block every alternative or lack safe passenger clearance | Assembly stays where it is | Remove an ordinary constraint; no death, consumption or global reset |
| Arrive at B | A different internal view and landing confirm co-travel | Step off, reobserve, and use the same inspectable exclusion scheme to return or retry |
| Step off at C | Player stands on a fixed safe landing | Escape does not depend on keeping the cradle present forever |

The fixed internal access and observation positions must allow the empty assembly to be brought back without entering an unavailable state. Validate this as a reachability table before building geometry.

| Present state | Recovery target | Constraint to retain | Observation to remove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A, player has not boarded | Remain at A for boarding | Inspect A directly or with its Beam | None until ready |
| B, with or without passenger | A | Aim the inspection emitter at C's candidate envelope | Beam at B and player view of B; A must be hidden |
| C, empty while player is inside | A | Observe B's candidate envelope with its Beam | Any view or Beam on C; A must be hidden |

Reobserve the arrived assembly before another release. These returns cannot depend on a previous-state ban, inaccessible controls, or walking across the absent cradle. Their actual sightlines are a Phase 1 paper-design gate, not a claim that the current hub already supports them.

An accidental C arrival is not failure of the rules. Do not add a knowledge check to reject it. If naive experimentation reaches C so often that the mystery has no time to form, revise the evidence distribution and access sightlines during design validation, not the underlying eligibility rules.

## Revised roadmap and decision gates

**Phase 1 — Design validation.** Test the laws and revelation using state tables, static diagrams and facilitated paper exploration. Try both knowledge routes, all failed escape attempts, observed members, rider clearance, return eligibility and recovery from each state. Ask participants to predict outcomes before showing them. Proceed only if they can revise a plausible early model into the recommended model without an internal-observer exemption or an explanation of the final solution.

**Phase 2 — World structure.** After a separate implementation authorization, establish bounded assembly feasibility in an isolated mechanism fixture and then draft the connected facility. Preserve a stable hub reference and walkable return routes. Plan access to evidence in several orders. Do not construct four new tutorial chambers under wing names.

**Phase 3 — Research areas.** Develop small, cross-linked evidence slices organized around functional workplaces. Validate the two independent evidence sources for each essential inference. Test prediction, not merely completion time. A safe personal entanglement trial precedes the finale in the available evidence, while remaining skippable for an informed player.

**Phase 4 — Final mystery.** Integrate the exterior configuration and complete escape, then validate the whole experience with players who have not seen the prototype or this document. Judge the ending by what earlier evidence they reinterpret and whether they can explain their action before taking it.

Go/no-go questions before any architecture expansion:

- Can players identify an assembly boundary through repeated evidence?
- Can they distinguish loss of observation from darkness, proximity and power loss?
- Can a supported player release the assembly without any visible member changing?
- Can all legal arrival states be made safe and recoverable?
- Can the exterior conclusion be inferred without reading a solution?
- Does the reveal change the meaning of the facility, rather than merely increase the size of a moving object?

If coherence fails, stop world expansion and revise the mystery model. If supported-player transport fails but coherence works, consider a weaker ending in which the player arranges a route and walks through it; acknowledge that this loses the personal “I am part of the experiment” payoff. Do not compensate by adding more mechanics.
