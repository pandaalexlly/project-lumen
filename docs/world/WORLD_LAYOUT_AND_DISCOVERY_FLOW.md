# World Layout and Discovery Flow

TASK-033.5 — design only. **REVISE before implementation remains in force.**

## 1. Design decision and boundaries

Organize the facility as a **fixed, walkable research workplace surrounding one shared architectural anomaly**, not four chambers containing four lessons. The surrounding areas offer different working views of the same bounded assembly, its receivers and the building that stays put.

The intended realization is:

> “That frame I treated as part of the building belongs to the object. Its other placement is the way outside.”

Sources and precedence:

- [Core Mystery and Knowledge Framework](CORE_MYSTERY_AND_KNOWLEDGE_FRAMEWORK.md): facility purpose and four research functions.
- [Coherent Assembly Law Contract](COHERENT_ASSEMBLY_LAW_CONTRACT.md): rigid membership, observation, passenger and recovery constraints.
- [Coherent Assembly Readability Audit](COHERENT_ASSEMBLY_READABILITY_AUDIT.md): boundary reinterpretation rather than a transport reveal.
- [Evidence Architecture and Misinterpretation Map](EVIDENCE_ARCHITECTURE_AND_MISINTERPRETATION_MAP.md): E1–E6 evidence and diagnostic experiments.
- [Central Hub Foundation](CENTRAL_HUB_FOUNDATION.md): existing physical anchor and current playable boundary.

The later, narrower contract takes precedence over whole-facility or independently rearranging architecture interpretations. This plan uses the proposed single rigid Cube–bearer–cradle–collar assembly in A/B/C placements. The apparent architectural element is its integrated doorway surround and threshold, not an additional remote wall or new mechanism.

The requested player baseline—Cube relocation, observation and Beam-as-observer—is an assumption for this design. It is not a claim that the implemented Awakening Chamber teaches Beam use. Do not retrofit Beam instruction into the opening. Empty-destination exclusion and passenger inclusion remain discoveries to support, not assumed prior knowledge.

No code, scene, hub expansion or existing-document revision is delivered here. Proposed connections below do not exist in the current build.

## 2. Facility macro structure

### 2.1 Preserve the hub as a reliable reference

Keep the implemented Operations Atrium's scale and relationships: the short Awakening vestibule opens into the taller hall; the circulation plant divides two connected walking routes; Records is west, Power east, Containment rear-left and Signal rear-right. “Rear” is called north below solely for design orientation, not a change to Godot axes or scene coordinates.

The circulation plant, hub floor, main pipework, operations desk, maintenance pocket and Awakening connection remain ordinary fixed infrastructure. The central landmark is **not** a quantum core. Walking around it offers choices and recognizable returns, not four sockets awaiting wing completion.

Preserve the opening restoration and return route. The initial confinement has already served the opening experience; do not repeat its restore-to-open structure four times.

### 2.2 Proposed fixed circulation

Beyond the existing approaches, arrange the four research areas around the hub with lateral working connections:

```text
                   C: outside receiver -> fixed onward ground
                   (no interior walking connection)
                              :
                         survey sightline
                              :
            Containment ----- Signal
                |                |
             Records           Power
                \                /
                 Operations Atrium
                         |
                 Awakening vestibule
                         |
                    Awakening
```

This is a relationship sketch, not a scaled floor plan. The complete walking graph also includes the hub's direct Containment and Signal approaches. All walking links are bidirectional. The colon denotes a view, never a walkable link or portal.

| Fixed connection | Former workplace purpose | Discovery value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awakening ↔ hub | Chamber service access through the existing restored bulkhead | A familiar small space anchors the larger facility; returning confirms the opening remains true |
| Hub ↔ Records | Operations access to archive and survey work | Offers the western route without an assembly experiment |
| Hub ↔ Power | Plant inspection and maintenance circulation | Offers an equipment-led alternative to the western route |
| Hub ↔ Containment | Personnel access to heavy research handling | Makes A's apparent architectural threshold available early |
| Hub ↔ Signal | Instrument servicing and survey access | Makes the exterior question available without touring the other areas |
| Records ↔ Containment | Archive staff's physical inspection access | Connects an oblique architectural view to the actual joint it shows |
| Containment ↔ Signal | Shared fixed inspection apron behind the research workfaces | Connects A, observation equipment and survey views without crossing the cradle |
| Signal ↔ Power | Instrument supply/service access beside B's receiver | Connects vacant hardware to its observer and to the other internal placement |

Together, the lateral links and the hub's west/east routes form a loop, with the rear hub approaches providing shorter returns. These are portions of the four working areas, not additional corridor levels or a fifth research wing.

All four areas, both internal receiving approaches, survey access and required Beam controls remain reachable on fixed ground for every assembly placement. A/B occupy side workfaces adjoining this circulation; they never replace its only floor or doorway. A player can investigate, compare and recover an empty assembly without first learning to ride it.

### 2.3 Current seals versus the future plan

Today all four hub approaches end at physical, noninteractive seals. They are content boundaries, not existing puzzles. This document does not open them or claim the loop is playable.

For the future connected-world version, recommend authoring the primary passage at each approach as already passable, with grilles/gates visibly parked clear. Keep the functional enclosures and orientation cues; do not add four power tasks, passwords, collectible permissions or a new restoration signal that unlocks them. Damage may constrain a work pocket, but must not turn the fixed comparison/recall route into an assembly-dependent gate.

This is a proposed later content change requiring its own authorization. It preserves the current build now and avoids silently inheriting four locked tutorial branches into the final layout.

### 2.4 Place the anomaly between functions

| Element | Proposed relationship to the working areas | What stays available when it is absent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: internal inspection | At Containment's eastern workface beside the shared Signal apron; looks like a service threshold with a specimen fixture beside it | Fixed approach, independent pier, receiving wall, inspection access and loop |
| B: internal return | At the Power–Signal workface; receives the same rigid structure in a different service relationship | Fixed maintenance floor, R's source/control access and return route to A |
| C: exterior transfer | Beyond Signal's outer survey wall, on the already proposed fixed exterior receiver | Survey view and internal recall remain accessible; C's onward ground remains safe |
| Inspection Beam I | On the fixed shared apron near A, with real possible aims at A and through an aperture toward C | Control access does not depend on A or C being occupied |
| Beam R | On the fixed B workface, its source and candidate coverage inspectable from the Power–Signal connection | Its actual power state remains inspectable whether B is occupied or vacant |

Prefer same-orientation rigid placements initially. Do not stretch the bearer across wings or connect distant architecture by an invisible relationship. A and B are near enough for practical comparison and fixed returns, separated enough by real walls/workfaces to allow independently hidden candidates. Exact distances, emitter positions and occlusion geometry are still open.

## 3. Major spaces: first impressions and physical reinterpretation

### Awakening and vestibule

**First impression:** a small abandoned experiment space whose restoration opens access to something larger.

**Physical evidence:** preserve the isolated Cube experiment, service equipment and existing restoration response. The vestibule retains a recognizable return silhouette from the hub.

**Later reinterpretation:** the experiment supplied a trustworthy small example; later apparatus applies the same observation principle to a larger, visibly integrated object. The opening itself was not secretly travelling.

**Misconception challenged:** “The first room was staged,” or “everything with this material must be one quantum building.”

Do not add retrospective clues claiming unseen structural membership. This space contributes a stable baseline, not a new assembly lesson or a required revisit.

### Operations Atrium

**First impression:** a partially restored coordination hall; the plant gives the player a reason to circle rather than choose a labelled level.

**Physical evidence:** existing manifold/risers, dormant operations equipment, interrupted maintenance and damaged cable tray establish distinct service functions. Through the rear approaches, future fixed working passages hint at a larger continuous workplace, not a row of sealed challenge entrances.

**Later reinterpretation:** the hall supplied stable bearings while selected structural pieces elsewhere occupied different placements. Its damaged infrastructure did not change just because a collar disappeared.

**Misconception challenged:** “The entire building moves,” and “I need to activate all four wings.”

Repeated returns should show the same plant-to-vestibule and pipework relationships. There is no progress display, wing-completion lighting scheme or final control podium. Spatial constancy is this area's contribution; it need not contain a puzzle.

### Records and survey work

**First impression:** an abandoned administrative/archive workplace, with cabinets and a survey workbench facing a physical inspection opening toward Containment.

**Physical evidence:** the cabinet backs and bench frame a walkable lateral route, not a mandatory reading station. Through the inspection opening, the player can see A's doorway-like collar beside an independently supported pier. At closer access, the doorway's underside rib and repaired asymmetric joint can be compared with the building's separate receiving seam. The same pier remains when A is empty.

The bench's ordinary measuring equipment suggests why somebody worked at this view. Optional drawings or photographs may corroborate former configurations, but removing every readable page leaves the live comparison intact. A photograph never acts as an observer.

**Likely wrong interpretation:** “That is an ordinary frame,” or “the archive's view is of a different, identical doorway.”

**Later reinterpretation:** the familiar frame is the travelling signature; the fixed pier and opening identify the same location after the structure has gone. Seeing the live collar through this opening really can hold A; no observation exemption exists for an archive viewpoint.

**Evidence contribution:** bounded identity, fixed surroundings, and an alternative collar-only observation position. It challenges both duplication and Cube-only control, rather than owning a “history lesson.”

### Containment workface

**First impression:** equipment inspection and handling around a Cube; its adjacent threshold seems to belong to the facility.

**Physical evidence:** expose the bearer from Cube mount to broad cradle deck to integrated collar. A docking gap separates it from the receiving wall. The asymmetric gusset and repaired joint are visible both from independent floor and, more closely, from the deck's inward-facing side. Human contact wear belongs where people could plausibly stand or service the joint.

**Likely wrong interpretation:** “The Cube powers a machine,” or “this is a lift that needs activation.”

**Later reinterpretation:** a view of the collar alone holds the entire structure, while a view of adjacent fixed architecture does not. The joint travels without changing its local relationship to the Cube. Boarding changes who is supported, not who counts as an observer.

**Evidence contribution:** E1/E2 boundary and non-Cube observation, E4 identity across placements, E5 a reason for a reversible supported trial. The apron continues around the workface; a transition never closes the room behind the player or grants permission to leave.

Do not make the deck mandatory merely to inspect a clue. A player may investigate from fixed ground, send it empty, or ride early. No experiment result must occur on their first visit.

### Power and cooling work

**First impression:** exchangers, fixed service runs and the maintenance floor continue the hub's industrial function. B appears to be an unused service interface.

**Physical evidence:** the receiver has ordinary contact wear and an asymmetric clearance matching the bearer. Fixed floor wear and supported pipework contrast with the travelling joint when B is occupied. From the connecting work path, R's source and its reach into the receiver can be inspected without consulting an electrical diagram.

Use ordinary, visibly independent supply routes to show that service connection is not rigid quantum membership. Do not introduce a machine that must be repaired to make B, C or another wing eligible.

**Likely wrong interpretation:** “This station lacks power,” “this is a second copy of the apparatus,” or “turning a switch calls it here.”

**Later reinterpretation:** the station receives the same structure, and an enabled observer can explain its vacancy. The receiving floor stays behind when the bearer leaves. A power control affects a particular observer, not the identity or availability of a secret exit chapter.

**Evidence contribution:** fixed-versus-travelling comparison, candidate exclusion and a second support/arrival context. It challenges both the “all power is progress” model and the assumption that every nearby service line joins the quantum object.

B's fixed landing permits a safe personal arrival and a walk back to A. There is no Power-room success condition or compulsory restoration sequence.

### Signal and survey work

**First impression:** inspection equipment faces working structures and apparently empty space; an outside landing can be seen but not reached by the interior service path.

**Physical evidence:** traceable fields, fixed apertures, independent hood supports and an exterior survey opening share the same apron. The outside receiver's contact shape and onward ground can be inspected from inside. The fixed aperture and outside structural landmark establish a durable spatial reference.

**Likely wrong interpretation:** “These instruments are broken or aimed badly,” “the outside station is unrelated,” or “a missing bridge needs repair.”

**Later reinterpretation:** observation can preserve a vacancy; the exterior receiver is a genuine possible placement of the object previously mistaken for a doorway. The fixed hood is useful because it belongs to the building, unlike the collar.

**Evidence contribution:** E3 fixed spatial reference, E6 exterior identity and fit, plus current-versus-candidate observation. This is not a Beam training room: its views explain phenomena encountered in Records, Containment and Power, and those areas explain the equipment here.

The interior survey path ends behind a real protective boundary at a service separation, not an invisible wall. Do not imply a repairable bridge with broken hinge parts, an empty item socket or a promising inaccessible button.

### Exterior receiver and onward ground

**First impression, from inside:** ordinary receiving hardware occupies an unreachable outside service landing. It appears to lack a useful connection.

**Physical evidence:** fixed receiver feet/contacts and asymmetric clearance correspond to the bearer. A real empty C arrival presents the same repaired joint and rigid arrangement against the already familiar outside landmark. The onward floor remains visible and independent.

**Later reinterpretation:** this was not an unfinished exit awaiting construction. A configuration of the familiar object already fits here, and a supported person can arrive with it.

**Misconception challenged:** “A different final machine will take me out,” or “the exit becomes available only after all research is complete.”

This is an arrival relationship and a short ordinary departure, not another challenge area. No final key, locked terminal, explanation screen or second new rule follows the successful arrival.

## 4. Nonlinear discovery and return loops

### 4.1 Three plausible routes, not three required tours

| Curiosity route | Available walking path and first question | Voluntary experiment and revision | Revisit with changed meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture-first | Hub → Records → Containment, or directly to Containment. “Why does this doorway's rib continue into that equipment?” | Inspect boundary; compare its signature at B or C; test collar-only sight with other observers accounted for | The Records view becomes a view of a possible occupant, not permanent architecture; the exterior receiver becomes a plausible fit |
| Observation-first | Hub → Containment, or Hub → Power → Signal → Containment. “Why does hiding the Cube not release it?” | Inspect actual Beam reach; with current inspection removed, compare collar-only observation and full concealment. Then distinguish B exclusion from current holding | The Power receiver and Signal instruments explain repeated absences; the previously ordinary collar now defines what must be hidden |
| Exterior-first | Hub → Signal, or Hub → Power → Signal. “Why is useful receiving hardware outside with no walking connection?” | Compare receiver geometry with A's bearer; inspect an empty C placement and recall; consider a supported journey | The service threshold seen earlier is reclassified as the missing connection, not a control panel for a separate exit |

The listed paths are examples of curiosity-driven movement; no ordering script or area-completion condition enforces them. Players can reverse direction, investigate another question halfway through, or combine routes.

A player who never enters Records can still inspect the joint and fixed pier at Containment. A player who never explores Power's work pocket can still inspect B and R from Signal's shared apron. A player can see C and source coverage from the shared survey access without searching every Signal rack. The essential apparatus remains accessible without clearing four areas.

### 4.2 Overlapping evidence, not four lessons

| Inference | Direct investigation | Another available perspective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apparent architecture belongs to the quantum object | Containment's traceable bearer and non-Cube hold experiment | Records' live oblique view; the same collar appearing at B |
| Only a bounded structure changes | Vacated A beside its fixed pier | B's stationary receiving wear; hub and exterior reference relationships |
| Empty observation excludes a configuration | Inspect R at B and compare actual candidate conditions | I aimed at C from shared survey access; compare all alternatives constrained versus one open |
| Full support can include a player without disabling sight | A's broad cradle, fixed cover and voluntary trial | Safe B arrival, fixed return access and repeat from independent floor |
| C is a placement of the same object | Survey fit and empty C occupation | Recognition of the signature already examined at A/B; fixed onward ground |

These perspectives need not supply equally convenient views, but no inference is owned by a wing. Records remain optional corroboration, not the source of the rule. Secured cargo or human wear can invite boarding but cannot replace a personal support experiment.

### 4.3 Pacing through changed questions

The plan offers an initial broad choice, a short local investigation, and an easy return with a more specific question. It does not prescribe minutes or require repeated cycles to prolong the mystery.

Useful return motivations are concrete: inspect a joint from its other side; find out whether a vacancy contains the same contact geometry; test whether watching the frame holds the Cube; compare the outside fit. They are not “finish the next wing.”

Keep contrast between active workfaces and quieter fixed circulation. No sweeping panorama should expose every current and prospective member all the time. Conversely, do not hide the anomaly behind a long, unbroken corridor whose only reward is a lesson at the end.

The initial assembly may be genuinely held at A by I, with R genuinely excluding B, as proposed in TASK-032. Both sources are inspectable and reversible; neither is a hidden teaching lock. If the player's first empty release goes to C, that is valid evidence. If a first supported release reaches C, accept escape. Evidence distribution must tolerate early inference and accidental success.

## 5. Final escape setup: encounter early, understand later

### 5.1 The three first encounters

**The apparent exit:** at A, the service collar frames an outward-looking opening and reads as a possible route toward daylight. Approaching reveals an internal inspection edge and the actual fixed separation from outside, not a locked door awaiting a badge. The nearby fixed apron remains walkable. Do not place an exit label or a false interaction prompt on the collar.

**The exterior receiver:** from Signal's independently accessible survey edge, the player can see the outside contact geometry and onward ground. It may be noticed before the Cube assembly. There is no interior walking link across the separation and no concealed ordinary path that makes the quantum hypothesis unnecessary.

**The moving assembly:** approaching A from Records initially emphasizes the doorway surround; approaching from Containment exposes its connection to the Cube. Approaching B from Power may first show a vacant receiver or the arrived structure, depending on actual state. These are different views of one apparatus, not a staged first encounter.

In the initial A configuration, a player can inspect the apparent exit, C and the assembly before any relocation. If exploration changes its state, the physical evidence and recall remain available. None is revealed only after visiting the other wings.

### 5.2 Reinterpreting the familiar threshold

At A the bearer fits an internal work opening; at C the same complete geometry fits the outside receiver. No ordinary door changes which room lies behind it. The collar, cradle and Cube preserve their relative positions while their relationship to fixed architecture changes.

The deck's physical support and the independent hood's cover are already usable during investigation. Their final use is not a new transport mode. The player has the necessary body, vision, ordinary interaction controls and apparatus access from the beginning of hub exploration in the proposed connected version.

The planned informed solution remains:

- Recognize C as the same assembly's real outside placement.
- Keep B excluded by actual Beam coverage.
- Remove current inspection if it is active, maintaining sight of A during boarding.
- Stand fully on the structural cradle and remove the last current observation using broad fixed cover.
- Arrive with the structure and step onto independent outside ground.

This is the explanation of a possible solution, not player-facing instructions or a mandated sequence of proof gestures. If I is already off, no redundant toggle is needed. Turning everything off may allow B as well as C; arrival at B is recoverable. A lawful lucky C arrival is not rejected.

### 5.3 Fixed access, sightlines and recovery are obligations

| Layout obligation | Why the mystery depends on it |
| --- | --- |
| I's A control approach keeps a real member in view while the player boards | Preparation is understandable holding, not a race against an invisible timer |
| A collar-only comparison hides the Cube while retaining a hidden, safe alternative and equivalent other observer states | “The frame belongs” must be a causal inference, not an unseen Beam or blocked-destination coincidence |
| Records' live view and the survey opening obey observation normally | A window cannot be a decorative exception when it reveals a member or candidate |
| Separate fixed view breaks allow A, B and C envelopes to be hidden when required | The whole map must not unintentionally pin the apparatus through every investigation sightline |
| C's identifying features are readable at normal viewing scale | No new zoom tool or text identification is needed |
| Fixed shielding works for current and mapped arrival poses | No visible transition, moving-hood exemption or unexplained camera-safety veto supplies the solution |
| Controls and inspection positions remain reachable with A/B absent | Empty relocation invites investigation rather than stranding the player |
| C has a safe fixed dismount and onward route | Arrival confirms understanding; it does not introduce a last-second challenge |

Recall requirements remain those of the law contract: at B, reobserve with R, exclude C with I and then release B with A hidden; at empty C, reobserve with I, retain R's B exclusion and then release C with A hidden. Actual reobservation rearms a spent release. These paths must fit real apertures and fixed access; this macro plan does not prove their ray geometry.

The full structural and secured-payload geometry counts, including direct silhouettes. Under [TASK-034.3/S2](OBSERVATION_EVIDENCE_CONTRACT_REVIEW.md), cast shadows on independent surfaces are indirect evidence, not observation; actual authored lighting receives a separate shadow-continuity presentation check. Looking into darkness is not release. Boarding does not stop the player's vision or make their body block a Beam. No support, safety or geometry is changed by this clarification.

## 6. Anti-portal / puzzle-room audit

Ask whether each major space could be reduced to “enter, operate its local device, unlock the next door.” If so, the following revision is part of this proposal.

| Space | Conventional puzzle-room risk | Revision in this layout | Relationship that must survive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Awakening | Repeat its restore-and-exit pattern throughout the world | Preserve the completed opening, but do not replicate its confinement structure in the wings | The original observation evidence remains true; later scale changes meaning, not the rule |
| Hub | Four branches feed a final central lock | Use a stable landmark, fixed loop and direct rear approaches; no completion scoreboard or four services to collect | Returning distinguishes stationary facility structure from the travelling object |
| Records | Read a code or explanatory log to open Containment | Keep the working connection passable; expose a live view of the same joint and fixed pier | Before/after identity and actual sight matter even with all text removed |
| Power | Restore a generator to enable the next wing or final destination | Keep circulation and candidate existence independent of restoration; expose B, fixed services and R's actual field | The difference between power supply, observation and membership explains the evidence |
| Containment | Perform a prescribed Cube trick to open an exit door | Put A beside the through-route, with independent inspection and optional boarding access | Watching an architectural member, rather than a local success trigger, constrains the same object |
| Signal | Solve a Beam switch sequence to unlock the final room | Share its apron with A/B and survey access; retain only real observer controls, no master escape switch | Empty-space exclusion and the distinction between moving collar and fixed cover explain the final possibility |
| Exterior relationship | Unlock a teleport chamber after four discoveries | Show C and its fit early; use the already encountered assembly; provide ordinary ground after arrival | The previously misclassified architectural piece supplies the exit through a different placement |

Ordinary switches remain legitimate controls of existing Beams. The design does not depend on remembering a switch sequence: the current geometry and observation state must explain every outcome. No keys, passwords, wing attendance, reset-to-start chambers or remote “solved” signals connect the areas.

A normal lift or portal could reproduce transportation outside, but would erase the explanatory role of collar-only observation, candidate exclusion, fixed cover and the mistaken boundary. If players describe only a discovered transport button, this layout has not achieved its purpose even if they finish.

## 7. Design assessment and handoff limits

Desk checks of this proposal:

- The future fixed walking graph connects all four areas, internal approaches and controls without assembly travel.
- Each research area contributes more than one interpretation and shares evidence with another perspective.
- The apparent exit, exterior receiver and integrated structure can be encountered before a full tour.
- The hub and Awakening remain fixed, truthful references; no whole-building quantum claim is needed.
- Every major space has an explicit revision against a self-contained solve-to-leave pattern.
- The plan uses the existing proposed assembly, passenger and Beam contracts; it adds no law or new mechanism.

Unresolved before approval:

1. Whether the proposed adjacency and aperture geometry can satisfy all current/candidate/arrival observation conditions at once.
2. Whether the collar reads as architecture first while its true boundary remains available on the same first visit.
3. Whether players voluntarily compare views and propose boarding without instructions.
4. Whether C's identity can be recognized at survey distance without an explanatory document.
5. Whether fixed support, partial-support safety and concealed arrival feel predictable rather than arbitrary.
6. Whether early legal escapes tend to express understanding or merely bypass most evidence by chance.
7. Whether unbriefed players reinterpret an earlier space before arriving outside.

These are remaining validation questions, not reported playtest results. The macro graph is a design proposal, not proof of buildability or spontaneous discovery.
