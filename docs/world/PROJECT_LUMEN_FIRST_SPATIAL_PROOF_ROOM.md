# Project Lumen — First Spatial Proof Room

**Status: design-only proof candidate; not approved for scene construction.** This document specifies an encounter the proposed room-exchange law *would have to support*. It creates no gameplay rule, scene, script, new room pair, or implementation task. The [spatial duplication validation](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_DUPLICATION_VALIDATION.md) still holds its **REVISE BEFORE DETAILED ROOM DESIGN** verdict: P/Q geometry, camera contrast, recovery, and human comprehension are unvalidated. The design below uses the same bounded M/N modules and fixed P/Q sockets from the [spatial rules](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md), [evidence contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md), and [facility layout V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_FACILITY_LAYOUT_V2.md). It does not repurpose the implemented single A/B/C assembly.

## Purpose and footprint

“The space itself changed; objects did not simply move” is player shorthand, not a new physics claim. Under the existing proposal, **the room shells are quantum objects**. The intended discovery is that a familiar fixed approach now meets a *different persistent inhabited structure*; its wall, floor, ordinary door, and attached equipment—not merely loose contents—have changed spatial relationship to the atrium. The atrium and both socket beds remain fixed.

The “proof room” is not a sealed teaching chamber. It is a compact **fixed comparison pocket** at the atrium's service edge: a P-facing threshold, a Q-facing survey view reached by the existing functional circulation, and two different ways between them. A comparison path must hand off a direct view of at least one current shell between ordinary openings, so checking the second socket does not itself create an unobserved interval. A separate blind return around the circulation plant must put **both** shells and candidate poses out of the player's direct sight for an ordinary walk. A camera mount and its plausible service access belong to the facility, not to a puzzle plinth. No new destination, switch type, passenger exemption, or third quantum module is needed.

```text
fixed atrium plant -- P threshold / near-shell view -- P [M or N]
       |                     |
       |          comparison path (overlapping shell views)
       |                     |
       `-- fixed blind return -- Signal-side survey -- Q [N or M]
                              (the same two persistent shells)
```

This is a **topological requirement**, not measured geometry. It must later be checked against full wall/floor visibility, both candidate placements, camera and Beam paths, safe walking, and the existing atrium. The comparison path needs a genuine overlap or handoff of member sight at normal player height—not one explanatory panorama—while the blind route must genuinely release both. The player must be able to inspect P and Q while they are held, then leave both unobserved without walking onto either travelling floor. If those two route conditions cannot coexist, this encounter is not a valid proof design.

## Initial state and first expectation

The proposed starting occupation is `N@P, M@Q`. P is approached from the fixed atrium: its threshold and pipe/contact scar are part of the familiar plant, while N presents a solid architectural face with its own later damage. Q is seen from a fixed survey position: M shares the same broad construction grammar but has a distinctive repaired structural seam and an ordinary door with its own persistent hinge state. M's door faces a safe blind service recess at Q. Both modules exist simultaneously; no runtime copy appears.

A player entering from Awakening can reasonably believe: **“The facility is fixed; quantum objects inside it are odd.”** After the A/B/C worksite, a second reasonable belief is: **“The Cube can carry a larger machine or change equipment.”** Near-matching service construction and partial views make P and Q look like two ordinary rooms or two ends of one departmental installation. The atrium's damaged systems give the player a mundane reason for a mismatched piece of equipment without telling them “quantum architecture.”

No scripted arrival resets M/N to this arrangement. It is a design starting condition, not a required player order. If a lawful exchange already occurred, the same fixed comparison routes must still let the player establish the opposite state and test it.

## First anomaly: a wrong wall at a known place

After the player naturally uses the fixed return route while **neither complete shell nor either proposed candidate is directly observed by player, Beam, or live camera**, the pair may lawfully exchange. On returning to the *same atrium plant and P contact scar*, the player sees M's repaired structural seam and ordinary doorway where N's solid, differently damaged face stood. At the Q view, N's damage appears where M's repair was. The atrium plant, P/Q contacts, and exterior reference have not changed.

The near experience is “I know this approach, but it now meets a different wall.” Moving a crate cannot explain a continuous load-bearing seam, door jamb, and attached equipment changing together while the socket scar stays. **The first P sighting alone is not proof**, however: a security shutter, machine-driven wall, renovation, or simple navigation error could still explain it. The reciprocal Q check—made by an observation-preserving comparison route—is the first decisive contradiction. It shows that N did not vanish or become M; the two identifiable shells traded fixed relationships. If that second check is unavailable or illegible, this is merely a striking anomaly, not a logical spatial proof.

The change must occur unobserved, not as a visible wall-morph or cutscene. A player who never leaves a current participating wall out of sight should see no exchange. A camera pointed at either current shell likewise holds it, even if its monitor is unwatched. Ordinary darkness does not remove observation.

## Evidence chain the player can assemble

These are separable facts, not a scripted four-step performance. Each remains available on repeat visits.

| Observation | Natural misinterpretation | Contradiction the space permits | New conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The atrium plant, P threshold scar, and return route are stable while the frontage's wear differs. | “I took another corridor,” or “maintenance replaced equipment.” | Retracing from the same plant to the same scar gives a different **structural shell**, not just another furnishing. | The fixed *place* and the thing occupying it must be distinguished. |
| M's repaired seam and ordinary doorway can be found at Q while N's different damage is at P; both exist at once. | “Two similar rooms were built here.” | After an unobserved interval the identities are found at opposite sockets, with their own door/damage states intact. | Two persistent room identities exist; a coordinate is not an identity. |
| M's wall/floor/door and attached state move together; P/Q contacts and independent pipes do not. | “The Cube moved some objects,” or “a central control synchronized two doors.” | No loose-item transfer or remote hinge change predicts a repaired *wall* arriving at one socket and N's whole shell taking its old socket. An ordinary changed door condition stays with M on a repeat test. | A bounded inhabited architectural volume participates as one quantum object. |
| Keeping direct sight of **either** participating shell holds both occupied positions; leaving both unseen permits a reciprocal change after the ordinary release interval. | “The room changes on a timer,” or “I pressed a transfer button.” | With sources and access otherwise constant, sight/no-sight makes opposite predictions; the player can repeat after reobserving both rooms. | The room pair obeys the existing observation law, not a new room-only trigger. |
| A powered live camera aimed at a member can hold the pair; the **same powered lens** aimed at fixed scenery or physically occluded cannot. | “CCTV operates a door lock.” | The effect tracks the actual sightline while unrelated power, player view, and Beam reach stay unchanged; stored footage has no hold. | Camera observation extends the already known observer family. It does not select a room or open the doorway. |

This chain proves a **present two-room exchange**, not the historical cause of their near-duplication. A shared old construction flaw or optional record can deepen that question later. It must not be the only way to identify M and N or authorize the exchange.

## Player-controlled experiment, not an activation sequence

The comparison pocket should invite a question: “If I keep that repaired wall in sight, will it still be there when I return?” There is no objective prompt. The player can intentionally run the following contrasts in either occupation:

1. **Identify before acting.** From fixed ground, notice the atrium plant and P scar; use the observation-preserving comparison route to identify the other shell at Q. M is the shell with the integrated repair and doorway; N has different later damage and a solid face. Both should be visible in normal exploration, not only through an exact peephole.
2. **Hold by personal observation.** With Beam paths and the camera known to miss both participating shells and proposed candidate surfaces, watch **one actual structural member** at P or Q through the normal release interval. Predict that *this shell* remains at *this socket* while it stays in view. Do not turn away to check Q as the proof of a hold: that turn could itself release the pair. Watching a fixed pipe or a shadow is not equivalent. A no-move result alone is weak until the release contrast works.
3. **Release without observation.** Reobserve **both** actual rooms to rearm them, then take the broad fixed return that conceals both complete shells and both proposed candidate poses. Keep active Beam and camera sightlines off members. After the established unobserved interval, revisit P and Q via the comparison path. Predict the other shell at each socket; inspect both identifying features and the unchanged fixed contacts. No button chooses the destination and no passenger ride is required.
4. **Repeat in reverse.** Reobserve M and N at their new positions from fixed access, then repeat the hold and full-release comparison. The prior state may return if lawful; the game must not use an invisible “never go back” rule. If a trial does nothing, the player should be able to find whether they still saw a member, a source still reached one, only one room was rearmed, or a candidate/safety condition prevented the exchange. None of these may be disguised as random delay.
5. **Camera corroboration, not first key.** Once a clean player-view contrast is possible, leave player and Beam sight unchanged and compare a **live, powered lens on a member** with the same lens aimed at fixed structure or physically blocked. Recheck both sockets after each trial and rearm between trials. This may also reinterpret an earlier stubborn state at the known A/B/C assembly; it must not first appear as a terminal beside a final door.

The proposed camera may be physically present from the first visit, but its role is initially mundane surveillance. The initial anomaly needs a lawful interval in which that lens does **not** observe M or N; if the only way to produce it is to flip a conspicuous camera switch, the room reads as a mechanism puzzle. The Beam can remain part of the player's prior knowledge without adding an I/R-style source to this pocket. Any existing Beam that genuinely reaches a current or candidate member must be accounted for; it cannot be ignored for staging.

## A prediction with consequences

Before a repeat release, the player should be able to say—in their own words, not M/N/P/Q terminology—something like: “The repaired room will be at the atrium scar; the dented room will be at the outside contact; that pipe will stay.” A correct prediction of **both room identities plus one fixed reference** is the minimum evidence that they have moved beyond “the wall randomly changed.” A second, stronger prediction is that watching either room wall or pointing a live lens at it will prevent this change.

M's existing doorway may align with the fixed exterior spur when M occupies P. That consequence must be physically lawful even on a player's first successful exchange; do not install a hidden stage gate to save the ending. But crossing it should be a *choice* after a verifiable comparison, not the only way to learn that a room moved. The first proof is strongest if the player can inspect, return, and predict another exchange from fixed ground before choosing any exit. The post-crossing perspective can confirm the spatial model; it must not supply the first essential clue.

## Anti-confusion audit

| Risk | Why a first-time player might believe it | Design check / corrective evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Random or scripted room swap** | A change happens only once, between visits, with no controllable comparison. | Both states are recoverable. Personal wall-sight holds; broad full concealment releases; after real rearm the contrast repeats. No entry trigger forces a state. |
| **Only loose objects teleported** | The camera frames props but crops out floor, wall, and socket seam. | Show the repaired *structural* seam, door jamb, floor boundary, and attached equipment arriving together; fixed contact/scar stays. Check N at the other socket. |
| **One room teleported / elevator** | Only M is ever identified, or the player experiences a ride before an empty exchange. | Establish two simultaneous histories, then an empty reciprocal exchange. The player stays on fixed ground; both sockets have a different known occupant afterward. |
| **Navigation error or copied level asset** | P and Q look interchangeable and the atrium provides no fixed anchor. | Keep a memorable plant-to-P path and distinct P/Q contacts; compare both from fixed routes in either state. Do not rely on a map label. |
| **Camera is a remote exit switch** | Powering it down coincides with a door becoming reachable. | Compare the same powered lens aimed on/off a *member*, with other observation unchanged; the doorway's hinge state travels with M rather than being commanded by the lens. |
| **The answer is obvious on entry** | Both unique scars, socket seams, camera view, and exterior spur appear in one explanatory panorama. | Distribute these facts across short, revisitable fixed views. Ambiguity may last, but an early correct inference remains lawful. No lore or color code names the rule. |
| **Identity difference is missed** | Repairs are tiny, decorative, similarly placed, or visible only from the exterior C yard. | Give each shell a distinct, structural *history* visible at ordinary player height from interior fixed access, and make the same feature recognizable after exchange. Human-test recognition, not designer familiarity. |
| **No move looks like a broken puzzle** | Player sees one sliver, a hidden camera/Beam holds, only one room was rearmed, or a candidate is unsafe. | Paper-prove every current/candidate sightline and recovery path. A camera's mount/aim must be inspectable; a blocked trial must have a discoverable cause. Do not claim candidate-only evidence where two occupied sockets make it impossible. |
| **Awakening was fake / the whole building moved** | Later rooms ignore the same observer rule, or fixed references are out of sight. | Keep direct observation, complete-member behavior, and rearm unchanged; retain atrium plant, pipe scars, and exterior ground in the before/after comparison. |

## What would make this feel like discovery?

There is no “correct configuration” indicator, level-completion cue, mechanic-revealing label, or mandatory demonstration. The important reward is a new prediction about a familiar place: the player can revisit the atrium, identify which room its threshold currently meets, and foresee what a real observer will prevent. The same facts can be found architecture-first, observation-first, or exterior-first. Knowledge changes the meaning of a previously ordinary service frontage and the player's route through the facility; no apparatus announces that a puzzle was solved.

**Gate before detailed design:** draw the complete two-state P/Q geometry and observer table; prove both fixed comparison views, full concealment, camera-only A/B contrast, two-room rearm, safe door poses, and recovery. Then test unbriefed people without this document: can they distinguish the two room histories, identify one fixed atrium reference, and predict the result of watching a wall versus leaving both rooms unseen? A “yes” inferred only from reaching an exit is not enough. Until those tests pass, this is a promising proof-space specification, **not a validated first playable room**.
