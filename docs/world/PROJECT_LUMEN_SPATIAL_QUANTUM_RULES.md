# Project Lumen - Proposed Spatial Quantum Rules

**Design contract, not runtime behavior.** The implemented baseline is direct-geometry player observation, real Stabilization Beam observation, candidate exclusion, and one supported passenger on the TASK-035/038B assembly. Live cameras, room-scale modules, and reciprocal room exchanges below are **new proposals requiring technical feasibility and human evidence tests**. They supersede earlier *design* deferrals of those ideas, not the implemented observation law. No scene or script has been changed.

## 1. Vocabulary and invariant boundary

- A **quantum object** has one physical identity, a bounded set of participating material surfaces, and a finite set of physically authored placements. Its Cube, casing, wall, door, and mounted equipment are members only if constructed as part of that object. Seeing **any** member constrains the whole object's current placement.
- A **room module** is a quantum object large enough to contain walkable floor, enclosing walls, a door, and attached equipment. “Space has a state” means that this inhabited architectural volume has different possible placements. Coordinates are not magical; there is no arbitrary camera-to-map remapping or scene substitution.
- A **socket** is fixed facility structure to which a module may align. Its bed, contacts, outer corridor, camera mount, and reference marks do not travel. The central hub, return circulation, exterior ground, and a limited observation/service spine remain fixed so a player can compare states.
- A **duplicated pair** consists of two distinct, materially present room modules, M and N. Their nearly matching original construction is the historical duplication mystery; later wear makes them distinguishable. Neither is a freshly spawned copy of the other. Their shared origin is a historical claim to prove with evidence, not a rule that creates matter during play.
- A **configuration** is the placement of every involved quantum object plus its own persistent internal state. Opening a door changes that object's ordinary hinge state; moving the object preserves that state. Mounted machinery, its switch setting, stored charge, and attached power source travel with it. Live output that depends on a *fixed* external socket may change when that connection separates or realigns; this is an ordinary electrical consequence, not a quantum reset. A fixed wall or pipe stays with its socket even if it once touched the module.

These definitions retain the opening's truth: a Cube may be the entire object, a Cube plus cradle may be one larger object, and an apparent room may be larger still. The fixed reference skeleton prevents the final claim from becoming “every wall in the universe moves.” The *facility's topology* changes because some of its actual rooms are quantum.

## 2. Observation is direct and source-based

An observer constrains a placement only when it has an active, unobstructed direct line of sight to a participating surface at that placement. The same current/candidate distinction applies at every scale:

| Source | When it counts | What does not count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Player vision | A member is in the existing guarded view with physics line of sight, including a silhouette or sliver of a room wall. The player still observes while aboard. | Looking at an independent wall, a cast shadow on that wall, an old image, or merely knowing where the room is. |
| Stabilization Beam | Enabled field actually reaches a member. It holds an actual placement or excludes an empty candidate. The existing player-transparency rule remains. | A visible beam projection that hits only fixed scenery; a powered indicator; an ordinary lamp. |
| Live facility camera (proposed) | Powered sensor/lens has a geometrically legible, unobstructed direct view of a member. It behaves as an artificial observer whether or not anyone watches its monitor or a recording is saved. | Unpowered/damaged camera, blocked lens, stored footage, still photograph, player watching a monitor, or a camera merely pointed at fixed scenery. |

A camera is an additional *source of the same direct-observation predicate*, not a new quantum power. Camera view geometry must be discoverable by where it points and what fixed occlusion interrupts it; no invisible all-seeing CCTV network. Ordinary darkness is not automatically blindness under the existing geometric contract. If a camera is authored as an active low-light sensor, its direct view still counts in darkness; ordinary lighting does not change the rule. Mirrors and relayed live-video observation remain unsupported and must not be required. A powered camera mounted on a travelling module that sees its **own** participating shell stabilizes it; the design may use this as a safety installation, but may not silently exempt it.

The phrase “remove all external observation” is insufficient for a passenger. Every observer path to current participating geometry must end, including the passenger's own view of the floor, wall, door, or Cube. There is **no internal-observer exemption**. At room scale, a supported passenger therefore needs an authored broad sightline to independent fixed shielding or another nonparticipating surface; simply closing a room door or declaring the passenger “inside” does not release it.

## 3. Current state, candidate state, and release

For an involved set of objects S, a transition is legal only when **all** current members of S are unobserved by every active source, every proposed member placement is unobserved from the present observer configuration, and complete geometry/occupancy/passenger checks are safe. A camera or Beam may hold a current room and may exclude a proposed room at an empty socket; neither summons a particular room. The player's current sight of a destination remains a veto even if the player would travel away at commit.

Candidate visibility is evaluated in a **vacated-source snapshot**: remove only the departing members of S and included passengers from their old poses, then place all candidate members together. Keep fixed architecture, unrelated objects, active observers, and people who are not included. This prevents a moving shell from falsely occluding its own destination and prevents an exchange from depending on a temporarily impossible intermediate state. Safety checks include every wall, door in its current open/closed pose, equipment, occupied volume, receiving seam, passenger capsule, and safe dismount. If anything intersects or would leave a person unsupported, that candidate is not legal. If none is legal, nothing changes.

Release has the established cancellable unobserved interval; reobservation cancels it. After one transition, an object does not repeatedly shuffle during the same uninterrupted unobserved opportunity. Actual reobservation rearms it. No “never return to the previous placement” rule exists: after rearm, any previous safe unobserved placement is available. Timing constants and sensor sampling are engineering approximations, not player knowledge requirements.

## 4. Room exchange without a magic map swap

The minimal proposed pair has two fixed sockets, P (interior) and Q (outer service), and two distinct modules, M and N:

| Composite state | P contains | Q contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | M, with M's door/walls/equipment/repair | N, with N's door/walls/equipment/repair |
| 1 | N, with N's complete persistent contents | M, with M's complete persistent contents |

The **exchange** is one atomic reciprocal candidate, not two sequential moves through an occupied bay. Both M and N must be released, both destination poses must be unobserved and collision-safe in the joint snapshot, and any carried passenger at either module must have a valid mapped arrival. An observer seeing *either* currently occupied room prevents the exchange. A camera seeing only one prospective shell in the joint candidate excludes the entire exchange. If only one module is rearmed after a previous exchange, the pair waits until both are eligible; fixed comparison views should make reobserving both easy, never a hidden finale trick. No other facility object becomes coupled merely because it is quantum.

At commit, both roots, their attached door states/equipment, and qualified passengers change pose atomically; then presentation and event signals follow. No frame may show a room gone while its wall, door, or occupant remains. The fixed P/Q corridors and contacts never move. An open doorway may align with an exterior spur in one placement and face a safe blank service recess in another. The doorway did not turn into a wall; a **different persistent room** now occupies the socket. “I entered left and emerged right” is possible only if the player actually travelled with a module under the passenger and concealment rules, or later walked a newly aligned ordinary route. It cannot be an unseen forced remap of a fixed doorway.

The historical duplication accident is a **starting condition**: one documented experimental design left two materially present room modules, M and N, with the same unusual pre-incident flaw. They subsequently acquired different repairs and states. No room is dynamically cloned when unobserved, and a present-day exchange conserves the two existing identities. A pre-incident plan for one module plus the shared flaw can corroborate their origin, but the player can prove their *present* two-room exchange physically without that record. The physical cause of the original duplication need not become a repeatable player-operated power. If the fiction later requires one room literally to exist in two places *at the same instant*, that is a different law and would need a new design decision, not a loose use of the word duplication here.

## 5. Passenger and camera safety at room scale

A player joins a transition only when grounded and fully supported by the travelling floor or cradle. Touching a wall, standing in a fixed threshold, spanning fixed and moving floors, or occupying a receiving recess does not qualify. Full support is not permission to move: the player's own view can still hold the room, external cameras/Beams can still hold it, and candidate observation can still exclude it. No attach button, closed-eyes command, or special teleport trigger exists.

The mapped passenger keeps their local stance and view orientation. Before commit, a candidate that would put their capsule in fixed geometry or immediately expose participating room surfaces to their mapped view is rejected. Provide physically broad independent shielding at both source and destination; do not solve this by ignoring the passenger camera. If an active camera travels with a module, its mapped post-arrival direct view also needs a conservative safety check; do not author such a moving live camera as a critical first example. The proposed playable model includes the player as its only temporary passenger. Permanently mounted equipment is a member; an unbound person or loose physics object on a departing floor is **not** silently carried and makes that transition unsafe. Author ordinary debris as fixed/mounted dressing rather than making loose clutter an opaque puzzle blocker. Avoid borderline indirect support as primary evidence.

## 6. Counterexamples that the law must predict

| Situation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Player watches only a visible participating wall while Cube is hidden. | The entire room module remains held. |
| Player watches a fixed wall and sees only a shadow or passive gauge change. | That information alone does not hold the room. |
| A live fixed camera observes M; player and Beam do not. | M cannot move; any P/Q exchange involving it waits. Turning the camera's actual sightline away may release it. |
| Player sees a monitor displaying M, but the sensor is off or aimed away. | The monitor image is indirect evidence, not an extra observer. |
| M and N are both currently hidden, but a camera looks through a fixed aperture where N's distinctive protruding member would be visible at P (while the present shell has no surface there). | The complete reciprocal exchange is excluded. No half-swap; this is candidate observation, not sight of the current occupant. |
| Only M's current observation is removed; N remains visible. | No exchange. |
| Player stands fully on M's floor but looks at M's doorframe. | No passenger movement; the player still observes M. |
| Player is fully supported, all participating geometry is concealed, but the mapped destination is blocked. | No passenger move or drop; wait for another legal complete state. |
| Player returns to a previously occupied socket after real reobservation. | Return is legal if all present observation and safety tests pass. |
| M appears at P with its previously opened door and distinctive repair; P's pipe mark is unchanged. | Persistent identity moved; neither a remote unlock nor a changing fixed room is needed. |

## 7. Design/feasibility gates

The pair exchange is a **substantial extension** of the isolated one-root implementation, even though it uses the same observation and safety logic. Before approving gameplay work, prove on paper and in a separate technical fixture that two occupied sockets can exchange atomically, including observer/camera cycles, passengers, every legal resting state, and recovery. Prove a broad fixed-shielded view from a room floor without exempting interior vision. Then test unbriefed whether players can tell a room identity from a socket and predict a camera hold. If these fail, reduce the room-scale ambition; do not quietly weaken the trusted observation rule or install a hidden knowledge gate.
