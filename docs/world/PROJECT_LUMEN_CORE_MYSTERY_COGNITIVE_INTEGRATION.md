# Project Lumen — Core Mystery Cognitive Integration

**Status: human-validation plan, not a readability result.** M/N spatial identity, the state-persistence A/B module, and camera observation have passed their respective **technical** greybox checks. No unbriefed session has shown that players discover all three or combine them into one causal model. This plan uses the [M/N Player Reasoning Audit](MN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_PLAYER_REASONING_AUDIT.md), [State Persistence Prototype Validation](STATE_PERSISTENCE_PROTOTYPE_VALIDATION.md), [Camera Observation Validation](PROJECT_LUMEN_CAMERA_OBSERVATION_VALIDATION.md), and [Knowledge Architecture V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md). It authorizes no gameplay change, combined scene, final room, or production gate bypass.

## The theory being tested

Can a player infer, in their own words, that **a bounded quantum object keeps its material identity and attached present condition as it changes lawful placement; fixed surroundings do not travel; any actual observer, including a live camera, can constrain a participating surface; and a room shell can be such an object**? This is a compact explanation of K1/K3/K5/K6/K7, not a new law. It must generate *before-action predictions* across scales and observer types. “I finished three tests,” a correct vocabulary recital, or a retrospective explanation after seeing an outcome is insufficient.

Observation's removal **permits**, not commands, a transition. The A/B and M/N isolated fixtures each have only one alternate occupation, so their deterministic returns must never be generalized into a destination-selection rule. This route does not validate K2 candidate-only exclusion, K4 passenger travel, K8 three-site history, stored-footage behavior, a final Q camera, or the ending.

## Minimal discovery route: four phases, one revisable model

This is a **research route using existing isolated experiences**, not a mandated order or room-by-room production plan. An insightful player may infer a later idea early; record it, do not suppress it. Present each scene with only a neutral invitation to investigate. Do not reveal K-numbers, “persistence,” “camera observer,” “exchange,” or the intended experiment in a participant briefing. Ask prediction questions only after the player's spontaneous account has been recorded; keep wording neutral and record whether a prompt was needed.

| Phase | Direct observation | Natural first misunderstanding | Player-controlled experiment and contradiction | Corrected understanding available | Later reuse to probe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Observation affects a quantum object** (completed Awakening, K1/K3) | A distinctive Cube can be found elsewhere after loss of sight; player view and reaching Beam can hold it. | Timer, scripted demonstration, power feed, or a Beam that *commands* a destination. | Compare an extended watched interval with an unseen interval while unrelated controls stay unchanged; compare a reaching Beam with one that misses. A watched hold challenges timer/command models. | A present, relevant observer constrains a bounded object. Losing observation can allow, not select, relocation. The facility still appears ordinary. | At A/B, predict a watched module will stay; at M/N, test whether a participating wall can be held. |
| **2 — Identity and attached condition persist** (isolated A/B module, K6) | The repaired module appears at A with an ON lever and fixed A lamp; it later appears at B with the repair and ON lever, while A's lamp is dark. | B copied the module, the receiver owns its condition, B switched it off, or A stores an old snapshot. | Inspect the same repair and attached lever at both beds. Change the lever to OFF at B, reobserve/release, then predict **before return** whether A's lamp and lever restore ON. A/OFF with a dark fixed lamp contradicts snapshot or socket-memory readings; an ordinary lever adjustment relights A. | The moving object carries its *current* identity and attached condition. The A contact and live output belong to fixed infrastructure and its present connection, not to a saved past. | At the camera fixture, separate a lens's effect on placement from the module's carried condition; at M/N, track integrated repairs and M's doorway rather than an address alone. |
| **3 — A camera is another observer** (isolated camera fixture, K5) | The familiar repaired module remains at A after player sight is removed, despite an eligible B. A powered lens has a clear view of a participating surface. | Security lock, broken module, automatic delay, or an E-operated cover that moves the module like a switch. | With camera power and module condition unchanged, compare clear lens-to-member sight with the optical path physically covered. Covering the lens while the player *continues* watching does not move the module; only removal of both views allows a lawful A→B move. Repeat after rearm. | The live lens supplies the same kind of constraint as player vision or reaching Beam. The cover removes a physical sightline; it does not select B or reset the object's state. | Predict what an ordinary lens **would** do if it saw an M/N wall, while noting that the isolated M/N fixture actually contains no camera. |
| **4 — Room identity differs from location** (isolated M/N pair, K7) | Two similar complete shells coexist at fixed A/B sockets: M carries its integrated repair and doorway; N carries its wound and solid rear face. The plant, rims, rib, stack, and outer run remain fixed. | Door animation, moved props, one teleporting room, random loading, player memory error, or the whole building shifting. | Inspect **both** identities and both fixed contexts, observe one wall for a no-change contrast, then release both from fixed ground and compare **both** sockets. Before a repeat, predict which repair/door package and wound/solid-face package will be at each socket, and which fixed references stay. | A bounded architectural shell can change placement while preserving identity and attached structure; its connection to fixed surroundings depends on occupation. The earlier Cube result remains true at a smaller scale. | Predict a future room/route relationship only as a hypothesis; do not score a true-ending solution or three-site explanation from this fixture. |

The transition from phase 2 to 3 deliberately reuses the **same kind of repaired module** so that the new variable is the observer, not a newly introduced object rule. Phase 4 then changes the *scale and boundary* of the object. It should not say “the whole facility moves”: fixed comparison references are part of the evidence.

## Dependency and arbitrariness audit

| Link | What is actually needed | What is helpful but **not** a knowledge lock | Risk of arbitrary appearance; required control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 → 2 | A repeatable notion of watched versus unseen relocation, plus a recognizable object at two fixed beds. | Beam equivalence helps but the A/B persistence contrast can be understood with player sight alone. | If A→B→A is scored as “memory,” the experiment is underspecified. The B-OFF → A-OFF counterexample is essential. |
| 2 → 3 | A known movable member and a way to separate player view from another source. | Prior understanding of attached condition gives an identity control; K6 is **not logically required** to discover camera sight. | The cover can look like a puzzle switch. Keep power and lever unchanged, show a covered-camera/player-watching hold, and require a prediction about the lens's *path*, not E itself. |
| 3 → 4 | No camera knowledge is needed for the existing M/N empty exchange; Awakening's observation rule and two distinguishable shells suffice. | K5 can prepare a later observer audit; K6 makes tracking a room's persistent repair/door more natural. | Do **not** insert an invisible camera veto into M/N to force a dependency. Its isolated proof uses player sight only; a hypothetical camera-on-wall question tests transfer, not implemented behavior. |
| 2 + 4 | The player can distinguish attached room history from fixed address/connection. | The A/B lamp/contact gives a small-scale analogy; M's rear door is already open and is not a player-changeable K6 condition in this fixture. | A player may know the lever travels but treat room scars as scenery. Require both reciprocal identities and fixed references in a *prospective* prediction. |

The apparent sequence is therefore a **minimal comprehension scaffold for testers**, not a causal dependency chain the final facility must enforce. V2 permits object-first, architecture-first, and other discovery orders. After a baseline cohort, vary the order of the camera and M/N fixtures for a small fresh cohort: if M/N only makes sense after a facilitator names “camera” or “persistence,” the physical room evidence is weak, not a reason to add a hidden prerequisite. Scene loading between isolated tests is an experimental convenience and may itself make the experiences feel like separate levels; this package cannot validate final-world pacing or continuity.

## Cross-prototype prediction probes

Ask these only after free exploration and free explanation. Record the player's exact prediction **before** they perform or see the relevant comparison. Do not teach the answer in the question; the clauses below are scoring targets for the researcher, not participant-facing text.

1. **Condition versus place:** At B after changing the attached lever to OFF, ask, “If it is at the other bed again, what do you expect to find there?” A coherent prediction keeps the same repair and OFF lever; A's fixed contact is still present, so its lamp stays dark. “A restores its old ON version” is a snapshot model.
2. **Observer versus control:** With the powered camera covered and the player still facing the module, ask, “What do you expect if you wait here? What if you step behind the cover?” A coherent prediction distinguishes player hold from complete release. “The cover makes it move” is a switch model.
3. **Source transfer:** After camera discovery but before a later explanatory prompt, ask which previously seen ordinary camera/lens or Beam line the player would inspect if a familiar object seemed stuck. The answer must specify **what surface it could actually see**, not merely “turn cameras off.” This is a reasoning probe, not evidence that an unbuilt Q camera is playable.
4. **Scale and ownership transfer:** Before a repeat M/N exchange, ask what will be at each side and what will remain where it is. A coherent prediction follows M's repair/opening and N's wound/solid face to opposite beds, keeps the plant/rib/stack/receiver rims fixed, and predicts the changed rear relationship. If they infer a live lens pointed at a wall would hold the pair, score that as a *hypothesis transferred from K5*, not a tested M/N-camera interaction.

The integrated theory is strongest when the player can explain **why** the same counterfactuals work without memorizing three fixture-specific commands. Accept ordinary language, including “teleport,” if the player still predicts persistent identity, actual sightline constraints, and fixed-world relationships. Do not accept a neat verbal theory that fails its next prediction.

## Human test design and scoring

Recruit unbriefed players who have not read these notes; record whether they previously played Awakening or know similar quantum-game conventions. The first cohort follows the four-phase route above, with the completed Awakening experience as phase 1 and the three isolated scenes launched separately. Give no scripted goal or answer-bearing title. Allow natural exploration, mistakes, and recovery; record first interpretation, voluntary experiments, camera/position, time to first contradiction, and any facilitator intervention. Ask neutral before-action questions at the existing repeat opportunities, then let the player test them. A later small order-variation cohort can distinguish true conceptual dependency from priming by sequence. Do not merge scenes or build a final room for this study.

Score each phase separately for: **noticed evidence**, **self-chosen discriminating test**, **prospective causal prediction**, and **post-result revision**. Mark spontaneous, neutral-question-assisted, or coached outcomes separately; coached success is not a discovery pass. Then score integration across phases:

- **Observer transfer:** player predicts a hold for a relevant live camera/Beam/player view, and explains why power alone, a recording, or a blocked line is insufficient. The current fixtures directly compare player/camera and Awakening's Beam, but not all three in one shared scene.
- **Persistence transfer:** player follows an object's repair and present attached condition across A/B and follows both M/N structural packages across fixed sockets; they do not invoke old-state recall or address-owned settings.
- **Boundary/relationship transfer:** player identifies fixed external references separately from moving members and predicts a changed ordinary connection without claiming the entire facility transformed.
- **Causal economy:** one model accounts for at least two *new* counterfactuals across fixtures before results are shown. Repeating the designer's phrasing or reaching a favorable position by trial and error does not satisfy this.

**Cognitive integration success:** an unbriefed player, without an answer-bearing prompt, makes correct prospective predictions for the B-OFF → A-OFF counterexample, the powered-clear versus powered-blocked camera with player-view control, and the reciprocal M/N identity/fixed-reference comparison; then uses the same observer-and-object-boundary model to answer at least one cross-scale/source probe. Report partial passes and wrong predictions, not just a binary completion rate. Do not set a production approval percentage before seeing pilot behavior; repeated failures of the same type trigger a focused evidence revision and a fresh unbriefed retest.

## Failure cases and what they would mean

| Observed behavior | Likely weak link | Response within this plan's scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Player predicts movement on a timer even after watching a hold. | Observation contrast or rearm timing is not legible. | Record viewing positions and whether the hold interval was comparable; diagnose evidence before changing any rule. |
| Player expects A's old ON state to return, or thinks B is a copy. | Attached versus fixed ownership, or the repaired identity, was missed. | Check whether they saw the repair/lever/contact at both beds and made the OFF prediction before return. |
| Player says “the cover unlocks it,” despite correct relocation. | Camera path and unchanged power are not perceived; E interaction dominates. | Seek a second physical sightline contrast in a future *separate* revision only if repeated blind tests support this diagnosis. Do not call it understanding now. |
| Player understands the camera alone but cannot apply sightline logic to a room wall. | Observer rule has not transferred to architectural members. | Ask which surface they believe is moving; inspect whether M/N shell boundary and repair cues were seen. Do not add a camera to M/N merely to force the answer. |
| Player understands the module but calls M/N one room with a changed door. | Scale/identity transfer fails; paired evidence or fixed comparison is too weak. | Record whether they inspected both shells *before* the exchange and checked both afterward. A door-only explanation cannot predict the reciprocal wound/repair packages. |
| Player explains each fixture correctly but cannot make a new cross-fixture prediction. | They learned local procedures, not a reusable theory. | Mark integration **not validated**; compare unbriefed retest and order-variation evidence before redesigning presentation. |
| Player reaches a “correct” result only after leading prompts or reading this vocabulary. | Study contamination. | Score coached behavior separately; recruit a fresh participant. |

Individual technical passes do not resolve an attention, memory, or conceptual-transfer failure. Conversely, an early correct inference should remain usable; there are no knowledge flags to unlock it.
