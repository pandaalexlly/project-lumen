## 1. Decision, scope and precedence

Seeing a cast shadow of a hidden assembly on independent architecture does **not** stabilize it. Seeing an actual participating surface, including its direct silhouette against a background, does. Existing enabled, reaching Stabilization Beams remain artificial observers.

This selects **contract S2** globally for the proposed world law, including singleton Cubes and later coherent assemblies; it is not a passenger or finale exception. At the time of this design review it did not change geometry, code, scenes, tests of implemented mechanics or configuration. A subsequent shared-implementation conformance pass removed the singleton Cube/Door shadow-footprint gameplay probes; §6 records the historical mismatch. No coherent assembly was implemented.

Read with the [law contract](COHERENT_ASSEMBLY_LAW_CONTRACT.md), [spatial proof](TASK034_SPATIAL_PROOF_AND_DECISION_GATE.md) and [evidence map](EVIDENCE_ARCHITECTURE_AND_MISINTERPRETATION_MAP.md). This review supersedes their shadow-as-observation requirement, not their membership, support, state-budget, candidate-safety or recovery rules. The law contract's old **A/B** comparison concerns geometric versus illumination-dependent observation; S2 retains geometric A and does not select darkness-based B. The spatial proof's **S1/S2 shields** are historical architectural trials, not these two observation contracts.

Keep three layers separate:

| Layer | Authority | What it must not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fundamental world law | Actual participating geometry seen directly, or reached by an enabled artificial observer, constrains state | Treat knowledge, every visible consequence or a shadow-catching wall as the quantum object |
| Engine approximation | Sampling, guarded frusta, raycasts and numerical grace approximate the selected law | Make a probe location, missing sample or timing number hidden lore |
| Presentation authoring | Keep lawful transitions readable and avoid conspicuous watched shadow discontinuities | Add an invisible hold, candidate veto, rearm or delayed commit to disguise a bad view |

## 2. Three categories and their causal effects

### A. Direct player observation

Direct sight reaches actual quantum geometry within the player's view without intervening opaque independent geometry: Cube, bearer, cradle, collar, other structural members and secured transported payload. A directly visible edge or silhouette counts without requiring conscious recognition of what it is. The observer is the existing player camera; boarding does not disable it.

Illumination is not a new predicate. Darkness, low contrast or turning off an ordinary lamp is not a release action. Author sufficiently readable participating surfaces instead of making players infer invisible geometric holds. Guard bands and sampling remain implementation tolerances, not additional kinds of observer.

### B. Artificial observation

An enabled Stabilization Beam with real volume coverage and an unobstructed path to participating geometry holds the current object or excludes a prospective placement. Player bodies remain transparent to it; relevant independent world geometry still occludes it. Current and candidate queries keep their established target/departing-root distinction. Direct and artificial observation combine with OR.

A Beam is an observer because it is that existing source, not because it emits visible light. Seeing its projection, watching its power indicator or illuminating a wall with an ordinary lamp is not another observer. This design review did not change the Beam API, field parameters, occlusion or release timing. Its then-existing shared shadow-only target probes were a compatibility issue, recorded in §6 and subsequently removed by the shared implementation conformance pass.

### C. Indirect state evidence

Cast shadows on independent surfaces, sound, diffuse light changes, independently supported passive gauges or instrument movement, old photographs, drawings, memory and historical traces provide information. They do not themselves supply quantum observation. Their evidence can be useful during or between changes, not only afterward.

| Category | Holds current state? | Excludes an empty candidate? | Rearms a spent opportunity? | Evidential role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct participating geometry, including direct silhouette | Yes, when actually visible | Yes, if the prospective geometry would be directly visible from the actual view | Yes, by actual current observation | Current presence, boundary and before/after identity |
| Enabled Beam reaching participating geometry | Yes | Yes, if it would reach a member/payload at that placement | Yes, by reaching the actual current object | Inspectable artificial constraint |
| Cast shadow on independent wall/floor | No | No, including a hypothetical candidate's projected shadow | No | May suggest presence, movement or a former placement |
| Passive independent instrument, sound or light variation | No | No | No | Correlation or ordinary response; not proof of membership |
| Photograph, drawing, memory or historical trace | No | No | No | Comparison and inference, never a stored observer |

An instrument physically integrated into the assembly, or secured payload, is different: directly seeing its housing observes a participating member even if the dial is passive. A fixed gauge is not made quantum by responding to the apparatus. An ordinary machine may also change real LOS or occupancy; those physical changes still matter, but its informational output has no additional quantum effect.

For current q, use the union G(q) of participating body surfaces. Current hold is direct visibility of G(q) OR enabled Beam observation of G(q). Candidate r is excluded by direct or artificial observation of G(r) in the already specified candidate world. Indirect evidence is absent from both predicates. Existing support, occupancy, release/grace, atomicity and one-move-per-opportunity conditions still apply. An inferred current state or remembered destination does not cancel release.

## 3. S1 versus S2

| Question | Contract S1 — shadows observe | Contract S2 — direct geometry observes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awakening | Adds a shadow-only holding cause beyond the opening's direct-view lesson | Preserves that lesson; does not claim the current probe implementation is already exact S2 |
| Current Cube implementation | Superficially resembles the footprint checks, but fixed probes do not evaluate actual cast shadows | Treats them as conservative approximation debt, not an approved physical observer |
| Large coherent assemblies | Requires all identifying projections to be hidden as well as all members | Requires the complete actual object to be hidden; indirect evidence gets a separate presentation check |
| Supported passenger | A hidden collar can still hold through a visible independent wall; near-total optical isolation may be needed | Fixed cover works without cancelling the rider's eyesight; a visible tongue or moving hood still holds |
| Candidate observation | Requires hypothetical lighting/shadow receivers for every placement and may exclude a hidden object via a visible wall | Tests hypothetical member/payload geometry and Beam coverage; the wall's projected shadow is not a candidate |
| Lighting authoring | Light position, extent and potentially contrast become state-legality inputs | Actual lights affect presentation, not quantum legality; geometric visibility remains authoritative |
| Hidden-member transitions | May be prevented by a distant projection even when no participating surface is visible | Lawful if the complete geometry and all other conditions allow; conspicuous shadow popping is still unacceptable presentation |
| Final escape | The architectural-boundary inference is insufficient without an additional shadow rule | Boundary, current hold, candidate exclusion and full support remain sufficient causal knowledge |
| Player intuition | “I can still see its shadow” can plausibly imply presence, but creates unexpected holds on fixed walls | “Seeing the object” differs from inferring its presence; simpler, but the distinction must be tested without explanation |
| Anti-elevator goal | Extra optical isolation can encourage cab-like enclosure; shadow complexity does not strengthen the intended revelation | Maintains the non-Cube hold, fixed-wall contrast and observed-empty-placement test; does not by itself guarantee mystery comprehension |

S1 is logically possible, but the mystery does not need its additional rule. Once a readable consequence becomes an observer, why not its reflection, indirect bounce lighting, light blocked at another surface, a gauge driven by that lighting, or a live image? S1 would need explicit limits on these chains and on what makes a shadow readable. A cast-shadow-only exception is not inevitably inconsistent, but it requires justification the existing evidence chain does not supply. Fixed shadow probes resolve none of these semantic boundaries.

S2 draws the boundary at direct access to the object, with the already established Beam as an explicit artificial source. It avoids recursive inference becoming observation. The cost is a real presentation obligation and a future implementation compatibility review, not permission to make obvious shadow images jump in view.

## 4. Silhouette, projection and mediated images

| Visible phenomenon | Selected interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Dark collar outlined against a bright opening, with sight reaching the collar itself | Direct observation; holds the whole. No light-level test or edge-recognition flag |
| Only a sliver of the actual deck or secured equipment is visible | Direct observation, even if the player mistakes it for architecture |
| Collar hidden behind fixed cover, its cast outline visible on a different wall | Indirect evidence; no hold, exclusion or rearm |
| Shadow falls across an already visible participating member | That directly visible member holds it; the cast shading is not the reason |
| Reflected image in a mirror or reflective display | Deferred/unsupported, not declared either a new observer or an exemption; do not author a required reflective encounter without reopening the contract |
| Live camera feed | Deferred/unsupported for the same reason; no current feed mechanic or stored-photo observation is added |
| Old photograph, sketch or remembered image | Non-live indirect record; no quantum effect |

The distinction is the surface being seen, not whether an image looks dark or recognizable. The projected outline of the collar is a **cast shadow**, not the direct silhouette of the collar. A player's casual vocabulary need not match this taxonomy; predictive behavior is the test.

## 5. Why this preserves the intended mystery

The later surprise remains “I misidentified the object's architectural boundary.” Directly watching only the collar must hold the hidden Cube. Watching only an independently supported wall must not, even if it supplies an indirect clue. The Cube is not privileged; all actual structural members and secured payload count.

Neither boarding nor a shadow discontinuity selects a destination. A real Beam or direct view can exclude an empty placement; ordinary support includes the rider. No lift button, new optical power, clue flag or special outside rule is added. Lawful early escape remains valid, including an accidental success with incomplete understanding.

## 7. Presentation contract, not a hidden state gate

> Cast shadows do not stabilize an assembly. Authored encounters should avoid a player comfortably watching a conspicuous, identifying state-dependent shadow discontinuity during a transition.

“Comfortably watching” includes ordinary reachable viewpoints and natural head movement, not only the nominal release pose. Check departure and arrival, empty and passenger transitions, the collar comparison, survey and recall. No player-intention detector or secret perception threshold is introduced.

| Presentation | Judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Recognizable full collar outline suddenly disappears on the wall in front of the player | Unacceptable |
| Clear full assembly shadow jumps between receiving surfaces | Unacceptable |
| A watched shadow exposes the exact destination-selection event, effectively showing the hidden transition | Unacceptable; distinguish from inspecting a stable indirect clue afterward |
| Small, soft, non-identifying variation | Possibly acceptable; check actual contrast, size, timing and context |
| Diffuse light shift without a traceable travelling outline | Possibly acceptable, not automatically safe because it is called diffuse |
| Passive environmental response after relocation | Acceptable in principle as indirect evidence; it must not masquerade as the cause or a new observer |
| Stable shadow or gauge helps someone infer where the object now is | Lawful evidence; does not hold/rearm it or exclude other placements |

If presentation fails, report an authoring defect and revise it in a separately authorized content pass. Do not silently hold the object, remove a candidate, postpone commitment until the player looks away from a shadow, script a light shutdown, suppress shadow rendering or add magic materials. Natural lighting and ordinary geometry can address presentation later; no such changes are made or proposed as a new mechanism here.
