# Project Lumen — Elevator State Transition Design

**Status: design only; no elevator, lower landing, ending, camera, or room exchange is claimed implemented.** This develops selected **Model C** from [Facility State Scope Validation](PROJECT_LUMEN_FACILITY_STATE_SCOPE_VALIDATION.md), the four-stop [Bad Ending and Replay Structure](PROJECT_LUMEN_BAD_ENDING_AND_REPLAY_STRUCTURE.md), the [Endgame Knowledge Reassembly](PROJECT_LUMEN_ENDGAME_KNOWLEDGE_REASSEMBLY.md), and the [Spatial Identity Contract](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md). It does not add gameplay rules, scenes, scripts, a global quantum facility root, or a final floor-selection lock. Human comprehension and all lower-site geometry remain **UNVALIDATED**.

## The design promise—and the literal boundary

**Facility rule:** fixed shaft, hub references, receiver beds, contacts, and exterior ground do not transform. Bounded quantum members can change their authored placements under the established observation/safety law; the resulting **facility configuration** is a relationship among those members and fixed connections. A landing is a persistent place on the shaft. The elevator is ordinary down-only transport between landings, **not** a source of quantum transitions.

The player's first account is “I am going down to the evacuation level.” The later, richer account is “Each stop admitted me to a different *valid arrangement of connections*; the familiar central plan did not guarantee the same routes.” Selecting a stop therefore selects **which existing configuration the player encounters**, not which quantum configuration comes into existence. The exact thought “I was never traveling through the facility; I was selecting which facility state I was entering” can work as the player's *emotional first formulation*. It is not a literal statement of Model C physics: the cabin **did** travel through a continuous shaft, and its control did **not** set M/N state. The final, testable correction is: **“I was travelling through a fixed frame into different connected versions of a familiar plan; at the upper site, even a room's connection can change because the room—not the frame—moves.”**

If a future design requires the cabin itself to jump without vertical travel, the same watched Atrium to shed exits, or a floor command to select M@P, that is a proposed **revision to Model C and the spatial law**, not a detail this document authorizes. Calling a loading cut “state transition” would make the mystery unsolvable by observation.

## Elevator interpretation: first reading, then corrected reading

| Moment | What the player can directly see | Reasonable interpretation | Later evidence that revises it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **At exterior C** | A real perimeter yard, an ordinary evacuation lift, a damaged upward drive/return brake, and a descending shaft with visible rails and service depth marks. The player may turn back before committing. | “I escaped the experiment; this lift reaches lower transit or shelter.” | The yard remains a genuine escape. The shaft is not a quantum substitute for C or a hidden failure trigger. |
| **First descent** | Continuous downward movement, not a black-screen remap. The first lower central volume resembles the upper Atrium and has four lateral research approaches. | “The facility repeats this plan at another floor,” or “I have looped to the Atrium.” | A landing-specific repair, fixed shaft joint, and depth mark show another persistent location, not the original Atrium returning. |
| **Later stops** | Comparable central silhouettes meet fewer complete lateral openings. No visible doorway turns into wall while watched. | “Lower wings were sealed or never built,” then perhaps “the facility has versions.” | Physical wall construction, different local wear, and continuous shaft references distinguish fixed landings. The repeated *pattern* raises an object-versus-connection question; it does not prove a quantum exchange. |
| **After investigating M/N** | At the upper P/Q system, two persistent shells exchange fixed sockets **empty**, with repairs/door conditions following them while contacts stay. A real camera sightline can hold one shell. | “The lift was not the mechanism behind the changed approach.” | The bounded-room proof explains why a familiar route can meet a different face. On replay, the four stops are seen as an architectural warning that a plan is not an identity. |

The misunderstanding must be **productive**. For the first two stops, a maintenance explanation should still fit. The ending should not declare the correct model when the lift opens. A player needs fixed-depth evidence to reject a supernatural loop, and later P/Q evidence to reject “someone simply closed corridors.” The elevator's function as a connector is the hinge between those readings; its floor control is never a quantum cause.

## Bad Ending sequence: four valid configurations to enter

“State 1–4” below is designer shorthand for the *player's encountered connection envelope*. It does **not** mean four runtime states of the same observed Atrium. Each stop is a distinct, materially persistent landing with a shared institutional central-plan grammar and its own ordinary wear. The elevator/shaft-side access remains separate from the counted **research approaches**. The final stop has a safe refuge/egress, so the escape attempt succeeds at survival rather than becoming a trap.

| State / stop | Visible changes from the player's last comparison | Likely player interpretation | Hidden implication to carry into replay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Four approaches** | The first lower central space echoes the upper Atrium's four-direction silhouette. Its shaft joint and one asymmetric repair are different from the upper hall. All four lateral approaches are actually present. | Relief: “This is a familiar transit hub below the experiments.” A loop remains a plausible momentary suspicion. | Shared form does not establish shared material identity. Note the *fixed* plant datum and which routes physically meet it. |
| **2 — Three approaches** | At the next real depth mark, one expected approach is an integral wall. The adjacent service contact and central construction rhythm remain; local damage differs from State 1. | “That wing was sealed after an accident,” or “the lower plan was reduced.” | A socket/contact can remain meaningful even when no walkable route currently meets it. Absence of a doorway is not proof that a particular door was locked. |
| **3 — One approach** | Two more expected research mouths are absent; one survives. A unique structural repair and unchanged shaft direction identify a third place. A visible lens can be understood as ordinary monitoring on first visit. | “The deeper facility was compartmentalized,” perhaps with growing doubt that floor plans map cleanly to rooms. | A live observer and a room-specific repair are different kinds of evidence. On return to the upper site, inspect what a lens actually sees and which structure owns a repair. |
| **4 — Zero approaches** | The central motif remains, but there are no lateral research approaches. The shaft terminates at a safe refuge/egress; the local footing and damage are unique, not a reset of State 1. | “I reached the protected end of the evacuation route. Why does the same plan survive when all the paths do not?” | A central reference can persist without its expected connections. The upper Atrium's apparently sealed P face may be a statement about its current *occupant*, not the permanent limits of the facility. |

The visible differences are spatial, not a timed door-removal sequence. A wall may be intact construction rather than collapse debris; this makes “it was simply blocked later” testable rather than assumed. At no stop do walls morph on camera, records spawn, or approach counts change because the player pressed Down. The four states are entered in a one-way order because the lift's **ordinary return mechanism is damaged**, legible before irreversible commitment, not because the quantum law forbids upward travel or return placements.

## Four document fragments: linked questions, no answer text

There is one short work fragment at each landing, on a fixed service surface used by the people who worked there. The player can encounter it naturally, but reading it is optional; no fragment is collected into an inventory, required to continue descent, or combined by a UI. These are *observations made by workers*, not revelations authored for the player. The sequence builds a comparison vocabulary—datum, fixed contact, live sightline, address—without naming a quantum room or telling the player to set M@P.

| State and physical source | Example fragment | Earlier clue it can reframe | Limit of its claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Dispatch slip at the central plant** | “Four returns checked from the notch this shift. Use the footing mark again tomorrow; the route names have been copied from the old board.” | The upper Atrium plant and address-based operations notes. | A repeated datum name does not establish that two landings are one physical room. |
| **2 — Utility slip beside the blank face** | “Side contact tests live. Survey found a sound wall beyond it. Leave the conduit in place until the frontage is measured from the center.” | Power's fixed contacts and the mismatch between a receiver and the structure that meets it. | A live contact does not prove a quantum transition or an electrical lock. |
| **3 — Signal handoff at a lens service niche** | “The monitor was dark; the lens was still live. The repaired jamb appears in the outer survey, though the earlier sheet filed it under the inner approach.” | Camera-as-observer suspicion, Records' conflicting repairs, M's persistent seam. | This is an honest discrepancy, not a statement that footage holds rooms or that the lens chooses their destination. |
| **4 — Survey margin at the refuge footing** | “The center measure agrees. There are no side lines to follow from this station. Keep the footing sheet beside the address list; neither replaces the other.” | Fixed P/Q marks and the distinction among address, material room, and current connection. | It cannot prove M/N exchange, three-site origin, or the True Ending route. |

Together they imply that **a familiar central reference can coexist with different connected approaches, and that staff sometimes confused an address with the structure seen there**. This is enough to motivate the hypothesis of spatial states; it is deliberately **not** enough to prove that the four landings dynamically swapped. That proof must remain physical and repeatable at M/N. If players cannot form the hypothesis without reading all four, strengthen the visible shaft/repairs/contacts. If players treat the four fragments as a password, remove that framing rather than adding a document-count gate.

## True Ending contrast: selection of a route, not selection of a quantum result

The Bad Ending follows **ordinary escape logic**. The player has mastered the bounded A/B/C passenger ride to C and sees a plausible evacuation lift. Descending is a reasonable attempt to leave a damaged research site. The final safe refuge shows that survival was real but that the player chose a path before asking whether the upper Atrium's “wall” was permanently part of the fixed building. Seeing this ending first can make a replay richer, but it is never a prerequisite.

The True Ending follows a **spatial prediction**. The player distinguishes M's repaired shell and attached doorway from N's damaged solid face, P/Q from either shell, and the fixed exterior spur from M's internal door condition. They have reason to suspect that a live camera aimed at M@Q is holding the pair because a comparable camera was testable earlier on a known A/B/C member. They remove that *actual* sightline and any other real player/Beam/camera views, then permit—not command—an **empty reciprocal exchange**. If M reaches P with its door in a usable ordinary condition, that door meets the fixed spur. The player crosses a route made possible by a correct relationship among existing things.

Understanding the elevator helps the player ask **which connection** each apparent entrance actually reaches, and prevents the assumption that “higher/lower” or a printed address identifies a persistent room. It is **neither necessary nor sufficient** to cause M@P. The “correct state” is `M@P, N@Q` with relevant observers absent and safe geometry, not “choose the right elevator floor.” An early player can reach it without descending. An elevator panel cannot select it; a camera-off button cannot select it; four document fragments cannot unlock it. This keeps the True Ending grounded in K6/K7 and the ordinary observation law.

## Consistency audit

| Rule or discovery | Elevator design must preserve | Failure to reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **K1 — Unobserved relocation** | The lift's visible mechanical descent is not quantum relocation. Bounded rooms still require a genuine unobserved interval before lawful movement. | A watched Atrium changes just because the lift passed a floor. |
| **K2 — Candidate exclusion** | Any observed proposed member placement remains ineligible. Do not present an occupied P/Q pair or elevator floor selection as a candidate-only proof. | The panel “calls” a quantum room to a chosen landing despite direct observation. |
| **K3 — Beam and player sight** | Actual direct reach to participating members constrains movement; shaft lights, switches, and Beam power alone do not. | A mechanical floor control overrides a Beam or player hold. |
| **K4 — Supported passengers** | The player's A/B/C ride to exterior C follows full-support/concealment rules. A lift passenger travels by ordinary mechanics; no interior-observer exemption is inferred for M/N. | The player rides a quantum building while seeing its participating wall. |
| **K5 — Live cameras** | A lens sees an actual member or it does not. An early on/off-member contrast at a known assembly precedes a consequential P/Q hold; footage does not count. | Descent automatically switches a global camera state or an invisible lens locks an exit. |
| **K6 — State Persistence** | M's repair, mounted door, and ordinary condition travel with M. Fixed shaft fixtures, landing documents, contacts, and corridors stay put. | A door condition is copied to a new landing by elevator selection, or loose papers follow a quantum shell. |
| **K7 — Spatial identity/exchange** | M/N are two persistent material shells exchanging fixed P/Q sockets; the four lower landings are distinct fixed places, not four copies dynamically created on arrival. | One apparently identical room repeatedly loses exits without any trackable shell identities or fixed socket comparison. |
| **K8 — Three-site history** | A continuous shaft may become a cross-site reference only after exact geography is specified and a player can physically trace it against a persistent shell/condition. | Four similar floors or four fragments alone are declared proof that all sites are one quantum object. |

**M/N room exchange:** unchanged from the spatial identity contract. The decisive evidence is two identifiable shells at two fixed sockets before/after an **empty** reciprocal exchange, with fixed atrium and exterior references preserved. The elevator neither causes nor replaces that experiment. **Camera observation:** fixed cameras can hold an actual room member but not an entire “facility state” by looking at ordinary shaft scenery. **Quantum State Persistence:** travelling internal conditions belong to bounded objects, while landing-specific documents and contacts remain fixed. **K1–K8 progression:** the lower sequence recontextualizes K6–K8 questions without retelling K1–K3 or claiming a new global rule.

## Design-validation gates

1. **Draw the continuous geography.** Show exterior C, the voluntary down-only commitment point, shaft depth marks, four distinct landing identities, safe final refuge/egress, the upper fixed Atrium, P/Q, and the exterior spur. If this cannot be one understandable physical layout, do not hide the gap with an unseen cut.
2. **Test the four-state reading.** At each stop, an unbriefed player should identify one similarity and one persistent difference from the previous central space. Ask whether they think a place moved, a door vanished, or they reached another built location; record what physical evidence supports their view. Ambiguity may invite curiosity, but a literal supernatural loop cannot be the only plausible model.
3. **Test the fragments as secondary evidence.** The player's interpretation should improve with them but not depend on reading all four. Each fragment must have an ordinary work reason to exist at that fixed landing and must not contain the phrase that solves the spatial mystery.
4. **Protect the True Ending.** Before the M/N release, ask players to predict both shell identities, fixed contacts, camera sightline, and spur alignment. If they predict only a floor number, revisit the area evidence. Recovery from either legal P/Q state and a comfortable blind return remain mandatory.
5. **Keep the world-rule choice explicit.** If the desired final line is meant **literally**—the player never descended and the lift selected a newly instantiated facility state—this document fails that requirement by design. Reopen the law and scope decision before any implementation; do not silently transform Model C into B.

**Result:** the elevator is a physical connector and a cognitive comparison device. It can make the player feel they were choosing among *valid ways the facility is connected* rather than merely gaining depth. It cannot, under selected Model C, be the cause or selector of those quantum configurations. The final Eureka belongs to recognizing a persistent room and its changing relation to fixed ground.
