# Project Lumen — Facility Layout V2

**Status: proposed spatial/evidence architecture, not a scene plan or implementation order.** V2 supplements the existing [Facility Layout](FACILITY_LAYOUT.md) and [Central Hub Foundation](CENTRAL_HUB_FOUNDATION.md); it does not change their implemented geometry. The actual F5 world currently has Awakening, the fixed Operations Atrium, four connected functional approaches, and **one** A/B/C coherent assembly documented in [TASK-038B](TASK038B_ASSEMBLY_WORLD_INTEGRATION.md). The M/N room pair, P/Q sockets, live-camera observation, and endings below are future proposals subject to the [spatial law](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md), [evidence contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md), and technical/human gates. This document introduces no new quantum rule or gameplay control.

## One fixed facility, two scales of anomaly

The existing atrium must remain the player's stable measuring instrument. Its plant, return vestibule, ordinary circulation, four work approaches, and service infrastructure do not move. The A/B/C worksite beyond Containment and Signal remains one bounded Cube–bearer–deck–collar assembly. The proposed M/N pair is a **separate later architectural scale**, not a relabeling of A/B/C receivers or the ordinary fixed Containment cradle.

```text
Awakening -- fixed vestibule -- [fixed Operations Atrium / plant loop]
                                 |-- Records -------|
                                 |-- Power ---------|-- fixed work apron J -- A/B
                                 |-- Containment ---|                     `-- S (survey)
                                 |-- Signal --------|------ Q survey view    :
                                 `-- fixed P approach -- [M or N]           : sight only
                                              | when M@P                     :
                                   fixed exterior spur                  C yard
                                              |                            |
                                        fixed exterior ground -----------+
                                                   `-- Q outer socket/inspection
```

This is a **relationship diagram, not coordinates**. In particular, it does not claim a new walking shortcut from the existing interior survey S to C. The C yard is reached by the already lawful supported A/B/C trip; Q may be surveyed from fixed interior access and inspected from exterior ground without making Q a bridge. P's fixed approach belongs to the atrium's service edge; Q belongs to the outer service context. The exact location, obstruction, and clearances of both sockets remain to be proven before any scene design.

## Fixed access and comparison topology

1. **Awakening ↔ atrium:** Keep the short vestibule, visible return, and restoration transition. The player should feel the scale difference between the incident chamber and an important workplace. Nothing in the later mystery changes Awakening's Cube or restoration truth.
2. **Atrium loop:** Both paths around the circulation plant remain ordinary fixed floor in every lawful state. Plant scars, pipe supports, a particular broken tray, and the Awakening-facing threshold provide repeated orientation. Those specific physical references matter because a global-building-motion hypothesis predicts they move with P/Q; they must not.
3. **Four functional continuations:** Records, Power, Containment, and Signal reconnect by the existing common work apron J and fixed returns. They are not four sealed challenges. A player can approach the same anomaly from occupationally different routes and return without using a moving room as required floor.
4. **P comparison access:** At an atrium-facing service edge, a fixed threshold/contact and at least one distinctive fixed scar remain visible whether M or N occupies P. A second fixed approach from a work route should allow a different angle on the shell/threshold seam. Neither route enters the moving floor inadvertently or blocks all release viewpoints.
5. **Q comparison access:** Signal's survey function and the fixed service spine provide an interior line to Q's shell identity and its independent contact; the existing C exterior yard can later supply a closer ground-level comparison. Interior access to a *view* of Q is not an ordinary walking edge to exterior ground. Both M@Q and N@Q must be readable without demanding the C trip.
6. **Exterior spur:** A fixed piece of ordinary infrastructure lies beyond P. It is spatially visible or inferable before the true-route crossing but cannot be reached from N's solid P face. M's existing doorway aligns with it at P; at Q that same doorway faces a safe blind recess. No wall converts into a door and no new portal appears.

All essential interiors, observation-source service positions, rearm viewpoints, and both socket comparison routes must remain reachable from fixed circulation in either `M@P, N@Q` or `N@P, M@Q`. If an authored room occupies or severs that circulation, the layout fails. The outer yard may remain separately reachable only by the existing C trip; this is not a failure of interior recovery if Q's observation and identity can still be assessed from fixed interior ground.

## The atrium as spatial evidence, not a menu

The atrium should give the player a **stable reference and a recurring discrepancy**. P should read on first approach as a damaged or inactive service frontage, not an isolated glowing puzzle doorway. Its fixed bed/contact belongs to the atrium's plant network; the current shell can appear to be part of that architecture. Repeated returns can show that the plant, pipe break, and P threshold have not moved while the shell's wear or door relation has. The central machinery also breaks sightlines naturally, so a player can leave both room shells unobserved through an ordinary circulation turn; darkness or a perfect reticle angle must not be the explanation.

The atrium must *not* have a single overlook showing both complete shells, both fixed contacts, the camera, and the exterior spur at once. That would reveal the full model immediately and might continuously observe the pair. Instead it should contain at least two meaningful fixed comparisons separated by a short ordinary walk: P's near threshold against the plant, and a survey/work-route look that relates Q to the same fixed infrastructure. Because these are fixed viewpoints, the player can make before/after comparisons without relying on memory of a featureless repeated corridor.

The room identity is not a color scheme. M and N share a construction grammar that makes “two wings built alike” a good first thought, but carry **different post-incident physical histories**: one identifiable repair/door condition versus a different damage and attached equipment state. A shared older flaw may suggest a historical duplication, but cannot substitute for distinct present identities. At least one ordinary state should be changeable or otherwise independently verifiable so “networked controls copied the settings” is testable. Exact meshes and sightline dimensions require later proof.

| **Power and Cooling** | Supported plant services and fixed contacts. | Conduits and Beam service plausibly appear to drive machines. | After movement, stationary contacts and pipe scars differ from travelling repair and attached equipment. | “The Cube operates a fixed machine” or “the socket stores a door setting.” |
| **Containment Works** | Inspection and safe handling at A/B/C. | Cube integrated with bearer, deck, collar and independent receiver. | Non-Cube observation and empty member movement make a later travelling wall credible; support explains the genuine C route. | “Boarding calls an elevator.” |
| **Signal Array** | Cameras, survey sightlines, and instrumentation. | A lens looks like ordinary surveillance; Q/exterior context may seem unrelated. | A camera-only test explains an earlier stubborn hold; survey links Q's room identity to a fixed exterior reference. | “Camera control opens the door” or “footage watches forever.” |

Each row has overlapping evidence in another row. There is no “Records = K6, Signal = K5” completion sequence. An architecture-first player may go from P to Records to Q; an observation-first player may go from A/B/C to Signal to P; an exterior-first player may see Q from C and return to the atrium. The same fixed paths and laws must support each.

## Sightline and revelation budget

| Exposure | Spatial job | Must not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First atrium entry** | Establish the plant/vestibule as memorable fixed ground; allow a peripheral question at P. | Show an obviously mobile room or a marked true-exit doorway. |
| **First worksite visit** | Make A/B/C's bounded collar and independent receiver inspectable; allow K2/K3/K4 through natural tests. | Present it as a mandatory tutorial before any other wing. |
| **First P or Q visit** | Show one distinctive shell feature *and* one fixed feature in ordinary view. | Require a document to tell which feature belongs to which side. |
| **Cross-area comparison** | Let the same shell repair, ordinary door state, and fixed contact be checked from another approach or after vacancy. | Put every piece of the pair's identity in one explanatory tableau. |
| **Camera-only contrast** | Show source orientation, real occlusion, and quantum consequence while other observers are controlled. | Act as a remote door lock or invisible veto. |
| **Empty reciprocal exchange** | Confirm M and N trade sockets while fixed references remain. It can happen before the player commits to either ending. | Depend on a passenger ride or a cutscene that swaps scenery under direct observation. |
| **True-route crossing** | Confirm M's pre-existing doorway against the fixed spur and the other room at Q from a later fixed vantage. | Supply the first evidence that the doorway/spur relation was possible. |

“Clues hidden until the true ending” means **final confirmation and human context**, not withholding a necessary causal fact. The M-door/spur relation must be prospectively inferable; the post-crossing view can make the earlier atrium wall emotionally and spatially legible. Records may then deepen how researchers misdescribed the two rooms, but no record-reading flag may authorize the exchange.

## Preventing a random-teleport reading

- **Two identities, never a respawn:** M and N must be inspectable as different persistent structures in the same world. Their wear and attached states follow them. A duplicate asset without individual history is not sufficient.
- **Fixed landmarks, not just a minimap:** P/Q contacts, atrium scars, the plant, survey boundary, and exterior ground stay where they were. A player can walk the comparison route again in either state.
- **Whole-shell evidence before spectacle:** Empty exchange, a non-Cube structural hold, and the reciprocal occupant at the vacated socket distinguish K7 from a player teleport or lift. A room ride, if ever authored, is corroboration and requires the same full-support/self-observation law, not an internal-vision exception.
- **Causal observer, not control puzzle:** A live camera's aimed-on/off-member contrast must be available before its final hold matters. Beam and player views obey the same direct-geometry principle. A failed release must have a diagnosable remaining observer or safety reason; never an invisible timer or hidden knowledge state.
- **Recoverable repetition:** Both composite room states retain fixed observer access and rearm. Repeated visits produce new *interpretations* of the same scars and relationships, not freshly spawned clues or stage-dependent room resets.

## Unresolved design gates

V2 is not approval to draft detailed P/Q rooms. A paper spatial proof must show two complete shells in both occupations; current and candidate sightlines from all accessible player/Beam/camera positions; camera-only contrast; independently rearming both rooms; broad room-floor concealment if passenger inclusion is ever required; fixed-access recovery; safe door poses at both sockets; and a non-obvious but legible M-door/fixed-spur relation. In an occupied two-socket pair, a clean candidate-only K2 demonstration may be impossible; the layout must not claim one without proof. Unbriefed playtests must then establish whether players actually distinguish the two room identities and predict the atrium change. Human readability and the two endings' emotional difference remain **UNVALIDATED**.
