# Project Lumen — Player Cognition Route

**Status: design only; human comprehension unvalidated.** This is a route through changing *beliefs*, not a chapter order or a set of required wing completions. It applies the [evidence contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md) to the [cognitive mystery framework](PROJECT_LUMEN_COGNITIVE_MYSTERY_FRAMEWORK.md) and proposed [spatial rules](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md). The adjacent [Facility Layout V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_FACILITY_LAYOUT_V2.md) assigns the evidence to physical viewpoints. Awakening and the single A/B/C assembly are existing technical spaces; cameras, M/N room exchange, and the endings remain proposals, not implemented facts.

## The changing question

The player's first conclusion is correct: a Cube's possibilities depend on observation. The mystery grows because the player keeps treating walls and doorways as the fixed container around that Cube. The intended final correction is narrower and more testable than “the whole building is quantum”: two distinguishable, bounded room modules **M** and **N** can occupy fixed sockets **P** and **Q**, changing what a familiar fixed approach connects to. A wall seen at P may belong to N, while M's existing doorway is somewhere else. The historical cause of the near-duplication is not proved by observing their present exchange.

The stages below describe a player's strongest plausible model at a moment in play. They can overlap, reverse, or be skipped by an unusually perceptive player. No rule waits for a stage flag, record, or scripted revelation.

| Cognitive stage | Belief that currently explains the world | Productive irritation, not an answer | Observation that can revise the belief | New prediction the player can make |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Normal facility, abnormal objects** | “The Cube relocates when unwatched; the research building stays put.” | A fixed-looking frame in the worksite has an odd independent underside; an atrium-side service threshold bears a mismatch, but damage or maintenance can explain it. | Repeated Cube holds/releases establish K1; a watched empty receiver and the Beam's actual reach can establish K2/K3 without claiming anything about rooms. | Looking at the Cube holds it; watching a vacant candidate or an actual Beam path constrains a later move. |
| **2 — Quantum effects alter larger systems** | “The Cube controls a machine, perhaps one that carries people or rearranges equipment.” | The same repaired collar and bearer recur at A/B/C while their fixed beds remain. A non-Cube surface can hold the whole object. | Empty assembly movement, fixed-contact comparison, and full-support travel connect K4 and an early K6 to K1–K3. The player need not ride to see what moved. | A structural member will remain with the Cube; boarding without concealment will not command a trip. |
| **3 — The space may be unstable** | “Rooms, or at least their contents, may change; perhaps this is a lift, remote security system, or confusing duplicate construction.” | A familiar atrium-facing approach has the same fixed threshold but different shell wear or wall/door relation. A powered lens seems to hold a state after the player's view is gone. | Revisit P and Q from fixed ground; compare a unique repair and an ordinary door/instrument condition with fixed contacts. A camera-only sightline contrast can establish K5, while non-Cube structural observation strengthens K6. | The next mismatch should follow a *particular shell*, not a named coordinate or a control panel. A live lens aimed off the member should cease to hold it. |
| **4 — Distinct spatial states** | “I misidentified the architecture: two persistent rooms exchange fixed placements.” | A single apparent doorway no longer tells the player which structure occupies it. | A controlled **empty reciprocal exchange** lets the player identify M and N at both P and Q, while atrium plant, socket scars, and exterior ground remain fixed. This is K7 and the full K6 relationship claim. | If M occupies P, its existing doorway should meet the fixed exterior spur; if N occupies P, its solid face should not. The player can predict this before crossing. |

The K labels are author shorthand, not a visible syllabus. K2 can be optional to the final two-occupied-socket proof if a clean candidate-only sightline is impossible there; it still matters to earlier A/B/C reasoning. K4 makes the real C exterior route possible, but the proposed true-route proof should use an **empty** M/N exchange so that riding an “elevator” is not its sole evidence. K5 is not a new power: it extends the already understood observer family to a live camera. All seven truths remain useful, but the final route is not a seven-item checklist.

## Awakening: reliable scale, not a false tutorial

Awakening's Cube, observation equipment, restoration, and opening bulkhead remain an honest local experiment. Its job in the mystery is to give the player one repeatable causal idea and the confidence to test it. The later room-scale discovery says “the object boundary was sometimes larger than I assumed,” never “the opening rule was staged.” The first atrium visit should be a credible abandoned workplace with a stable return silhouette; it should **not** immediately advertise a quantum room. Early architectural discrepancies are ordinary enough to be read as disrepair until the player has a reason to compare them.

Restoration reveals a larger facility, not a quest board. The player can return to Awakening to retest sight as a control case. That return matters when a camera or moving wall makes the player doubt the rule: the Cube still behaves as before.

## The atrium's repeated cognitive role

The fixed circulation plant, vestibule, floor scars, pipe routes, and views into the four functional approaches are **measurement references**. The atrium is therefore a major spatial puzzle, not only a junction. A future atrium-facing approach to P and a separate fixed look toward Q let the player compare *where* a structure sits against something that never travels. Neither first visit nor a single panoramic reveal should supply all four necessary facts—two room identities, fixed sockets, whole-shell movement, and observer cause.

| Atrium visit | Reasonable reading then | What the same physical place can mean later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First arrival from Awakening | A large, partly offline coordination hall; a sealed-looking service face is part of normal damage. | The plant and vestibule are the stable frame against which room occupancy can be measured. |
| Return after the A/B/C worksite | The apparatus can travel, but the hall is still the container. | A fixed contact/threshold near P resembles an enlarged receiving interface; the apparent “wall” need not be its permanent member. |
| Return after distinctive room wear or camera contrast | Repeated architecture may be two builds, a synced security system, or a map error. | Compare P's unchanged threshold and M/N's different damage against the same plant and pipe scars; the player has a concrete question to test at Q. |
| Return after an empty exchange | The same fixed approach meets the other identified shell. | A doorway relationship, not merely an object position, changed. The player can predict the exterior alignment and attempt to reproduce it. |

These visits are opportunities, not triggers. A player may notice M/N first from Records or exterior ground and use the atrium as confirmation. The atrium must allow useful comparison even if the paired rooms are in either lawful state; it may not reset them on entry.

## Four research functions, overlapping evidence

- **Records:** Physical survey work and later repairs invite a *which structure?* question. A fixed viewing approach can show a shell feature without explaining it. Optional dated records may support a shared pre-incident history, but the two present identities and exchange must be provable by wear, geometry, and revisits without reading.
- **Power and Cooling:** Fixed conduits, contact scars, and independent machinery make it plausible that a Cube operates a larger machine. After movement, the same contacts remain with the building while a repair and mounted equipment follow an object. This area also makes Beam power/aim and camera power legible as services, not four “restore the wing” objectives.
- **Containment Works:** A/B/C reveals the bounded Cube–bearer–deck–collar assembly and the difference between fixed landing and travelling support. A later room shell should echo this construction grammar without being a giant copy of the worksite. Seeing a non-Cube member hold the familiar assembly prepares the question of whether a wall can count.
- **Signal Array:** Survey routes and actual lens sightlines permit a controlled camera-only comparison. A camera should first explain a stubborn known quantum state, then reinterpret an earlier room hold; it cannot debut solely as the final exit switch. A view toward Q and exterior ground offers a second spatial reference, not a monitor that states the answer.

Each area should contribute evidence to more than one belief change, and each major belief change should be supported from more than one area or fixed viewpoint. The four names are development vocabulary; no mechanic-revealing wing labels, completion lights, or required traversal order appear to the player.

## Clue cadence and what the ending may withhold

**Available early, but ambiguous:** the fixed atrium reference; a P threshold that does not quite share the wear of the apparent wall; the A/B/C collar's independent construction; a recognizably powered but mundane camera; a glimpse of exterior receiving infrastructure. These should attract investigation without naming “rooms as objects.”

**Available through revisits and comparison:** a repair belonging to M rather than P; different later damage on N; an ordinary changed door/instrument state that follows M; fixed socket scars remaining after absence; a camera aimed-on versus aimed-away contrast with player and Beam views controlled; an empty M/N exchange visible in its results from fixed access. No single item must be noticed first.

**Reserved for final confirmation, not hidden behind a knowledge gate:** M's already-existing doorway aligning with the fixed exterior spur when M occupies P; N simultaneously identifiable at Q; unchanged atrium and exterior landmarks visible after crossing. A player may infer this alignment before the intended dramatic moment and may lawfully use it. The game may preserve the best *perspective* for after the crossing, but not conceal a causal premise required to predict the route. The historical duplication event and human consequences can remain open to optional corroboration; they are not an ending password.

## Non-linear reasoning routes

- **Architecture-first:** A player spots the atrium/P wear mismatch, compares it with Records' view and Q, then tests whether a structural member follows the same law as the Cube. The camera becomes an explanation for why change was intermittent.
- **Observation-first:** A player exhausts K1–K3 at A/B/C, tests a non-Cube hold and then a camera-only hold. The visible wall becomes a candidate quantum member before the player has named M or N.
- **Exterior-first:** A player reaches C by lawful passenger travel or surveys the outside from Signal, sees Q and the fixed spur, then revisits the atrium to ask why P's face does not connect. The C/perimeter escape is genuine; turning back to investigate is curiosity, not a blocked exit.

All three routes should converge on a falsifiable prediction about **both** M and N and **one** fixed frame, rather than on the same optional record. Reaching a route early is acceptable. Reaching it by cycling controls without understanding remains possible, so human validation must separate completion from inference.

## Readability tests before detailed room design

1. After a first unexplained atrium change, can an unbriefed player identify one unchanged fixed landmark and describe *what* they think changed, without being told about sockets?
2. After an empty exchange, can they identify **both** room histories, not just “the room teleported”? Can they predict which mark should appear at the other position?
3. Does a camera-only comparison cause a prediction about its *sightline*, or merely teach “press this control to open the wall”?
4. Before taking the proposed true route, can the player predict M@P, N@Q and why M's door will meet the fixed spur? If they only explain this afterward, the route is spectacle rather than a solved mystery.
5. Can a player who leaves via C describe a real unresolved architectural question without feeling punished for choosing a valid escape?

No unbriefed session has yet answered these. If players report random level swaps, a fake Awakening lesson, or a quantum elevator as their only model, revise the physical evidence and sightlines—not the observation law or UI vocabulary.
