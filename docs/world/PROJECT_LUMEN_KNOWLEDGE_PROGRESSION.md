# Project Lumen - Knowledge Progression Through Revised Hypotheses

**Design only.** These are possible encounters and tests, not instructions, room gates, or a promised implementation order. The first-person Awakening and TASK-038B assembly remain the only relevant implemented demonstrations. The proposed camera and room exchange need separate feasibility and unbriefed readability validation. See [the cognitive framework](PROJECT_LUMEN_COGNITIVE_MYSTERY_FRAMEWORK.md) and [proposed physics](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md).

## A dependency graph, not a route list

```text
Awakening: direct observation changes a Cube's possibilities
        |                     \
        |                      +-- artificial observer comparison (Beam -> later camera)
        v
Current sight holds / prospective sight excludes
        |
        +-- non-Cube member holds one assembly -- persistent identity across sockets
        |                                      \
        |                                       +-- room wall may be a member
        +-- full support + self-concealment ----+-- passenger may travel with room
                                               |
Fixed reference + two persistent room histories --+-- reciprocal room exchange
                                                      \
Camera sightline explains a missing exchange -----------+-- exterior alignment inference
```

Only the first object rules are a prerequisite for reasoning. A player may notice a duplicate wall, exterior socket, or powered camera *before* they can explain it. The graph records what evidence is needed to justify the final inference, not what the game demands the player visit first. The correct law is live from the outset: no learned-rule variable, research-token count, or “now cameras count” moment.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. | **2. Candidate observation.** Multiple Cube/assembly receiver positions visible from fixed routes. | The object chooses a favorite pad or the machine selects a destination. | Watch one vacant candidate while losing sight of the current object; repeat after changing only the vacant view. | Empty space can be excluded without a lock; a room socket may be unavailable because an observer could see the arriving shell. |
| **3. | **6. State belongs to an object, not its coordinate.** The same repaired structural member at two sockets; a door and mounted instrument whose setting persists. | The Cube remotely copies machine settings; two rooms share a control network. | Change an ordinary door/instrument condition on one identifiable module, observe its absence from the old socket, and compare both sockets after a legal move. | **7. Architectural space can be quantum.** Two similar rooms at fixed P/Q sockets, compared from the hub/service spine. | An elevator moved contents, one room was rebuilt offscreen, or the building rotated. | Establish both room identities; observe that a visible participating wall or a camera holds the *pair*, while watching only fixed hub architecture does not. After a lawful joint release, both distinctive shells, doors, and equipment exchange sockets together. | Reinterpret earlier mismatched corridors and apparent wall/door changes as a change in the facility's actual room occupancy. None of these tests needs an instruction card or a specially named tutorial chamber. The experiments should be possible more than once, with safe return and fixed access, so a player can correct a mistaken conclusion. A single random event is weak evidence. Controlled contrasts should use stable Beam/camera settings and the same fixed vantage whenever possible.

## Distribution through an explorable workplace

The existing Awakening chamber gives a trustworthy small-scale case. The connected facility workface provides Cube-to-structure continuity, D's non-Cube observation, B's excluded empty position, and the exterior receiver. These are not to be replayed as a lesson series. The four surrounding research functions can later contribute overlapping evidence without “wing completed” status:

- **Records:** architectural surveys disagree with present doorways; duplicated pre-incident manufacturing details and later different damage invite comparison. Physical plans or dated notes can corroborate *when* the discrepancy began, but a player should be able to test identities without reading them.
- **Power:** independent fixed conduits meet different attached machinery depending on which module occupies a socket. Beam supply and camera power are understandable services, not arbitrary keys. The same fixed pipe scar surviving an exchange is a strong fixed-world reference.
- **Containment:** an apparent doorway member continues into a bearer or room shell; service thresholds reveal which surfaces are fixed. Full support and partial support can be compared safely here and at the existing assembly.
- **Signal:** camera sightlines, a dead lens, and a live lens provide a repeatable observation contrast; a survey view compares both duplicate sockets and exterior geometry. No monitor says “camera counts as observer.”

These are evidence roles, not four future puzzle rooms. A useful path may be Signal -> Records -> existing assembly, or exterior survey -> Containment -> Power. Keep H/J-style fixed circulation open enough that a player can revisit the same object from another job's angle. Do not hide the critical experiment behind an area-completion switch.

## Three non-linear discovery routes

1. **Architecture-first:** The player notices a repaired wall whose seam continues across an apparent fixed threshold. A later vacant socket shows the fixed contact remains. An opened door carrying the same repair elsewhere resolves “remote machine” into “larger object.” The camera explains why that comparison did not happen earlier.
2. **Observation-first:** The player exhausts Cube and Beam explanations, then finds that a camera's aim predicts an otherwise puzzling hold. Watching only a room wall prevents exchange. They infer that the wall is a participating member before they fully understand why there are two similar rooms.
3. **Exterior-first:** The player sees an outer receiving relation early and may even lawfully use the existing assembly to reach it. From fixed exterior ground they compare a second socket and a duplicate room; the old interior “dead wall” becomes a question about which shell occupies it. Early escape must remain possible, not be rejected for lacking insight.

Each route should converge on **two room identities + fixed sockets + reproducible observer cause**, not on a particular document. The simultaneous existence of M and N matters: a before/after of one room alone is still compatible with teleportation or scene replacement.

## Diagnostic evidence against rival explanations

| Rival model | What it predicts | Physical comparison that would disprove it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cube controls a machine | Non-Cube walls have no independent observation effect. | With controls unchanged and Cube hidden, seeing only the room/collar member holds the same state. |
| Ordinary elevator | A passenger-triggered trip moves occupants but not the entire persistent architectural shell; a watched empty receiving socket should not veto service. | Empty exchange, wall/door/repair following room identity, and observed empty-candidate exclusion. |
| Two fixed rooms share networked controls | Door setting may synchronize, but unique physical damage and full wall geometry stay at their coordinates. | Damage, opened door, attached instrument and intact seam move together; fixed contact mark stays. |
| Building rotates or teleports globally | Hub/perimeter references shift with the rooms. | Compare from a fixed survey route before/after: the same window, pipe, floor scar, and exterior ground remain. |
| Camera footage stores observation | A recording could keep a room fixed after the lens is off. | Stored footage remains while an off/misdirected sensor no longer holds; a live unobstructed lens does. |
| Secret switch opens the final wall | Pressing the control changes a door irrespective of room identity. | The control changes only an observer sightline; the pass-through doorway belongs to the module that later occupies the socket. |

## Pacing, evidence budget, and failure conditions

Allow a quiet interval after the first believable “Cube controls machine” theory. If contradictory architecture arrives before the player has a stable object rule, it will read as arbitrary level design. Later, increase the *scope* of evidence, not the number of laws: a repaired frame; then a preserved door condition; then two room identities trading fixed positions. The first personal room transition should confirm an inference already available from empty movement, not be the sole proof.

Important comparisons need a fixed vantage, a lawful rearm/recovery path, and a memorable physical identity. A camera must be visibly powered and aimed, but not highlighted as an objective. A different room arrangement must be observed after an actual unobserved interval, never through a visible “morphing” cutscene. Use optional records to motivate revisiting an anomaly or date a duplication accident, not to disclose a solution vocabulary the world cannot demonstrate.

In an unbriefed test, ask after each self-directed experiment: “What did you expect?”, “What changed?”, “What stayed where it was?”, and “What would happen if you watched the other socket?” Do not ask leading questions about quantum rooms. Progress is genuine when the player revises a prediction and can transfer it to a new location. If players can finish only by cycling switches, or if they say a camera behaves like a magical lock, reduce the evidence scope and retest. Human discovery remains **UNVALIDATED**.
