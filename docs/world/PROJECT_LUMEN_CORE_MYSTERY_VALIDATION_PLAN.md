# Project Lumen — Core Mystery Validation Plan

**Status: proposed risk-validation plan only.** No prototype, final scene, script, mechanic, or player study is created or claimed here. This plan derives from [Complete Player Journey](PROJECT_LUMEN_COMPLETE_PLAYER_JOURNEY.md), [Spatial Topology Design](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_TOPOLOGY_DESIGN.md), [Knowledge Discovery Events](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_DISCOVERY_EVENTS.md), and [Endgame Knowledge Reassembly](PROJECT_LUMEN_ENDGAME_KNOWLEDGE_REASSEMBLY.md). Awakening's K1/K3 discoveries are the truthful starting point; K5–K8 and the M/N topology remain unvalidated proposals.

## The decision to make before production

Do not measure success by reaching an exit or repeating designer vocabulary. Measure whether an unbriefed player can (1) distinguish a fixed place from a persistent object, (2) identify a live observer by a controlled sightline comparison, and (3) use those discoveries to predict a changed connection **before** causing it. The smallest useful package is three isolated, disposable tests followed by one short transfer test. Paper geometry and sightline checks precede any interactive greybox; neither is final production content. No result from these tests should silently authorize additional quantum laws.

**Important topology constraint.** The request asks whether the Bad Ending reads as “spatial configuration rather than underground floors.” Under the current Model C contract, those are not alternatives: L1–L4 **are distinct, fixed lower floors** reached by an ordinary down-only lift. Their *built connection patterns* differ, and those differences should make the player question the upper Atrium's apparent room identity. The lift does not change a facility-wide quantum state. If the desired pass condition is that the player believes the same Atrium transforms or that the elevator selects world states, the present design cannot pass honestly; that would be a separate world-rule revision, not a polish issue. Here the test asks whether players understand both facts: distinct physical landings **and** the significance of their differing spatial connections.

| Priority | Smallest test | What it can decide | What it cannot prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0: paper spatial feasibility** | P/Q/H0/X plan and sightline overlays; camera-to-member rays; C/shaft/L1–L4 cross-section | Whether both room occupations, fixed comparison, comfortable blind release, observer control, safe recovery, and actual landing identities are geometrically possible. | Player comprehension or emotional effect. |
| **1: M/N identity exchange** | Two-shell, two-socket interactive greybox with no final art or lore | Whether K6/K7 can be inferred from material identity and fixed references rather than a teleport spectacle. | Camera discovery, lower-site history, or a complete ending. |
| **2: live-camera observation** | One known A/B/C member, one inspectable lens, one repeatable release | Whether K5 is noticed through sightline causality rather than treated as a power/key switch. | Whether the later P/Q route itself is readable. |
| **3: lower-landing sequence** | C threshold, ordinary lift, four distinct minimal landings | Whether a real evacuation and the 4/3/1/0 comparison prompt the intended question without asserting a transforming facility. | Quantum room exchange or K8's historical three-site truth. |
| **4: joined transfer check** | Short route connecting the three proven evidence situations to P/Q and a safe M-at-P/X crossing | Whether players apply the earlier camera and identity tests to make a prospective route prediction. | Full-game pacing or production-ready content. |

Each later test is authorized for consideration only after its prerequisite geometry or causal contrast works. The joined check is a thin validation harness, not a fourth wing or a vertical-slice production commitment.

## Prototype 1 — spatial identity and reciprocal M/N exchange

**Core question.** Can a player say “M is the repaired room, P is its current address; N is the wounded solid-faced room at Q,” then predict both identities after an **empty** reciprocal exchange? If they only say “the room teleported,” ask what happened to the other identifiable room and the fixed sockets. The test must not win by a caption, camera cut, or one dramatic wall swap.

| Design element | Minimum test specification |
| --- | --- |
| **Minimal geometry** | Fixed H0 patch with a memorable plant/vestibule and P contact scar; fixed Q outer buttress/contact; two bounded, near-matching complete shells M and N, both inspectable in either occupation; M's integrated repair and existing mounted doorway/ordinary condition; N's different integral wound and solid face; fixed P↔Q comparison access; broad blind return; fixed exterior spur X adjacent to P but not a secret shortcut from Q. No complete research wings. |
| **Required mechanics** | The existing observation/rearm/candidate/safety contract applied to one reciprocal *empty* two-shell exchange; attached identity and an ordinary mounted condition persist with each shell, while socket contacts and supply remain fixed. Watching a participating wall holds; genuine unobserved release permits, but does not select, the other legal occupation. Safe diagnosis/retry from both resting states. Camera disabled or absent for this isolated test so it cannot become an invisible confound. |
| **Player goal** | Investigate why a familiar H0 frontage disagrees with the adjacent repair/contact history and whether an apparent blocked approach can meet the visible fixed spur. No instruction to “swap rooms.” |
| **Expected wrong interpretation** | Renovation, moving furniture, one teleporting room, a hidden elevator, a scripted change of the same wall, or the entire building shifting. These are useful hypotheses only if the test gives the player a way to disprove them. |
| **Player-controlled experiment** | Mark M/N by integrated structure, inspect both simultaneously from fixed ground, remember P/Q scars and one carried door condition, hold one shell under view for contrast, then permit a lawful empty release and inspect **both** sockets again without accidental second release. Repeat or recover from either occupation. |
| **Success criteria** | Before release, player predicts which *two* shell identities will be at P/Q and which doorway condition follows M; after release, they can point to unchanged fixed references and explain why a swapped prop, single-room teleport, or whole-building motion is insufficient. Before crossing X, they predict that M@P—not a newly unlocked wall—provides the ordinary connection. Successful crossing alone does not count. |
| **Failure risks** | One identity cue is too small or looks like a decal; Q is not visible/reachable for comparison; a release requires exact camera angles; arriving at P immediately forces the true route without a chance to inspect N; a no-move interval is read as disproof; the unobserved return accidentally re-exchanges; a candidate or observer invisibly vetoes transition; the fixed spur looks like an arbitrary invisible barrier. |

**Fixed-reference minimum:** H0 plant/vestibule and P scar establish the upper place; Q buttress/contact establishes the second place; a continuous ordinary comparison route prevents “different level” misread; independent exterior ground/X makes the changed *connection* testable. M/N must have multiple **integrated** identity cues that travel together, plus one carried ordinary condition. A label, color code, loose prop, or document cannot substitute for this chain.

**Revision gate:** If players can identify M and N only after being told their names, strengthen material cues and comparison geometry. If they correctly identify two shells but call the event teleportation, determine whether they mean lawful relocation or an unexplained portal; require a prediction about **both** shells and fixed ground before judging comprehension. If no physically comfortable, recoverable P/Q layout can provide the contrast, keep the spatial-identity design at **REVISE** rather than compensating with exposition.

## Prototype 2 — the live camera as an observer

**Core question.** Can an ordinary-looking surveillance lens become meaningful *after* the player notices a stubborn hold, and can the player isolate its **actual direct sight** as the cause? The first proof belongs to a known A/B/C member, not the consequential final Q doorway.

| Design element | Minimum test specification |
| --- | --- |
| **Minimal geometry** | One fixed A/B/C current bed and at least one eligible alternative with an unmistakable fixed seam; one already recognizable Cube/collar member; one visible powered camera with inspectable aim/occlusion and a mundane service access; enough cover for the player's own view to be genuinely absent. A monitor or retained footage can be present only if it does not create a second active observer. No full Signal wing or final route. |
| **Required mechanics** | Existing quantum hold/release and rearm behavior; existing Beam/player observation held constant or made clearly absent; **proposed** live camera direct-sight observation on a participating surface. Compare the *same powered lens* aimed on-member with it aimed at fixed scenery or physically occluded. Candidate eligibility and all other observer rays must be known and unchanged across trials. Footage alone cannot hold. |
| **Player goal** | Explain why a previously understood assembly will not move after the player and Beam have visibly stopped looking. The lens is encountered as plausible facility equipment, not as an illuminated solution control. |
| **Expected wrong interpretation** | Electrical lock, broken apparatus, camera power as a destination selector, a security switch, or stored video preserving the state. |
| **Player-controlled experiment** | With power unchanged, compare a live clear lens-to-member line to an off-member or blocked line, rearm, and repeat. Watch whether the assembly can be released only when no actual observer sees it. If footage persists, verify that the image's continued existence does not reproduce the hold. |
| **Success criteria** | Player notices or investigates the lens without a prompt, predicts the effect of changing its **line of sight** before doing so, and transfers the rule: “a camera at Q could hold a room member if its live view reaches it.” They do not infer that turning a console automatically chooses a room or opens an exit. |
| **Failure risks** | The camera looks like a puzzle switch; no visible ray/target relation can be inferred; power and aim change together; the player's own view or Beam remains an unnoticed confound; a recorded monitor appears to observe; one unlucky no-relocation trial suggests the hypothesis failed; the lens is discovered only by random interaction. |

**Environmental recontextualization check:** After the controlled test, show an earlier ordinary-looking lens position or image of the Q work frontage without naming its function. Ask what the player would now check there. A spontaneous prediction about a *live sightline* is stronger evidence than merely remembering “cameras matter.” If this transfer fails, revise camera placement/target visibility before adding clues or a final control panel.

## Prototype 3 — genuine evacuation through distinct lower landings

**Core question.** Does the progressive 4/3/1/0 loss of *built research approaches* make players reconsider the reliability of an address and a repeated plan, while still understanding that the lift passes through **different fixed floors**? The bad outcome must feel like actual survival with an unresolved upper-facility question, not death, a fake exit, or a quantum elevator.

| Design element | Minimum test specification |
| --- | --- |
| **Minimal geometry** | A small, unmistakably fixed exterior C arrival/decision area; visible separation from spur X if X is shown; one down-only cabin in a continuous readable shaft; L1–L4 as central landing volumes with four, three, one, and zero **integral** lateral research mouths; successive depth/joint references and unique structural repairs; ordinary safe refuge/egress at L4. The lift threshold/egress are not counted among the research mouths. No built lower wings beyond short purposeful service stubs. |
| **Required mechanics** | Ordinary downward lift travel, voluntary boarding/commitment with damaged return mechanism legible in advance, doors to distinct stationary landings, and safe final egress. **No quantum transition, room exchange, disappearing walls, hidden knowledge flag, or floor selector.** Optional fragments can be omitted in the first causal pass and added later as a separate interpretive check. |
| **Player goal** | Use a plausible emergency evacuation after a genuine exterior arrival, while remaining free to inspect/turn back before committing to the damaged lift. |
| **Expected wrong interpretation** | Same Atrium looping, one hall losing doors, staged simulation, or merely ordinary lower floors whose different layouts carry no question forward. The last interpretation is geographically correct but may indicate insufficient mystery payoff. |
| **Player-controlled experiment** | Leave the cabin at each stop, compare actual built openings, local integrated damage, shaft joint/depth, and the same service datum; revisit the cabin threshold before descending again. Ask which features would still be there if they could return. No landing changes while watched or unwatched. |
| **Success criteria** | Player recognizes four separate fixed places in one shaft; does **not** attribute the missing approaches to lift commands or whole-building transformation; recognizes the repeated central grammar with different connections; and asks or predicts something testable about H0/P—e.g., whether the apparently sealed upper face belongs to the address or to a persistent structure. The player accepts L4 as genuine safety even if the larger history remains uncertain. |
| **Failure risks** | Continuous shaft depth is too subtle, so the sequence reads as a supernatural loop; landings look like arbitrary copies; “missing” routes appear to be shutters; one-way travel feels like a punitive trap; fragments explain the mystery directly; the ordinary-floor reading is so complete that no earlier anomaly is reinterpreted; the player falsely learns that observation makes whole floors vanish. |

**Interpretation rule:** “They are underground floors” is **not a failure by itself**—it is the correct literal topology. Failure is either believing the elevator *caused* a state change, or seeing only mundane floor variation with no renewed question about upper P/Q. If the intended emotional payoff depends on the former false belief, record a design conflict and revise the world-rule decision explicitly; do not silently animate changing walls into this test.

## Method, evidence, and decision gates

1. **Feasibility before recruitment.** On paper, enumerate both legal P/Q occupations and actual observer/candidate rays; prove a fixed P↔Q comparison route, broad blind release, safe rearm, M door/X clearance, and recovery from either state. Separately draw the continuous C–L4 shaft and unique landing identity cues. If any proof fails, change geometry before building a playtest harness.
2. **Isolated unbriefed sessions.** Recruit players who have experienced Awakening's observation rule but have not read these documents. For each test, state only the local practical situation, not a hypothesis or K-number. Record first explanation, voluntarily chosen comparisons, failed attempts, and—most important—a **before-action prediction** in the player's own words. Do not coach during a session. A small first cohort is sufficient to reveal repeated misreadings; repeat after a revision rather than treating a numerical completion rate as proof.
3. **Counterfactual probes.** After a spontaneous attempt, ask neutral questions such as “What do you expect at the other socket if this changes?”, “What would happen if that lens still saw the collar?”, and “Were those landings the same physical hall? What lets you tell?” A correct retrospective story without a prospective prediction is weaker evidence.
4. **Joined transfer check.** Only after isolated contrasts are readable, let an unbriefed player move from known A/B/C camera evidence to P/Q, then inspect X and the evacuation alternative. The test passes when they can account for the Q lens, predict both shell identities and the M-at-P connection, and regard C/descent as a real alternative. Do not require actual Bad Ending visitation or document collection before the true crossing.
5. **Decision record.** For each prototype record **PASS / REVISE / NO-GO** separately for geometry/technical consistency, spontaneous discovery, before-action prediction, and transfer. Preserve counterexamples and exact player language. A participant reaching an ending through trial-and-error is not a cognitive pass. A technically legal layout with repeated unbriefed confusion is not production-ready.

### Stop conditions before full production

- **M/N gate:** No detailed room production until two persistent shells, fixed P/Q references, empty reciprocal exchange, observation-preserving comparison, and safe reattempts all work and are legible without labels. The current design's earlier **REVISE** status stays in force until then.
- **Camera gate:** No consequential Q hold until a same-powered-lens on/off-member contrast works on a known member and players can transfer that inference to Q. If a hidden camera veto is required, revise the setup.
- **Elevator gate:** No final lower-site sequence until players can identify distinct fixed landings, accept the evacuation as genuine, and still connect the changing route pattern to a question about upper spatial identity. If tests demand a literal facility-state-switching lift, pause for an explicit world-rule decision; this plan does not authorize one.
- **Ending gate:** No claim that K8's original/duplicated/core history has been proven without a continuous, player-inspectable cross-site fixed reference and an object-specific comparison. K8 remains optional context, not a true-route flag. The proposed true route must remain legal on a first playthrough.

**Current verdict: NOT YET VALIDATED.** These are the minimum falsifiable prototypes and human questions to commission next, not evidence that any proposed mystery already works. No production content should be justified solely by this document.
