# M/N Spatial Identity — Player Reasoning Audit

**Status: design/readability audit; no unbriefed player sessions have occurred. Verdict: technically testable, cognitively UNVALIDATED.** This audit uses [M/N Prototype Validation](MN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_PROTOTYPE_VALIDATION.md), the [Spatial Identity Contract](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md), and the [Core Mystery Validation Plan](PROJECT_LUMEN_CORE_MYSTERY_VALIDATION_PLAN.md). The isolated fixture starts `M@A, N@B`; A/B are test sockets, not the proposed final P/Q layout. It tests player vision and an empty reciprocal exchange, not cameras, a passenger ride, historical duplication, or the true ending.

## The inference being tested

A player need not use the words *identity*, *socket*, or *quantum assembly*. The desired prospective thought is: “The repaired room with the rear opening and the wounded room with the solid back are still two different structures. After they exchange, each will appear beside the other fixed landmark. The plant and those landmarks will stay where they were.” That is stronger than noticing a changed door, calling the event a teleport, or walking through a new opening by chance.

The current scene technically supports this comparison. Two same-size shells exchange complete node hierarchies; M's vertical structural repair and open rear leaf remain with M, N's different wound and solid rear face remain with N. The central plant, receiver rims, A-side rib, B-side stack, rear service run, and blind recess are fixed. Watching either room holds the pair; both unseen for a short interval permits the only other occupation; reobservation permits a repeat. Automated checks establish those invariants, **not** what a human sees, remembers, or concludes.

## Likely first interpretations and discriminating evidence

| Player explanation | Why it is reasonable here | What would distinguish the intended model | Audit risk in the present greybox |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **“The rooms teleported.”** | The exchange is instantaneous and offscreen. “Teleport” may simply be the player's word for quantum relocation. | Ask them to predict **both** complete shells, the stationary receiver edges, and the rear connection after another release. If they can, the word itself is not a failure. | A one-sided view of M alone cannot disprove a single-room teleport or a scene substitution. |
| **“They move randomly.”** | Turning away does not visibly show the cause, and a no-move interval can feel arbitrary. | Hold either visible wall, then fully release both after reobserving; predict the paired opposite occupation. | The two-state fixture always exchanges after its delay, which may read as a timer or scripted toggle unless players run the watched contrast. |
| **“I mixed up the rooms.”** | Their footprints and front silhouettes match; repairs and door differences are on the rear faces. | Reuse the same central plant, A rib, B stack, and receiver rims; compare the rear structural details at **both** slots before and after. | The first visit may not leave a strong memory of N's wound, especially if the player glances from the front and turns away. |
| **“The game loaded another version.”** | A complete offscreen change can resemble level redressing. | Repeat the exchange in reverse, hold it by watching one shell, and verify that the same two material histories trade places while the fixed world does not. | A technical no-cut swap alone cannot rule out this belief. Only player-controlled, repeatable counterfactuals can. |
| **“The building moved around me.”** | A changed opening can feel like global reconfiguration. | Identify the unchanged plant and A/B-specific exterior structures in the same positions while *both* shells reverse. | If a player tracks only the door and not the outer references, the stable-building evidence is present but unused. |
| **“A door opened or the rooms were renovated.”** | M has an open rear leaf; N has a solid rear face, so a first changed view may look like a door animation. | Track integrated repair/wound, floor and wall extent, and both rooms simultaneously—not the doorway alone. | M has the stronger distinctive feature; N risks being remembered merely as “the room without the door.” |

These are productive wrong hypotheses if a player can falsify them in the fixture. They become confusion, not mystery, if the alternative comparison is hidden, requires exact camera manipulation, or depends on the player remembering a small detail they had no reason to inspect.

## Minimum evidence chain

The links are logical dependencies, **not a forced event sequence**. A player may encounter a later clue first and return to establish an earlier comparison.

| Evidence available to the player | Plausible first reading | Later observation that corrects it | What can legitimately be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Two rooms coexist.** From fixed Atrium ground the player can inspect M's repaired rear opening and N's wounded solid face, with shared footprint and construction grammar. | Two ordinary similar rooms, or one “important” room and one plain copy. | After a change, the same structural combinations recur on opposite sides. | There are two candidate *material identities* to track, not one remembered frontage. This alone does not prove movement. |
| **Two receiver contexts differ.** The plant stays central; A has a fixed rib and short rear service run, B a fixed stack and blind recess; each has a fixed rim. | Ordinary architectural asymmetry. | After the rooms change, those exterior details are still at A/B while repairs and rear faces have traded places. | “Where” can be checked independently from “which room.” A color or written label is unnecessary. |
| **A first unseen change occurs.** At a familiar A-side reference, the rear face differs. | Memory error, door animation, one room teleporting, or a loaded variant. | Inspect B before drawing a conclusion: the *other* full repair/door or wound/solid-face package is there. | The event involved a pair, not just a changed prop at A. One before/after view remains weak evidence. |
| **Both packages recur at opposite receivers.** The shell details and bounded floor/walls accompany their respective rooms; fixed landmarks and ground do not. | A scripted swap of two room sets is still possible. | Reobserve a shell, keep it in view to prevent another change, then release both and check the reversal. | Empty reciprocal exchange is the best current evidence that complete rooms, not just contents, change position. |
| **The same opening meets different surroundings.** M's rear doorway faces A's service run in one occupation and B's blind recess in the other; N's solid face follows N. | A special door was switched or a passage was spawned. | The opening and repair move *together* while the run and recess remain at their fixed places. | The connection depends on room identity **and** location. The current short run is evidence of relationship, not a meaningful final route. |

The observation contrast adds a causal layer: keeping either participating shell visible should prevent another exchange, whereas releasing both can permit it after rearm. This links the new scale to Awakening's truthful small-scale rule. It does **not** prove why M and N exist, that the whole facility is quantum, or that a two-occupied-socket pair teaches candidate-only observation.

## Before-action prediction test

**Set-up.** Recruit someone who has played Awakening but has not seen the prototype notes or M/N terms. Give no task beyond investigating this unfinished space. Record whether they independently look into **both** rooms and notice the plant plus A/B landmarks *before* the first exchange. If the first exchange happens earlier, do not reset or instruct them; note that the initial evidence chain was incomplete for that participant.

**First anomaly and free account.** Let them notice the changed view and explain it in their own words. Record whether they revisit both slots, what they treat as fixed, and whether they attempt to repeat or hold the change. Do not ask “Did the rooms swap?” or point to the rear scars.

**Prospective probe.** Once they voluntarily propose another attempt, ask only, “What do you expect to see on each side afterward, and what would stay where it is?” Record their prediction *before* they turn away. Then let them choose how to test it. If needed after this first prediction, ask the neutral counterfactual, “What would you expect if you kept one side in view?” The questions test a model; they must not supply names or the paired-exchange premise.

**Evidence of understanding.** A strong response predicts M's repair **and** doorway together at the opposite receiver, N's wound **and** solid face together at the other receiver, at least one fixed A/B landmark unchanged, and the changed rear connection. It also predicts that observing either participating room can hold the pair. Exact nomenclature is irrelevant. A partial response may recognize two changed rooms but not fixed locations; record it separately from a full cognitive pass. An after-the-fact description or lucky arrival at a doorway is not a pass.

**Counterexample worth preserving.** If they predict a timer, a door toggle, or a building shift and the next observation does not dislodge that belief, the prototype has not furnished a fair contradiction. Do not score mere puzzle completion as understanding. Record their exact phrasing and the route/camera position that produced the belief.

## Fairness audit of the current fixture

**Before the first exchange, the world must make available:** both simultaneous shells; a usable view of each room's *integrated* identity details at normal player height; the central plant and slot-specific external references; a perceivable shell/receiver boundary; and the contrast between M's rear opening and N's solid face. The player should be able to inspect them from fixed ground without a map, label, or document. Awakening provides the observation hypothesis, but the local space must still support a natural watched/unwatched comparison.

**It need not reveal:** that the rooms are quantum, that they will exchange on a particular countdown, which occupation is “correct,” a final exterior route, any historical origin, or M/N/A/B terminology. An early insightful inference must remain legal. A player should discover the boundary through comparison, not be warned of it by glowing seams or an objective marker.

**Current strengths:** the two shells coexist in one compact field; the swap is empty and reciprocal; fixed references stay; two occupations are recoverable; there is no story or instructional text; the lack of a button avoids “machine selected the room.” These are necessary technical conditions for a fair proof.

**Current unresolved risks, grounded in the actual scene:**

- The controller becomes armed after **either** shell is seen. A quick first look-away can exchange the pair before the player has inspected N or learned the A/B references. This is not a logic fault, but a potential *evidence-order* failure. Adding a hidden “inspect both” flag would violate the intended world law; test the natural sightline and pacing first.
- The key repairs, doorway, and wound are near the **rear** of two similarly shaped rooms. Automated visibility tests show the rooms can be observed from fixed ground; they do not show that a human can distinguish and remember those particular marks under the current lighting and distance.
- M's doorway plus repair forms a stronger mnemonic than N's wound plus solid face. If players describe “the door room” and “the other one,” ask whether they can prospectively identify the latter after a swap rather than merely infer by elimination.
- The A service run and B blind recess are both short and do not create a consequential route. They can demonstrate a connection difference, but the prototype cannot validate the full P/Q-to-exterior revelation or emotional payoff.
- The exchange is always the opposite state after roughly the same unseen interval. A repeated, deterministic two-state toggle may be mistaken for a timer unless the player can comfortably test a **watched hold** and rearm without coaching. The result should not be presented as proof of general destination choice.
- No rendered human-readable sightline audit or unbriefed session is recorded. The automated validator checks transforms, node ownership, and selected direct-visibility probes, not human attention or inference.

**Solvability assessment:** The required information exists in principle and no developer explanation is logically necessary to form and test the intended hypothesis. However, the current evidence has not been shown to attract attention or survive a player's memory. On present evidence, the correct verdict is **READY FOR UNBRIEFED TEST, NOT PROVEN READABLE**. This does not lift the broader spatial-identity contract's **REVISE before detailed production** gate.

## Minimal revision recommendations, conditional on test results

1. **If players miss the second identity before exchange:** improve the natural first-view framing or fixed walking comparison route so both rear faces can be inspected from ordinary positions. Preserve free movement and the same observation law; do not wait for an invisible inspection flag.
2. **If scars read as decorations or loose props:** make the existing repair/wound more visibly continuous across an integral wall or floor join, and keep a second attached cue on each shell. Use form and material continuity, not labels, color-coded M/N paint, or lore.
3. **If the building seems to move:** make the existing receiver rims, A rib, B stack, and plant readable in the same before/after viewing sequence. A modest fixed seam or service contact can clarify the shell boundary; a glowing quantum outline would answer the mystery too early.
4. **If the change looks like a door animation or one teleport:** improve two-slot sightline comparison and the rear opening's relationship to A's run/B's recess. Do not add a swap animation, fade, or cutscene that substitutes spectacle for evidence.
5. **If players call it a timer or random loading:** ensure a comfortable repeat path where watching either shell prevents change and a broad look-away permits it after reobservation. Adjust only spatial cover or presentational timing shown by tests; do not add a player-facing countdown or control button.

Test one small revision at a time against fresh unbriefed players. If the same incorrect model persists despite clear reciprocal identity and fixed-ground comparisons, return to the spatial proof design rather than layering explanation onto this prototype. No gameplay changes are authorized by this audit.
