# Project Lumen — Spatial Identity Contract

**Status: design contract only; human readability unvalidated.** This document adds no mechanic and authorizes no scene or script work. It specifies what a player must be able to recognize in the proposed M/N room exchange described by the [spatial quantum rules](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md), [evidence contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md), [spatial duplication validation](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_DUPLICATION_VALIDATION.md), and [first spatial proof room](PROJECT_LUMEN_FIRST_SPATIAL_PROOF_ROOM.md). The existing Awakening Cube and A/B/C assembly remain the smaller, truthful precedents. Live cameras and room-scale exchange are not implemented or human-validated.

## The identity claim

The opening model—“quantum objects move inside a stable facility”—is true at its scale. The later correction is not that every wall or coordinate is quantum. **Some bounded inhabited architectural structures are quantum objects, so which structure occupies a fixed socket can change the facility's routes.** M and N are two distinct material room modules, not a single room changing its decoration and not dynamically spawned copies. P and Q are fixed positions with their own contact beds and approaches. The hub and exterior ground remain stable.

For design discussion, separate four questions a player might otherwise collapse into “which room is this?”

| Question | What it refers to | What must remain true |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Which room?** | Persistent physical identity of M or N: the bounded shell, floor, structural damage/repairs, mounted door and equipment. | A room retains this identity through power changes, door operation, and lawful relocation. |
| **Where?** | Fixed socket P or Q and its independent approach, bed, contact scars, camera mount, pipe supports, and exterior/atrium references. | These references do not travel with M or N. |
| **In what condition?** | A room's ordinary internal state: door hinge angle, mounted instrument setting, attached source or stored charge, subsequent wear. | A changed condition does not by itself make a new room; an attached condition follows that same room when it moves. |
| **Connected to what?** | The current relationship between a particular room's doorway/equipment and the fixed socket's corridor, contacts, power supply, or exterior spur. | This may change when M and N exchange positions even though neither room and neither fixed route transforms internally. |

“The room changed” is therefore an ambiguous player phrase. At P, **the occupant changed**; M's identity did not turn into N's. If M's door opens, **M's internal state changed**; P's identity did not. If M@P gives a path to the exterior spur, **a spatial relationship changed**; a wall did not magically become a door. The contract should make those differences discoverable without asking the player to use this vocabulary.

## What makes a room recognizable?

Every exchangeable room needs a physical identity that survives a change of position and ordinary use. This is an authoring rule for any future duplicated/exchangeable room, instantiated here only for M and N:

1. **Shared architectural grammar supports the initial mistake.** Proportions, construction methods, repeated service ribs, and an old manufacturing feature can make M and N plausibly read as one remembered room or two ordinary rooms built alike. Shared features establish family resemblance, **not** individual identity.
2. **At least two independent, attached identity cues survive relocation.** One should be structural—an asymmetric repair crossing a load-bearing seam, a particular damaged jamb, or a continuous wall/floor join. Another should be encountered for a different reason—an ordinary door arrangement or mounted work instrument with a recognizable local relation to the structural cue. Both must belong to the module, not the receiving socket or a loose prop.
3. **Different later histories distinguish the two modules.** M's repair and doorway remain together. N has a different physical wound and the solid face where M has the doorway. These are consequences of use and abandonment, not game-color coding or giant symbols. They must be legible at ordinary player height from fixed access in both occupations.
4. **Human evidence is attached to place, not exposition.** Repair quality, interrupted service work, instrument wear, and plausible use patterns can suggest that people worked in each module after their histories diverged. Optional records can date the work, but no note may be the only source of a shell's identity or instruct the player to compare it.
5. **The boundary is inspectable.** A seam or small clearance must let a player see that the module's floor/wall/door are bounded relative to a fixed receiver. A shell cannot look seamlessly welded to the hub until a reveal cutscene suddenly declares it portable. Conversely, a glowing border would make the answer obvious too early.

Fixed infrastructure is crucial, but it is **not a room identity cue**. P's pipe scar and atrium plant say “this is the same fixed place”; Q's outer contact and buttress say “this is the other fixed place.” M's repair and N's damage answer “which room is here?” The reveal works only if a player can compare both kinds of clue.

| Proposed room | Invariant identity to track | Ordinary condition that may vary | First reasonable misreading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **M** | Integrated repaired seam plus the same attached doorway/instrument relationship. | Door may open or close by its ordinary handle; mounted setting may change; a live lamp may respond to a socket contact. | A fixed service room whose equipment is being controlled elsewhere. |
| **N** | Different structural damage and persistent solid face; at least one second attached detail in normal view. | Its mounted equipment and live power may change without turning it into M. | A visually similar room, a repaired version of M, or the same room seen from another approach. |

These are proposed evidence roles, not final art specifications. If the second cue for N cannot be made as memorable as M's doorway, the pair risks reading as “one real room plus a filler duplicate” and should be revised before layout. Historical proof that an accident produced the pair remains a separate, optional claim; present-day exchange cannot establish the origin by itself.

## What may change without changing identity?

| Change the player may see | Correct interpretation | What would be misleading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A mounted door opens/closes while M stays at P or Q. | M is the same room in a different ordinary internal condition. Its hinge state should follow M if it later moves. | Treating an open door as evidence that a new room appeared. |
| A mounted instrument is adjusted or its stored state changes. | The instrument remains part of that module; its setting travels with it. | A synchronized duplicate display that appears to copy settings without a physical comparison. |
| Lighting or active machinery changes when a module meets a different fixed contact. | The *interface* changed; live output can depend on the socket's supply. Identity did not reset. | Using light color alone to tell M from N, or calling loss of external power a quantum mutation. |
| A fixed camera, Beam, or player sees a participating surface. | The current placement is held, or a candidate is excluded under the existing observation rule. | Treating source power as an “assign this room to P” command. |
| An atrium approach meets a solid face in one occupation and M's doorway in the other. | The fixed approach's connectivity depends on which persistent room occupies P and M's ordinary door condition. | A scripted wall-to-door morph, teleport portal, or secret lock that changes P itself. |
| Loose, unbound objects are moved by people or physics. | Weak evidence about room identity; under the proposed passenger/safety law they are not silently carried with a module. | Using movable clutter as the only room fingerprint or letting it inexplicably follow a room. |

Observation is a **constraint on placement**, not a source of identity. A camera may hold M, but the lens does not make M “the camera room.” A room's current socket, power output, and accessibility are all mutable; its persistent structural history is the player's anchor.

## Proof threshold: contents, room, and relationship

The statement “objects moved, not the room” remains a reasonable hypothesis after one changed view. The evidence has to rise through distinguishable thresholds:

| Evidence level | What can safely be inferred | What remains possible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P looks different on return.** | An anomaly is worth checking. | A memory mistake, different corridor, service shutter, furniture transfer, or scripted redress. No spatial claim yet. |
| **P's fixed scar is unchanged, while a structural seam/door has changed.** | The discrepancy crosses beyond loose contents. | A movable wall, rebuild, or single room relocation still fits. |
| **M and N are simultaneously identifiable at P and Q from fixed access.** | There are two persistent room identities. | They could still be two stationary, similar rooms. |
| **After an empty unobserved interval, M and N occupy the opposite sockets with their attached states; P/Q contacts and hub/exterior references stay.** | The complete shells exchanged positions and fixed connectivity changed. | Observation causality versus a one-off scripted swap is not yet proven. |
| **Holding any current shell member prevents a repeat; a full release permits it after both rooms are rearmed; a camera-only sightline contrast behaves the same way.** | The exchange follows the known observation law rather than a location switch or arbitrary scene edit. | The historical origin of M and N remains open. |

The decisive physical proof is an **empty reciprocal exchange**. A passenger ride alone can be read as a hidden elevator. A pre/post view of M alone can be read as one room teleporting. A doorway changing at P alone can be read as a remote door or shutter. Two conserved identities, fixed sockets, whole-shell members, and a repeatable observer contrast are needed together. Candidate exclusion at a two-occupied-socket pair must not be claimed as independently taught unless a true candidate-only sightline is spatially proven; earlier A/B/C evidence can carry that knowledge.

No player should need to watch a shell morph. The law forbids that while observed. The proof must be made through fixed-ground comparisons before and after a lawful unseen interval, with a return path that does not itself accidentally release the pair while the player checks the second socket. The [first proof room](PROJECT_LUMEN_FIRST_SPATIAL_PROOF_ROOM.md) therefore proposes both an observation-preserving comparison path and a separate blind return; whether these can coexist is **not yet proven**.

## First spatial proof room: player's reasoning path

| Question | Player-facing content |
| --- | --- |
| **Observation** | From the familiar atrium plant, P has the same pipe/contact scar but now meets M's repaired structural seam and doorway rather than N's different damaged solid face. From the fixed Q comparison, N is present where M was. The player has not ridden either room. |
| **Initial explanation** | “I took the wrong service corridor,” “a machine moved a door and equipment,” or “a quantum Cube altered the room.” Each is reasonable after a single P view. |
| **Contradiction** | The player retraces the same plant-to-P route; the P scar has not moved. M's *whole* identifiable shell and ordinary door state are now at P; N's intact face and different wear are at Q. After reobserving both rooms, watching one participating wall holds the pair while the broad blind return can allow the reverse exchange. A powered camera aimed at that member can produce the same hold, but its aim at fixed scenery cannot. |
| **Final deduction** | “The repaired room and the damaged room are two different physical spaces. They trade places at fixed interfaces when neither is observed. What this corridor leads to depends on which room is here.” The player need not know M/N/P/Q terminology or the pair's historical origin. |

This deduction must be **prospective**. Before a repeat release, ask whether the player can predict which shell will appear at the atrium scar, which will be at the outer socket, what will stay fixed, and what looking at one wall will prevent. A lucky walk through M's door is not evidence of understanding. The ordinary exterior spur may align early if the world permits; there must be no hidden knowledge lock preserving a planned reveal order.

## Anti-confusion: rival explanations and discriminating tests

| Rival explanation | Why it is attractive | What a fair player can test or compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Random teleportation** | Changes happen offscreen and may not happen on every look-away. | Hold one actual member versus release both while all other sources are controlled; reobserve both to rearm; compare both legal occupations repeatedly. A no-move trial must have a diagnosable observer, candidate, or safety reason, not an invisible stage flag. |
| **Hidden elevator** | A player may already have travelled on the A/B/C deck and may call anything large a lift. | Stay on fixed floor during an **empty** M/N exchange; compare walls, floor boundary, door condition, and both socket contacts. No boarding or floor-selection control is involved. |
| **Object relocation inside fixed rooms** | Awakening established Cube movement; equipment can be moved or replaced. | Track an integrated wall repair and door jamb, not loose props; show the other complete shell at the vacated socket and fixed pipes/bed left behind. A room-scale bounded object moved, changing a route relationship. |
| **Memory or navigation mistake** | The two modules share a construction grammar and the facility has repeated service corridors. | Reuse one recognizable atrium plant, short fixed approach, P scar, and separately recognizable Q contact. Let the player compare both states by the same routes and ordinary viewing height, without map labels. |
| **Scripted event or scene substitution** | A single spectacular offscreen change looks authored rather than lawful. | Permit a second exchange in reverse after true reobservation of both modules, and a controlled no-exchange condition while a wall or live lens is seen. No entry trigger restores a preferred state. |
| **Camera/Beam is a door switch** | Changing source state and reaching a new route may coincide. | Keep source powered while changing only actual aim/occlusion; hold player and other source sightlines constant. Door hinge state belongs to M; the camera cannot command a preferred room or open P. |
| **The whole facility moved** | A changed path can feel like global geography rearranging. | Atrium plant, fixed P/Q scars, survey spine, and exterior ground have the same relationships before and after the pair exchanges. |

If a rival theory predicts every observed result equally well, the evidence is not yet a mystery proof. Strengthen the fixed/attached comparison, not the explanatory UI. If a player can only find the answer by cycling controls or reading a note that names room identities, the contract fails its knowledge-based progression goal.

## Authoring and validation gate

Before any exchangeable room is detailed, its design must answer in concrete geometry: which two attached features identify it from **each** socket, which fixed features identify those sockets, which ordinary internal state can change without confusing identity, and how a player compares both shells without causing an unintended release. Show both legal occupations, every relevant player/Beam/camera current and candidate sightline, independent rearm, fixed access, and safe recovery. If room-floor passenger movement is ever involved, preserve full support and the player's own direct observation; the first identity proof must not rely on a special internal-observer exemption.

Unbriefed testing must then ask players, without M/N language, which *place* stayed fixed, which *structure* returned, and what they expect after watching a wall versus leaving both unseen. Human recognition, room-scale technical feasibility, and the emotional revelation remain **UNVALIDATED**. This contract defines the evidence burden; it does not declare the proposed room ready to build.
