# Project Lumen — Facility State Scope Validation

**Decision: recommend Model C — an elevator connecting valid spatial configurations, with Model A's bounded-object law as its physical substrate. Reject Model B as the current world rule.** This is a design decision, not an implementation or proof that the proposed lower landings, cameras, M/N exchange, or ending are feasible. It compares [Spatial Quantum Rules](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md), the [Spatial Identity Contract](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md), the revised [Bad Ending and Replay Structure](PROJECT_LUMEN_BAD_ENDING_AND_REPLAY_STRUCTURE.md), and [Endgame Knowledge Reassembly](PROJECT_LUMEN_ENDGAME_KNOWLEDGE_REASSEMBLY.md). No scenes, scripts, or gameplay are authorized here.

The question is not whether a facility-scale quantum object is imaginable. It is whether that claim can be made **without changing the trusted observation contract, erasing the fixed evidence frame, or reducing the final discovery to a lift ride**. Under the present rules, the answer is **no**. The strongest revelation remains narrower: the player mistook some bounded architecture for the permanent building, and therefore misunderstood which existing doorway could meet a fixed route.

## Use “state” precisely

Four meanings must not be blended:

| Term | Meaning in the selected model | Example | Not implied |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Object state** | A bounded quantum object's placement, persistent identity, and attached ordinary conditions. | M's repaired shell and open door at Q or at P. | The coordinates or fixed corridor move with M. |
| **Facility configuration** | The current relationship of bounded objects to fixed sockets, plus unchanged fixed infrastructure. | `N@P, M@Q` versus `M@P, N@Q`, with a different connection to the exterior spur. | The entire facility is one quantum root. |
| **Landing** | A distinct, persistent location on a continuous fixed elevator shaft. | Four Atrium-like lower landings with four, three, one, then zero research approaches. | One watched Atrium sheds doors during descent. |
| **Elevator transition** | Ordinary downward movement of player/cabin between landings, changing the player's viewpoint on facility configurations. | An evacuation lift passes fixed depth marks and stops at each lower level. | A floor button causes quantum relocation or chooses a room state. |

The fixed hub, service spine, shaft, P/Q beds, and exterior ground are the evidence frame. A facility's *topology* can change because a bounded room changes which fixed socket it occupies. “The facility has a state” is acceptable shorthand for this **composite configuration**, not a new global observer target. K6's persistence is local to each travelling object; K7's present proof is M/N reciprocal exchange. K8's three-site historical relationship is still a proposed inference whose cross-site geography has not been proven.

## Model A — Local Quantum Objects

**Claim.** Only bounded objects, coherent assemblies, and explicitly bounded room modules can change quantum placement. Sockets and the reference skeleton remain fixed. M and N may exchange P/Q atomically under the established direct-observation, candidate, safety, and rearm rules.

| Evaluation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| **K1–K8 consistency** | Strong for K1–K7: each discovery enlarges the *recognized boundary* of an object without changing the law. K8 can describe one connected history across sites, but does not become proof that a whole building moves. Exact core geography remains open. |
| **Player comprehension** | Strong if every shell has persistent cues and every socket has different fixed cues. The player can falsify “the room was redecorated” by an empty reciprocal exchange. Risk: if the boundary is visually arbitrary, “only bounded modules” sounds like an author exception. |
| **Mystery escalation** | Cube → collar/deck → inhabited room is a clear scale escalation. The final surprise is a mistaken architectural boundary, not unlimited map mutation. Risk: some players may expect a still larger root and feel the climax is small if the final spatial relationship is under-presented. |
| **Camera** | A live camera aimed at an actual member holds that object; aim off-member or occlusion removes that observer. It does not globally freeze every corridor. The causal test can happen first on known A/B/C geometry. |
| **Duplicated rooms** | M and N remain two material, persistent identities; neither is spawned or rewritten. P and Q remain fixed. This is the current spatial proof contract. |
| **True ending** | M's existing door and attached condition can reach P and align with the fixed spur after a lawful empty exchange. A player's prediction is testable from fixed ground. |

**Advantage:** lowest rule debt and strongest physical proof. **Risk for the new Bad Ending:** A alone says nothing about why a down-only elevator shows four decreasing-connection Atrium-like spaces. Those would be ordinary repeated construction unless the facility's layout gives them a purposeful role. Model A is therefore the **law**, but by itself is not a complete ending structure.

## Model B — Facility-Level Quantum State

**Claim.** The entire facility is one quantum object with several possible spatial arrangements; elevator descent may apparently move the player through those arrangements. This is the literal version of “the whole building changes state.”

| Evaluation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| **K1–K8 consistency** | Weak without rewriting the law. K1/K2 require every current and candidate participating surface to be unobserved. Inhabited walls, player vision, Beams, and cameras make whole-facility release implausible. K4's full support and self-observation constraint would apply to every occupant. K6 would carry an enormous, ill-defined internal state. K7's bounded M/N exchange loses its special explanatory role or requires unproven nested roots. K8 would be asserted, not inferred from fixed cross-site evidence. |
| **Player comprehension** | A changed whole map is vivid but difficult to test. Without an independent fixed frame, a player cannot tell “the building moved” from loading, memory error, scripted redress, or a supernatural loop. Explaining exceptions would become tutorial or lore-heavy. |
| **Mystery escalation** | Large in spectacle, weak in deduction: once any hallway can change, earlier damage and address discrepancies stop constraining hypotheses. The first Cube experiment risks feeling like a small example of a different, hidden rule rather than a truthful precedent. |
| **Camera** | If cameras are mounted inside the participating facility and see its walls, they would self-stabilize the entire root. Letting the elevator or a camera-off control override that would add an exemption or global switch. An exterior camera/fixed observer frame would also have to be defined. |
| **Duplicated rooms** | Two persistent M/N shells at fixed P/Q become ambiguous if P, Q, and their fixed survey references can themselves relocate. Preserving the present proof would require a nested hierarchy and explicit observer/candidate semantics not in the contract. |
| **True ending** | The temptation is “select the exterior version of the building” from an elevator. That makes the final action a floor/state choice, not the discovered M doorway meeting a fixed spur; the existing physical prediction is displaced. |

**Advantage:** an immediate literal explanation for disappearing approaches. **Cost:** it silently changes the ontology, observer audit, safety law, evidence frame, and ending. A bounded “facility” with an independently fixed outside frame could in principle be specified later, but that would be a **new law-design and feasibility project**, not a reading of current K7 or a small ending adjustment. **Recommendation: do not adopt Model B.**

## Model C — Elevator connecting valid spatial configurations

**Claim.** The facility's reference skeleton remains fixed. Its local quantum members follow **Model A's existing bounded-object law**. An ordinary elevator runs downward through a continuous fixed shaft and connects distinct, persistent Atrium-like landings. Its ride changes the player's *view* of the facility's differing connection patterns; it does not trigger quantum changes. At the upper P/Q system, a bounded room exchange can independently alter which fixed route connects. The title “state transition device” describes the player's passage between configurations, **not** a new quantum actuator.

| Evaluation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| **K1–K8 consistency** | K1–K3 remain Awakening's true local rules; K4 remains the separate supported A/B/C trip to C, not a special elevator power; K5 applies only to a lens seeing an actual bounded member; K6/K7 still govern M/N identity and P/Q connectivity. The fixed shaft could eventually help K8 by supplying a continuous cross-site reference, but four similar landings and four fragments do **not** prove a shared history by themselves. |
| **Player comprehension** | The player can verify real descent using tracks, depth marks, and landing-specific repairs. The repeated central plan and missing approaches invite a question about identity versus connection, while the separate M/N experiment supplies the causal proof. Risk: if each landing has no distinct identity cue, players will infer an arbitrary loop or whole-building state swap. |
| **Mystery escalation** | The Bad Ending becomes a pattern that widens doubt without answering it. On replay, the player can test the upper P/Q structure using the smaller laws. The emotional scale expands from one specimen to the topology of a lived-in facility without giving any floor button quantum authority. |
| **Camera** | Fixed service cameras may hold a bounded room when their live, unobstructed ray reaches a member. A camera aimed at fixed shaft/landing scenery does not hold the whole facility. Turning a camera away can permit M/N exchange but cannot choose an elevator stop or create a missing corridor. |
| **Duplicated rooms** | M and N keep their material identities, attached conditions, and reciprocal P/Q exchange. The four lower Atrium-like landings are **not** M/N clones or four states of the same room; they are different fixed places with shared institutional design. Their visual rhyme can make the player examine M/N more carefully, but cannot substitute for the empty exchange proof. |
| **True ending** | Unchanged: predict M@P/N@Q, remove all real observation, allow a lawful empty exchange, verify both shells and fixed scars, then use M's ordinary door at the fixed exterior spur. The elevator offers a genuine other route and a different interpretation, not a “wrong floor” puzzle. |

**Advantage:** preserves the existing causal rule and gives the down-only sequence a coherent physical spine. **Risk:** the lower landings are a *thematic/comparative* state sequence, not demonstrated quantum state transitions. If the creative requirement demands the exact same Atrium physically lose connections after each floor, C does not fulfill it; that requirement must be reconsidered openly rather than disguised with scene swaps.

## K1–K8 stress test across models

| Knowledge claim | A: bounded objects | B: whole facility root | C: bounded objects + fixed elevator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **K1, unobserved relocation** | Directly preserved. | Every participating wall must lose observation; presently implausible. | Preserved for modules; elevator motion is ordinary. |
| **K2, candidate exclusion** | Testable at a truly vacant A/B/C candidate; do not overclaim at occupied P/Q. | Candidate world geometries and their observers lack a fixed comparison frame. | Same as A; a floor selection is not a quantum candidate. |
| **K3, Beam = vision** | Real reach to a member matters. | A Beam striking any facility member could globally pin the building. | Real reach to bounded members only; shaft scenery remains fixed. |
| **K4, supported passenger** | Existing A/B/C law; proposed room passengers require full support/concealment. | Potentially every person inside becomes a passenger and self-observer, creating major safety exceptions. | A/B/C trip remains quantum; lift passengers travel mechanically. |
| **K5, live cameras** | Lens holds only what it directly sees. | Internal cameras viewing facility surfaces would self-hold the global root. | Fixed lens may hold M/N; recording and lift controls do not. |
| **K6, persistence** | Attached condition follows its bounded object; fixed supply stays. | Boundary between “inside facility” and external interface becomes too broad to read. | M's door state travels; four landing documents/fixtures stay at their distinct fixed places. |
| **K7, spatial exchange** | Two identifiable shells exchange P/Q, changing connection. | P/Q fixedness and two-shell identity are undermined by global motion. | Same M/N proof; the elevator provides perspective, not the exchange cause. |
| **K8, three-site history** | Possible, but needs physical cross-site reference. | Whole-facility claim would be assumed before being demonstrated. | Continuous shaft *may* become one reference if geography supports it; lower resemblance alone is insufficient. |

The matrix prevents a false shortcut: **Model C is not a third quantum law.** It selects an ending and world-layout relationship on top of Model A. Model B would require a formal revision of K1–K8, not merely bigger art assets.

## Worked player sequence under Model C

1. **Opening remains truthful.** Awakening establishes Cube relocation and observation/Beam equivalence. The player initially trusts the facility as a fixed container.
2. **The genuine escape attempt remains available.** A lawful supported A/B/C ride reaches exterior C. From there a visibly damaged down-only evacuation lift leads to lower shelter/transit infrastructure. The player may choose to descend; no bad-ending-first flag is required.
3. **The descent widens the question.** Four physically distinct Atrium-like stops show four, three, one, then zero lateral research approaches. Tracks and depth marks prove downward travel; unique damage proves different landings. One non-explanatory fragment at each stop links back to fixed contacts, repair histories, cameras, and address-based documents. The final stop remains a safe egress/refuge, not a death or fake exit. This pattern makes the player doubt that the upper Atrium's familiar connections defined the whole place, but does not itself prove a quantum transition.
4. **A replay or early investigation supplies the proof.** At the upper facility, the player compares M and N against P and Q, then observes a repeatable **empty reciprocal exchange** while the hub/shaft/exterior remain fixed. The same camera law previously tested on a known member can explain a stubborn hold. This is the falsifiable event, not the elevator descent.
5. **The true route remains a relationship.** With M at P in a usable ordinary door condition, its doorway can meet the fixed exterior spur. Neither the lift nor a document unlocks that route. A player who infers it before the lower descent may use it immediately.

The stronger final thought is **“I treated an address, a room, and a route as the same thing. They were different physical relationships.”** The player misunderstood the *kind of place* the facility was because parts of its apparent architecture were persistent quantum objects, not because an elevator secretly changed the global map.

## Recommendation and non-negotiable follow-up gates

**Select Model C for further design**, explicitly inheriting bounded-object physics from A. Do **not** adopt B or describe C's elevator as an active quantum state selector. Keep the Bad Ending's lower landings distinct and fixed; keep the True Ending's M/N exchange and spur alignment as the decisive rule-based action. The revised Bad Ending supersedes only the earlier C-route presentation, not the spatial law or the true-route proof.

Before any detailed room or ending implementation:

1. **Draw the fixed skeleton:** Upper Atrium, P/Q, exterior C, spur, shaft, all four lower landings, and the safe final egress/refuge. Show what is physically continuous and what is merely architecturally similar. If the shaft is to support K8, specify its exact cross-site reference and a prediction the player can test without documents.
2. **Prove lower-landing identity:** Each stop needs persistent damage/repair and a shaft/depth reference; the missing approaches must be built walls, not doors that vanish between views. The one-way mechanism and voluntary commitment must be legible before the player cannot ride back.
3. **Preserve M/N proof:** Two complete shells, two fixed sockets, both legal occupations, actual player/Beam/camera sightlines, observation-preserving comparison path, separate blind release, independent rearm, safe recovery, and an ordinary M door/spur alignment. The spatial-identity contract's **REVISE** gate remains in force.
4. **Test camera causality:** First use a same-powered-lens on/off-member comparison at known A/B/C geometry. A fixed camera must not silently observe its own supposed global facility root, and a camera switch must not become the exit command.
5. **Test unbriefed cognition:** After the lower sequence, ask whether players believe they saw four distinct places, one place transforming, or a loop—and which physical observation supports that belief. Before the true route, ask which room, socket, camera ray, and door connection they predict will change. If they succeed only by collecting fragments or choosing the “correct elevator floor,” revise the evidence layout.

**Scope verdict:** the entire facility should **not** be one quantum spatial state under current Project Lumen law. Bounded modules can change the facility's composite spatial configuration. Model C lets the elevator expose that larger pattern without causing it. This preserves the feeling of a mistaken world model while keeping every consequential change accountable to a recognizable object, observer, and fixed reference.
