# Project Lumen — Complete Player Journey

**Status: design only.** This is a model of changing player beliefs, not a required room order, an implementation plan, or a claim of human validation. It follows [Knowledge Architecture V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md), [Knowledge Discovery Events](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_DISCOVERY_EVENTS.md), [Area Mystery Layout](PROJECT_LUMEN_AREA_MYSTERY_LAYOUT.md), [Spatial Topology Design](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_TOPOLOGY_DESIGN.md), [Bad Ending and Replay Structure](PROJECT_LUMEN_BAD_ENDING_AND_REPLAY_STRUCTURE.md), and [Endgame Knowledge Reassembly](PROJECT_LUMEN_ENDGAME_KNOWLEDGE_REASSEMBLY.md). The newer bad-ending design replaces the older C-only ending presentation; it does not replace the proposed M-at-P true route.

## Journey contract

Awakening is the completed, truthful small experiment: an unobserved Cube can relocate, and a Beam that actually reaches it observes as player vision does. The useful *incomplete* belief is that these are unusual objects inside ordinary architecture. Later evidence changes the inferred **boundary of the object**, the distinction between a room and its address, and the meaning of a connection. It must not reveal that the first experiment was fake or that the entire facility transforms.

The eight phases below describe a plausible first-run path through questions. Research visits, observer tests, and assembly experiments can overlap or occur in another order. Nothing records a “knowledge stage,” requires the bad ending, or withholds a lawful true route from an unusually perceptive first-time player. K1–K8 are author shorthand, never an in-world checklist.

## First playthrough — a plausible cognitive path

### 1. Awakening — a dependable small rule

- **Player goal:** Understand where they woke, restore a functioning route, and leave the chamber.
- **Player belief:** The facility is normal; a peculiar Cube and its apparatus are the exception. Beam power may initially look like a machine driving the Cube.
- **Discovered anomaly:** The Cube's position depends on genuine observation. Its behavior changes when sight is lost, while a Beam with real reach can maintain a hold. The existing Plate and restoration interaction show that this matters to the facility.
- **New knowledge:** K1 and K3 are established through action, not exposition. The opening does **not** need to prove K2, passenger travel, cameras, or moving rooms.
- **Unresolved question:** What exactly counts as the observed object, and why was this facility built around it?

The chamber's restoration and the passage into the Atrium are a consequence of the existing opening, not a promise that every later anomaly is another guided test.

### 2. First Atrium arrival — a credible fixed reference

- **Player goal:** Orient in a much larger workplace and choose a promising route by visible purpose, damage, activity, or access.
- **Player belief:** The Atrium is stable ground connecting ordinary research wings. Its service face at P appears to be part of the building.
- **Discovered anomaly:** A frontage, contact scar, or repair history does not quite agree with the fixed plant and adjacent infrastructure. On a first visit this is minor enough to suggest disrepair, replacement, or a bad survey.
- **New knowledge:** Not another law yet. The player acquires an *observational baseline*: plant, vestibule, conduit, P bed, and routes can be remembered and compared on return.
- **Unresolved question:** Was the inconsistent frontage repaired, misrecorded, or is the player mistaking a structure for its location?

The Atrium is the recurring comparison instrument, not a menu of four lessons. Its P-side discrepancy must be visible without announcing a quantum wall.

### 3. Research area exploration — accurate clues, wrong frame

- **Player goal:** Follow specific abnormalities and practical facility questions across Records, Power, Containment, and Signal; no wing is a required first stop.
- **Player belief:** Maintenance errors, copied plans, broken power, a transfer machine, or surveillance faults can explain the inconsistencies individually.
- **Discovered anomaly:** Records has incompatible but locally credible repair surveys; Power separates a fixed contact from an attached condition; Containment has a repaired collar and independent A/B/C beds; Signal sees a live lens, Q's fixed outer contact, and an exterior place that does not match the apparent internal route.
- **New knowledge:** Previously learned observation is useful outside Awakening. The player begins to separate an object's persistent marks from the ground under it (a precondition for K6), while an empty A/B/C receiver can support a clean K2 candidate-exclusion experiment if the geometry permits. No document alone proves a spatial law.
- **Unresolved question:** Are these several unrelated failures, or one mistaken assumption about what belongs to what?

Architecture-first, observation-first, and exterior-first exploration are all viable. A player can find late-significance evidence early without a scene pretending they have already interpreted it.

### 4. First understanding of the wider observation system — the unseen witness

- **Player goal:** Explain why a known assembly member remains fixed after their own and the Beam's view are removed, or why a possible receiver stays empty.
- **Player belief:** The camera is merely recording; its console, power, or stored footage might operate the apparatus. Power might select a destination.
- **Discovered anomaly:** At Signal, the *same powered live lens* holds a known A/B/C member only when it actually sees that member, not when aimed at fixed scenery or occluded. Player and Beam sight, candidate eligibility, and rearm are held constant. A separate vacant-receiver comparison can show that observing a possible destination excludes it without holding the current assembly.
- **New knowledge:** K5 extends K1/K3 to a live camera; footage is not an active observer. K2 is established only where a candidate-only view can genuinely be isolated. This is the first understanding of the **broader observer network**, not a second introduction to Beam/vision equivalence.
- **Unresolved question:** Which apparent environmental fixtures are being held by witnesses the player has not counted?

The camera test must precede any final Q camera hold becoming the sole obstacle, so the endgame can be predicted instead of solved by trying a switch.

### 5. Assembly discovery — the object's boundary was wrong

- **Player goal:** Account for the moving structure at A/B/C and investigate the reachable exterior C yard, not “activate a lift.”
- **Player belief:** The Cube drives a machine; the collar and deck merely surround it, and boarding may trigger transportation.
- **Discovered anomaly:** Watching a non-Cube collar member holds the assembly when the Cube itself is hidden. A repair and an attached condition travel with the same bounded structure; A/B/C receiver beds and independent contacts remain. A fully supported, completely unobserved rider can travel with it, while merely standing nearby cannot.
- **New knowledge:** K4 is a lawful passenger relationship, not an elevator command. The first concrete K6 insight is that identity and attached condition persist with the object rather than the address. The early Cube rule remains true at a larger, surprising boundary.
- **Unresolved question:** If the apparent machine was one object, could a wall or doorway also belong to a persistent object rather than to the Atrium?

C is real exterior ground and can be reached as soon as the physical conditions permit. The player may discover this before naming the assembly or before proving the room exchange.

### 6. Spatial duplication discovery — an address is not a room

- **Player goal:** Reconcile P's apparently permanent face with Q's other frontage, the contradictory work records, and a possible fixed exterior spur X.
- **Player belief:** One room was remodeled, its contents moved, an elevator transferred it, or the Atrium itself changed.
- **Discovered anomaly:** M and N can be identified **simultaneously** by different integrated repairs and conditions at fixed P and Q. After a lawful **empty reciprocal exchange**, both identities appear at the opposite sockets while the P scar, Q buttress, fixed contacts, and Atrium plant stay put. M's mounted doorway/condition follows M; N's solid face follows N.
- **New knowledge:** K6 becomes predictive, and K7 distinguishes two persistent bounded room shells from a changing location. The player can predict which shell would meet the fixed spur at P. This is not proof that an entire facility moves or that the historical three-site account (K8) is solved.
- **Unresolved question:** Can the player create the other lawful occupation, and why did staff describe different structures by the same addresses?

This Eureka depends on repeatable before/after comparison from fixed ground. The proposed P/Q comparison path and blind release geometry remain design-validation work, not assumed solved by this narrative.

### 7. Escape attempt — a real route, an incomplete theory

- **Player goal:** Leave danger using a route they can physically reach. A player who has already inferred M-at-P may instead test that route now.
- **Player belief:** The supported trip to C and the ordinary emergency lift are sufficient escape. The mismatched P wall can remain an unsolved maintenance problem.
- **Discovered anomaly:** C is genuine exterior ground, but its evacuation route is separate from X, the fixed spur that M's doorway could meet at P. The down-only lift has a visible damaged return mechanism before commitment; it is not the A/B/C assembly and does not select a quantum state.
- **New knowledge:** K4 works in a consequential place, and “I escaped the research interior” is true. The unresolved P/Q relationship shows why physical survival and comprehensive understanding need not coincide. There is no new escape-only law.
- **Unresolved question:** What would have been reachable if a different room shell had occupied the upper Atrium's P bed?

The player may choose to keep investigating rather than descend. A lucky or insightful early true-route crossing must remain valid.

### 8. Bad Ending — safety without a complete map

- **Player goal:** Follow the damaged evacuation lift downward to a protected, usable refuge/egress.
- **Player belief:** The familiar central plan may recur because this is one Atrium looping, a building that sheds corridors, or ordinary lower floors with blocked wings.
- **Discovered anomaly:** Four distinct landings present **four, three, one, then zero** built research approaches. Continuous rails, depth joints, and unique repairs prove separate fixed places, not an observed hall morphing or the lift switching the facility. One optional work fragment at each landing reconnects earlier contacts, repairs, lens orientation, and route counts without supplying an answer key.
- **New knowledge:** The player can safely leave and recognize that a place name or repeated plan was a poor substitute for material identity and connection. Descent itself does **not** prove K7 exchange or K8 history; those require the upper M/N experiment and future cross-site physical evidence.
- **Unresolved question:** Why did the upper P frontage disagree with the fixed world, and how might M's existing doorway meet X? What actually happened across the original, duplicated, and core sites?

This ending is not death, a fake exit, or a required failure screen. It closes an honest survival journey while leaving a precise, return-worthy spatial question.

## Second playthrough — the same evidence, a changed observer

Replay is an opportunity for the *player* to make different predictions, not New Game+ state. The world need not remember a document read, a failed route, or a first ending.

| Revisited evidence | Likely first reading | Second reading and test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Awakening** | A strange Cube in an otherwise normal facility. | The Cube experiment was accurate at its scale. Apply the same watch/release distinction to a collar member and, later, to a room surface; do not discard the opening as a staged trick. |
| **Camera** | Security record, power control, or decoration. | A live lens might be the observer still seeing a member. Compare that same powered lens on-member and off-member at A/B/C before predicting a hold at Q; recorded footage alone cannot hold. |
| **Documents** | Conflicting maps mean careless or dishonest staff. | A work note can be correct about a fixed *address* yet wrong about which shell occupied it. Match repairs and attached conditions to material objects, using papers as corroboration rather than route credentials. |
| **Room identities** | A room was renovated or its furniture moved. | M and N retain different wounds and conditions. Inspect both at once, then predict both after an empty exchange; P and Q contacts do not travel. |
| **Atrium** | A hub with a sealed or damaged side. | H0's plant, vestibule, and P scar are a fixed control. The changing face belongs to whichever room shell occupies P; repeated visits now test identity rather than merely navigation. |
| **Elevator** | A quantum loop or an escape selector. | The down-only lift is ordinary transport past distinct L1–L4 landings. Their repeated central grammar makes differing connections memorable, but the proof of quantum exchange remains upstairs at P/Q. |

The four lower fragments can sharpen questions on replay, but none is mandatory and none should tell the player to “return to P.” A first-time player who never sees the Bad Ending may still assemble the same physical proof.

## True Ending route — a prediction, not a completion ledger

The proposed start state is `N@P, M@Q`: N's solid face makes P look sealed, while M's repaired doorway faces a safe blind recess at Q. A fixed exterior spur X exists at P independently of either shell. The player can see or infer this geometry before attempting to change it; C does not secretly connect to X.

To act differently, the player needs to understand that an unobserved bounded object may change placement (K1); any real player, Beam, or live-camera sight can prevent the relevant change (K3/K5 **where those observers reach**); M's doorway and ordinary condition travel with M rather than P (K6); and M/N are two identifiable shells able to exchange fixed P/Q sockets when empty (K7). The player can then predict: if every relevant observer is removed and the lawful empty exchange yields `M@P, N@Q`, M's existing usable doorway will meet the *fixed* X route. Reinspect both identities from fixed ground and walk through an ordinary threshold. Turning a camera away removes a witness; it does not command M to P. Looking away permits a legal outcome; it does not guarantee one, so rearm and recovery from either resting state must be physically possible.

K2 helps reason about a genuinely visible empty candidate at A/B/C, but the occupied binary P/Q pair must not pretend to offer the same proof. K4 makes C and the evacuation outcome possible, not the empty M/N exchange. K8—the relationship of original, duplicated, and deep/core accounts—can enrich the full human and historical truth, but its nontextual cross-site proof remains unvalidated and cannot be a hidden eligibility condition. No fragment count, wing completion, exact terminology, or prior Bad Ending may open X. If the geometry and current state law already permit the crossing, the game permits it on the first run.

## Mystery pacing and fairness audit

| Risk | What would go wrong | Design check before room detail or implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **K7 announced too early** | A marked quantum wall or explanatory note turns investigation into following instructions. | Present P as a plausible damaged frontage; distinguish fixed scar from shell repair only through return visits and two-site comparison. An observant player may still infer early; do not block them. |
| **Camera introduced too late** | The final Q hold feels like an arbitrary surveillance switch. | Make the same-powered-lens contrast possible on a known A/B/C member first, with Beam/player views and rearm controlled. Keep footage visibly distinct from live sight. |
| **Assembly read as an elevator** | Passenger motion is learned, but the object-boundary revelation is missed. | Collar-only hold, recurring attached repair, fixed receiver beds, full support, and independent C ground must be perceptible before the ride is treated as proof. |
| **Duplication read as moved props or random teleportation** | K7 appears to be an author assertion. | Both M and N must be inspectable in one state and again after an empty reciprocal exchange; their integrated marks travel while P/Q references remain. |
| **Bad Ending mistaken for building transformation** | Four lower halls accidentally assert a forbidden facility-scale quantum law. | Show continuous shaft depth, immutable landing-specific repairs, and *built* differences in approach count. Do not animate one landing losing routes. |
| **K8 made mandatory without proof** | A player must accept documents or guess a hidden historical flag. | Treat the three-site history as provisional until an inspectable fixed cross-site datum and object-specific comparison exist. The M-at-P route must work without a K8 declaration. |
| **Concepts stack without a useful question** | The player is confused rather than curious. | Each anomaly should support a plausible temporary model and a voluntary physical comparison that can break it; revisit evidence should answer a question the player already had. Test with unbriefed players who verbalize a prediction *before* acting. |

## Emotional trajectory and validation boundary

| Experience | Cognitive turn | Intended feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Curiosity** | A credible abandoned workplace contains one impossible Cube. | “What happened here?” |
| **Confusion** | Honest local evidence disagrees with the player's normal-building assumption. | “These repairs and routes cannot all mean what I thought.” |
| **Experimentation** | Player varies sightline, support, camera aim, and comparison position. | “I can test an explanation.” |
| **False understanding** | “It is a transfer machine / damaged hub / evacuation loop” explains enough to proceed. | Competence with a nagging exception. |
| **Revelation** | An assembly member and then M/N identities move while fixed beds do not. | “The boundary I drew around the object was wrong.” |
| **Reinterpretation** | Lower landings, old notes, cameras, and P's face gain different meanings. | Survival mixed with unease and renewed attention. |
| **Mastery** | A predicted M-at-P connection is physically verified and crossed. | “I already had the rule; I finally asked the right spatial question.” |

The progression is an intended player experience, **not a validated human result**. Camera readability, full concealment and passenger support, empty M/N comparison/recovery, X alignment, lower-landing identity, and the continuous K8 cross-site reference still require spatial proof and unbriefed playtests. Until those tests, the design must not promise that players will reach these deductions naturally or that the true ending has a proven nontextual history reveal.
