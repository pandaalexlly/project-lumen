# Project Lumen - Ending Structure as a Knowledge Test

**Design proposal only.** No ending scene, credits, trigger, camera system, room exchange, or progression flag is implemented by this document. The current TASK-038B exterior C placement remains a technical assembly result, not an authored ending. The two outcomes below are possible future spatial routes, not judgments of the player's character or a count of collected clues. They depend on the [proposed spatial rules](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md), which must be validated first.

## Shared physical setup

The player can already reach the exterior receiving yard by correctly using the bounded Cube/bearer/cradle/collar assembly. That route is real escape from the research interior. It should remain lawfully available even to someone who never reads a record, visits the paired-room area, or names the deeper rule.

A second architectural question is visible before either ending. The hub's fixed interior approach meets socket **P**, presently occupied by room **N**, whose east face is a solid wall. A fixed exterior service spur is visibly adjacent but does not connect through N. At outer socket **Q**, room **M** has the same original construction grammar but a distinctive later repair and an ordinary pass-through doorway already open or openable by its normal handle. At Q that doorway faces a safe blind service recess; it is not a destination selector. Both rooms and both sockets have stable physical identity. The hub, survey vantage, contact scars, outer spur, and exterior ground remain fixed.

The proposed initial composite placement is `N@P, M@Q`. A fixed, powered service camera looks directly at M at Q, holding the room exchange. Its lens direction, ordinary mounting, power state, and actual occlusion path can be inspected; it is not an invisible master lock. Existing Beam/player observation may impose additional ordinary constraints if they genuinely reach a participating wall. The camera can be redirected or occluded through physically plausible service access; its control is a sightline intervention, not an “open final exit” switch. The exact access geometry must be designed and tested later so the player is never asked to perform a tiny reticle trick.

With both modules genuinely unobserved, the reciprocal candidate `M@P, N@Q` can occur under the same complete safety rule as every other quantum transition. M's unchanged open doorway now aligns with the fixed exterior spur at P. The player can walk through a normal threshold onto fixed exterior ground. No door becomes a wall by animation, no corridor silently remaps, and no final-only quantum law appears.

## Bad ending: correct small theory, incomplete world theory

The player uses the known assembly to reach C, steps onto fixed exterior ground, and follows the obvious evacuation/perimeter route away. This is a genuine survival outcome, not a fake escape, death trap, or punishment for missing lore. The ending's perspective may show the facility still held in its puzzling configuration: the fixed camera continues its watch and the apparently sealed P approach remains unexplained. The player has correctly learned that observation can move a bounded object and carry a person, but still treats the building as a stable container around that object.

Avoid a failure caption such as “you did not learn the truth.” The player's incomplete explanation should be inferable from the path chosen and the unresolved visual question, not declared by a narrator. If the design permits post-escape exploration of the yard, crossing the farther perimeter boundary—not merely reaching C—marks this outcome. The player should be free to examine Q and turn back before leaving. Whether to offer a reversible continue point afterward is a usability decision, not a quantum rule.

## True ending: corrected architectural boundary

The player recognizes M and N as two persistent room structures, not a room and its teleporting contents. They can predict that the service camera's direct view is preventing a joint exchange, and that removing that view without watching either participating shell could put M at P. A lawful empty exchange is sufficient: a passenger ride is not required as an ending button. After rechecking from fixed circulation, the player sees M's repair and already-open doorway at P, N's different damage at Q, and unchanged fixed socket contacts. Walking through M at P into the exterior spur physically confirms the corrected model. A supported room-scale passenger transition may offer another lawful vantage later, but cannot be the sole proof of the revelation.

The emotional turn is not “I found a better elevator.” It is “I called a travelling piece of architecture the building, and mistook the other room's wall for a permanent boundary.” The scene beyond the spur should allow a last fixed-ground comparison of both sockets and the hub, so earlier contradictions become legible without an explanatory monologue. Human records may reveal that staff described two incompatible evacuations because they recorded *places* rather than room identities. The duplicated pre-incident fracture and distinct post-incident repairs give physical support to that history. Documents may deepen what happened to the researchers, but their wording must not be the only proof of room exchange or a prerequisite read flag.

There is no “true-ending eligible” variable. The true route opens whenever the live world satisfies its observation, candidate, exchange, and ordinary door/geometry conditions. A perceptive or lucky player may reach it early. Completion cannot prove they understood it; unbriefed testing must ask what they predicted *before* the exchange.

## What must be established before the final action

| Prior evidence | Why it is needed | Failure if omitted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M and N both exist as trackable identities, with shared construction and later distinct repairs. | Makes a reciprocal exchange testable instead of a duplicate asset or scene trick. | “The same room teleported,” “the map loaded a variant,” or “the door changed.” |
| P/Q have independent fixed contacts, hub routes, and exterior ground. | Gives the player a coordinate reference. | “The whole building moved,” with no way to disprove it. |
| A door's hinge state and attached equipment follow a module. | Establishes that *relationships*, not just a Cube, travel. | “A remote switch copied the door state.” |
| Watching a non-Cube structural member holds a current state; observing an empty candidate excludes it. | Makes room-scale observation a lawful extension of Awakening. | “A final camera/Beam puzzle has different rules.” |
| A live camera's aimed and blocked states make different predictions while a recording does not. | Explains the prior stubbornness without a hidden lock. | “I happened to turn off the correct switch.” |
| The open M doorway and fixed exterior spur are visible as a mismatch before alignment. | Allows a prospective inference, not only hindsight. | “A secret exit appeared at the end.” |

The exterior C yard can expose Q and the camera before the player chooses the obvious perimeter route. The Records wing can supply optional time-stamped plans or testimony; Power and Signal provide physical source/sightline comparisons; Containment and the existing assembly establish structural membership and passenger support. No “visit all four wings” requirement follows from this distribution.

## Anti-elevator and anti-checklist tests

An ordinary lift could explain a supported trip from P to Q. It cannot explain why an empty room with its repaired wall and opened door trades position with a second persistent room, why watching only one wall holds the pair, why an observed empty candidate forbids the reciprocal placement, or why a fixed exterior spur becomes walkable after the *empty* exchange. These are the indispensable causal differences. If the final route can be rebuilt as “operate camera, choose floor, ride, exit,” revise the evidence and geometry rather than add a twist speech.

Likewise, a file-reading checklist could unlock a true-ending flag but would prove no understanding. Records should help a player form or date a hypothesis, not activate the physics. No code, password, inventory item, wing completion, minimum relocation count, forced bad-ending-first requirement, or B-before-C condition belongs to either outcome. The world must remain recoverable from wrong observer settings; a camera can be re-aimed, rooms can be reobserved/rearmed, and each legal resting state retains fixed access.

## Open validation gates

1. **Spatial proof:** Draw and verify P/Q, both complete shells in both placements, camera/Beam sightlines, fixed circulation, safe doorway clearance, broad room-floor concealment, and passenger/empty recovery. A door facing the blind Q recess must have a nonintersecting safe pose.
2. **Technical feasibility:** Prove atomic two-occupied-socket exchange with persistent hinges/equipment, complete candidate observation, camera semantics, passenger safety, and no one-frame duplicate or unsupported occupant. Preserve the existing single assembly and Awakening behavior.
3. **Unbriefed cognitive test:** Before the final exchange, ask what participants think will move, what will stay, and what the camera is doing. Afterward ask them to explain the earlier wall and the two histories without solution vocabulary. Test whether players who take C still notice an unresolved question rather than feeling tricked by an arbitrary “bad” label.

The bad/true labels are designer shorthand. Surprise, reinterpretation, comprehension, and emotional payoff remain **UNVALIDATED** until actual unbriefed players demonstrate them.
