# Project Lumen — Quantum State Persistence Validation

**Status: design validation only; no prototype or unbriefed human test has occurred. Verdict: REVISE the proposed wording before treating “return to a previous state” as a new core law.** This document uses [Knowledge Architecture V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md), [Knowledge Discovery Events](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_DISCOVERY_EVENTS.md), the [Spatial Identity Contract](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md), and [M/N Prototype Validation](MN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_PROTOTYPE_VALIDATION.md). It neither changes existing systems nor authorizes a final-game device, new room, or scene.

## Resolve what “state” and “return” mean

The current K6 contract already says that a bounded quantum object carries its **material identity, attached internal conditions, and relationships among its members** through relocation. A fixed receiver retains its own contacts and corridors. Occupying an earlier receiver again may restore a *former external connection*, but it does not restore an old snapshot of the object's internal condition. Observation constrains a current placement or candidate; it is not a command to load a saved state.

For a testable vocabulary, describe an observed configuration as:

`S = (object identity, attached condition, current placement, external connection at that placement)`.

The object's prior placement remains a possible *legal placement* if current observation, candidate, rearm, and safety conditions allow it. Its earlier `S` is not a stored timeline. If its attached condition has not changed, a return to the same receiver can reproduce the same visible configuration and output. If someone changed that condition in the meantime, the old placement may recur without the old full state recurring. The distinction is causal, not terminological.

| Reading of the proposed sentence | Does the current K6 law already cover it? | Prediction on return | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Re-entry:** the same object can later occupy a previously valid place and reconnect to the same fixed interface. | **Yes**, provided that placement is lawful now. | The object's *current* attached condition follows it; fixed contact at the old place is available again. The resulting output may resemble an earlier scene. | Validate as a readable **application of K1/K6**, not a ninth rule or a separate memory mechanic. |
| **Snapshot recall:** losing observation can restore an earlier door angle, charge, setting, or whole world configuration even though it was changed afterward. | **No.** This contradicts K6 continuity unless a new, explicit history-selection law is defined. | An altered attached setting would revert without an ordinary cause; players could reasonably infer rewind or time travel. | **Do not promote or implement** on this brief. It needs a separate rule, limits, safety model, and independent design decision. |

The concept to test now is therefore: **a bounded object can lawfully revisit a former placement; its persistent attached state and the fixed connection together determine what the player sees there.** If the game needs literal restoration of an old internal snapshot for its mystery, this test must expose that as a different proposal rather than smuggle it into “persistence.”

## Relationship to existing discoveries

| Existing phenomenon | What it proves | What “previous state returns” must not be mistaken for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ordinary relocation (K1)** | An unobserved bounded object may occupy another eligible position. An earlier position can be eligible again. | The mere A→B→A itinerary does **not** prove memory; a two-position object has nowhere else to go. |
| **Observation hold/release (K1/K3, later K5)** | Direct sight of a participating member holds a present placement; sight of an empty candidate can exclude it where that contrast exists. Breaking sight **permits** a lawful outcome. | Looking away does not trigger, select, or rewind state A. A live camera can delay the return by continuing to see a member; it cannot store or recall the object's former setting. |
| **Quantum State Persistence (current K6)** | Same identifiable object and its *present* attached door/setting survive movement; a fixed supply/contact does not travel. | A receiver “remembering” an old setting or the object resetting to an earlier version. An ordinary change to the attached setting remains real. |
| **Room identity exchange (K7)** | M and N are two persistent shells trading fixed sockets; their distinct histories and attached conditions remain theirs. | One room reverting into an earlier self, the Atrium transforming, or a room's socket address becoming its identity. The existing M/N greybox has fixed identity cues and an open leaf, but **no player-changeable attached condition**; it cannot validate the stronger K6 contrast by itself. |

This leaves K6 valuable without multiplying laws: the player can predict not just *where* a room may be, but what its already-existing door or mounted apparatus will be like when it arrives, and which fixed connection can then form.

## Player-discovered evidence chain

Use a small service object before asking players to reason about a room-sized one. The player should not be given a “state persistence” label, a timeline UI, or a document that states the answer.

| Step | What the player directly observes | Reasonable first explanation | Contradiction or controlled comparison | Revised understanding available |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A: familiar configuration** | A recognizable bounded object sits at fixed receiver A. Its mounted, visible two-position setting is ON; a fixed A-side contact powers a small lamp through that setting. The A contact scar is independent of the object. | “A is the powered station; the lamp belongs to the place.” | Remember the object's repair and setting as well as the fixed contact, without having to read a label. | Baseline only; no quantum claim yet. |
| **B: lawful relocation** | After a genuinely unobserved interval the **same repaired object** is at receiver B. Its mounted setting is still ON, but the lamp is dark because B has no matching supply. A's contact remains behind. | “The object lost power,” “B copied the machine,” or “A remembers how it was.” | Inspect the setting and repair at B and the unchanged A contact. Watch the object for a no-move contrast, then release and rearm. | Identity and attached setting travel; live external output can differ. |
| **Return attempt** | The player can predict what will happen if the object lawfully returns to A while the setting remains ON. They remove all relevant observation and leave A eligible. | “Looking away orders it back,” or “the apparatus cycles on a timer.” | A repeat can bring the object to A and reconnect it; keeping it watched prevents that contrast. In this two-position fixture the opposite placement is the only alternative, **not** evidence that the player selected it. | The former placement is revisitable; fixed A supply can recreate the old visible output. |
| **Counterfactual condition** | On another B visit, the player changes the **attached** setting to OFF by an ordinary physical interaction while seeing the same repair. After a lawful return to A, the setting is still OFF and the A lamp remains dark. | “A restores its prior saved ON state,” or “the whole sequence rewound.” | The A contact is still live and fixed, but the altered setting followed the object. Restoring ON by the ordinary control—not by looking away—relights it. | Re-entry is not snapshot recall. The old *place* returned; the old full state did not unless its causal conditions still held. |

The fourth comparison is essential. Without it, A→B→A plus a relit lamp is observationally compatible with ordinary relocation, receiver power, a scripted loop, and historical-state recall. A surprising return alone is not a new Eureka. An insightful player may infer the distinction before the counterfactual; the experiment must still allow them to test it.

## Smallest proposed greybox proof (not built)

**Geometry:** one bounded, repaired specimen/bearer or compact service module; fixed receiver A with visible contact and fixed lamp; fixed receiver B without that supply; one mounted, reversible ON/OFF setting integral to the moving object; a safe fixed viewing lane to both; a broad cover position from which neither current object nor eligible destination is directly observed. No room exchange, Beam, camera, passenger, final art, document, or ending route is needed for the first test. If B's dark output would be hard to read, keep the mounted setting itself visible so “power lost” and “setting reset” can be separated.

**Required behaviors for a future feasibility build:** preserve the same object's repair and setting through A↔B; let the fixed A contact affect output only while that object is actually there; allow an ordinary player action to change the mounted setting; hold placement while a participating surface is watched; allow a release only after genuine loss of all relevant observation, rearm, destination eligibility, and safe clearance. No observation event changes the mounted setting. The two locations make a return *possible and repeatable*, not player-commanded or evidence of a general preference for A. Avoid a control whose only effect is “restore A.”

**Player goal:** investigate why a previously lit work module is dark at B and whether its appearance at A can be predicted. The apparatus should look like plausible maintenance equipment, not a state-selection tutorial. A player who has experienced Awakening's K1/K3 is the principal blind-test cohort; record other prior knowledge separately.

**Before-action prediction checkpoint:** while the object is at B, ask neutrally, “If it appears at the other place, what do you expect its setting and the lamp to be like? Why?” Record a prediction with the setting unchanged, then repeat after the player has changed it. A full causal answer predicts **position, attached setting, fixed contact, and output** for both trials. A location-only prediction is K1, not K6. If the object stays at B for a legitimate observer or safety reason, one no-move interval is not disproof; let the player diagnose and retry.

**Technical acceptance before human testing:** A/B positions and contact are fixed; the repair and setting retain identity; each reachable condition has a safe return; watched hold, genuine release, and rearm are repeatable; no hidden flag restores an older value; no fixed output stays lit at an empty receiver; no player overlap or observed candidate is silently ignored. A paper state table must cover at least `(A,ON)`, `(B,ON)`, `(B,OFF)`, `(A,OFF)`, plus return and recovery transitions.

**Cognitive acceptance:** an unbriefed player notices the same repaired object, distinguishes its mounted setting from A's fixed supply, predicts the lamp's state at A **before** each return, and uses the OFF counterfactual to reject “the old A state was saved.” Completion or a correct retrospective explanation is insufficient. Record whether the player calls the effect “memory”; ask what specifically was remembered before scoring the claim.

## Mystery role if the narrow contract survives testing

- **Camera:** a live lens is another present observer that may hold the object at B or exclude an eligible arrival if its actual sightline reaches the relevant member/pose. Turning or occluding that lens removes a constraint. It does **not** preserve yesterday's position or old attached setting. This can reframe an earlier “stubborn” hold without making the camera an A-return switch.
- **Hidden environmental objects:** a later apparently fixed casing, collar, or room may reveal itself as a bounded object because its repair and changed attached condition recur at another socket while independent contacts remain. The player should trace continuity, not hunt for a saved-state icon. What *looks* like an old scene returning can be an old connection re-forming.
- **True ending's passive spatial device:** the current design has **no separate final machine**. M's existing doorway and ordinary condition belong to M; the exterior spur belongs to fixed ground. When a lawful M/N exchange places M at P, that doorway may meet the spur. This is the consequential application of K6 plus K7, **not** a device that recalls an earlier world snapshot. If the doorway was ordinarily closed while elsewhere, it remains closed until opened by an ordinary cause.
- **Replay interpretation:** earlier records that described “the same state at this address” may have conflated a fixed socket's output with the attached state of whichever object occupied it. On replay the player can identify which evidence was fixed, which traveled, and which condition truly persisted. No replay flag, time loop, or document-count gate is implied.

## Failure risks and decision gate

| Risk | What a player may conclude | Required discriminating evidence or response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Random teleport / automatic cycle** | The object just alternates A and B on a timer. | Compare a long watched hold with a genuine unseen release after rearm; record a prospective setting/output prediction, not merely the next location. Binary alternation alone cannot prove any destination preference. |
| **Time travel / old snapshot** | Returning to A restored the whole past, including an old switch or door condition. | Change the mounted condition at B; show that it remains changed at A while A's fixed contact is unchanged. No other world wear or event rewinds. If the design *wants* restoration, it needs a new explicit law. |
| **Receiver memory or hidden synchronization** | A stores ON, or a controller copies a setting between separate objects. | Track one integrated repair and one mounted setting on the **same** bounded object; inspect the old empty receiver and then the object at both sites. Avoid duplicate live displays that obscure ownership. |
| **No player prediction** | The player sees an impressive return but cannot say what will happen to setting or output. | Pause the interpretation at B for a neutral before-action question; compare ON and OFF return predictions. If both look equally plausible after all evidence, refine the contrast rather than add text. |
| **Overloaded experiment** | The player sees relocation, electrical supply, a switch, and observation as unrelated rules. | Keep only one object, two fixed receivers, one attached reversible setting, and one fixed output; use Awakening's observation rule as prior knowledge. Test the attached/fixed distinction separately from cameras and M/N rooms. |

**Decision:** Keep the existing K6 continuity law as a candidate core knowledge point and validate whether players can *predict its consequence*. Do **not** elevate “previous valid state returns” as an additional core mechanic merely because A is revisited. If the A→B→A / OFF counterfactual is legible and useful to later M/N reasoning, describe it as **re-entry with persistent attached state and re-established fixed connection**. If players cannot distinguish that from ordinary relocation after fair, minimal evidence, K6 may still be mechanically true but lacks an independent player-facing Eureka; reduce its claimed mystery role rather than forcing a new rule. Literal restoration of an earlier internal snapshot remains **unapproved and unvalidated** pending a separate world-rule decision.
