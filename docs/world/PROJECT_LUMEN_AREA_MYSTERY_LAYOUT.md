# Project Lumen — Area Mystery Layout

**Status: spatial design only.** This document places the experiences in [Knowledge Architecture V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md), [Knowledge Discovery Events](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_DISCOVERY_EVENTS.md), [Four-Area Core Puzzles](PROJECT_LUMEN_FOUR_AREA_CORE_PUZZLES.md), and [Player Cognition Route](PROJECT_LUMEN_PLAYER_COGNITION_ROUTE.md) into a connected facility. It does not authorize scenes, scripts, new rules, or a redesign of the completed Awakening Chamber. A/B/C is an existing technical precedent, not proof that a finished Containment wing or an unbriefed player's understanding exists. Live cameras, M/N room exchange, the deeper/core site, and their sightlines remain proposals.

The five names below describe work functions for designers, not player-facing labels announcing discoveries. K1–K8 are knowledge shorthand, not wing objectives. **No area owns one rule:** each offers a known-rule control, an honest but incomplete reading, a new discrepancy, a repeatable physical comparison, and a reason to reinterpret it later.

## Fixed-world organizing plan

Awakening's existing restoration leads to a **fixed atrium** that people once used to coordinate research. Four ordinary work approaches branch from its circulation spine: Records' survey route, Power's service route, Containment's handling route, and Signal's survey route. They should reconnect through plausible maintenance/perimeter access where spatial proof requires it, rather than terminate as four isolated challenge chambers. Those links are **design obligations**, not a claim that their geometry is solved. The player can return to Awakening and all reachable fixed work routes without a wing-clear flag.

The central plant, vestibule, conduit supports, and P receiver bed belong to fixed ground. The proposed Q receiver has an independently recognizable fixed outer approach and contact, visible from Signal-side survey access. M and N are **two bounded, material room shells**, not the atrium or entire facility; their identities and attached conditions persist when they reciprocally exchange P and Q. A/B/C has its own independent receivers and travelling Cube–bearer–deck–collar assembly. C/perimeter ground provides a real outside perspective and escape, not a failed ending. A proposed fixed exterior spur could connect to M's existing doorway at P, but no camera or document “opens” that doorway. Original, duplicated, and deeper/core **sites** are perspectives on a still-undefined larger geography; this plan does not silently draw a second moving building.

| Fixed approach | Why a person would use it | What it lets a player compare | What it must not become |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Awakening → atrium | Return from an occupied research chamber to the facility's shared hall. | The opening Cube/Beam case against later stranger-scale observations. | A prologue gate that secretly changes the observation law. |
| Atrium ↔ Records/Power | Survey and utility staff could reach the same frontage from different jobs. | Fixed P scar versus attached repair and mounted condition. | A document route that announces which shell is quantum. |
| Atrium ↔ Containment | Handling staff needed fixed access to A/B/C beds and service equipment. | Travelling collar/deck versus independent receiver; support versus fixed floor. | Three sequential tutorial pods or a mandatory elevator to the room proof. |
| Atrium ↔ Signal/perimeter | Survey staff needed sight toward Q, cameras, and exterior infrastructure. | Q's fixed contact versus the shell there; a known-member camera sightline. | An exit-camera switch or monitor that states the spatial answer. |
| P ↔ Q comparison / blind return | A future, ordinary fixed maintenance connection, if geometry permits. | Both room identities in one resting state, then again after lawful empty exchange. | A route that accidentally releases the pair while comparing, or a pixel-perfect gaze trick. |

The last route is **not yet spatially proven**. A valid layout must permit an observation-preserving P/Q comparison and a separate comfortable release where both current shells and relevant candidate poses lose every real observer. It must also permit rearm and recovery in either legal resting state. If these cannot coexist, revise the spatial plan before detailing rooms; do not patch the proof with a scripted swap or an invisible observer exception.

## Atrium — a trustworthy place with an untrustworthy frontage

**Spatial identity.** A large, fixed coordination hall with a distinctive circulation plant, continuous pipe run, worn vestibule threshold, and work routes used by several departments. Its scale says the facility is larger than Awakening; its repeated use makes it the player's measuring frame. The P contact/scar is part of that fixed frame, while the apparent service wall beyond it may belong to M or N.

- **First visit experience:** Emerging from Awakening, the player sees an abandoned but legible workplace: partial power, routes with different practical purposes, and a service face that can plausibly be blocked by damage. The plant and threshold are more memorable than the face. K1/K3 remain usable, but there is no initial panorama of two room identities, both sockets, the camera, and the exterior route.
- **Initial interpretation:** “The hall is stable; only the Cube is strange.” A misaligned seam or different wall wear reads as ordinary maintenance or a remembered-corridor error. Even after one offscreen change, a shutter or renovation is a reasonable explanation.
- **Investigation opportunities:** Retrace the short plant-to-P approach; compare P's unchanging scar with a shell's structural repair or damage. From a separate fixed route, inspect Q and return without inadvertently releasing either observed shell. Check whether an ordinary attached door condition follows the repaired structure rather than the P contact. On a later trial, hold one participating wall, then attempt a genuinely blind release with Beam/camera views accounted for. These comparisons reuse K1/K3 and test K6/K7; P/Q is **not** a claimed K2 candidate-only lesson.
- **Discovery moment:** After a lawful **empty reciprocal exchange**, the same P threshold meets M's repaired seam and existing doorway while N's different shell is at Q, or vice versa. Both shells retain their own ordinary conditions. Plant, contacts, and exterior ground have not shifted. The player can predict the two identities on another trial; a watched wall prevents a comparable change. The moment is a revised boundary of “object,” not a cutscene in which a wall morphs.
- **Required previous knowledge:** No stored prerequisite and no required wing visit. Awakening's watched/unwatched rule gives a useful experimental method; Containment's non-Cube member and Records' independent histories make the inference easier. A player who reasons early may proceed early.
- **Future revisit:** The first dead-looking face is recognizable as whichever shell occupies P. The hall's plant becomes an instrument for comparing changing connections; M@P suggests that M's existing doorway could meet the fixed exterior spur. Nothing is unlocked by recognizing this.

**Environmental storytelling:**

- **Visible documents:** Ordinary shift sheets at the plant describe P as an *address* used for dispatch, not as an invariant room identity.
- **Hidden documents:** An optional maintenance comparison tucked with tools at a fixed service alcove records a past mismatch but gives neither the exchange law nor route instruction.
- **Damaged infrastructure:** A worn P contact and interrupted pipe support show repeated servicing while remaining fixed.
- **Suspicious but explainable objects:** A slight wall/receiver clearance and two unlike repairs can read as replacement building work.
- **Later-revealed meaning:** The clearance bounds a room shell; the fixed scar identifies P, while the repairs identify M or N. Papers deepen the human error only after the player has a physical comparison.

## Records — surveys that disagree for an honest reason

**Spatial identity.** A used survey and records workplace connected to fixed circulation, with measuring benches, sight toward a shell feature, repair samples, and filing where staff actually worked. It is **one source of perspective**, not the facility's answer archive. Its fixed survey support can be compared with the changing structure it once measured.

- **First visit experience:** The player finds tools and interrupted work, not a glowing dossier. A familiar-looking frontage is drawn or measured more than once with incompatible wear details. The visible structural feature is close enough to seek physically elsewhere but not enough to label M and N from a single glance.
- **Initial interpretation:** Standardized construction, a mistaken address, renovations, or unreliable paperwork explain the differences. If the player only knows Awakening, ordinary rooms still seem like passive containers.
- **Investigation opportunities:** From Records' fixed view, note a load-bearing repair seam rather than loose furniture. Walk the same fixed route to P and to the independent Q comparison, seeking whether that seam, a mounted doorway, and an ordinary setting co-occur. Repeat after a lawful empty change; contrast the survey support and socket contact with the travelling features. K1's repeatability guides the test, while K6 prevents a door state from being confused with room identity.
- **Discovery moment:** Two different histories are simultaneously present—M's repair/door relation and N's distinct damage—and after exchange each follows its own complete shell, not the survey's named address. The documents become intelligible as local observations recorded at different occupations, not as the player's first proof.
- **Required previous knowledge:** None to enter or notice an inconsistency. K1 makes a repeat visit purposeful; K6's attached-state distinction strengthens the deduction. K7 is not required to find either survey view; it is the conclusion the comparison may support.
- **Future revisit:** A note that once appeared erroneous can be read as an accurate account of what a worker met at P. The player's question shifts from “which file is correct?” to “which structure did each person see, and when?” Optional human history gains force after physical proof without becoming a collectible sequence.

**Environmental storytelling:**

- **Visible documents:** Routine address-based survey sheets beside the instrument that produced them.
- **Hidden documents:** An optional earlier repair comparison in a plausible drawer or work tray, discoverable by ordinary inspection and never needed to identify the shells.
- **Damaged infrastructure:** A survey mount bent and shimmed after repeated measurement attempts, but anchored to fixed ground.
- **Suspicious but explainable objects:** Duplicate-looking repair casts and slightly incompatible plans initially suggest two teams or clerical error.
- **Later-revealed meaning:** The casts correspond to two persistent shell histories; the address in a record refers to a fixed approach, not necessarily one enduring room. The same physical evidence must remain understandable to a player who reads nothing.

## Power and Cooling — supply stays; attached condition travels

**Spatial identity.** A working utility route of fixed conduits, cooling plant, receiver contacts, and service access to the existing Beam I/R equipment. Its machinery has a credible job supplying/maintaining the research infrastructure. Fixed socket wiring is visibly distinct from any bounded moving apparatus.

- **First visit experience:** The player hears/sees partially functioning services and traces a supply to a fixed receiver. An illuminated contact or dead branch may make the moving structure look electrically controlled. Beam apparatus is familiar from Awakening, but applied here to a larger member or a truly vacant candidate, not introduced as a new rule.
- **Initial interpretation:** A generator selects destinations, a low-power fault holds the apparatus, or equipment at two beds is synchronized. A change in live lamp output may be mistaken for loss of the object's internal state.
- **Investigation opportunities:** With other views controlled, compare actual Beam reach to a current A/B/C member, a genuinely empty candidate if geometry isolates it, and fixed scenery while power is unchanged. Compare the same fixed contact before and after empty assembly movement. Later inspect whether M's mounted door/setting and repair move together while P's utility scar stays. Separate an attached condition from live behavior dependent on which socket supplies it.
- **Discovery moment:** Reach, not the switch's existence, predicts a hold or candidate exclusion. The repaired non-Cube member leaves one contact intact and arrives at another with its attached condition. If a lamp differs, the fixed supply explains the output without erasing the carried setting. K2/K3 are applied alongside K6; no button assigns a preferred destination.
- **Required previous knowledge:** Awakening already establishes Beam/vision equivalence. No mechanical gate requires the player to recall it; an uninformed visitor can see a puzzling electrical relationship and later return. K2 requires a true candidate-only view and cannot be inferred from the occupied P/Q pair.
- **Future revisit:** Power's fixed contacts become reference marks for identifying what did **not** travel in an M/N exchange. A stubborn room state may be observational rather than electrical, and M's doorway connecting to a fixed spur is a placement relationship, not a utility unlock.

**Environmental storytelling:**

- **Visible documents:** Maintenance cards beside the contacts record real supply faults and staff attempts to explain interrupted transfers.
- **Hidden documents:** An optional technician's comparison of attached instrument settings and socket output sits at the relevant service bench, not in Records; it poses a discrepancy but names no quantum law.
- **Damaged infrastructure:** Scorched fixed contacts, repaired cable strain relief, and cooling leaks have ordinary physical causes.
- **Suspicious but explainable objects:** Apparently duplicated contact heads at multiple beds initially imply a powered transport machine.
- **Later-revealed meaning:** The receiver heads are fixed; the repaired member and its internal condition move between them. A note blaming supply can be historically honest yet causally incomplete when an observer was also present.

## Containment — the boundary is larger than the specimen

**Spatial identity.** A hazardous-material handling workplace around the existing A/B/C technical assembly: independent fixed beds/hoods, inspection positions, maintenance access, and a travelling Cube–bearer–deck–collar structure. Its seams and repair history make object membership inspectable. The C placement meets real exterior/perimeter ground rather than a decorative “escape outcome.”

- **First visit experience:** The Cube is familiar, but its substantial cradle, repaired collar, and several receiving beds suggest a machine built to control it. Fixed service geometry offers places to watch the specimen, inspect a collar, or look toward a vacant receiver. The worksite feels like a place researchers used, not three sequential exercises.
- **Initial interpretation:** The Cube powers a lift; identical frames wait at each bed; standing on the deck activates travel. A distant exterior bed may look like a machine's destination rather than another lawful placement.
- **Investigation opportunities:** Hide the Cube while observing only a non-Cube collar member; compare with broad full concealment and controlled Beam/camera views. Track the same asymmetric collar repair against bed/hood marks after an **empty** relocation. Isolate an empty candidate from the current structure for K2 if the sightline permits. From safe seams compare full deck support plus visible member with full support plus complete concealment; verify fixed landmarks before and after a lawful passenger transition. No partial-contact trial should put the player in an unsafe crossing position.
- **Discovery moment:** A collar-only view holds the whole assembly; after release the collar/deck repair is at another receiver while its old bed remains. Boarding alone does nothing, but a wholly supported passenger who ceases all relevant observation can accompany the same structure. This connects K1/K3 to K2/K4 and foreshadows K6 without requiring the player to recite object-boundary terminology.
- **Required previous knowledge:** Awakening's Cube/Beam observations provide a useful hypothesis, not an entry credential. K2 and K4 can be investigated in either order and neither is a knowledge flag for the atrium. K4 can lead to C, but the first M/N spatial proof should be empty and reachable from fixed ground.
- **Future revisit:** The once-“architectural” collar becomes a small precedent for a repaired wall or floor at P. C/perimeter ground gives a different fixed perspective on Q and the exterior spur; leaving by C is a valid survival path, and returning to investigate is curiosity rather than a mandated correction.

**Environmental storytelling:**

- **Visible documents:** Ordinary handling sheets near restraints and receiver inspection points describe what staff touched and feared, not how to solve the movement.
- **Hidden documents:** An optional repair account behind the relevant workbench ties a particular collar wound to human maintenance; it is not the only way to recognize the wound.
- **Damaged infrastructure:** Scraped bed edges and a deliberately repaired collar seam distinguish fixed landing wear from travelling damage.
- **Suspicious but explainable objects:** Repeated receiver hoods and a deck sized for a person look like a conventional lift.
- **Later-revealed meaning:** Beds and hoods are fixed; the bounded carrier and its passenger support travel only under the same observation law. The repair's recurrence supplies nontextual identity evidence.

## Signal — a sightline with consequences, and a view of Q

**Spatial identity.** A fixed survey and communications workplace with cables, inspectable live camera mount, views toward a known A/B/C member, and a separate approach/view toward Q and exterior ground. It should feel built to monitor risky experiments, not designed as an exit-control booth. The camera's physical lens path matters more than its console or recording.

- **First visit experience:** Cameras and survey equipment look mundane; an archived image may be visible as ordinary history. The player can see a stubborn known assembly state or a plausible lens line without being told that the lens is an observer. From another vantage, Q's fixed outer contact and whichever shell occupies it are partly comparable against the perimeter.
- **Initial interpretation:** The lens is surveillance dressing; a console is an electrical lock; footage preserves a state; Q is an unrelated but similar room or an arrival station. These readings should remain possible until a controlled contrast.
- **Investigation opportunities:** At a **known** A/B/C member, hold camera power, player sight, and Beam reach constant; compare the same live lens on-member with off-member or physically occluded, then rearm and repeat. Stored footage remains without a live hold. Only after that causal test need the player apply the prediction to a consequential P/Q camera line. Separately compare Q's fixed buttress/contact with its occupant's repair and condition, using P and the atrium as the other reference after a lawful empty exchange.
- **Discovery moment:** The held assembly responds to what the live lens can actually see, not to footage or the mere state of a switch. Later the fixed Q reference remains while M/N identity changes there reciprocally with P. These are two linked realizations, not a single Signal-room solution: K5 explains a missing observer; K6/K7 explain what the outer view contains.
- **Required previous knowledge:** K1/K3 from Awakening make the camera inference fair; the known-member comparison must occur before a final camera hold is consequential. A player may inspect Q without K5 or K7 and return. No camera console may require an information flag or become an “open route” button.
- **Future revisit:** Earlier surveillance and a stubborn room state acquire a causal explanation. A live lens on M@Q can hold a proposed reciprocal exchange; removing only its true sightline helps make a lawful release possible but cannot select M@P. The Q view and fixed exterior ground can then corroborate a prediction made at the atrium. A later core-site account may explain why the monitoring network existed, not what it mechanically does.

**Environmental storytelling:**

- **Visible documents:** Camera service sheets and ordinary shift observations beside the mount; a worker may honestly assume stored footage was still “watching.”
- **Hidden documents:** Optional fault analysis tucked in the service access compares camera orientation with unexplained stability but stops short of stating the rule.
- **Damaged infrastructure:** A skewed mount, broken cable tray, and fixed survey supports explain why line of sight can vary while power remains live.
- **Suspicious but explainable objects:** A conspicuous lens toward a quantum worksite and a Q survey aperture initially read as security and perimeter monitoring.
- **Later-revealed meaning:** The live lens is a real observer; the Q aperture supplies an independent fixed reference, while footage is only historical evidence. The player must be able to inspect the active ray's target; no invisible omniscient CCTV veto is allowed.

## Environmental evidence is distributed, not collected

Visible paperwork lives at the equipment whose use produced it. Optional tucked-away documents reward looking at a damaged bench, service tray, or survey support; **“hidden” means easy to miss, never prerequisite to the physical experiment or a route**. Records supplies no master answer. Power holds supply-side accounts; Containment holds handling history; Signal holds monitoring history; the atrium holds address-based operations notes. If the proposed duplicated or deeper/core site is later laid out, its own workers' traces must offer independent but partial perspectives, not a final terminal that retroactively certifies K7/K8. A document attached to a moving shell can travel only if its holder is explicitly part of that shell; fixed noticeboards do not.

Damage should be evidence with a physical owner. M's integrated repair and mounted door/setting belong to M; N's separate damage belongs to N. P/Q contact scars belong to fixed beds. Loose clutter is not a reliable identity marker. Lights can reveal whether a fixed connection is live but cannot, by color alone, label a shell. These distinctions must remain legible without glowing highlights, objective UI, explanatory room titles, or text that states a law.

## Mystery pacing through repeated use, not timed reveals

1. **Opening scale:** Preserve Awakening's completed Cube, Beam, Plate, restoration, and its honest belief that abnormal objects sit inside a stable building. The first atrium arrival should establish a memorable fixed place and several human work purposes, not announce a quantum architecture puzzle.
2. **Productive middle ambiguity:** Containment can enlarge the apparent object boundary; Power can distinguish source reach, attached condition, and fixed supply; Records can challenge address-based identity; Signal can expose an uncounted live observer. These are overlapping investigations. A player may arrive in any order, notice partial evidence, and leave without a completion stamp.
3. **Late *deduction*, not late spawning:** M/N marks, P/Q contacts, and exterior relationships should exist from the start, but their decisive comparison requires repeated familiarity with fixed ground, two distinct shell histories, and a lawful empty exchange. Do not put all proof in one first-view panorama or withhold a critical clue until a stage flag fires. An early correct prediction remains actionable.
4. **Camera-to-ending connection:** First allow a controlled camera-only test on a **known** assembly member. A later real lens on a room shell may explain why M/N remains held, but power toggling cannot command the desired occupation. The proposed true route is predicted from M's persistent doorway, P's fixed connection, and the exterior spur; the ordinary C route remains genuine.
5. **Three-site question:** K8 may deepen the final reading only after a continuous fixed cross-site reference and connected geography are spatially specified. Present M/N exchange does not prove how near-duplication originated or that an entire building moves. Until the K8 physical test exists, optional documents may corroborate history but may not be required for escape or called a solved rule.

## Layout acceptance questions before any detailed room design

- From **both** P/Q occupations, can a player reach two fixed identity views, compare M and N without releasing them accidentally, then take a different broad route that genuinely removes all relevant player/Beam/camera current and candidate views? Can both shells be rearmed and every resting state recovered?
- Can a player demonstrate K2 at a truly vacant A/B/C candidate without seeing the current member? Does a failed arrival have a visible, diagnosable cause rather than appearing random?
- Can the same powered camera's on-member/off-member contrast be read at a known assembly **before** its P/Q hold matters, without another source or electrical state changing?
- Can one ordinary attached condition and two independent structural cues per shell be recognized at both sockets? Are fixed P/Q scars still memorable on return?
- Can an unbriefed player identify what stayed fixed, predict **both** shell identities after release, and explain why M's doorway would meet a fixed route—without having read a document or being led through the areas in order?

These are unresolved spatial and human-test gates, not claims of validation. The current room-duplication design remains under the existing **REVISE before detailed room design** decision until the comparison/release geometry, camera contrast, recovery, and unbriefed readability are proven. This document defines how a believable facility can distribute the mystery; it does not start that implementation.
