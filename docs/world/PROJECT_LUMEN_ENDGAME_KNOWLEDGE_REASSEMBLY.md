# Project Lumen — Endgame Knowledge Reassembly

**Status: design only; ending, cameras, M/N exchange, and three-site geography are not implemented or human-validated.** This revises the proposed [Ending Structure](PROJECT_LUMEN_ENDING_STRUCTURE.md) through [Knowledge Architecture V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md), [Knowledge Discovery Events](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_DISCOVERY_EVENTS.md), [Area Mystery Layout](PROJECT_LUMEN_AREA_MYSTERY_LAYOUT.md), and the [Spatial Identity Contract](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md). It adds no mechanic, scene, script, final-only rule, or knowledge flag. The current spatial-duplication decision remains **REVISE before detailed room design**.

The endgame question is not “Have I collected the explanation?” It is: **Which physical structure is here, what condition is it in, what fixed route can it meet, and who is still observing it?** The player should be able to make a prediction, change one real condition, and inspect the result from fixed ground. The game never needs to know whether the player can name K1–K8.

## Two lawful outcomes, not a grade

The designer-facing names “bad” and “true” describe completeness of the player's *world model*, not moral success or a secret quality score. Neither outcome may require seeing the other first.

### Bad ending — a real escape with an unanswered question

- **What the player believes:** The Cube and its apparatus are abnormal inside an otherwise stable building. Observation affects that apparatus; a fully supported person can ride it. The atrium's apparently sealed face may be damage, a locked service wall, or a mystery not worth solving to survive.
- **Action:** Use the known Cube–bearer–deck–collar assembly lawfully to reach exterior C, step onto fixed perimeter ground, and choose the ordinary evacuation route away. Merely arriving at C does not force the ending; the player can inspect Q, the fixed spur, or the facility and turn back before leaving.
- **Why it seems correct:** The exterior is real, the support and observation conditions are real, and the route provides genuine safety. The player's local quantum theory successfully predicted a consequential trip. It is not a fake escape or a death trap.
- **Why it is incomplete:** The player has not accounted for why the same fixed P approach can meet different structural histories, why a visible exterior spur fails to connect from N@P, or why a lens holds a room state. The outcome can leave those physical questions in view without a failure caption, punitive narration, or a later statement that the player “missed the truth.”

If unbriefed players feel punished for choosing C, or if C looks like an obviously inferior exit, this outcome fails its purpose. It should honor a correct small-scale discovery while leaving a compelling unresolved spatial question.

### True ending — a corrected architectural boundary

The player realizes that M and N are **different persistent room shells** capable of a reciprocal exchange between fixed P and Q. M's repaired seam, mounted doorway, and ordinary door condition belong to M; N carries its distinct damage and solid face. The atrium, socket scars, camera mount, and exterior spur belong to fixed ground. A live camera observing M@Q is one real hold, not a final switch. When that view and every other relevant observer are removed, an **empty** lawful exchange can place M at P and N at Q. M's existing doorway can then meet the fixed spur. Crossing that ordinary threshold confirms a relationship the player predicted; it does not trigger a new kind of teleportation.

The stronger outcome is not “more files read.” It is reaching a route that *was already physically possible* under the same rules. If a player discovers it unusually early or by luck, the world must allow it. Human testing, not an eligibility variable, determines whether the designed revelation was understood.

## K1–K8 reassembled at the end

The eight points are the **complete interpretive vocabulary**, not eight locks. “Essential to predict this route” below means the causal idea must be available in the world and recognizable to a player, not stored as a completion bit. Some knowledge gives an alternate vantage or a deeper historical reading without being logically required for the empty M/N crossing.

| Knowledge | What earlier experience contributes | What it predicts or reinterprets here | Role in the proposed M@P route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **K1 — Unobserved relocation** | Awakening's Cube holds under sight and may move after genuine release. | A watched room member can hold; looking away permits but does not command another legal placement. | **Essential causal basis.** No special endgame timing rule. |
| **K2 — Observed candidates are excluded** | A genuinely empty A/B/C receiver can be watched without seeing the current assembly. | A visible possible destination can be ineligible even when the current object is unseen. | **Useful constraint, not an asserted P/Q selector.** The two occupied P/Q sockets may not afford a candidate-only proof; the route still obeys all actual candidate observations. |
| **K3 — Beam equals vision** | Completed Awakening Beam/Cube/Plate contrast; Power applies it to larger surfaces. | A Beam reaching either shell is another real observer; source power alone does not choose M@P. | **Necessary observer audit if a Beam reaches the final pair;** otherwise an established analogy for K5, not a Beam-specific final switch. |
| **K4 — Supported observers can travel** | Containment's bounded deck can carry a fully supported, genuinely concealed player to C. | The C outcome is valid; “large quantum object” does not mean “elevator activated by boarding.” | **Needed for the C route, not for the true route.** The decisive M/N exchange is empty, viewed afterward from fixed ground. |
| **K5 — Live cameras observe** | Signal's same-powered-lens on/off-member contrast at a *known* A/B/C member. | A live lens on M@Q can hold the pair; removing its actual direct view may remove one hold. Footage cannot hold it. | **Essential if the proposed fixed service camera is present.** It must be discoverable before it becomes consequential. |
| **K6 — Quantum State Persistence** | The repaired A/B/C member and later M's attached door/setting follow their physical object while fixed receiver contacts do not. | M's doorway and ordinary condition arrive with M; its connection to the fixed spur changes with placement. A live lamp can change with supply without changing M's identity. | **Essential.** Without it the doorway seems remotely copied or newly unlocked. |
| **K7 — Two room identities exchange** | Atrium P, Signal Q, Records repairs, and an empty reciprocal exchange distinguish M/N from P/Q. | N's solid face at P is not a permanent wall; M's existing opening can occupy that same fixed approach. Both shells and both sockets can be predicted. | **Essential spatial revelation.** One room teleporting or a passenger ride is insufficient proof. |
| **K8 — Three sites, one persistent history** | Proposed cross-site fixed reference and object-specific condition; different human accounts gain a common physical frame. | Original, duplicated, and deeper/core perspectives can be reconciled without declaring the entire building a moving quantum root. | **Needed for the fullest interpretation, not a route gate.** Core geography and a nontextual K8 test remain undefined. If that proof cannot be authored, K8 stays optional historical inference; it may not be silently required for the ending. |

The minimum predictive chain for the proposed true route is **K1 + legible real observers (K3/K5 as applicable) + K6 + K7 + the fixed spur geometry**. K2 may eliminate a candidate where its sightline exists; K4 supplies an alternate outcome and exterior view; K8 supplies the broadest reconciliation. This is not a reduction in their importance to the mystery. It prevents the ending from pretending that a player has proven a three-site history merely by crossing a door.

## Required reinterpretations before the final action

| Earlier impression | Corrected reading | Physical evidence available before the crossing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The Cube controlled a machine.” | The collar/deck and Cube belonged to one bounded moving assembly. | Collar-only hold, recurring repair, independent fixed beds at A/B/C. |
| “A powered device selects a destination.” | Player, Beam, and live lens constrain current and candidate placements through real sightlines. | Awakening reach contrast, vacant A/B/C candidate test where geometry permits, Signal's camera-on/off-member contrast. |
| “The same room at P changed its contents or door.” | P is a fixed socket; M and N are two persistent complete shells with different internal histories. | Two identifiable shells at P/Q, attached condition following one, reciprocal **empty** exchange, unchanged P/Q scars. |
| “The sealed P wall must be opened.” | N's solid face is occupying P. M's ordinary doorway could meet the fixed exterior spur there. | M's doorway visible at Q, N's face at P, the spur's fixed relation to P, safe predicted door alignment. |
| “Surveillance is just a record or exit control.” | A powered lens seeing a participating surface is an active observer. | The same lens, still powered, holds a known member only when its actual view reaches that member. |
| “Conflicting site accounts mean one map is false.” | Staff described different real structures by familiar addresses from different positions and times. | Persistent repairs/conditions against fixed approaches; a future physical cross-site reference is still required to support the full K8 claim. |

These contradictions must be encountered across Atrium, Records, Power, Containment, and Signal by normal exploration. Records may date or humanize a discrepancy, but no two papers alone establish room exchange. A player who reads nothing must still be able to predict the present physical route.

## Camera final mystery: remove a witness, not a lock

The **first camera proof** belongs to a known A/B/C member at Signal, not to the final doorway. A visible, powered lens has a believable view of that member. With camera power unchanged and player/Beam views absent, the player can compare lens-on-member against the *same live lens* aimed at fixed scenery or physically occluded; a stored recording remains but does not hold. The cause follows the direct ray. This lets a previous “stubborn” state become intelligible and lets the player predict what a similar lens might do at Q.

At the proposed final setup, N occupies P and M occupies Q. A fixed service camera directly observes a participating M surface at Q. If the player redirects or physically occludes that lens through ordinary, inspectable service access, **only that observation constraint is removed**. Turning off its power could have the same observational effect if the power change is independent and legible, but a power-off/door-open coincidence is weak evidence and must not be the sole designed comparison. Other player, Beam, or camera views may still hold either shell or exclude a candidate. A recording, monitor, or player's memory of an image is not a substitute live observer.

Disabling a camera does **not** move architecture, open M's door, select P, or flip a “true ending” bit. It enlarges the set of lawful transitions. The facility appears to change only if an otherwise legal reciprocal exchange later occurs while every relevant view is absent. The player must be able to inspect the camera's target and access a broad blind release without standing in an unsafe or pixel-perfect position. If a camera aimed at an unrecognizable hidden surface is the only reason a room holds, the mystery becomes an invisible lock and must be revised.

## Final spatial puzzle: the facility as a passive device

There is **no separate final machine** to activate. The “device” is the existing spatial relationship among two bounded rooms, two fixed sockets, an ordinary mounted doorway, and a fixed exterior spur. It works only in a compatible *physical arrangement*, not because the game judges the player's knowledge. The initial proposed arrangement is `N@P, M@Q`; the possible route arrangement is `M@P, N@Q`.

| Feature | `N@P, M@Q` | `M@P, N@Q` | What persists |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Atrium approach and P contact | Meet N's solid face. | Meet M's repaired shell and existing doorway. | P scar, plant, approach, and fixed wiring. |
| Q outer contact | Holds M, possibly under live camera sight. | Holds N after a lawful reciprocal exchange. | Q buttress/contact and exterior ground. |
| M's door/attached condition | Travels with M at Q; Q-side opening faces a safe blind service recess. | Travels with M at P and can meet the fixed exterior spur if its ordinary door condition permits passage. | M identity, repair, hinge/setting; not fixed supply or a corridor. |
| N's solid face/damage | Makes P seem permanently sealed. | Remains N's identifiable face at Q. | N identity and damage; no wall-to-door morph. |

Quantum State Persistence is indispensable: the route depends on **M's already-existing door and its ordinary state** arriving with M. It is not enough for a Cube to appear at P while a fixed wall is scripted to vanish. The external connection is not carried: P's approach and spur remain exactly where they were; they become walkable in relation to M only when the bounded shell occupies the compatible socket. If the door is closed, the ordinary handle or existing door behavior—not a final knowledge key—governs opening. A source can constrain occupancy but cannot command which state appears.

The anti-elevator proof is an **empty** exchange. The player stands on fixed ground, identifies both complete shells before and after, and checks that the fixed hub and exterior did not move. Riding M would be lawful only under the existing passenger/sight rules if such geometry were ever built, but it cannot be the only evidence or the required final action. An ordinary lift cannot explain a reciprocal exchange of two persistent rooms and a fixed spur changing connection without moving.

## Final physical experiment and recovery

1. **Predict before release.** From fixed access, identify N's damage at P, M's repair and doorway at Q, the P/Q contact scars, and the exterior spur relative to P. State the testable prediction: if M and N exchange, M's doorway should meet the spur while N appears at Q. The game does not request or score this statement; it is the design's human-test criterion.
2. **Audit observers.** Determine whether the live Q camera sees M. Use the earlier known-member contrast to justify changing only its real view. Check any Beam, player, or additional camera lines that truly reach current shells or candidate poses. Do not infer that a dark console guarantees release.
3. **Make a lawful attempt.** Ensure the relevant mounted door has an ordinary usable condition, then leave the pair empty and use a comfortable fixed blind return that removes all applicable direct observation. The exchange is **permitted, not ordered**; no single wait interval, button, or spoken solution guarantees M@P.
4. **Check the prediction.** Return by fixed circulation that permits inspection without causing an accidental second exchange. Confirm M's repair and doorway at P, N's damage at Q, and unchanged P/Q scars. If the original occupation remains, look for a real observer, ineligible candidate, safety constraint, or need to reobserve/rearm; the world must allow diagnosis and another attempt from either resting state.
5. **Cross and confirm.** Walk through M's ordinary threshold to the fixed exterior spur. From the other side, retain a view of the unchanged hub/exterior references and both room identities. The physical vista confirms that the player misidentified a piece of architecture, not that a secret door was spawned for the finale.

No hidden minimum relocation count, all-wing completion, document inventory, mandatory C ending, or “now enable the true exit” flag belongs in these steps. A lucky early M@P is legal. Conversely, a player may reason correctly yet require another lawful attempt because observation release does not select a destination; the recovery loop must not feel like random punishment.

## Emotional structure: the same evidence, newly legible

The emotional sequence should arise through *recognition* rather than a closing lecture:

1. **Survival confidence:** Awakening and the C route prove the player's first model was useful, not fake. The research facility initially appears to surround its quantum experiments.
2. **Unresolved human error:** Ordinary staff notes name rooms by addresses; repairs, contacts, and camera coverage make some reports conflict. Their authors were often accurate about what they saw, but not about what counted as the enduring room. The player's own early inference mirrored theirs.
3. **Prospective reversal:** Before the final release, the player sees that the “sealed wall” is N's wall at P, that M's door has its own persistent history, and that the camera is actively preserving this arrangement. The moment of insight is the prediction, not the crossing animation.
4. **Quiet confirmation:** M@P and N@Q, the same fixed plant, and the unchanged exterior spur can all be inspected after the exchange. Earlier collar seams, Power contacts, Records surveys, and Signal footage acquire another meaning without captions naming K-values.
5. **Optional deeper grief:** Distributed site evidence may explain why people maintained contradictory maps and what the incident cost them. The historical origin of near-duplication is not established merely by watching today's exchange. If the deeper/core site is eventually defined, its strongest material should corroborate a physical inference already available, not deliver the first usable answer or gate the exit.

The desired thought is: **“I did not open the building. I learned which part of it was the object, and which doorway it could already bring to this fixed place.”** The C escape remains honorable; the M@P route reveals a broader, corrected picture rather than a higher score.

## Decision gates before detailed ending design

- **Spatial:** Prove two complete P/Q occupations, recognizable M/N cues in each, fixed access and safe door poses, an observation-preserving comparison route, a distinct comfortable blind return, independent rearm, and recovery from every legal resting state. Do not assume that the two occupied sockets provide K2's candidate-only contrast.
- **Camera:** Demonstrate a same-powered-lens on/off-member comparison at a known assembly before the final hold. Map every actual player/Beam/camera current and candidate sightline at both occupations. No invisible CCTV veto or power-switch shortcut.
- **Persistence:** Show one ordinary attached condition travelling with the *same identifiable shell* while fixed contacts do not, and show that changing live supply is not a reset or a copied door state.
- **Three-site truth:** Define continuous, player-inspectable fixed references and core/duplicated-site access before claiming K8 has been physically proven. If these cannot be authored, keep the historical account optional and do not call it a required final deduction.
- **Human:** In unbriefed tests, ask for the predicted M/N/P/Q and camera result **before** release. Observe whether players interpret C as real survival, distinguish empty reciprocal exchange from an elevator, and recognize the architectural boundary after crossing. Completion alone is not evidence of comprehension or emotional payoff.

Until those gates pass, this is an endgame knowledge design, not a validated ending or implementation specification.
