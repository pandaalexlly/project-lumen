# Project Lumen — Knowledge Discovery Events

**Status: design only.** These are player-testable encounter contracts, not scheduled reveals, tutorial beats, objectives, or claims that the proposed cameras, M/N exchange, and three-site geography are implemented. K1 and K3 describe discoveries already supported by the completed Awakening Chamber; they do not call for its redesign. The K1–K8 allocation follows [Knowledge Architecture V2](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_ARCHITECTURE_V2.md). The [Evidence Contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md), [Player Cognition Route](PROJECT_LUMEN_PLAYER_COGNITION_ROUTE.md), and [Spatial Identity Contract](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md) supply the causal and spatial limits.

An event succeeds when the player can notice a discrepancy, choose a comparison, and predict a later result. Relocation is **permitted**, not commanded, by a release; a single no-move interval is therefore not a disproof. Fixed references, repeatable access, and a diagnosable observer state matter more than dramatic timing. The names below are designer shorthand and never appear as player-facing labels. No document read, stage flag, or completion checklist is a prerequisite.

## K1 — The Cube that waits for a witness

- **Initial player belief:** The abandoned facility is ordinary. A strange Cube may be on a timer or respond to nearby service equipment.
- **Trigger event:** In the completed Awakening Chamber, a distinctive Cube is present; after a natural loss of sight it is found at another plausible placement while the facility surroundings remain familiar.
- **Natural experiment:** Keep the Cube continuously in direct view through a comparable interval, then break sight without operating an unrelated control. Return by the same route, identify the Cube again, and repeat when possible.
- **Expected wrong explanation:** A timed demonstration, remote machine, or scripted first surprise moved it.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Waiting while watching fails to produce the same displacement; losing sight can permit it again without a machine command. The Cube's identity persists at the new placement.
- **Knowledge breakthrough:** A bounded quantum object may relocate during a genuine unobserved interval. Observation holds its current placement; looking away does not select where it goes.
- **Future application:** The player can use the same watched/unwatched contrast on an apparent machine member, a live camera hold, and eventually a room wall. Awakening remains a truthful small-scale case even after the boundary of a later object proves surprising.

## K2 — The empty place that stays empty

- **Initial player belief:** Once the current object is unseen, its destination is chosen by chance, a favored receiver, or the machinery.
- **Trigger event:** At the proposed A/B/C worksite, a normally possible **empty** receiver fails to receive the assembly while that receiver alone is visibly observed; another lawful outcome remains possible.
- **Natural experiment:** From fixed access, hide every member of the current assembly and hold Beam/camera conditions constant. Watch only the vacant candidate, compare repeated eligible outcomes, then remove that candidate view and try again after lawful rearm. Check an alternative candidate so a global hold is not mistaken for exclusion.
- **Expected wrong explanation:** The apparatus prefers a different bed, or looking at a receiver somehow *calls* the object elsewhere.
- **Contradicting evidence:** The watched candidate stays ineligible without the player's view touching the current object, yet it can receive the object when that view is removed under otherwise comparable conditions. Observation does not guarantee any particular alternative arrival.
- **Knowledge breakthrough:** Observation of a possible destination can exclude that placement; it is not a destination selector. This differs from observing the current object, which can hold its present placement.
- **Future application:** The player can reason about A/B/C availability and return routes instead of cycling controls. A two-occupied-socket P/Q exchange is **not** claimed to provide a candidate-only proof; K2 must be evidenced where that sightline really exists.

## K3 — A second witness with a visible line

- **Initial player belief:** The Beam is a power feed, clamp, or relocation command rather than another observer.
- **Trigger event:** The completed Awakening Cube/Beam/Plate situation exposes a difference between Beam power and its actual reach to the Cube while the player can compare their own viewing effect.
- **Natural experiment:** Preserve the ordinary equipment state and change whether the active Beam actually reaches a participating surface, while the player's own view is absent for the release comparison. Compare that with directly watching the Cube oneself. Later, apply the same prediction to a collar or candidate receiver in Power/Containment rather than relearning the rule there.
- **Expected wrong explanation:** Switching Beam power moves the Cube or energizes a particular pad.
- **Contradicting evidence:** The result follows unobstructed **sightline to a relevant surface**, not power alone or a named destination. Player sight produces the same kind of hold; where candidate-only geometry exists, either source may exclude a candidate instead.
- **Knowledge breakthrough:** The Beam is an artificial observer governed by the existing observation rule. Its aim and occlusion matter; it does not command a preferred placement.
- **Future application:** The player can diagnose apparently stubborn larger objects and form a falsifiable suspicion about a live camera. Power supplies a new application to attached/fixed infrastructure, not a first Beam lesson.

## K4 — The deck that does not depart on boarding

- **Initial player belief:** A travelling cradle is an elevator, or touching it is enough to trigger a trip. Being inside it may seem to exempt the player's own sight.
- **Trigger event:** A distinctive Cube–bearer–deck–collar assembly is found at a different A/B/C receiver after an empty unobserved interval; its fixed landing did not move. Boarding offers a tempting but initially unsuccessful extension of that observation.
- **Natural experiment:** Compare standing wholly on the bounded deck while a participating member remains visible with standing wholly supported in a broad concealment position. Keep other actual observers off the relevant surfaces and candidate geometry. Use fixed-floor seams and landmarks to check support and arrival; compare partial/fixed support only from safe positions.
- **Expected wrong explanation:** Boarding activates a hidden lift or teleport volume, or any rider is automatically invisible to the system.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Boarding while still seeing the assembly does not release it. With complete support and complete loss of external observation—including the passenger's own direct sight—a lawful transition can carry the player and the same repaired structure while fixed ground stays put. Touching across a seam does not establish passenger inclusion.
- **Knowledge breakthrough:** A supported passenger can accompany that bounded object's relocation, but remains an observer. The event is neither an elevator command nor a special immunity granted by enclosure.
- **Future application:** The player can reach the genuine exterior C vantage and later recognize that a room floor could support a passenger under the **same** conditions. The first M/N room-identity proof should remain an **empty** exchange, not depend on a ride.

## K5 — The lens that keeps looking

- **Initial player belief:** CCTV records or communicates information; if player and Beam look away, nothing is still observing the known assembly. A stubborn state suggests a power lock or broken machinery.
- **Trigger event:** A known A/B/C member remains held after the player and Beam have clearly lost sight, while an inspectable powered camera lens has a plausible direct line to it. This anomaly precedes any consequential final P/Q camera hold.
- **Natural experiment:** Keep camera power, player view, Beam reach, and object/candidate eligibility constant. Compare the **same live lens** aimed at a participating member with it aimed at fixed scenery or physically occluded. Rearm and repeat; compare stored footage after the live view is absent.
- **Expected wrong explanation:** A camera console controls the apparatus electrically; a recording or someone knowing what is on the monitor keeps it stable.
- **Contradicting evidence:** The hold tracks the live lens's clear path while its power remains unchanged. An off-member or blocked lens does not hold merely because it is powered; retained footage does not recreate a live observer.
- **Knowledge breakthrough:** A live camera with actual sight is another observer, extending K1/K3 rather than introducing a new “camera switch” rule.
- **Future application:** An earlier unexplained hold becomes intelligible on a revisit. The player can diagnose a later P/Q hold by inspecting lens coverage, not by toggling a presumed exit control. Camera-only comparison geometry and human comprehension remain to be validated.

## K6 — The condition that follows the structure

- **Initial player belief:** Only an object's location travels. Equipment settings, door state, and power behavior belong to the receiver or are synchronized between destinations.
- **Trigger event:** At A/B/C, a repaired non-Cube member recurs with the Cube while the landing bed stays fixed. Later, a recognizable mounted door or instrument on a proposed M/N shell has an ordinary condition the player can inspect or change.
- **Natural experiment:** Record the object's distinctive structural repair and one attached, ordinary condition (for example, a mounted door's open position or an instrument's setting). Allow an **empty** lawful relocation and inspect both the same object at its new socket and the old fixed contact. If live output differs, check whether fixed supply changed independently of the attached setting.
- **Expected wrong explanation:** The destination receiver copied the setting, the old place remembered it, or a controller synchronized two otherwise stationary installations.
- **Contradicting evidence:** The same repair and altered attached condition recur together at the new placement; the old socket retains its own scars/contact but does **not** inherit that condition. A different live lamp output at a new supply does not erase the carried door angle or setting. Seeing a participating non-Cube surface can also hold the whole assembly under K1.
- **Knowledge breakthrough:** The bounded object's identity, attached internal conditions, and relationships among its members persist through relocation. Connections to fixed supply, corridor, and contacts are external relationships re-established by placement. No hidden “solved” state travels.
- **Future application:** The player can track M versus N independently of P versus Q and predict that M's existing doorway condition follows M, while its connection to a fixed exterior spur depends on the socket. This is the bridge between a moving specimen and architecture with persistent history.

## K7 — The wrong wall at the same scar

- **Initial player belief:** The atrium and its rooms are stationary; a changed frontage means moved contents, repairs, a shutter, confusing corridors, or perhaps one room teleporting.
- **Trigger event:** On a return to a recognizable atrium plant and P threshold, the fixed pipe/contact scar is unchanged but the apparent room face has a different structural history. A separate fixed approach to Q permits comparison with the other shell.
- **Natural experiment:** From fixed ground, identify **both** room modules by independent attached cues—M's repaired seam and doorway, N's different damage and solid face—at P and Q. Note an ordinary carried condition and the fixed P/Q scars. After a fully unobserved **empty reciprocal exchange**, revisit both sockets by an observation-preserving comparison route. Rearm both modules, watch a participating wall for a no-exchange contrast, then permit a lawful release and predict both resulting identities. Do not rely on a room ride.
- **Expected wrong explanation:** A single room was redecorated, its contents moved, a hidden elevator shifted it, or the whole building changed around the player.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Two persistent shells exist at once and trade the **same two fixed sockets** with their structural repairs, attached door/condition, and floor/wall boundaries intact. Hub plant, socket contacts, circulation, and exterior ground stay fixed; a watched shell member can hold the pair. Neither a swapped prop nor a whole-building move predicts the reciprocal identities and unchanged frame.
- **Knowledge breakthrough:** Some apparent architecture is a bounded quantum object. A familiar fixed approach can meet a *different persistent room* after an exchange; the coordinate has not become a new room and the room has not changed its identity.
- **Future application:** The player can predict which existing doorway will align with a fixed route when M occupies P, and can reinterpret contradictory Records, Power, and Signal views. This proves present exchange, **not** the historical cause of near-duplication. Fixed comparison paths, sightlines, recovery, and unbriefed recognition are still spatial-validation gates.

## K8 — Three accounts of one physical history (provisional event)

- **Initial player belief:** The original, duplicated, and deeper/core sites are independent maps, or one complete building replaces another offscreen. Conflicting site records seem to describe separate worlds.
- **Trigger event:** A repeated address or repair account from another site conflicts with a structure already identified by K6/K7. A fixed plant/conduit/contact visible from the original atrium approach appears again from a proposed core or duplicated-site vantage, but the attached room history differs from what that address led the player to expect.
- **Natural experiment:** Physically retrace an ordinary continuous fixed reference between accessible viewpoints; before returning, record which *identified shell* carries a particular ordinary condition and which *fixed socket* carries a contact scar. Predict the shell and connection visible from the other site after a controlled known M/N exchange, then check both views without reading a solution document. Optional site accounts can be compared afterward to ask why staff used the same address differently.
- **Expected wrong explanation:** A loading trick, complete building teleport, independent replica, or unreliable journal accounts explain the mismatch.
- **Contradicting evidence:** The continuous fixed reference and its scars do not reset between visits; the **same** repaired shell and attached condition are traceable across the viewpoints, while its connection changes with P/Q occupancy and the other shell occupies the vacated socket. Independent maps or wholesale replacement do not predict that conserved pair and fixed frame. Different staff descriptions can each have been accurate from their own time and position.
- **Knowledge breakthrough:** The three sites can be read as perspectives and histories of **one connected spatial system** with persistent bounded members and changing relationships, not three disconnected levels or a quantum root that carries the whole building. K7 proves present shell exchange; human evidence may corroborate the origin and cost of duplication but cannot substitute for physical proof.
- **Future application:** The player can reconcile misleading address-based accounts and prospectively choose a route using a known room identity, attached condition, and fixed connection. **Design gate:** the exact core geography and a continuous, player-inspectable cross-site reference do not yet exist in the spatial contract. Until they are specified and tested, this is a proposed discovery event, not a proven K8 Eureka or a justification for a true-ending requirement. If the physical comparison cannot be built, historical origin remains optional contextual inference rather than an essential rule.

## Overlap: one anomaly can change meaning more than once

These are revisits and cross-checks, not an encounter order. Every proposed conclusion remains available whenever its physical conditions are met; an insightful player may infer it early.

| Encountered detail | First reasonable reading | Later reinterpretation and where it is tested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awakening Cube and Beam | A strange specimen plus a machine. | K1/K3 remain true when K5 makes a camera another witness and K7 makes a wall another participating surface. Returning to Awakening still gives a small control case. |
| A/B/C repaired collar and fixed landing | A Cube operating a lift. | K4 tests passenger support; K6 tracks the repair and attached condition while fixed beds remain. The same observations prepare, but do not prove, a room boundary. |
| An empty receiver that never fills while watched | A preferred destination or failed machine. | K2 separates candidate exclusion from current hold; Beam or camera comparisons may reinforce the same distinction if actual isolated sightlines exist. |
| A stubborn held assembly under CCTV | Faulty power or security lock. | K5 explains the hold through live direct sight, then makes a later P/Q camera diagnostically legible. K3 already supplied the artificial-observer analogy. |
| Atrium P's familiar scar and unfamiliar wall | Maintenance, map error, or local mutation. | K6 moves identity and condition away from coordinate; K7 checks both M and N against P/Q and fixed ground; K8 may later reframe why different site accounts used the same address. |
| An opened mounted doorway that remains with M | A convenient route opened by a switch. | K6 says the condition belongs to M; K7 says its connection to a fixed spur depends on M@P. Neither a door flag nor a camera command creates an exit. |

Records, Power, Containment, Signal, the atrium, and the proposed sites may each supply several rows of evidence. Documents can provide provenance, dates, and human stakes; they never deliver the sole causal contrast. No area owns a K-number, and the player does not receive K-numbered feedback.
