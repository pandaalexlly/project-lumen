# Triple Coexistence — Mental-Model Scoring Revision

**Status:** scoring authority for future blind sessions; human comprehension **UNVALIDATED**. This document changes interpretation of playtest evidence, not the spatial canon, prototype, puzzle, or test procedure. Use it with the [canonical spatial rulebook](PROJECT_LUMEN_CANONICAL_SPATIAL_RULEBOOK.md) and [document authority index](PROJECT_LUMEN_DOCUMENT_AUTHORITY_INDEX.md).

## The scoring problem

Recognizing three atrium details is not equivalent to recognizing three facilities. The intended conclusion is that three materially distinct, fixed QER–Atrium–Awakening stacks coexist. The QER above, atrium at the center, and Awakening space below belong to **each** stack. Horizontal adjacency or a route between units does not make them one material unit. The player need not know the designer names, QER transfer algorithm, or facility history.

Score the **spatial model the player can support with physical evidence**, not a phrase they repeat. “Three facilities” without an account of the upper/central/lower relationships is insufficient; “three separate structures, each with its own upper chamber and lower room around a hall” may be sufficient without canonical vocabulary. Seeing identity cues in a recording establishes an *opportunity*, not attention or understanding.

## Evidence dimensions and level assignment

Retain the existing baseline and evidence codes in the [cognitive blind playtest protocol](../../tests/manual/TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_COGNITIVE_BLIND_PLAYTEST.md). Add an analyst-coded **V (vertical-unit mapping)** dimension: `none`, `partial`, or `all three`. `V = all three` means the player's explanation, sketch, or unprompted comparison assigns a distinct QER-like upper space and Awakening-like lower space to **each** of the three atrium identities. A gesture or drawing counts; the player need not name those spaces. Merely seeing nine placeholders, or naming only three halls, does not establish V.

| Level | Mental model demonstrated | Minimum player evidence | Typical statement or sketch | Common misunderstanding / upper bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0 — No stable spatial model** | The player cannot give a consistent account of what places they visited or now see. | No repeatable identity/location mapping after neutral clarification; uncertainty is recorded in the player's own terms. | “I can't tell whether I returned or went somewhere else.” The sketch changes incompatibly without new evidence. | **Do not** use Level 0 for a stable but wrong theory such as “one hall changed.” Record that theory separately. |
| **1 — Multiple spaces** | The player distinguishes more than one visible space, but cannot establish durable identities at fixed positions. | Points to or sketches multiple spaces, with no supported persistence or stable before/after mapping. | “There are several halls out there; maybe they appeared when I came here.” | Counting rooms, seeing three cues, or assuming the spaces were newly generated is not evidence of fixed coexistence. |
| **2 — Persistent fixed locations** | The player maps distinct atrium identities to separate positions and understands that those locations persist rather than one atrium simply changing into another. | Connects at least two previously visited atrium identities to their present separate positions, identifies the third as a distinct simultaneous atrium, and cites physical continuity such as wear, structural detail, fixed landmarks, or parallax. The mapping must survive a neutral “what was where before?” follow-up. | “The repaired hall I was in is still there, and the other two halls have their own spots. I might have been moving between wings.” | This can still be a normal multi-wing building, three atria within one facility, or a set of separate rooms. No credit yet for three complete facility units. |
| **3 — Complete independent facilities** | The player understands three simultaneously existing, fixed material units: each familiar atrium belongs with its **own** upper QER-like and lower Awakening-like structure. The earlier visits were to different units, not successive states of one unit. | Level 2 **plus** `V = all three`; an explanation/sketch links each atrium to its own upper and lower structure, uses simultaneous physical placement and structural separation/depth to reject one transforming stack, and reassigns earlier visits to the distinct units. The evidence can be gathered by looking up/down and moving, not necessarily in one frame. | “That hall with the paired seams has its own chamber above and lower room below. The slanted and capped halls each have theirs too. All three structures are still there, so I must have entered different ones before.” | The word “facility” alone, three atrium cues alone, or three wings attached to one upper/lower core cannot earn Level 3. “Independent” means separate material identities, **not** no horizontal connections. |

Assign the highest level **demonstrated** by the player's own map, prediction, comparison, and explanation. Record contrary evidence as well as supporting evidence. If the player holds a coherent alternative model, record it verbatim and code its content separately; do not force it into Level 0 merely because it is canonically wrong. If evidence is ambiguous, choose the lower demonstrated level and mark uncertainty, rather than completing the player's explanation for them. Moderator-supplied vocabulary or leading prompts cannot earn a level.

### What counts as proof rather than a guess

For Level 3, the player should be able to locate the three identities, associate each with its own vertical stack, and explain why seeing the structures together revises the earlier one-changing-facility interpretation. A spontaneous explanation is strongest. A neutral request to draw or describe connections can expose an already held model, but a yes/no question about “three facilities” cannot establish it. If the answer changes only after an explanation, screenshot annotation, or suggestive question, mark the session contaminated and preserve the pre-hint score.

The score describes **understanding at a timepoint**, not the desired cognitive reversal. A player who correctly infers three stacks before the proof can reach Level 3, but did not experience the intended wrong-to-right transition at the reveal. Separately record baseline one-changing-facility belief, whether it persisted until the proof, the first revision time, the physical contradiction cited, spontaneity, and confidence. Claim a **full reversal** only when the player held the intended one-changing-facility model before the proof, did not correct it before exposure, and independently reached Level 3 through cited physical evidence afterward. Level 2 is a meaningful partial deduction, not full facility comprehension, even if an earlier atrium-only code would have marked it successful.

### Opportunity and non-assessability

Keep the existing threshold for an adequate **atrium-cue** view separate from an opportunity to inspect **complete stacks**. For the latter, the recording must show that the participant could physically inspect the upper, central, and lower structures of all three stacks, with their connections and separations legible. This may occur through several looks or movement; Candidate A need not fit every tier in one camera frame. Log whether the player actually inspected those relationships, but do not require a particular camera gesture as proof of understanding.

If the geometry, occlusion, navigation, or session ending prevented a fair vertical-stack inspection, report the observed Level 0–2 evidence and mark **Level 3 not assessable**. Do not score absence of Level 3 as a cognitive failure. If inspection was available but the player did not pursue it, record both facts; that may reveal an attention/readability problem rather than a reasoning impossibility. Never infer the unseen lower relationship from the prior-visit sequence, which shows only part of the vertical structure.

The prior codes remain useful: atrium identity (`I`), simultaneity (`C`), evidence (`E`), revision (`R`), baseline model, and spontaneous/probe provenance. **V is additional, not a replacement.** No previous “full” result should be promoted to Level 3 solely because `I = all three` and `C = yes`. Revisit any provisional interpretation against the recorded words and sketches; if the necessary evidence was not captured, mark it indeterminate rather than inventing it.

## Validation boundary

**Technical validation:** the prototype has three fixed stacks and observation paths that permit a view of their simultaneous geometry. That establishes an available physical test, not a player's mental model.

**Human comprehension validation:** requires unbriefed sessions with usable pre-proof and post-proof records. It remains **UNVALIDATED**. The next decision must be based on whether players independently map three complete stacks and, separately, whether the intended initial false model is revised by the physical view. Neither a correct label nor a technically valid sightline is a substitute.
