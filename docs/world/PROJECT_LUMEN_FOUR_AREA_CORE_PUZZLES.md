# Project Lumen — Four-Area Core Puzzles

**Status: design only; no implementation authorized.** This develops investigation loops from the [four-area knowledge design](PROJECT_LUMEN_FOUR_AREA_KNOWLEDGE_DESIGN.md), [evidence contracts](PROJECT_LUMEN_EVIDENCE_CONTRACTS.md), [player cognition route](PROJECT_LUMEN_PLAYER_COGNITION_ROUTE.md), [spatial identity contract](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md), and [spatial duplication validation](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_DUPLICATION_VALIDATION.md). It does not replace the existing Awakening Chamber, alter its Cube/Beam/Plate/restoration flow, add a room pair, or claim that proposed live cameras and M/N room exchange already work. Detailed P/Q room design remains under the existing **REVISE** gate until geometry and human-readability tests pass.

## Design premise and the scope of the final thought

Awakening is the successful prologue: the player discovers a real observation rule through Cube, Beam, and Plate and initially concludes, reasonably, that the *facility* is the stable container around abnormal objects. The later mystery enlarges the boundary of what counts as an object. The phrase **“the facility itself is a quantum object”** is the desired *player feeling*, not a license to make the entire map one moving root. Under the current law, M and N are bounded, material room modules that can exchange fixed P/Q sockets. The atrium, socket beds, service spine, and exterior ground stay put; their **connectivity** changes because of room identity. A literal whole-building transition or runtime creation of duplicate rooms would be a different, unapproved law.

Each “puzzle” below is a field investigation, not a sealed test chamber. It has a perceivable discrepancy, at least one reasonable but incomplete interpretation, a controlled comparison the player can initiate, and a later prediction. None should produce a solved-room banner, completion flag, keycard, or required research token. The existing four approaches connect through fixed circulation after Awakening restoration; seeing a clue early and understanding it later are both valid.

## Central Atrium — the room at the wrong address

**Core question:** Is the atrium's apparent dead service face part of the fixed building, or does a particular room currently occupy that fixed approach? This is the **main spatial investigation**, not a level selector.

1. **Player motivation.** Leaving the intimate Awakening chamber reveals a larger coordination hall, a central circulation plant, dormant workstations, four visible work routes, and a service frontage near fixed socket P. The player naturally returns through it while pursuing equipment, records, outside views, and a way onward. Familiarity with the plant and vestibule is earned through ordinary travel.
2. **Existing belief.** On first entry, “the hall is stable; only the Cube or transfer apparatus is quantum.” After Containment, the player may broaden this to “a large machine can move, but the hall is still its container.” A Records-first visitor may instead suspect duplicated construction before knowing any causal rule.
3. **New anomaly.** P's pipe/contact scar and its relation to the plant remain where the player remembers, but the frontage may show N's damaged solid face on one visit and M's repaired seam and ordinary doorway on another. The fixed Q survey, reachable through normal work circulation, can show the complementary shell. No entry trigger should force this change.
4. **Natural wrong interpretation.** The player took a different corridor, maintenance opened a shutter, the Cube changed nearby equipment, or the whole building shifted. A single changed P view supports several of these; it is not yet proof.
5. **Player experiment.** From fixed ground, compare the same plant-to-P route and P scar against M/N's structural features, then use an **observation-preserving** comparison path to Q. Reobserve both modules, leave both full shells and their proposed candidate poses unseen via a separate broad fixed return, and inspect the results of an **empty reciprocal exchange**. Hold a participating wall on a repeat attempt; with other observers controlled, predict no exchange. Both comparison and release paths remain unproven spatial obligations, not assumed geometry.
6. **Discovery moment.** M's repair, doorway, and attached ordinary condition arrive together at P while N's different shell appears at Q; P/Q contacts, atrium plant, and exterior ground have not moved. After a true rearm, holding either shell prevents a repeat but a lawful full release can reverse it. The player can no longer explain the result by moving loose contents, opening a door, riding an elevator, or misremembering a corridor.
7. **Knowledge gained.** K6/K7: “which room” is not “which coordinate”; two persistent inhabited structures can exchange fixed places, changing what a familiar approach connects to. Observation still constrains them under the same law that held the Cube. K2 need not be re-taught by a two-occupied-socket pair if no candidate-only view is possible.
8. **Future reinterpretation.** The first “wrong wall” becomes N's solid face at P, not a wall that morphed. M's existing doorway can align with a fixed exterior spur when M occupies P. The player may infer this early and lawfully cross; the game cannot delay it with a knowledge gate to protect pacing.

The atrium must retain an independently memorable plant, vestibule, floor/pipe scars, and stable routes in **both** M/N occupations. It must not present both complete shell identities, both socket contacts, the camera ray, and the exterior spur in one explanatory panorama. Its two epistemic jobs—**hold a fixed reference** and **allow an unobserved interval**—must coexist without a pixel-perfect gaze trick. If this cannot be paper-proven, the atrium puzzle is not ready to lay out.

## Records — two histories attached to one address

**Core question:** Did staff document a room's *location* or the *physical room* they were studying? History should provoke a comparison, not state the solution.

1. **Player motivation.** The west work route contains physical survey benches, abandoned measurements, repair material, and signs that researchers were trying to reconcile inconsistent observations. Someone seeking an explanation for the empty worksite, altered frontage, or abandonment has a natural reason to inspect it.
2. **Existing belief.** Records were filed badly, a standard room design was repeated, or post-incident repairs explain apparent differences. An early visitor may know only Awakening's Cube rule; later visitors may already know a bounded assembly can travel.
3. **New anomaly.** A fixed survey view associates the same apparent architectural opening with incompatible physical histories: one shell carries M's integrated repair and doorway relationship; another carries N's different later damage and solid face. Old construction similarity makes the first confusion plausible. Dated records or photographs may hint that staff used one address for different sightings, but they do not name M/N or describe the exchange law.
4. **Natural wrong interpretation.** One room was renovated, two ordinary rooms were built alike, a maintenance log is inaccurate, or a photograph is of the wrong place. None is foolish before the player compares live geometry.
5. **Player experiment.** Identify a *structural* feature from Records' fixed view, walk the fixed P/Q comparison routes, and find whether that feature belongs to a shell or to its receiving contact. After any lawful empty exchange, make the same comparison again. If an ordinary door or mounted instrument state can be changed, check whether it stays with the repaired shell rather than being duplicated by a fixed control network. Optional records are corroboration only.
6. **Discovery moment.** M's repair and associated condition follow M to the other socket; N's separate damage persists on N. The fixed survey support and socket scars do not inherit either history. “This address was repaired twice” ceases to predict the evidence as well as “two distinguishable rooms occupied it at different times.”
7. **Knowledge gained.** K6 and a necessary part of K7: identity, internal state, socket, and current connection are separate. Present exchange is physically provable; a historical accident *creating* near-duplicates remains an optional, separately supported claim.
8. **Future reinterpretation.** Earlier contradictory staff accounts become understandable errors of reference rather than unreliable narration. At the proposed ending, Records may deepen what happened to people who trusted addresses, but no page may unlock the camera, authorize M@P, or substitute for the player's own prediction.

The Records investigation works even if visited first: it raises “which structure?” before answering “how can it move?” It should contain incomplete, humanly motivated evidence—not a dossier titled with the quantum rule or a sequence of required files.

## Power and Cooling — a live source is not a destination command

**Core question:** Does facility power move quantum material, or does a source's *actual observation path* constrain where a bounded object may be?

1. **Player motivation.** The atrium's partial restoration leaves some machinery live and some services dead. Power's independent conduits, exchanger work, fixed contacts, and access to existing I/R Beam equipment make it an ordinary place to investigate why the facility behaves inconsistently.
2. **Existing belief.** The Beam charges the Cube, runs a transfer machine, or selects which receiver will receive it. A player who has only seen one activation has good reason to think an electrical control commanded the move.
3. **New anomaly.** The same powered field can hold an *actual* non-Cube member at one placement, exclude a genuinely empty candidate at another, or hit only fixed scenery and do neither. A receiver's pipe scar stays fixed while the repaired assembly member or later room shell appears at a different contact. Live output can differ at different contacts without changing the object's identity.
4. **Natural wrong interpretation.** The generator calls a platform, synchronizes two doors, or repairs a dead branch. “Beam ON means movement” is a tempting but incorrect short rule.
5. **Player experiment.** Keep unrelated supply and player view constant. Compare I/R reach to the actual A/B/C member, to a vacant candidate where geometry isolates it, and to fixed structure. Reobserve and recover between trials. From the Power-side fixed route, compare the same contact before and after an empty assembly relocation; later compare P's fixed contact with M/N's travelling features and attached condition. Do not use a two-occupied-socket room swap as K2 proof unless a real candidate-only sightline is demonstrated.
6. **Discovery moment.** The field's *line to a participating surface*, not the switch's label or the plant's power state, predicts whether the known object stays or a destination remains vacant. The fixed contact and pipe support remain after the repaired structure departs. A changing lamp at a socket cannot by itself identify which room is there.
7. **Knowledge gained.** K2/K3, with K6 beginning to matter: observation of current members and candidate placements has different effects; an artificial source behaves like player sight, and a supply interface is not part of the travelling identity.
8. **Future reinterpretation.** A stubborn P/Q state need not mean the room system broke, and an illuminated socket does not mean a room was summoned. Camera observation later reads as another *sightline* in the same family. M's door meeting the exterior spur is a changed relationship, not a generator unlocking a wall.

Power should not become “restore three substations to open the next wing.” Its equipment explains why observation sources and contacts exist in a research facility; the important action is a controlled comparison, not a completed repair checklist.

## Containment Works — the specimen has a larger boundary

**Core question:** Which parts of the apparent machine are actually one quantum object, and when is the player a supported passenger rather than an external observer?

1. **Player motivation.** The rear workface holds the visibly unusual Cube inside a credible handling apparatus. Independent receiving beds, inspection openings, a bearer/deck, and maintenance access invite a close look at how researchers contained and moved it. A glimpse of the outside C receiver makes the apparatus relevant to leaving the interior.
2. **Existing belief.** The Cube is the only quantum member; the collar and deck are ordinary equipment. Boarding the deck may appear equivalent to pressing an elevator button.
3. **New anomaly.** At a fixed inspection view the Cube can be hidden while a doorway-like collar remains visible—and the **whole** assembly stays. Elsewhere the same repaired collar travels with Cube, bearer, and deck while the independent receiver and hood remain. The player can stand wholly on the deck yet still hold the apparatus by looking at one of its members.
4. **Natural wrong interpretation.** The Cube powers a machine; the frame is copied at every station; the Beam calls it; a hidden boarding trigger carries the player. The apparently architectural collar makes the first reading useful rather than absurd.
5. **Player experiment.** With Beam and candidate states accounted for, compare collar-only sight against full concealment from fixed ground. Inspect the same asymmetrical repair at A, B, or C and the now-vacant bed/hood it left. Watch a vacant receiver without seeing the current assembly to test exclusion where possible. A curious player can compare standing fully supported *while still seeing a member* with a genuinely concealed, supported trial; their own gaze remains an observer. The existing fixed returns and recall behavior make tests recoverable.
6. **Discovery moment.** A non-Cube structural part independently holds the entire object; an empty move preserves its repair and leaves its “architectural” receiver behind. Boarding does not command travel, but a fully supported, self-concealed player may lawfully accompany the same bounded object. The facility is not simply teleporting the player.
7. **Knowledge gained.** K2/K4 and the bridge to K6: the object boundary is larger than the Cube, candidate sight can veto a placement, and passenger inclusion depends on actual support plus loss of **all** relevant direct observation—including the passenger's own. K1/K3 remain true.
8. **Future reinterpretation.** A later repaired wall/floor seam at P can be read as possible object membership rather than scenery. The outside C yard provides a genuine escape and a fixed exterior perspective on Q, not a “wrong answer.” Room-scale passenger movement is unnecessary for the first M/N proof; the key spatial test is an **empty** exchange.

This is one connected A/B/C apparatus, not three teaching chambers. The ordinary fixed Containment cradle remains ordinary. The existing technical validation of this assembly does **not** establish that unbriefed people spontaneously infer collar membership, candidate exclusion, or passenger logic.

## Signal Array — an uncounted observer and the second spatial reference

**Core question:** Why does a known quantum structure remain held after the player and Beam seem to have stopped watching, and what does the outer survey reveal about an apparently stable building?

1. **Player motivation.** The facility's survey instruments, cable runs, visible camera mounts, and sightline toward exterior receiving ground imply that researchers monitored the workface and the perimeter. A player looking for why an assembly will not move, or where the outside receiver leads, has a concrete reason to come here.
2. **Existing belief.** Cameras are recording devices, monitors are the important observer, or CCTV controls are security interlocks. A player may already suspect that two similar rooms exist but still assume their *locations* are fixed.
3. **New anomaly.** A visibly live lens has a direct view of a known quantum member and explains an otherwise stubborn state; another aimed/occluded state does not. From a fixed survey point the Q socket, whichever M/N shell occupies it, and exterior ground can be compared independently of the atrium's P approach. An old image may preserve historical evidence but does not hold the live object.
4. **Natural wrong interpretation.** Turning a camera operates a remote door or destination lock; stored footage somehow keeps a quantum state frozen; the outer room is an unrelated duplicate build or teleport arrival point.
5. **Player experiment.** On a *known* assembly member, keep camera power, player sight, and Beam reach constant while comparing live lens-on-member against the same lens aimed at fixed scenery or blocked by fixed structure. Repeat after actual rearm. Only then use that hypothesis to predict a hold on a room shell. Separately, compare Q's fixed outer contact and buttress with M/N's distinct repair histories at P and Q after an empty exchange. The camera path and this comparison must be inspectable without a monitor announcing the rule.
6. **Discovery moment.** The real lens path, not a console toggle or archived image, predicts a hold under the same law as Beam and player sight. Later, the outer fixed socket is unchanged while the *other persistent room* occupies it after P's frontage changes. Signal does not need to deliver the full revelation alone; it supplies the missing observer cause and the second spatial reference that make the atrium's proof conclusive.
7. **Knowledge gained.** K5 plus K6/K7 corroboration: a camera is an artificial direct observer, and the apparent facility boundary includes room-scale quantum objects. The Q view helps disprove “the whole building moved” because exterior ground and its contact remain fixed.
8. **Future reinterpretation.** The earlier stubborn state and records of incompatible room locations become mutually intelligible. If a fixed camera watches M@Q, the player can predict why a reciprocal exchange is held; removing that *actual view*, then hiding all other observers, can lawfully permit M@P. That is not an “open exit” switch.

Signal prepares the player's thought **“the facility itself participates in the quantum system”** through spatial evidence. The literal whole facility does not move. A camera's causal role must be established with a known member before it is essential to a proposed final P/Q hold, and no invisible all-seeing CCTV network may silently veto the player.

## One connected mystery, not five solutions

| Cross-area question | First plausible reading | Where a player can challenge it | Later payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What is being observed? | “Only the Cube.” | Containment collar-only view; Power Beam reach; later an atrium room wall. | The same observation law applies to larger bounded objects. |
| What stayed fixed? | “The whole apparatus/room is anchored to plant services.” | Power contacts, Containment vacant receiver, atrium P scar, Signal Q contact. | A room's identity separates from its socket and live supply. |
| Why did nothing move? | “The machine is broken.” | Power current/candidate contrast; Signal live camera-only contrast; atrium wall sight. | A remaining real observer or unsafe candidate explains a held state without random timing. |
| Were there one or two rooms? | “One room was repaired or redecorated.” | Records' separate damage histories; atrium P view and Signal Q view before/after an empty exchange. | Two persistent rooms trade fixed positions. |
| Where is the route out? | “A wall must be unlocked.” | Signal/exterior survey of fixed spur; M's doorway tracked in Records/atrium. | M@P can make an ordinary doorway align with fixed ground. |

No row should map to one required room visit. Architecture-first, observation-first, and exterior-first players can assemble these comparisons in different orders. A player can discover the M/N law early; the game does not set a “now rooms are quantum” variable or require all four wings to be cleared.

## Access, dependencies, and the reveal envelope

- **Awakening is the only opening boundary.** Its existing restoration opens the atrium. The four current approaches are connected once reached; this design does not add arbitrary Records/Power/Containment/Signal locks. Some *perspectives* require ordinary walking around plant infrastructure, and exterior C ground requires lawful supported A/B/C travel; that is not a wing-completion gate.
- **Knowledge is useful, not checked.** K1 helps a player test Power or Containment, K3 suggests the camera hypothesis, K6 helps read Records, and K5 explains a held room pair. None is a stored prerequisite to enter an area. Early observations may be confusing but must remain honest, revisitable, and recoverable.
- **Essential evidence must not be gated behind the ending.** M and N must be distinguishable from fixed interior access in both occupations; P/Q contacts and the fixed exterior spur must be prospectively comparable. Records may date the duplication incident, but its text is not required to prove present-day exchange. A player who reaches M@P early may use the route.
- **Do not reveal everything on first entry.** The first atrium view should not show both full shell histories, both sockets, the camera ray, and the spur at once. Separate these across normal work viewpoints and repeated visits, while keeping each clue physically present from the start. Ambiguity should come from incomplete comparison, not stage-specific clue spawning.
- **The C outcome remains valid.** The player can leave via the coherent assembly without mastering M/N. The proposed deeper route should reward a predicted reinterpretation of architecture, not punish survival or require a “bad ending first.”

## Foundation gates before layout or implementation

1. **Spatial proof:** Draw both complete M/N occupations at P/Q; all player, Beam, and proposed camera current/candidate sightlines; an observation-preserving P↔Q comparison path; a separate broad blind return; both rooms' independent rearm; fixed access from either state; safe door poses; and recovery without hidden waiting. The [spatial duplication validation](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_DUPLICATION_VALIDATION.md) remains **REVISE** until this passes. Do not claim K2 is taught by P/Q if no isolated candidate-only sightline exists.
2. **Causal proof:** On a known member, demonstrate a powered camera aimed-on versus aimed-off/occluded comparison with player/Beam sight held constant. Confirm that a door state follows M rather than an electrical console, and that an empty M/N exchange is possible without a passenger ride or visible morph.
3. **Cognitive proof:** In unbriefed tests, allow different first-wing choices. Before showing a result, ask what a player predicts will move, what will remain fixed, and why a room or camera might hold it. Afterward ask which earlier atrium or Records observation changed meaning. Completion alone is not evidence of understanding.
4. **Payoff proof:** Before any proposed true-route crossing, test whether players can predict M@P/N@Q and M's ordinary doorway meeting the fixed spur. If they merely discover a camera switch and a new door, revise the environmental evidence; do not add an explanatory objective or a hidden knowledge gate.

This document is the **puzzle-purpose foundation**, not a construction specification. Awakening remains intact. Camera observation, two-room exchange, and unbriefed comprehension remain **UNVALIDATED**.
