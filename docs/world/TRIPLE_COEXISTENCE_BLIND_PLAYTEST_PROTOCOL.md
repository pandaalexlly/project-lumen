# Triple Coexistence — Blind Player Test Protocol

**Status: protocol only. Human comprehension is UNVALIDATED.** The isolated [greybox validation](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_GREYBOX_VALIDATION.md) establishes working geometry, not a player-discovered revelation. This protocol tests whether a player can replace a *naturally held* one-changing-facility model with three simultaneously existing fixed places, using physical evidence rather than an author's explanation. It neither changes the prototype nor decides that either viewpoint is final.

## 1. Study boundary and participants

The primary cognitive test requires an **unbriefed prior-exposure experience** in which each participant has actually encountered the three relevant locations and their persistent structural details before arriving at the H0 Atrium reveal. That experience must be identical across A and B assignments, must not announce the number of facilities, and must allow a reasonable one-changing-layout interpretation. The current isolated greybox permits walking in H0 but **does not supply prior visits to H1 and H2**. Therefore the primary study is **pending an authentic prior-exposure setup**; under the present no-gameplay-change constraint, a cold launch of this scene is actionable as a *visual-legibility pilot only*. It cannot answer the primary question. Do not replace genuine prior visits with a facilitator's explanation, a labeled diagram, or three screenshots and then report a full mystery pass.

Recruit people who can use first-person controls with their usual accessibility accommodations and have not seen this scene, its design notes, recordings, the answer, or another participant's session. Record prior puzzle/FPS familiarity and any relevant vision or motion-comfort needs; these are analysis context, not automatic exclusions. Obtain consent for screen/audio capture and use anonymous session IDs.

| Classification | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Eligible for the primary test** | Unspoiled participant completed the same uncoached prior-exposure route, could physically encounter all three location identities, and received no answer-bearing help. Their initial theory may be right, wrong, or uncertain. |
| **Exclude from blind inference analysis** | Prior spoilers/developer knowledge, discussion with another participant, visible designer labels or diagrams, a mechanics explanation, or a technical fault that prevents the required prior exposure or reveal. Keep the session as a logged diagnostic, with the reason. |
| **Do not exclude as “bad data”** | Failure to recognize a place, refusal to adopt the intended wrong theory, an early correct inference, slow navigation, or a confidently wrong answer. These are outcomes. Report how often the intended initial misconception forms. |

Before the reveal, classify the participant's **own** mental map as `one changing place`, `multiple coexisting places`, `uncertain`, or `other`, using their sketch and words. Do not offer these categories to them. The core *revision* rate is reported for those who independently held the one-changing-place model, but report **all eligible participants** and their baseline-model distribution as well. Never induce the target wrong model just to make a reversal measurable.

## 2. Setup and moderator controls

- Use the same build, camera FOV, controls, prior-exposure route, H0 spawn, room-cue visibility, and unassisted viewing window for all participants. Reset the scene between sessions. Hide scene filenames, H0/H1/H2 node names, developer markers, validator output, maps, and this protocol from the participant display. Confirm the three stacks render and the player can walk before each session.
- Use a private assignment sheet. Balance A-first and B-first among participants with comparable baseline models. Do **not** let free-choice route preference stand in for a fair A/B comparison; it is useful naturalistic observation but confounded by who chooses which path.
- For the controlled comparison only, give one of two symmetric, navigation-only instructions from the same H0 spawn: **“At the first branching path, explore the left route first; afterward, go where you like.”** (A) or the same sentence with **“right”** (B). Do not name a viewpoint, tell them where to stand, point at a repair, or turn their camera. Log this route instruction as a deliberate navigation control, not spontaneous route discovery. The routes differ in travel length; score reasoning time from the first adequate three-stack viewing opportunity and report route time separately.
- If someone sees a revealing three-stack view **en route** or from the other candidate before the assigned one, timestamp it and classify their first exposure by what they actually saw. Keep them in the assigned-route count and report an additional actual-first-view count; do not silently filter a difficult approach. If en-route exposure is common, the A/B *viewpoint* comparison is inconclusive because route and viewpoint were not isolated. If neutral control help is needed, use the same help policy in both conditions and log it. Any answer-bearing hint ends independent-discovery scoring from that point.
- Two logistics pilots may check briefing, recording, controls, and route exposure. If the setup changes, do not count those pilots in the locked comparison. A small balanced first round (for example, 6–8 new eligible participants per first-view condition) can reveal repeated design failures; it is not a statistical proof of population success. If few participants form the intended baseline belief, report that as a lead-in finding rather than recruiting until only convenient cases remain.

Participant-facing opening, read verbatim after the prior-exposure phase:

> “This is an unfinished space. Please explore as you normally would. Tell me what you notice and what you think is happening whenever you wish. There is no need to explain anything before you are ready.”

If asked for an objective: **“Explore whatever interests you.”** Ordinary control help (“WASD moves; the mouse looks; Esc releases the cursor”) is allowed and logged. No participant-facing wording should include “proof,” “three facilities,” “copies,” “correct answer,” “quantum state,” or the designer's H labels.

## 3. Procedure and evidence capture

### Before the reveal — establish the player's model

After the same prior-exposure route and **before** entering the triple-view scene, provide blank paper and ask:

1. “Could you sketch the places you have been and how you think they relate?”
2. “What makes you think those connections work that way?”
3. “If you returned to a familiar place now, what would you expect to find?”

Capture the sketch and exact phrases. Do not correct errors, supply location names, ask “Is it one facility?”, or select an answer from options. Record which physical details they remember without prompting for repairs, QER, or Awakening. A participant who already predicts several fixed coexisting places remains in the study, but cannot demonstrate the particular *wrong-to-right* reversal.

### During the first assigned viewpoint — observe, do not teach

Let the participant walk continuously from the actual H0 Atrium spawn. Start separate clocks for route-finding and for first **adequate exposure**. Before sessions, define this from the recording as **at least two uninterrupted seconds with all three atrium identity cues simultaneously within the player camera and no full occlusion**, regardless of whether the player later understands them. Code `opportunity visible` separately from `apparently attended`; do not decide adequacy from the player's answer. Allow **five minutes of unassisted exploration from the first adequate exposure**. If no such exposure occurs within eight minutes of spawn, stop the scored window and record a route/attention outcome rather than a cognitive failure. Do not force the camera toward a landmark. Record screen/video with consent, time-stamped field notes, and exact spontaneous statements.

Record, without inferring understanding from gaze alone:

- whether the player recognizes H0 as the place they occupy and points out H1/H2 details from earlier visits;
- whether they look across all three at once, compare upper–atrium–lower relationships, move laterally, or backtrack to test real depth;
- what they believe moved, remained, or was duplicated; whether they test that belief by a self-chosen movement or prediction;
- which alternative first appears (one changing layout, ordinary wings, screens, a loading trick, or something else), and exactly what observation changes it;
- time to first complete sightline, first identity recognition, first expressed hypothesis, and any explicit revision of the pre-reveal sketch;
- missed cues, occlusion, control trouble, motion discomfort, or any moderator intervention.

Silence is not automatically confusion. If the unassisted window ends without a volunteered interpretation, ask once: **“What do you make of what you have seen?”** Record this as *neutral-elicited*, separate from spontaneous discovery. Do not turn the question into “How many facilities are there?” or list possible explanations.

### Lock the first-view answer, then probe

Before the player visits the second viewpoint, ask for a fresh blank sketch: **“Where do the places you visited earlier fit in what you are seeing now?”** Then ask **“Has anything changed about your earlier sketch? What made you change it, or why not?”** These are neutral elaborations; do not praise, confirm, paraphrase toward the answer, or reveal labels. Lock the primary A-first/B-first score at this point. A correct theory supplied only after a leading prompt or debrief is not an independent pass.

After the primary score is locked, the player may freely visit the other viewpoint. Record whether it strengthens, reverses, or fails to change the model. This **crossover is diagnostic**, not another independent first-reveal observation: someone who learned the answer at A carries it to B, and vice versa. Finally ask: “What else could explain what you saw?” “What would you expect to remain if you left and returned?” “Which observation would distinguish your explanation from that alternative?” These counterfactuals test the model without teaching it, but cannot retroactively create a spontaneous pass.

## 4. Predeclared scoring — concepts, not vocabulary

Score the first-view response from the participant's own sketch, pointing, words, and self-directed tests. Use a second coder who has not been told which viewpoint the participant saw when possible; resolve disagreements from the recorded evidence. Keep spontaneous and neutral-elicited scores separate.

| Level | Required player model |
| --- | --- |
| **Full understanding** | Independently maps the current place and **both previously visited identities** onto three distinct, simultaneously present **fixed physical stacks**; cites **at least one prior-identity cue and one coexistence/topology cue** (co-visibility, separation, vertical continuity, parallax, or stable occlusion); and, **if they held it**, explains why their earlier one-place-changing model cannot account for this view. Exact words such as “facility,” “copy,” or “quantum” are not required. The later counterfactual answer corroborates but does not determine this locked first-view score. |
| **Partial understanding** | Correctly recognizes several prior details or simultaneous structures, but cannot map all three prior places, does not establish fixed separation, retains an unresolved one-place theory, or offers the correct count as an unsupported guess. Also code the model reached only after a neutral question as `elicited`, not spontaneous. |
| **Failure for the core question** | After adequate unassisted exposure, continues to treat the view as one changing place, interchangeable images, unrelated ordinary rooms, or a random effect; cannot identify the earlier locations; or can only repeat the intended conclusion after the moderator explains it. Preserve the specific misconception, not just “failed.” |
| **Indeterminate/invalid** | No adequate view, technical rendering/access failure, answer-bearing help, spoiler exposure, or missing prior-exposure prerequisite. Do not put these sessions in the comprehension denominator. Report them separately. |

Do not fail on the noun **“wing”** alone. A player may call three fixed coexisting facilities “wings” and still have the correct spatial model. “Ordinary multi-wing building” is a substantive failure only when their map reduces the sight to three adjacent rooms of the *same* previously understood place and cannot connect earlier visits or reject the old changing-place account. Likewise, an early correct guess without evidence is partial until the player can justify or predict from it.

For analysis, record four component codes alongside the overall level: `I` prior identity mapping (none / some / all three), `C` simultaneous fixed coexistence (no / uncertain / yes), `R` revision of baseline one-place belief (no / tentative / evidence-backed), and `E` physical evidence cited (none / one type / both identity and coexistence types). Two decorative identity details do **not** make `E=both`. The overall “full” designation requires `I=all three`, `C=yes`, `R=evidence-backed` **when the old belief was held**, and `E=both`; for an early-correct-baseline player, report stable correct understanding without claiming a reversal.

## 5. A/B comparison and failure diagnosis

Compare **first assigned adequate views only** among equal-exposure participants, while reporting all assigned-route sessions and their actual first revealing views. Report raw full/partial/failure counts for A-first and B-first, split by baseline model and by spontaneous versus neutral-elicited response; also report time from adequate view to evidence-backed revision, missed-cue frequency, and the explanations each view leaves alive. B's cleaner rendered composition is a hypothesis, not a result. A's bridge-like gallery is a specific competing interpretation to watch for. A free-exploration cohort can reveal which route people naturally choose, but cannot isolate viewpoint quality from self-selection.

Do not select a winner from one compelling quote or from technical sightline counts. A viewpoint is provisionally clearer only if repeated first-view sessions show more independent evidence-backed revisions **and** fewer unresolved alternative explanations, without more access or comfort failures. If the pattern is mixed or baseline beliefs differ between groups, the comparison is inconclusive; balance or revise the study before changing geometry.

| What the player does | Primary diagnosis to check | Distinguishing evidence to review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cannot map H0/H1/H2 to earlier visits | **Identity-memory or prior-exposure failure** | Did they ever inspect each persistent cue before reveal? Can they recognize a cue in a still frame afterward without being told its site? Do not call this a coexistence-law failure yet. |
| Counts three visible spaces but treats them as one site changing rooms | **Scale/topology inference gap** | Did they notice separate upper/lower stacks and uninterrupted co-visibility? Can they explain what remains physically present when one is out of view? |
| Insists on another quantum state of one facility | **Old model survives contradictory evidence** | Which simultaneously visible feature do they discount, and why? Did the view appear continuous or like a scene load/screen? |
| Calls them an ordinary multi-wing building | **Possible false positive or genuine correct topology** | Do they map *all prior visits* to fixed, separate stacks and abandon the one-changing-place model? If yes, terminology is harmless. If not, distinguish adjacency from remembered identity. |
| Thinks the view is a screen, mirror, or backdrop | **Depth/physicality unreadable** | Did movement produce noticeable parallax and stable occlusion? Was a gallery frame mistaken for a display surface? |
| Never reaches or attends to the assigned view | **Navigation/attention problem** | Route time, camera trace, accessible path, instructions, and control assistance. Mark first-view comprehension indeterminate, not a cognitive failure. |

If they can state the answer only after an author supplies it, the mystery is not yet proven—even though every technical check passes.
