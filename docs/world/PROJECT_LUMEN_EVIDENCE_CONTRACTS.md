# Project Lumen — Evidence Contracts

**Status:** Design contract for future evidence authoring; not a claim that cameras, room-scale modules, exchanges, or endings are implemented. This document adds no rule to the [spatial quantum proposal](PROJECT_LUMEN_SPATIAL_QUANTUM_RULES.md). It translates the [cognitive framework](PROJECT_LUMEN_COGNITIVE_MYSTERY_FRAMEWORK.md), [knowledge progression](PROJECT_LUMEN_KNOWLEDGE_PROGRESSION.md), [ending proposal](PROJECT_LUMEN_ENDING_STRUCTURE.md), and [critical review](PROJECT_LUMEN_MYSTERY_REVIEW.md) into things a player could actually test. Human discoverability is **UNVALIDATED**.

## What counts as evidence

- An **observation** is a perceivable before/after fact, not a designer's interpretation. A player must be able to return to a fixed reference, recognize what persisted, and repeat or contrast a relevant event. One offscreen change alone is not proof.
- A **contradiction** must make a plausible earlier model predict the wrong result while changing as few conditions as possible. It cannot depend on an unread log, hidden completion flag, unexplained delay, exact camera angle, or a control named after its solution.
- A **final understanding** is a useful prediction about another lawful situation. The game does not check whether the player can recite it. Early discovery and early escape remain possible.
- Proposed room exchange means two bounded, distinct, material modules **M** and **N** reciprocally occupy fixed sockets **P** and **Q**. The hub, socket beds, circulation, and exterior ground do not travel. “A room has a quantum state” is not free-form rewriting of the map.
- Proving that M and N exist and exchange **does not prove how they were originally duplicated**. A shared pre-incident flaw and later divergent damage can motivate historical investigation; a dated plan or testimony may corroborate origin. Historical certainty is not a prerequisite for the spatial solution.

The seven contracts below are knowledge dependencies, not seven mandatory rooms or a prescribed encounter order. Existing Awakening and prototype evidence can serve K1–K3; K4 has a technical assembly precedent; K5–K7 are future design proposals. Every required condition is an authoring and test obligation, not an instruction to add a new mechanic.

## K1 — Quantum objects relocate when unobserved

1. **Initial player observation.** A distinctive Cube is present while watched. After a natural occlusion or a look away, it is absent from that position and can be found at another physically recognizable placement; continuous sight holds it. Its identity is recognizable across the change.
2. **Natural wrong interpretation.** The Cube follows a timer, is remote-controlled by equipment, or disappears because of a scripted first encounter.
3. **Contradictory evidence.** From the same accessible vantage, waiting while keeping the Cube in view does not produce the same change; removing sight permits another change without using a control. Repeating the contrast at a later opportunity breaks the one-off-script explanation.
4. **Final understanding.** Direct observation of a participating object holds its current placement; an unobserved interval permits, but does not command, a lawful relocation.
5. **Required puzzle conditions.** A readable object identity, at least two safe placements, a broad and intentional way to gain/lose sight, a way to re-find the Cube, and repeatable access. The apparent result must not coincide only with an unrelated door or switch operation. No text needs to name “observation.”

## K2 — Observed positions cannot become destinations

1. **Initial player observation.** A previously available empty placement remains empty while the player watches it; after attention moves elsewhere and all relevant views are removed, the object can appear there.
2. **Natural wrong interpretation.** The object prefers one pad, follows a fixed cycle, or is being selected by nearby machinery. A failed arrival may also look like random chance.
3. **Contradictory evidence.** With the current object already unobserved and other observer states unchanged, watching only one candidate prevents that candidate; releasing that view restores its eligibility. A comparison involving another legal candidate separates exclusion from a global “nothing can move” effect. A binary two-socket case may demonstrate only a veto, not destination selection, and must not be presented as stronger proof.
4. **Final understanding.** Current-state observation can hold an object, while observation of a *candidate placement* can exclude that placement. Neither sight nor Beam selects a destination by itself.
5. **Required puzzle conditions.** A genuinely empty candidate visible without seeing any member of the current object; a known alternative or repeatable comparison; controlled Beam/camera states; fixed return access; and enough unobstructed geometry for the player to distinguish current hold from candidate veto. If this sightline is impossible in a proposed occupied P/Q exchange, K2 must be evidenced elsewhere, not faked with a scripted room result.

## K3 — Beam and artificial observation behave like player vision

1. **Initial player observation.** An active Beam visibly reaches a quantum member after the player has stepped out of direct view. The expected relocation stops; redirecting or blocking the Beam's actual path permits it again.
2. **Natural wrong interpretation.** The Beam powers a clamp, charges the Cube, controls a destination, or is simply a colored lock on nearby equipment.
3. **Contradictory evidence.** With Beam power and unrelated equipment held constant, only whether its unobstructed line reaches the *participating surface* changes the result. Aimed at a candidate rather than a current member, it can veto that candidate without holding the current placement. The player's equivalent direct view produces the same class of effect.
4. **Final understanding.** A real artificial sightline is another observer under the same current/candidate rule. The Beam does not move a quantum object or directly open a quantum door.
5. **Required puzzle conditions.** Visible source, aim, path, occluder, and target; an accessible player position that does not accidentally observe the target during the comparison; safe and repeatable release/rearm; and no simultaneous power change that could be mistaken for a machine command. An effect that occurs merely because a power switch was flipped does not establish K3.

## K4 — A supported observer can travel with a quantum object

1. **Initial player observation.** From fixed ground, a bounded platform/cradle changes placement while unobserved. Later, after standing wholly on its floor and losing sight of its participating surfaces, the player finds both the same identifiable structure and their own position relative to it at another placement.
2. **Natural wrong interpretation.** A hidden elevator, a player teleport volume, or an interaction activated by boarding took the player somewhere; alternatively, merely touching the object is enough.
3. **Contradictory evidence.** Boarding alone while still seeing a participating member does not release it. Partial contact or standing across a fixed/moving seam does not produce a lawful passenger move. Under comparable release conditions, full support plus broad concealment allows the object and player to arrive together, with the object's distinctive non-Cube features and the player's local stance preserved. Fixed landmarks verify that the world did not move around them.
4. **Final understanding.** A fully supported passenger belongs to a particular object's lawful transition, but remains an observer. The passenger's own sight, the Beam, and later cameras must all lose the relevant direct view; there is no special “inside the room” exemption.
5. **Required puzzle conditions.** Legible support and fixed-floor seams; a comfortable, non-pixel-perfect way to conceal *all* participating current geometry; equivalent arrival cover and a safe dismount at each lawful placement; no collision or newly visible member at arrival; a fixed route to compare before and after; and a recoverable empty-object move. If any sightline or support state makes this only work by accident, K4 is not ready for player use.

## K5 — Cameras are observers

1. **Initial player observation.** A visible facility camera is powered and aimed into an area where a known quantum object or member remains unusually stable. Earlier “stubborn” states may have been encountered before the player connected them to the lens.
2. **Natural wrong interpretation.** The camera is security dressing, its console is a remote lock, the camera power circuit runs the machinery, or someone watching a monitor holds the object. A recording may seem to preserve observation after the camera is gone.
3. **Contradictory evidence.** With player view and Beam reach held absent and the camera still powered, a lens aimed through a clear path at the participating member holds the state; the *same powered camera* aimed at fixed structure or blocked by fixed geometry does not. The effect follows the lens's direct view, not the console action. An unpowered lens and stored footage do not hold a state. Repeating the contrast at another known quantum member lets the player reinterpret the earlier hold, instead of learning a one-off final switch.
4. **Final understanding.** A live, unobstructed camera sensor is an artificial direct observer under the existing rule. A monitor, archive, photograph, and human knowledge of a recording are not substitute observers.
5. **Required puzzle conditions.** Inspectable camera mount, power state, orientation, and occlusion path; a repeatable camera-only A/B test in which no player/Beam sightline changes inadvertently; a known object response visible from fixed access after each trial; and rearm/recovery between trials. Camera controls, if used, must remain plausible service equipment, not an “open exit” button. No invisible CCTV coverage may be required to explain failure. Candidate-only camera exclusion may be tested only where its geometry is demonstrable; an occupied pair does not automatically supply it.

## K6 — Quantum states belong to objects and relationships, not locations

1. **Initial player observation.** A Cube sits in an apparent machine frame; an ordinary door state, repair, instrument, or attached architectural detail appears associated with a particular placement. At first, the fixed facility seems to operate the device at that spot.
2. **Natural wrong interpretation.** The Cube commands a machine; fixed circuitry synchronizes two doors; a socket remembers its setting; the destination room was rebuilt or redressed.
3. **Contradictory evidence.** A distinctive non-Cube member crosses the apparent machine/architecture seam with the Cube. After an empty relocation, its repair and manually changed door or instrument state appear with that *same bounded structure* at another placement. Fixed socket contacts, scars, and conduits remain behind; a different occupant can meet the same contact and produce a different physical relationship without inheriting the first object's internal state. Seeing only a non-Cube member can hold the current placement under the ordinary observation rule.
4. **Final understanding.** Identity and internal state travel with the bounded quantum object. Some effects depend on which persistent object is aligned with which fixed surroundings; a coordinate alone is not “the state.” This is the bridge from a moving Cube to a misidentified piece of architecture, not a new global teleport rule.
5. **Required puzzle conditions.** At least one trackable non-Cube feature and one ordinary, independently changed attached state; fixed marks/contacts visible before and after; return access to both placements; and a contrast that rules out synchronized control. The traveling boundary must be physically readable without color coding or a log telling the player what belongs to it. If the only remembered feature is the Cube, the assembly inference is unsupported.

## K7 — Entire spaces can have quantum states; distinct facility spaces can exchange

1. **Initial player observation.** A familiar doorway or apparent room shows a small mismatch: equipment differs, an old damage mark seems absent, or a doorway relationship is wrong. A second near-matching space can be found elsewhere, but the player may not yet see the two as related.
2. **Natural wrong interpretation.** Loose objects were moved, equipment was repaired, local quantum instability altered a room, identical facility construction confused navigation, or the whole building shifted. These are productive hypotheses if the world lets the player test them; they become confusion if distinct identities and fixed references are unreadable.
3. **Contradictory evidence.** The player can track **two** persistent shell identities: M carries its particular repair and door/equipment state; N carries a different later mark. Fixed P/Q thresholds, contacts, hub connections, and exterior ground retain their own scars. After a lawful interval in which both room shells and both candidate placements are unobserved, M and N occupy the opposite sockets *together*. Their identifying features and internal states travel with them, while fixed references do not. A view of a participating wall or a live camera line can prevent a repeat under controlled conditions. Neither “contents were moved,” “one room changed décor,” nor “the whole building moved” predicts all of that.
4. **Final understanding.** “The room did not change; I entered a different spatial copy” means a different persistent room module now occupies the same fixed approach. M and N are material inhabited spaces whose placements and relationships to fixed circulation differ. They exchange; neither is conjured on demand, and the whole facility does not become quantum. The historical cause of their near-duplication remains a separate, optional question.
5. **Required puzzle conditions.** Two simultaneously existent, distinguishable modules; a pair of unchanging fixed sockets and reachable comparison points; recognizable whole-shell boundaries rather than just similar interiors; a reciprocal, not sequential or one-sided, change; safe visibility/release and independent rearm for both; recovery from either legal resting state without a hidden knowledge gate; and an ordinary route whose connectivity visibly depends on which room occupies the socket. If these spatial conditions cannot be satisfied, a repeated-room visual trick is not acceptable evidence for K7.

## Spatial duplication: disclosure without an early answer

The evidence should accumulate as *revisable anomalies*, not as a sequence of labeled revelations:

| Player's working model | Perceivable evidence that keeps it reasonable | Later test that model cannot explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Someone moved objects.” | A familiar work surface has different loose-looking equipment. | A wall/floor seam, old repair, and attached equipment share the change; fixed socket marks do not. |
| “The room's equipment changed.” | The facility is damaged and partly powered. | An ordinary altered state and a unique repair recur together at a second fixed socket while the first socket retains its fixed contact. |
| “Local quantum instability changed this room.” | Awakening already proved quantum relocation. | M and N can be identified at the same time, then trade sockets while preserving their *different* internal histories. |
| “The room teleported, or the whole building moved.” | One module vanishes from an approach. | The other persistent module occupies its old socket in the same interval; fixed hub/exterior references never move, and a fixed route connects differently afterward. |
| “I entered a different spatial copy.” | Two identities, reciprocal exchange, and fixed references jointly predict which shell will greet the player at P/Q. | A later controlled exchange must confirm the prediction at both sockets. The explanation remains testable, not a phrase supplied by a document. |

Do not place every decisive mark, both socket views, and the causal camera contrast in one immediately legible panorama; that collapses the mystery. Do not separate them so completely that the player cannot revisit and compare. Physical wear can invite an early suspicion, and a perceptive player may solve early. The design must permit that rather than insert a lock. Optional records can help date the duplication and humanize its cost, but may not be the only source of either room identity or exchange proof.

## Camera mystery: reinterpretation, not a key

Before a camera controls a consequential final hold, the player needs one *diagnosable earlier anomaly*: a known quantum object remains stable even after player and Beam views seem removed, with a plausible visible lens in the environment. Later access to a camera-only comparison should allow the player to return, recognize that the lens had the missing view, and predict the effect of its real aim or occlusion. The decisive comparison is **live lens on member versus live lens off member**, with the rest of the observer configuration fixed. “Camera off, door opens” is insufficient because it is equally consistent with an electrical lock. Stored images should remain visible as evidence of history, never as lingering observers.

The camera cannot be a secret omniscient veto. If the player cannot inspect what it sees, or if turning it changes unrelated power and sightlines simultaneously, the correct interpretation is not discoverable. A camera aimed at an empty candidate may exclude it under the same rule, but this is not necessary to establish K5, and should not be asserted where the joint room geometry offers no isolated candidate view.

## True ending: evidence before route, confirmation after

The intended prediction is concrete: with the fixed camera directly observing M at Q, the proposed reciprocal exchange is held. If that direct view and all other relevant player/Beam/camera views are removed and both modules are eligible, **M can occupy P while N occupies Q**. M's existing doorway then aligns with a **fixed** exterior spur that was never a magic wall; N's different solid boundary explains why the P approach previously seemed sealed. The player's route follows this changed physical relationship. The ordinary exterior C/perimeter escape remains a legitimate outcome, not a failed answer.

Minimum evidence available *before* attempting the true route:

1. The player can distinguish M and N by persistent features, not just by coordinates.
2. The player can locate P and Q relative to a fixed hub/exterior reference and see what does **not** travel.
3. Prior controlled observations make it reasonable to predict that an unobstructed live lens can hold a quantum member, and that release alone does not guarantee a preferred state.
4. M's door and the fixed spur can be compared spatially closely enough to predict alignment at P; this must not be revealed solely after success.
5. Both legal resting states have safe fixed access, rearm, and recovery, so a failed prediction can be investigated rather than brute-forced through a hidden wait.

The resulting view from fixed ground should confirm M's identifying mark and doorway at P, N's mark at Q, and unchanged fixed socket/hub references. That is the spatial answer. Optional records may explain *why* there are two historically related rooms or what happened to the staff, but understanding that history is not a required flag. A player may lawfully reach the route by luck; the game must not pretend to measure cognition. The design succeeds when an unbriefed player can state the P/Q and doorway prediction **before** the final release, and the corrected architectural boundary makes the true route feel materially different from the already genuine C escape.

## Decision gates before detailed room design

- **Geometry:** Paper and technical proofs must cover both P/Q occupations; every player, Beam, and camera current/candidate sightline; independent rearm; full passenger support/concealment; arrival visibility; fixed circulation; and recovery. The two-occupied-socket candidate-only test remains an open geometric question, not assumed evidence.
- **Causality:** At least one camera-only comparison and one non-Cube structural observation contrast must be repeatable without changing unrelated power or player sight. A controlled state-following-object comparison must rule out synchronized equipment.
- **Cognition:** In unbriefed tests, ask what players saw, what stayed fixed, what they expect at the other socket, and what would change if the camera looked elsewhere. Do not ask whether they “understood quantum rooms.” Watch for accidental camera-switch solutions, wrong-room navigation confusion, and post-hoc explanations.
- **Payoff:** Test whether players see the true route as a corrected picture of the facility, not simply a second exit. Physical exchange can prove the present rule; the proposed accident origin and emotional meaning need separate evidence and should not be smuggled in as mechanically proven facts.

Until those gates pass, this is an evidence architecture, not a validated discovery path.
