# Triple Coexistence Cognitive Probe — Pilot and Session Checklists

**Purpose:** verify that the blind test can measure a player's spatial model without supplying the answer. This is not an evaluation of the puzzle. The prototype's technical checks have passed; **human comprehension is UNVALIDATED**. Use the [blind playtest run sheet](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_COGNITIVE_BLIND_PLAYTEST.md) for eligibility, exact scoring definitions, and first-wave decisions. Run two separate, unscored logistics pilots (one assigned A-first, one B-first) before any scored session. Do not pool pilot interpretations with the scored sample.

## 1. Pre-test checklist — complete before each pilot

### Build and scene

- [ ] Record session ID, date, Godot version, executable/build identifier, and the exact project/scene version. Freeze the same build and settings for both pilots; record any change before a new pilot.
- [ ] Confirm `res://scenes/tests/triple_coexistence_cognitive_probe.tscn` launches **directly in a game window**. Do not use F5, which opens Awakening. Keep the editor, scene tree, filenames, console/validator prints, maps, and study documents off the participant display.
- [ ] On an operator-only dry run, confirm H0 rear-pocket spawn; H0 → H1 → H2 → H0 receiver handoffs; H2 can be recognized by the moderator before its source receiver; H0 return reaches the front pocket; both A gallery-ramp and B ground approaches remain walkable. Confirm no clear triple-stack view leaks during the three visits. This is a process check, not a new gameplay validation claim.
- [ ] Confirm the final fork permits the prewritten A “path toward the ramp” or B “path that stays on the ground” instruction **before** a full proof view. Do not substitute left/right based on the player's facing. If the branch cannot be named neutrally, do not run a scored A/B comparison.
- [ ] Close and relaunch the scene for each participant; there is no local restart key. Verify the fresh H0 spawn before the participant sees the screen.

### Input, room, and observers

- [ ] Verify movement and mouse look, Escape cursor release, and left-click recapture. Keep a single verbatim controls response available **only if asked**. No interaction key is required.
- [ ] Match monitor, resolution, FOV, brightness, audio level, controls, chair, and room lighting. Record accessibility or motion-comfort adjustments; stop if the participant requests it.
- [ ] Seat the observer where notes can be taken without pointing at the display. Assign one moderator to speak and, if available, one silent observer to timestamp events. Neither should react visibly to a theory or discovery.
- [ ] Prepare the same nonverbal prior observation experience for every participant in the scored wave; pilots must exercise that exact setup. Do not explain quantum, observation, facilities, copies, or the research hypothesis.
- [ ] Keep participant briefing and all previous sketches, clips, findings, and debriefs out of sight and hearing of the next participant. Screen for prior exposure before showing the game.

### Recording and materials

- [ ] Obtain recording consent. Test screen capture **and** participant speech with a short playback; confirm cursor movement, scene detail, and spoken words are recoverable. Ensure capture does not show editor/debug information.
- [ ] Synchronize the recording clock and observation-sheet clock. Prepare two blank, ID-only sketch sheets (H2 baseline and first-view account), pens, a timer, and a timestamped event sheet. Do not put a facility diagram or answer vocabulary on the sheets.
- [ ] Prepare an intervention log for every moderator utterance, route instruction, control hint, accidental leak, technical issue, and participant discomfort. Record exact wording, time, and who initiated it.
- [ ] Conceal the A/B assignment until after the H2 sketch. For the two pilots, use distinct unbriefed participants and test each assigned route once. Pilots test logistics and measurement quality; their conclusions are not evidence that the mystery works.

## 2. Pilot/session execution checklist

Use the [run sheet's verbatim script](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_COGNITIVE_BLIND_PLAYTEST.md#3-verbatim-moderator-script-and-session-sequence). Check boxes in order; do not improvise explanatory prompts. The two pilots should run as much like real sessions as possible.

### Intake and initial visits

- [ ] Screen neutrally: “Have you seen or discussed any material from this Project Lumen test before today? What did you see?” Record the answer and apply the run sheet's eligibility rules. Obtain consent and assign an anonymous ID.
- [ ] Give only the run sheet's neutral opening instruction and the fixed prior playable experience. If asked for a goal, say “Explore whatever interests you.” Explain controls only if requested. Do **not** say that the player will see three places, that a proof is coming, or what conclusion is expected.
- [ ] Launch the cognitive scene. Timestamp H0, H1, and H2 arrivals; note which repair features and upper support the player actually sees, the player's own remarks (`T0`), receiver searching, assistance, and any early sightline leak. Say nothing routine during these visits.
- [ ] If stalled in a receiver for two minutes, use only the run sheet's neutral “What are you trying to do?” / “What have you tried?” Record the time and exact response. Do not identify the source receiver or destination. If navigation assistance becomes necessary, mark that portion assisted.

### Before and during the proof

- [ ] **At H2, before its source receiver:** ask the participant to stop moving; do not open an editor or imply an in-game pause control. Ask exactly: “Could you sketch your route so far and talk me through it?” Follow with “What made you draw it this way?” and “If you returned to the first area, what would you expect to find?” Preserve the sketch and the last pre-proof model (`T1`) before allowing movement.
- [ ] After the T1 account is complete, ask “How confident are you in that account?” Record the player's own words; do not confirm or challenge them. In the pilots, note whether even this question changes the account. Use this confidence prompt at the same point in every scored session only if the pilots show it is non-leading.
- [ ] Let the participant enter the final receiver and return to H0. Ask **no question** before the exterior view. Timestamp any return-triggered inference (`T2`); it is not an A/B viewpoint success. Do not tell the player to turn.
- [ ] At the final fork, if pilot-verified and before any full view, read only the assigned route sentence from the run sheet. Record whether it was delivered, whether the participant followed it, and the **actual** first revealing approach (A, B, or en route). Never orient the camera or point.
- [ ] Timestamp the first adequate view and total first-approach exposure. Log whether all three identity cues were on screen for at least two uninterrupted seconds, what the player seemed to inspect, any movement/parallax, and the first **spontaneous** explanation. If there is no adequate view within eight minutes of H0 return, record a navigation/attention outcome rather than a comprehension failure.
- [ ] Allow up to five unassisted minutes after the first adequate view. If there is no interpretation, the permitted neutral prompt is “What do you make of what you have seen?” If the player is about to cross to the other approach sooner, stop for the first-view sketch and record the shortened exposure; do not treat it as a comparable A/B dose.
- [ ] **Before second-view crossover:** ask exactly: “Please sketch what you think you have seen. Talk me through it.” Then “What led you to draw it this way?” Preserve the first-view account (`T3`) and exact words. Do not praise, correct, paraphrase, or introduce “change,” “copies,” or a count.
- [ ] After the T3 account is complete, ask “How confident are you in that account?” Record the player's own words. As with T1, keep this prompt only if the pilots show it does not steer the explanation. Lock the primary record before the second viewpoint or diagnostic questions.

### After the primary record is locked

- [ ] Permit exploration of the other viewpoint. Use only the run sheet's diagnostic questions: “What else might explain what you saw?” “What would you expect if you retraced your route?” “What observation would help you decide?” Label all of this `T4`; it cannot retroactively create a T3 success.
- [ ] End the recording, then debrief. Ask the participant not to tell future participants what they saw. Store recordings and sketches under the anonymous ID, separate from consent details. Relaunch a clean scene before the next pilot/session.

**Timing is event-based, not a speed test:** the two-minute receiver assistance trigger, eight-minute H0-return navigation threshold, and five-minute first-view window are the run sheet's measurement limits. Record actual durations and any early crossover; do not hurry exploration or extend one participant's opportunity selectively.

## 3. Data capture and pilot-only measurement check

For each pilot, confirm that the record could support these distinctions, even though the pilot is **not scored for puzzle success**:

| Required record | Minimum observable evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Before proof | `T0` volunteered words; `T1` H2 sketch, explanation, return prediction, confidence, and whether a model first appeared *because of* the sketch. Record one-changing, multiple-fixed, uncertain, or other without suggesting any option to the participant. |
| During proof | `T2` insight before view; assigned route versus actual first view; adequate-view frame/time, exposure duration, visible identity cues, gaze/movement/parallax, first spontaneous words, and all moderator interventions. |
| After proof | `T3` first-view sketch and account **before crossover**; whether the player connects prior visits to visible structures and uses physical evidence, or merely guesses a count; confidence in the player's own words. `T4` alternative explanations and retrace predictions remain diagnostic only. |
| Source of conclusion | Mark **spontaneous**, **neutral-question elicited**, **sketch elicited**, **pre-proof**, or **moderator-influenced** with exact timestamps. If the moderator supplied the answer, do not treat the later answer as independent. |

After each pilot, have the moderator and a second reader, if available, independently reconstruct the `T0` → `T1` → `T2` → `T3` sequence from the recording **without being told what the participant “really meant.”** Disagreement that cannot be resolved from audio, video, and sketches is a measurement problem. Record the participant's own confidence wording; do not infer confidence from tone or speed.

## 4. Pilot decision: is the *procedure* ready?

The two logistics pilots pass only if **both** produce an interpretable timeline: clean scene reset and full visit order; a neutral H2 baseline captured before return; no clear early proof leak; recording and sketches recoverable; moderator help and confidence prompts logged; A/B route choice deliverable before the proof; actual first view and exposure measurable; first-view account locked before crossover; and no answer-bearing intervention. This says only that the experiment can measure the cognitive transition, **not** that either participant made it.

Change the **protocol before scored recruitment** if any pilot reveals: ambiguous A/B instructions or uncontrolled first-view exposure, failure to identify the H2 pause point, unusable audio/video, leading confidence or follow-up questions, missing timestamps that blur a volunteered statement with a sketch-elicited one, inconsistent primer/help, early proof visibility, or an account that cannot be assigned to a pre- or post-view timestamp. Revise wording, moderator timing, recording, or eligibility rules; document the revision and rerun the affected pilot. An unspoken belief before the sketch is inherently unknown; do not claim the sketch caused it merely because it first appeared at T1. Do not quietly adjust the prototype, geometry, or scoring after seeing an answer.

If the fixed scene itself prevents a clean measurement despite protocol adjustments, **stop the scored test and report that limitation** for a separate design decision. A participant's wrong model, early correct model, low confidence, or refusal to commit is **not** a pilot logistics failure.

## 5. Contamination watchlist

- Moderator names the goal (“prove there are three facilities”), gestures toward a cue, moves the mouse, confirms a theory, or asks a leading count/change question.
- Participant sees this checklist, the answer, another participant's drawing/result, developer labels/console, or a previous session's recording; participants discuss the test between sessions.
- Different prior observation experiences, display settings, hints, or exposure windows are given to A and B; assigned route is mistaken for the actual first view.
- A baseline sketch, confidence prompt, or debrief question creates an interpretation that is later misreported as spontaneous; an H0-return deduction is credited to A/B; a second-view answer is credited to the first view.
- A screen/recording failure, early sightline leak, or assisted navigation is omitted from the session log.

For every incident, write down **what happened, when, and which measurement it affects**. Preserve the raw session record; classify it as a diagnostic or contaminated case under the run sheet rather than deleting it. Technical validation remains a separate prior result; human comprehension cannot be marked validated until actual unbriefed, uncontaminated sessions are analyzed.
