# Awakening Chamber First-Player Playtest

Use a participant who has not seen the prototype rooms, design documents, or prior Project Lumen footage. Launch the project with F5. Explain only the standard movement, mouse-look, E, and Escape controls. Do not mention the Cube, observation, relocation, service handle, Beam, plate, or bulkhead condition.

## Observe silently

Record timestamps for:

- First look at the Cube.
- First voluntary movement away from the spawn position.
- First time the Cube relocates.
- First clear sign the player noticed the relocation.
- First deliberate attempt to reproduce the behavior.
- First spoken or demonstrated observation hypothesis.
- First inspection of the vacated containment apparatus.
- First notice of the opened maintenance approach and first entry into it.
- First sight of the service handle from inside the maintenance pocket.
- First attempt to use the rotary service control.
- Local facility restoration; note whether the player expects the bulkhead to open immediately.
- First notice of the automatically started Beam, its fixed breaker, and the empty load platform.
- First Beam-OFF toggle and whether the player connects it to the Cube's available destinations.
- First Cube occupation of the load platform; whether it appears intentional or accidental.
- First loss of that load and whether the player notices the plate and door-release control depower.
- First successful use of the Beam to hold a loaded Cube while turning away.
- First notice of the left-side bulkhead release, first attempt while dark, first attempt while powered, and whether E use is inferred.
- First visible movement of the bulkhead after an actual release interaction.
- First notice of the opened bulkhead and first entry into the hub (TASK-030).

Also note the player's route among the containment floor, maintenance bay, observation booth, facility control station, gurney, and bulkhead. Record whether the player mistakes passive cameras, analogue instruments, or paperwork for controls.

## Comprehension evidence

Evidence of emerging understanding includes:

- Returning to the Cube after noticing it absent from a previous position.
- Comparing a short glance away with a longer break in line of sight.
- Keeping the Cube in view intentionally to test whether it stays still.
- Using walls, the rack, or a workstation to break sight deliberately.
- Predicting that the Cube will remain while watched.
- Investigating the newly opened maintenance side without prompting.
- Comparing what happens when the Beam sees an empty location versus the Cube itself.
- Repeating an experiment after a plate load is lost, rather than treating it as failure requiring reset.
- Reaching the fixed breaker while still watching the Cube on the platform.

Potential confusion includes:

- Attributing movement only to proximity, walking, or elapsed time.
- Treating the analogue sensor response as an objective signal.
- Searching for a highlighted destination or missing inventory item.
- Repeatedly pressing E on the Cube or passive observation cameras.
- Missing the opened bulkhead and continuing to search for another opening-room action.
- Finding the handle from the main room without investigating the maintenance side.
- Reading the damaged racks as deliberate maze walls rather than a collapsed work area.
- Assuming that the handle is an exit button and stopping after it powers the inspection system.
- Treating the Beam as a tractor beam, force field, or colored target rather than an observer.
- Missing that the powered Beam initially excludes an empty destination, or failing to consider switching it back on after the Cube arrives there.
- Repeatedly waiting for a random plate landing without manipulating what remains observable.
- Reading the physical load platform as a glowing puzzle pad or mistaking its dial for an instruction.
- Expecting plate load alone to open the door, or missing that the door-side release loses power when the Cube leaves.

Do not intervene unless the player is physically stuck, requests control help, or chooses to stop. Let the session continue long enough to identify the actual blocker; do not reveal the rule during timed observation. Record both local restoration and bulkhead release times because these are now separate events.

## Ask afterward

Ask these without confirming a correct answer first:

1. What do you think happened to the Cube?
2. What seemed to make it move or stay still?
3. What did the nearby instrument response mean to you?
4. Why did you operate, or overlook, the rotary control?
5. What changed when facility services returned?
6. Which part of the chamber felt most worth investigating?
7. Did anything feel like an instruction or puzzle marker rather than part of the facility?
8. Did you remember where the Cube was when you first saw it? What made you look at the equipment again?
9. What led you into the service side, and when did you first notice its control?
10. What did using that control change, and why do you think the bulkhead stayed shut at first?
11. What do you think the Beam does when its path is empty? What about when the Cube is there?
12. How did you get the Cube onto the west platform? Did it feel repeatable?
13. What did the platform and door-side control tell you when the Cube arrived, left, or stayed?
14. Why did the bulkhead finally open? Did any equipment feel placed solely as a puzzle exhibit?
15. Did the central work area look damaged or deliberately arranged to block you? Where did the service control seem connected?

Report the participant's words before interpreting them. A successful pass shows deliberate observation testing and a causal explanation in the player's own language; restoration time alone is not sufficient evidence.

## Discovery-pacing regression record

This is a technical and rendered-scene check, not an unbriefed human playtest. Repeat tests 4 and 5 with a first-time participant before judging discovery pacing. The Beam/load addition also requires the observational questions above; technical validation cannot establish those inferences.

| Test | Check | Current evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Watched Cube | Keep direct sight on the Cube. | Automated: initial state and every resting state stay put while watched. |
| 2. Brief look-away | Reobserve before the shared hidden-grace window completes. | Automated: no relocation after the interrupted glance. |
| 3. Sustained loss | Break sight during ordinary exploration. | Automated: one systemic move occurs after the 0.05-second lead-in plus retained 0.2-second candidate grace; exploration routes remain walkable. |
| 4. Accidental first move | Return to the apparatus without searching for a control. | Rendered spawn/vacated views: the access opens but the handle does not face the room. Human reaction unvalidated. |
| 5. Maintenance discovery | Enter the vacated service approach. | Rendered player-height entry and handle views; existing interaction ray focuses the control inside. Human discoverability unvalidated. |
| 6. Destinations | Try every noninitial Cube resting place. | Automated capsule sweeps: all leave the service route clear; exterior views do not focus the control. |
| 7. Local restoration | Use E at the handle. | Automated real input: lights, instrument faces, ventilation, Beam and release circuit receive power; emitter starts ON and bulkhead stays closed. |
| 8. Hub transition | Stabilize the Cube on the plate, use E at the powered bulkhead release, then pass into Operations Atrium. | Automated: physical load alone does not open the door; release interaction opens it, and the hub route remains continuous. |
| 9. Return/regression | Explore the hub and return toward Awakening. | Automated: all four sealed wing approaches and the return route remain traversable as intended. |

## Beam and load regression record

| Case | Technical result | Unbriefed human question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beam ON, empty plate | Plate candidate excluded by the existing artificial observer. | Does the player investigate why the Cube does not arrive? |
| Beam OFF, empty plate | Candidate becomes available. Direct body observation of the alternatives can make it uniquely legal from each nonplate state. | Does the player discover a repeatable approach rather than wait randomly? |
| Cube on plate, Beam OFF | Physical plate depresses and door release powers; when sight breaks, normal relocation unloads it and depowers the control. | Is the loss legible and informative? |
| Cube on plate, Beam ON | Breaker can be used while the Cube remains in direct view; Beam then holds it when the player turns away. | Does the player recognize artificial observation as the same rule? |
| Stabilized load and release | The fixed door control stays live; E operates it, and the bulkhead permanently latches open. | Does this read as physical consequence rather than scripted reward? |
| Plate loaded before service power | Service restoration starts the Beam ON and may hold the Cube; the door stays closed until the player uses the powered wall release. | Does this edge case still read as physical causality rather than automatic completion? |
| Failed load and recovery | No reset; after a normal intervening move, observation can select the plate again. | Is recovery intuitive or frustrating? |
