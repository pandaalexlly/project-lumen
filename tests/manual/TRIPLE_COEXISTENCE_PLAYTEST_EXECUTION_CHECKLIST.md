# Triple Coexistence — Playtest Execution Checklist

**Staff-facing operating sheet. Human comprehension: UNVALIDATED.** Copy this checklist for each logistics pilot or formal blind session. It organizes, but does not replace, the [operative cognitive blind run sheet](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_COGNITIVE_BLIND_PLAYTEST.md): that run sheet controls exact participant-facing wording, eligibility, timing, and primary codes. The [pilot checklist](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_COGNITIVE_PILOT_CHECKLIST.md), [session tracking sheet](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_PLAYER_SESSION_TRACKING_SHEET.md), [mental-model scoring revision](../../docs/world/TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_MENTAL_MODEL_SCORING_REVISION.md), and [pilot result review guide](../../docs/world/TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_PILOT_RESULT_REVIEW_GUIDE.md) supply the detailed preparation, raw record, scoring, and pilot decision rules. Do not use the older proof-only protocol for this integrated H0 → H1 → H2 → H0 test.

## 1. Pre-session preparation

### Build and test environment

- [ ] Confirm the frozen build/version and scene are the planned ones. Launch `res://scenes/tests/triple_coexistence_cognitive_probe.tscn` **directly in a game window**, not F5; keep editor, file/scene names, console, validators, maps, and staff documents off the participant display.
- [ ] On an operator-only check, verify fresh H0 rear-pocket spawn, H0 → H1 → H2 → H0 handoffs, final A gallery-ramp and B ground approaches, and no clear triple-stack leak before the final fork. Do not show this check to the participant.
- [ ] Verify movement, mouse look, Escape cursor release, left-click recapture, display/FOV/brightness, and audio. Use the same settings and the same predeclared nonverbal prior observation experience across comparable sessions; log accessibility/comfort changes.
- [ ] Close and relaunch for **every** participant; there is no local restart key. Confirm the clean H0 spawn before the participant sees the screen.
- [ ] Arrange a quiet test room with a participant-only game view. Seat the silent observer where note-taking cannot cue the player. Only the moderator speaks; neither staff member reacts to a theory.
- [ ] Test screen **and** microphone capture with playback, confirm scene detail and speech are recoverable, and synchronize capture, observation, and sketch timestamps. Obtain consent **before** recording.

### Materials and assignment

- [ ] Have the current run sheet, blank [session tracking sheet](TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_PLAYER_SESSION_TRACKING_SHEET.md), intervention log, timer, two ID-only blank sketch sheets (H2 baseline and first-view account), and recording/consent procedure ready. Keep all staff materials hidden from the player.
- [ ] For pilots: schedule two **different unbriefed** participants, one A-first and one B-first. Mark them unscored and keep them out of the later formal sample.
- [ ] For formal sessions: begin only after both pilots pass process review. Use the locked primer, wording, confidence-prompt policy, settings, and Level 0–3/vertical-unit rubric; follow the run sheet's concealed balanced A/B assignment. Do not switch policies mid-wave.
- [ ] Hide assignment until **after** the H2 baseline sketch. Keep earlier players' sketches, clips, results, and discussion away from the next participant.
- [ ] Screen prior exposure neutrally using the run sheet's exact question; record details and eligibility without listing the expected answer. Assign an anonymous ID. Store consent identity separately from gameplay notes.

## 2. Moderator workflow and session timeline

Use **event checkpoints**, not a target completion time. Setup and consent, prior nonverbal experience, the three visits, the proof-view observation, diagnostic discussion, and storage may vary in duration. The existing two-minute receiver-stall, eight-minute no-view, and five-minute post-view triggers below are measurement rules from the run sheet—not a reason to hurry the player.

| Phase | Moderator action and permitted wording | Observe and record; do not intervene when... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Preparation / introduction** | Obtain consent and perform spoiler screen. Read the run sheet's neutral opening: “You will explore an unfinished first-person space. Play as you normally would. You may say what you notice or think is happening, but you do not have to talk constantly. I may ask you to pause at a few points to make a sketch. Take the time you need.” If asked for a goal: “Explore whatever interests you.” If asked for controls, give only the run sheet's control answer. | Do **not** explain quantum rules, observation, copies, facility count, why the view matters, or the intended conclusion. Do not display a diagram or label. Log every unplanned utterance. |
| **Gameplay: H0, H1, H2 (`T0`)** | Give no routine questions. If asked whether a theory is correct: “What makes you think that?” After **two minutes without progress in a receiver**, use only “What are you trying to do?” or “What have you tried?” under the standardized assistance rule. | Timestamp arrivals/departures, repair-cue and upper-support availability, apparent inspection, spontaneous comments, receiver search, and control help. Do not name a receiver, destination, repair, or route. If navigation help becomes necessary, timestamp and mark assisted. |
| **H2 baseline (`T1`)** | Before its source receiver, ask the player to stop moving. Use exactly: “Could you sketch your route so far and talk me through it?” After they finish: “What made you draw it this way?” and “If you returned to the first area, what would you expect to find?” Use “How confident are you in that account?” **only if the pilot-approved policy requires it at both T1 and T3**. | Save the sketch and words before movement resumes. Distinguish a volunteered T0 theory from one first stated during drawing. Do not ask whether it is “one place” or confirm a model. |
| **H0 return (`T2`)** | Say nothing before the exterior view. Do not tell the player to turn or move the camera. | Timestamp recognition, spontaneous model change, and any premature sightline. A T2 deduction is pre-proof, not an A/B proof success. |
| **First exterior approach** | At the final fork, **only if** the route is still assignable before a full view, read the run sheet's assigned sentence: A, “For this run, take the path toward the ramp first; after that, explore as you like.” B, “For this run, take the path that stays on the ground first; after that, explore as you like.” Then no routine question. | Record assigned route, whether sentence was delivered, **actual** first revealing approach (A/B/en route), first adequate three-atrium-cue view (at least two uninterrupted seconds without full occlusion), first-view exposure, whether all three upper/central/lower relationships were *available* to inspect, what was apparently attended to, parallax/movement, and spontaneous words. Never point or orient the camera. If no adequate view within **eight minutes of H0 return**, record navigation/attention, not comprehension failure. |
| **Observation period / first-view account (`T3`)** | Allow up to **five unassisted minutes after first adequate view**. If no interpretation, the permitted neutral question is “What do you make of what you have seen?” If the player is about to cross to the second approach sooner, capture the first-view account first and log shorter exposure. Before crossover ask: “Please sketch what you think you have seen. Talk me through it.” Then “What led you to draw it this way?” Use the same confidence prompt as at T1 only if approved for both. | Lock sketch, explanation, exact words, timestamps, and exposure **before** second-view exploration. Do not ask how many facilities exist, praise, correct, paraphrase, or imply the prior belief must change. A cue in camera is not proof it was noticed. |
| **Diagnostic discussion (`T4`) / interview** | Only after T3 is locked, permit the other viewpoint. Use only the run sheet's diagnostic questions: “What else might explain what you saw?” “What would you expect if you retraced your route?” “What observation would help you decide?” Debrief and explain the test purpose **after** these questions. | Note alternative explanations and later insight as T4, never a retroactive T3 pass. Ask the participant not to brief future participants. Stop immediately for withdrawal or motion discomfort. |
| **Data storage / post-session review** | End capture, secure files, and reset the scene for the next participant. | Complete the session record from recording and sketches, not memory alone. Log deviations before assigning interpretations. |

## 3. Data handling and version control

- [ ] Use a non-identifying ID such as `TC-P01`/`TC-P02` for pilots or `TC-B001` onward for formal blind sessions. Example filenames: `TC-P01_screen.mp4`, `TC-P01_audio.wav`, `TC-P01_T1_sketch.jpg`, `TC-P01_T3_sketch.jpg`, `TC-P01_tracking.pdf`, and `TC-P01_interventions.csv`. Keep the same ID on every artifact; do not put names on drawings or file paths.
- [ ] Store recordings, sketches, and notes in the team's approved **access-restricted research location**, not the public/project repository. Keep consent and identifying information in a separate restricted location keyed to the anonymous ID. Follow the team's consent, retention, and deletion policy; this checklist does not invent one.
- [ ] Record exact build or commit identifier **read-only**, direct scene path, date, display/input/audio settings, prior experience/primer, assigned A/B route, actual first view, moderator, and any deviation. Do not mix builds or primers in an A/B comparison without explicitly flagging the confound.
- [ ] Preserve raw timestamps, participant quotations, sketches, and visible behavior separately from later analyst codes. Record `T0` voluntary statements, `T1` pre-proof sketch/model, any `T2` correction, locked `T3` first-view account, and `T4` diagnostics. Keep the moderator's exact prompts and help in a separate intervention log with times.
- [ ] Log **atrium-cue opportunity**, **complete-stack opportunity**, apparent attention, and participant explanation as different fields. At later coding, distinguish Level 2 persistent locations from Level 3 complete QER-like upper–Atrium–Awakening-like lower units; words such as “three facilities” alone do not earn Level 3. If full-stack inspection was unavailable, mark Level 3 **not assessable**, not a failure.
- [ ] Preserve spoiled, assisted, interrupted, early-correct, and non-assessable cases with reason. Do not silently delete them or pool pilots into formal outcome counts. Arrange independent coding of formal recordings/sketches; resolve disagreements using frames and quotes, not moderator recollection.

## 4. Pilot versus formal decision boundary

| Stage | What it checks | Allowed conclusion and next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Two logistics pilots: A-first and B-first** | Can staff deliver neutral prompts, identify H2 and the fork, record clean T0–T3 timing, measure actual first-view exposure, separate atrium-only from complete-stack opportunity, preserve usable sketches/recording, and identify hints or technical faults? | **Process ready / repair and repeat / prototype issue for separate investigation.** Use the [pilot result review guide](../../docs/world/TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_PILOT_RESULT_REVIEW_GUIDE.md). A participant's correct or wrong answer is at most a diagnostic hypothesis, not validation. Repeat an affected pilot after a material process correction; repeat both if comparability changes. |
| **Formal blind sessions, after pilot sign-off** | Among clean unbriefed players, does a participant-generated one-changing-place model arise (`G1`), and does a first adequate proof view independently replace it with an evidence-backed model of three complete fixed units (`G2`)? | Analyze with the [result template](../../docs/world/TRIPLE_COEXISTENCE_PLAYTEST_RESULT_ANALYSIS_TEMPLATE.md) and scoring revision. Report all recruited/eligible/clean denominators, early-correct and contaminated cases, actual A/B exposure, and Level 0–3/V evidence. The first wave is exploratory design triage, not population proof. |

**Technical validation** means the isolated experiment can run and its geometry/traversal can present the intended evidence. **Human validation** means clean, unbriefed players actually make the intended cognitive reversal from physical evidence. Neither pilot completion nor a correct phrase establishes the latter; it remains **UNVALIDATED** until formal results are collected and analyzed.

## 5. Closeout checklist — before ending each session

- [ ] Screen and audio files saved and playable; sketch images legible; clocks/IDs match; no identifiable information in the gameplay record.
- [ ] H0/H1/H2 visits, T1 baseline, T2 return, first adequate view, T3 lock, and T4 diagnostic are timestamped; missing events have a reason.
- [ ] Participant's pre-proof and first-view spatial explanations are captured **verbatim** with sketches/predictions, or explicitly marked unavailable. Spontaneous, neutral-elicited, and moderator-influenced statements are distinguishable.
- [ ] Assigned versus actual first view, atrium-cue versus complete-stack opportunity, exposure duration, and crossover timing are recorded.
- [ ] All controls/route help, moderator hints, visible staff material, early sightline leaks, recording faults, and discomfort are logged with time and affected measure; **do not simply tick “no contamination” from memory**.
- [ ] Each issue is provisionally categorized as procedure, reproducible prototype, possible comprehension, mixed, or unknown. No-view/navigation and unavailable full-stack evidence are not coded as comprehension failures.
- [ ] Pilot only: route the case to the pilot review guide; do **not** score puzzle success. Formal only: retain raw data for later independent coding; do **not** announce a session-level or overall human-validation result on the spot.
- [ ] Participant debrief completed after T4, future-session secrecy requested, files stored under anonymous ID, consent record separated, and scene closed/relaunched before next participant.

If any checkpoint cannot be completed, preserve the partial record, state exactly which inference is compromised, and use the appropriate pilot/protocol decision process. Do not fill gaps with a moderator's assumption or change the prototype during a test wave.
