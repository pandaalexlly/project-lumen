## 1. Purpose and limits

Distinguish a participant who reaches outside from one who predicts and explains the travelling boundary, fixed surroundings, observer constraints and their own inclusion.

This is a physical-hypothesis interview with stateful paper exploration, not a quiz about the design document. The participant chooses actions. Do not impose an architecture-first, observation-first or exterior-first tour.

The linework cards are **schematic visual evidence**, not measured camera renders. They omit designer classifications, dimensions, ghost destinations and arrows. Use the dimensioned proof for adjudication, not apparent distances in the sketches. They can test comparisons and hypotheses; they cannot certify attention, atmosphere, realistic spatial perception or the full rendered reveal.

Known limitations to record:

- P2 certifies body concealment only within its dimensioned stance/head range, not arbitrary views or human comfort.
- Side boarding now has a 0.35 m obstacle margin; whether the structural tongue reads as direct support remains unvalidated.
- A low-angle exterior collar shadow can be visible on the fixed hood. Under S2 this is not an observer; conspicuous watched discontinuity remains a presentation concern requiring the actual-lighting check.
- Cards are not a continuous first-person view and may make shape matching easier than the final game.
- Fixed cover looks broadly similar between placements; this may itself suggest transportation.
- Some freely requested views are not illustrated. Never invent a favorable visibility result to keep a session moving.

## 3. Participant-facing introduction

Read only the following, then allow exploration:

> You are exploring an abandoned research facility. Earlier, you saw a Cube change position when it was no longer watched. Looking at it kept it still; a working Beam could also keep it still.
>
> You can describe where you walk, what you look at and which reachable controls you use. I will show what you can see from there. You can revisit places and try things again. Please tell me what you are thinking, when you want to.
>
> What would you like to do?

These are the prior-knowledge assumptions requested for this study, not a claim that the current Awakening build teaches all of them.

Do not add the words “assembly,” “coherent,” “passenger,” “candidate,” “entangled,” “exclusion,” “receiver,” “exit configuration” or “fixed cover” to the participant introduction or subsequent prompts. If a participant introduces a term, you may quote it back without endorsing it.

If asked about controls, describe only visible physical affordances: movement, looking, reachable source power and aim controls. No jump, crouch, carrying or special travel interaction is available. Do not offer “try looking away,” “stand on it,” “watch the frame” or “keep that Beam on.”

Do not state an explicit mission such as “use the machine to escape.” Curiosity and interpretation are what the test measures.

## 5. Stateful adjudication and reveal timing

### At every action

Privately log:

1. Actual position, view and grounded support after the requested movement.
2. Which actual current surfaces are seen, including deck/collar, and which prospective placements would be observed.
3. Actual source state and reach.
4. Whether the opportunity is armed, pending or spent.
5. Legal complete alternatives, including body/camera/source-support safety.
6. Which card or neutral physical description is justified.

A card about an empty location never rearms an object elsewhere. The participant remembering a previous image never holds it. Looking at the real current surface, or a reaching Beam, does.

### When changes occur

- A viewed current member or current Beam holds the object. Do not move it because the participant says “I want it to move.”
- Loss of every current observer starts the ordinary interval. For the paper model use the current shared 0.05 s release lead-in plus 0.2 s hidden grace; do not announce these numbers as a puzzle code.
- A return of observation cancels the pending release.
- After the intervals, choose among the actually legal alternatives. With no legal alternative, wait without consuming the opportunity.
- On a valid change, update the entire object and a valid supported player atomically, then mark the opportunity spent.
- **Reveal only the view the participant currently has.** If they are looking at an opaque surface, keep the opaque-surface card. Do not say “you moved,” display the destination diagram or turn their view for them.
- Show an absence, new surrounding relationship or actual outside view only when their chosen look/walk makes it visible.
- A supported arrival is not yet a required dismount command. Let them choose their next action.
- Accept a lawful early outside arrival and a safe walk onto onward ground. No understanding check prevents completion.

Do not present before/after object states simultaneously unless the participant is expressly reconstructing them in the post-session interview. During exploration they have memory, not an omniscient board.

### Handling known failures and unspecified actions

D10 records an exterior cast shadow, not P1's removed deck-strip failure. Under TASK-034.3/S2 that shadow supplies no hold, exclusion, rearm or arrival veto; seeing an actual participating edge still does. The source ray remains a presentation stress case, not a required light in the authored plan. Do not show blank P05 to hide a known visible effect or fabricate a projection from an unauthored source. Record unresolved lighting/view limitations and pause that branch as below. Do not explain the contract distinction to the participant as a solution hint.

Use the revised side landing and actual support footprint. Do not snap a participant into the certified stance or assume they chose it merely by saying “get on.” A paper traversal still does not validate walking comfort or prove that they identified which surface supports them.

For a requested view/stance not resolved by the proof:

- ask neutrally, “Where are you looking from, and in which direction?”
- if still unresolved, record “spatially unadjudicated,” retain the actual state and pause that branch;
- allow another participant-chosen investigation, or end the session if blocked;
- do not fabricate a no-move result as if it were a world rule, and do not pretend uncertain geometry allows travel.

Loosely supported cargo, partial footprints and rider-only obstructions belong to optional post-exploration counterfactual checks, not scripted obstacles introduced during the first discovery. Do not place the hidden test blocker during a participant's ordinary attempt.

## 6. Neutral questions and intervention discipline

During exploration, use sparingly:

- “What do you think you are looking at?”
- “What would you like to do next?”
- “What do you expect to happen?”
- “What did you notice?”
- “What, if anything, would you try differently?”

Ask predictions before resolving an action when possible, but do not interrupt every look or demand a theory. Silence and spontaneous experiments are evidence.

After exploration:

- “How would you explain what happened in your own words?”
- “Which earlier detail, if any, means something different to you now?”
- “What evidence changed your mind?”
- “What are you still uncertain about?”
- “What would you investigate next?”

Do not ask “Did you realize the frame is part of the Cube?” or “Why does watching the empty place exclude it?” Those questions provide the missing model.

If a participant has introduced a hypothesis, you may ask for a new prediction using their terms. Point to an already encountered surface without naming its classification, ask what they expect, and record the response as a **prompted transfer check**, separate from spontaneous understanding. Do not use new terms to make the hypothesis correct.

No solution hints are scheduled. If a facilitator inadvertently gives one, quote it exactly, timestamp it and mark all subsequent relevant evidence as assisted. An unassisted discovery score cannot be recovered by asking the participant to repeat the supplied answer.

## 8. Completion versus understanding

Report the two independently.

**Completion:** reached outside and chose safe onward ground / arrived outside but did not leave / incomplete / spatially inconclusive. Include time and the exact source/view/support state.

**Understanding evidence:** for each dimension below mark spontaneous, neutrally prompted, assisted, contradicted or not observed, with a timestamp and quote.

| Dimension | Evidence that counts | Insufficient evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object boundary | Predicts that a non-Cube member holds the same change | Merely calling it a machine, lift or assembly |
| Fixed versus travelling | Identifies an unchanged reference and a carried feature | Reporting only that “the room changed” |
| Current observation | Accounts for direct sight and reaching sources, including while boarded | Blindly toggling until something happens |
| Candidate constraint | Predicts what happens when an empty prospective part is watched | Remembering a button order |
| Inclusion | Predicts supported versus safely independent outcomes | An accidental first ride |
| Exterior relationship | Links the earlier seen fit/identity to an expected outside placement | First recognizing outside after arrival |
| Reinterpretation | Describes which earlier architectural assumption changed and why | Repeating facilitator vocabulary |

A person may complete accidentally and understand later; record the timeline. A person may form the correct model but be stopped by defective cover or the paper interface. Do not mark that as lack of understanding. “Quantum elevator” is acceptable terminology if the predictions demonstrate the relationships.
